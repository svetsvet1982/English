# THE CORNER HOUSE — 5부 90화 (최종화)

## A Regular Tuesday at The Hook (더 훅의 평범한 화요일)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 5부 18화(통산 90화, 최종화) · 시기: 2027년 6월 8일(화) 오전 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 바 The Hook (밴 브런트 스트리트 모퉁이, '로더 훅 협동조합' 건물 1층)
> - 등장인물: GINA, TASHA, EDDIE, MARCUS, LUIS, JESSE, NORA, MRS. ESPOSITO, TOMMY, LUCA
> - 사건: 1화와 같은 화요일 오전. 이번에는 토미 대신 지나가 문을 열고, 타샤와 함께 칠판을 내건다. 커피는 이제 타샤가 끓인다. 11시 정각, 에디가 들어와 같은 말을 한다. "그냥 커피. 평일이잖아." 마커스는 루이스와 함께 온다. 제시는 다음 주 3B 추첨을 기다린다. 11시 40분, 처음 보는 젊은 여자 노라가 문 안을 들여다본다. 1년 전의 제시처럼. 이번에는 제시가 말한다. "그 스툴만 빼고요. 에디 자리예요." 부인이 간판 위치를 지적하러 들어오고, 화요일이라 쉬어야 하는 토미가 루카와 부두로 가는 길에 문 앞을 지나간다.
> - 학습 포인트: 1화 표현의 재낭독과 확장(Can I get…? / Are you open? / How ya doin'? / Some things should never change.), 1년의 변화를 담은 대비, 총정리
> - 미국 현안: 1년 전과 같은 물가 걱정(맥주 8달러, 라임, 팁 화면) 속에서 달라진 것 — 안정된 임대료, 주민 소유, 이웃 공동체
> - 연결: 1화 전체(10:45 개점 준비, 칠판, 커피 농담, 8달러 맥주, 1974년 50센트, 에디 스툴, 마커스 '그럼 기분이나 가져', 제시 첫 등장과 팁 화면, 부인과 간판, 'Welcome to The Hook'). 86화 지나 대표 동업자·칠판. 89화 '네 가지는 안 변한다'. 88화 3B 추첨 6/15.

## 대본

[Scene: Tuesday, June 8, 10:45 a.m. The Hook, before opening. Gina behind the bar. Over the register: Sal's hardware sign, a bus map, a framed recipe card, and an old cash register. Tasha comes in.]

TASHA: Morning, Gina. You're here early.
GINA: I'm always here early on Tuesdays now. You're here on time. That's not new anymore.
TASHA: Ha ha. Very funny. What's that smell?
GINA: Coffee. You made a fresh pot. Ten minutes ago. Remember?
TASHA: Oh, right. That's coffee. Real coffee. Not a crime. Okay. Help me with the board.
GINA: What are we writing today?
TASHA: Same as always. Wings, burgers, fish tacos. Abuela's empanadas. And the beer prices.
GINA: Wait. Don't erase the eight.
TASHA: Why not? Did the distributor raise prices again?
GINA: He did. Fifty cents a keg. But we're not changing the board.
TASHA: We're not? Your dad would change it. He'd make the eight a nine. A small nine.
GINA: Our rent didn't go up this year. It's fixed. Seven thousand. So the beer stays at eight. That's my decision.
TASHA: People are gonna be happy, you know.
GINA: People are never happy. Then they order another one.
TASHA: Okay. "Pint of Bud, eight dollars. Same as last year." Like that?
GINA: Nice. Make the "same as last year" a little bigger.
TASHA: Gina, a big "same" is still the same.
GINA: I know. But it looks friendlier.
TASHA: What time is it?
GINA: Ten fifty-eight. Two minutes. Put the board outside. Not in the middle. Remember Dolores.

[Scene: 11:00 a.m. The door opens.]

EDDIE: Morning, Gina. Morning, Tasha.
GINA: Eddie! Right on time. Eleven o'clock on the dot.
EDDIE: I've been on time for eighty-four years. I'm not stopping now.
TASHA: Hi, Eddie. How ya doin'?
EDDIE: Not bad, sweetheart. My knee hurts, but my heart works. And I caught... well, Diego caught a fish.
GINA: The usual?
EDDIE: Just coffee. It's a weekday.
TASHA: Are you sure? I made it.
EDDIE: Oh. In that case, no milk. Black. Like a man with good luck.
EDDIE: What's this? Eight dollars for a beer? Still?
GINA: Don't start, Eddie. It didn't go up. That's good news.
EDDIE: In nineteen seventy-four, a beer was fifty cents on this corner.
TASHA: And in nineteen seventy-four, you had hair.
EDDIE: I had beautiful hair. Ask Peggy. Ask the lake.
GINA: Your coffee. Black. From Tasha.
EDDIE: Thank you. How much is the coffee? Did that go up?
GINA: Coffee's two bucks. Same as last year. Same as next year.
EDDIE: Good. Some things should never change.
TASHA: Like you on that stool.
EDDIE: Exactly. This is my stool. It knows me. It's known me for twenty-one years. And now I own one fifth of the floor under it.

[Marcus comes in from the bike shop next door. Luis follows him in a Bell Bikes T-shirt.]

MARCUS: Yo, Gina. You open?
GINA: Since eleven. Come in, Marcus. Hi, Luis.
MARCUS: Can I get two coffees to go? Black, no sugar. One for me. One for my employee.
TASHA: Are you feeling brave today?
MARCUS: I don't need to be brave anymore. Tasha makes the coffee.
LUIS: Can I pay for mine? It's my second week. I want to buy my own coffee.
GINA: Two dollars, Luis. Welcome to the club.
LUIS: Here. Keep the change.
GINA: You gave me two dollars. There's no change.
LUIS: Then keep the feeling. Marcus taught me that.
EDDIE: Busy morning at the shop?
MARCUS: Busy morning. Six delivery guys are already waiting outside. For the free battery checks.
EDDIE: At eleven? What happened?
LUIS: Nothing happened. That's the point. Now they come before something happens. Not after.
GINA: Tell 'em to come in for wings later. I'll give 'em a deal.
MARCUS: Okay, gotta go. See you later.
EDDIE: Don't work too hard, kids.
MARCUS: Too late, Eddie. But now it's Monday off. Every Monday.

[Scene: 11:20 a.m. Jesse comes in with his laptop. He sits next to Eddie's stool.]

JESSE: Morning, everybody. One week. One week until the lottery. I'm not nervous.
TASHA: You're shaking the stool, Jesse.
JESSE: I'm shaking with excitement. Not nerves. Technically different.
EDDIE: Two hundred and forty applications. Diego says point four percent.
GINA: Whatever happens, the first dinner is free. I promised.
JESSE: And if we lose, Tess says we'll apply again. Next year. There's always another lottery.
EDDIE: That's the spirit. "Not this time" is not "never."

[Scene: 11:40 a.m. A young woman with a backpack looks in through the door.]

NORA: Hi. Um, are you open?
TASHA: We're open. Come on in. Sit anywhere.
NORA: Thanks. Is it okay to sit at the bar?
JESSE: Sure. Just not on that stool. That's Eddie's.
EDDIE: Hello, young lady.
NORA: Oh, hi. Sorry. I'm Nora.
EDDIE: Eddie. I live upstairs. Three A.
NORA: No way. I just moved in next door. The building on the left. My first week in New York. From Pittsburgh.
TASHA: Oh, you're the new tenant! In the Lopez building! The third floor!
NORA: Yeah. How do you know?
TASHA: Honey, I know everything on this block.
JESSE: It's true. She's better than the news. I learned that a year ago. On that exact stool.
NORA: Okay. Good to know. Can I see a menu?
GINA: The menu's on the board. Right outside.
NORA: Oh. I just saw it. A beer is eight dollars?
EDDIE: That's what I said! Last year!
GINA: Do you want a beer at eleven forty?
NORA: No, no. Just asking. Can I get a breakfast sandwich?
GINA: Bacon, egg, and cheese?
NORA: Yes, please. On a roll.
GINA: That's nine fifty. Same as last year. Eggs are still expensive. But we didn't raise it.
NORA: Everything's expensive. Can I pay with a card?
TASHA: Sure. Here. Tap it right there.
NORA: Okay... Wait. It's asking for a tip. Twenty, twenty-five, thirty percent?
TASHA: That's the machine. Don't look at me.
NORA: Thirty percent? For a sandwich?
GINA: You don't have to tip on a sandwich. Press "No tip."
NORA: I'll do twenty. Is that normal?
TASHA: That's very normal. You're my favorite customer now.
EDDIE: She says that to everybody. She said it to Jesse. A year ago.
NORA: So, how long has this bar been here?
GINA: Twenty-one years. My parents opened it. In two thousand six.
NORA: Twenty-one years? That's a long time. Is it still yours? With the rents in this city?
GINA: It's ours. The building is ours too. The people who live in it bought it. In April. Look at the sign. Over the door next to us.
NORA: "Roode Hoek Co-op." I saw it. I thought it was a Dutch bakery.
EDDIE: It's a long story. I'll tell you. It's free. I tip in stories.

[Scene: 12:05 p.m. The door opens again. Mrs. Esposito, with her clipboard. Tasha has gone to the kitchen. Eddie tells Jesse about the lake at the end of the bar.]

MRS. ESPOSITO: Gina. Excuse me. Your sign is on the sidewalk again.
GINA: Good morning to you too, Dolores.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Good morning. It's in the middle. People can't walk. Walter's going to write it in the minutes.
GINA: It's a small sign. It's two feet wide.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Two feet is two feet. Mrs. Lopez has a stroller. And a new tenant on her third floor.
NORA: That's me. Hi. I'm the new tenant.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Oh! Welcome! I'm Dolores. 2B. In the co-op. Right next door to you. If you need anything, knock. Twice.
NORA: I'll move the sign. I'm closest to the door. No problem.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Thank you, dear. You're a good girl.
NORA: I'm twenty-six, but thank you.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And Gina? Eight dollars for a beer? Still? Shame on you. I know it didn't go up. I'm complaining anyway.
TOMMY: Morning, everybody! Don't worry. I'm not working. It's Tuesday. I'm just walking by.
LUCA: Nonno is taking me to the pier! To fish! Like Diego!
GINA: Dad. It's Tuesday. Don't come in.
TOMMY: I'm not in. I'm in the doorway. The doorway doesn't count.
GINA: A regular Tuesday, Dad. Finally. How does it feel?
TOMMY: Strange. Wonderful. Like the first day. Twenty-one years ago.
NORA: Is it always like this here?
GINA: Every day. Every single day. Welcome to The Hook.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **1화 표현의 재낭독** — 같은 문장이 1년 뒤 다른 사람의 입에서 나온다.
  - *You're here early. / Are you open? / Sit anywhere. / Just not on that stool. That's Eddie's.*
  - *How ya doin'? / Not bad, sweetheart. My knee hurts, but my heart works.*
  - *Just coffee. It's a weekday.* — 에디의 1년 내내 변하지 않은 주문.
  - *Then keep the feeling.* — 마커스에서 루이스로 넘어간 농담.
  - *Is it always like this here? — Every day. Every single day. Welcome to The Hook.* — 토미에서 지나로.
- **1년의 변화를 담은 대비**
  - *It didn't go up. That's good news.* — 1화의 '또 올랐어'와 반대.
  - *Same as last year. Same as next year.* — 안정된 임대료가 만든 안정된 가격.
  - *I don't need to be brave anymore.* — 커피를 마시는 데 용기가 필요 없어졌다.
  - *Now they come before something happens. Not after.* — 사고 뒤가 아니라 사고 전에 오는 배달 기사들.
  - *"Not this time" is not "never."* — "이번엔 아니다"는 "절대 아니다"가 아니다.
- **The doorway doesn't count.** — "문간은 안 쳐." 화요일에 바에 들어오지 않겠다는 약속을 지키려는 토미의 변명.
- **I know it didn't go up. I'm complaining anyway.** — "안 오른 거 알아. 그래도 불평할 거야." 변하지 않는 부인.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 이 화는 1화를 옆에 두고 함께 읽는 화다. 같은 대사는 1화와 같은 리듬으로, 바뀐 단어(*Gina*, *Tasha*, *Luis*, *Nora*)에서 살짝 힘을 준다.
- 1화 A2~B1의 짧은 문장 위에, 1년 동안 쌓인 B2~C1의 문장(*Now they come before something happens. Not after.*)이 얹혀 있다. 두 층을 구분해 읽는다.
- 에디의 *Just coffee. It's a weekday.*는 1화, 74화와 똑같이. 90화 동안 변하지 않은 문장이다.
- 노라의 대사는 1화 제시처럼 조심스럽고 머뭇거리게.
- 마지막 지나의 *Every day. | Every single day. | Welcome to The Hook.* — 토미의 1화 마지막 대사를 딸이 그대로 받는다. 천천히, 미소 지으며. 시리즈의 마지막 문장.
