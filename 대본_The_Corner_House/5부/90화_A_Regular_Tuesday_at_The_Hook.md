# THE CORNER HOUSE — 5부 90화 (최종화)

## A Regular Tuesday at The Hook (더 훅의 평범한 화요일)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 5부 18화(통산 90화, 최종화) · 시기: 2027년 6월 8일(화) 오전 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 바 The Hook (밴 브런트 스트리트 모퉁이, '로더 훅 협동조합' 건물 1층)
> - 등장인물: GINA, TASHA, EDDIE, MARCUS, LUIS, JESSE, NORA, MRS. ESPOSITO, TOMMY, LUCA
> - 사건: 1화와 같은 화요일 오전. 이번에는 토미 대신 지나가 문을 열고, 타샤와 함께 칠판을 내건다. 커피는 이제 타샤가 끓인다. 11시 정각, 에디가 들어와 같은 말을 한다. "그냥 커피. 평일이잖아." 마커스는 루이스와 함께 온다. 제시는 다음 주 3B 추첨을 기다린다. 11시 40분, 처음 보는 젊은 여자 노라가 문 안을 들여다본다. 1년 전의 제시처럼. 이번에는 제시가 말한다. "그 스툴만 빼고요. 에디 자리예요." 부인이 간판 위치를 지적하러 들어오고, 화요일이라 쉬어야 하는 토미가 루카와 부두로 가는 길에 문 앞을 지나간다.
> - 학습 포인트: 1화 표현의 재낭독과 확장(Can I get…? / Are you open? / How ya doin'? / Some things in this neighborhood should never change.), 1년의 변화를 담은 대비, 총정리
> - 미국 현안: 1년 전과 같은 물가 걱정(맥주 8달러, 라임, 팁 화면) 속에서 달라진 것 — 안정된 임대료, 주민 소유, 이웃 공동체
> - 연결: 1화 전체(10:45 개점 준비, 칠판, 커피 농담, 8달러 맥주, 1974년 50센트, 에디 스툴, 마커스 '그럼 기분이나 가져', 제시 첫 등장과 팁 화면, 부인과 간판, 'Welcome to The Hook'). 86화 지나 대표 동업자·칠판. 89화 '네 가지는 안 변한다'. 88화 3B 추첨 6/15.

## 대본

[Scene: Tuesday, June 8, 10:45 a.m. The Hook, before opening. Gina behind the bar. Over the register: Sal's hardware sign, a bus map, a framed recipe card, and an old cash register. Tasha comes in.]

TASHA: Morning, Gina. You're here early today.
GINA: I'm always here early on Tuesdays now, because Tuesdays are mine. You're here on time, and that's not the new part anymore.
TASHA: Ha ha, very funny. What's that smell in here?
GINA: It's coffee, Tasha. You made a fresh pot ten minutes ago, before you went to get the lemons. Remember?
TASHA: Oh, right. That's coffee, real coffee, and not a crime. Okay, can you help me with the board?
GINA: Sure. What are we writing on it today?
TASHA: The same as always. Wings, burgers, fish tacos, Abuela's empanadas, and the beer prices.
GINA: Wait a second, Tasha. Don't erase the eight, because it's staying exactly where it is.
TASHA: Why not? Did the distributor raise his prices again? A year ago, I stood right here and said, "Since when? Last week it was seven."
GINA: He did raise them, by fifty cents a keg. But we're not changing the board this time.
TASHA: We're not? Your dad would change it in a second. He'd turn the eight into a nine, and he'd make it a very small nine.
GINA: Our rent didn't go up this year, because it's fixed at seven thousand now. So the beer stays at eight dollars, and that's my decision as the boss.
TASHA: People are gonna be happy about it, you know. They might even say thank you.
GINA: People are never happy, not even when the price stays the same. Then they order another one.
TASHA: Okay. "Pint of Bud, eight dollars. Same as last year." Do you like it like that?
GINA: Nice. But can you make the "same as last year" a little bigger, so people see it from the street?
TASHA: Gina, a big "same" is still the same. It's exactly as same as a small one.
GINA: I know, but it looks friendlier that way. My dad taught me that trick, only backward.
TASHA: What time is it now?
GINA: It's ten fifty-eight. We have two minutes, so put the board outside, but not in the middle of the sidewalk. Remember Dolores.

[Scene: 11:00 a.m. The door opens.]

EDDIE: Morning, Gina. Morning, Tasha.
GINA: Eddie! You're right on time. Eleven o'clock on the dot, like every other morning.
EDDIE: I've been on time for eighty-four years, and I'm not going to stop now, even though I'm a homeowner.
TASHA: Hi, Eddie. How ya doin' this morning?
EDDIE: Not bad, sweetheart. My knee hurts, but my heart still works. And last week I caught... well, to be honest, Diego caught a fish, but I was holding the worms.
GINA: Do you want the usual?
EDDIE: Just coffee, please. It's a weekday.
TASHA: Are you sure? I made it myself this morning, with the good beans.
EDDIE: Oh. In that case, I'll take it black, with no milk at all. I'll drink it black, like a man with good luck.
EDDIE: Wait, what's this on the board? Eight dollars for a beer? Still, after a whole year?
GINA: Please don't start, Eddie. It didn't go up, and that's good news for once.
EDDIE: In nineteen seventy-four, a beer was fifty cents on this corner.
TASHA: And in nineteen seventy-four, you still had hair.
EDDIE: I had beautiful hair. You can ask anybody on this block. Ask Peggy, or ask the lake, because the lake saw it all.
GINA: Here's your coffee, black, and made by Tasha. You're a lucky man.
EDDIE: Thank you. How much is the coffee? Did that go up too?
GINA: Coffee's still two bucks. It's the same as last year, and it'll be the same next year, too.
EDDIE: Good. Some things in this neighborhood should never change.
TASHA: Like you on that stool every morning.
EDDIE: Exactly. This is my stool, and it knows me, because it's known me for twenty-one years. And now I own one fifth of the floor under it.

[Marcus comes in from the bike shop next door. Luis follows him in a Bell Bikes T-shirt.]

MARCUS: Yo, Gina. Are you open yet?
GINA: We've been open since eleven. Come in, Marcus. Hi, Luis, how's the second week going?
MARCUS: Can I get two coffees to go? Black, with no sugar. One is for me, and one is for my employee, because I'm a boss now.
TASHA: Wow. Are you feeling brave today?
MARCUS: I don't need to be brave anymore, Tasha, because you make the coffee now. Tommy's coffee needed bravery, but yours just needs a cup.
LUIS: Can I pay for mine? It's my second week, and I want to buy my own coffee with my own paycheck.
GINA: That's two dollars, Luis. Welcome to the club, because everybody here pays for their own coffee eventually.
LUIS: Here you go. Keep the change.
GINA: You gave me exactly two dollars. There's no change.
LUIS: Then keep the feeling. Marcus taught me that one on my first day.
EDDIE: Is it a busy morning at the shop?
MARCUS: It's a busy morning, but the good kind. Six delivery guys are already waiting outside my door for the free battery checks.
EDDIE: At eleven o'clock? What happened? Was there an accident?
LUIS: Nothing happened, and that's the point. Now they come in before something happens, and not after, when it's too late.
GINA: Tell 'em to come in for wings later. I'll give 'em a good deal, the same deal my dad always gave.
MARCUS: Okay, I gotta go. See you all later, and save us two stools for lunch.
EDDIE: Don't work too hard, kids.
MARCUS: It's too late for that, Eddie. But at least now I take Mondays off, every single Monday, just like I promised at New Year's.

[Scene: 11:20 a.m. Jesse comes in with his laptop. He sits next to Eddie's stool.]

JESSE: Morning, everybody. It's one week, just one week until the lottery, and I want everyone to know that I'm not nervous at all.
TASHA: You're shaking the stool, Jesse. I can see it from here.
JESSE: I'm shaking with excitement, not with nerves. Technically, those are two different things, and my body knows the difference.
EDDIE: There are two hundred and forty applications, Jesse. Diego says that's point four percent, and he did the math on a napkin.
GINA: But whatever happens, the first dinner is free for whoever moves in. I promised that, and I keep my promises.
JESSE: And if we lose, Tess says we'll just apply again next year. She says there's always another lottery, and she's usually right about things.
EDDIE: That's the spirit, son. "Not this time" is not "never." I waited fifty-nine years for one fish, so you can wait one year for an apartment.

[Scene: 11:40 a.m. A young woman with a backpack looks in through the door.]

NORA: Hi. Um, are you open right now?
TASHA: We're open. Come on in and sit anywhere you like.
NORA: Thanks. Is it okay if I sit at the bar, or is that only for regulars?
JESSE: Sure, you can sit at the bar. Just not on that stool, because that's Eddie's.
EDDIE: Hello there, young lady. Don't worry, I don't bite, and neither does the stool.
NORA: Oh, hi. Sorry, I didn't mean to stare. I'm Nora.
EDDIE: I'm Eddie. I live upstairs, in Three A, and I've lived there since nineteen seventy-four.
NORA: No way. I just moved in next door, in the building on the left. It's my first week in New York, and I'm from Pittsburgh.
TASHA: Oh, you're the new tenant in the Lopez building! You're on the third floor, right?
NORA: Yeah, I am. How do you know that already? I only moved in on Saturday.
TASHA: Honey, I know everything that happens on this block.
JESSE: It's true. She's better than the news. I learned that a year ago, sitting on this exact stool, and I was on a couch back then.
NORA: Okay, that's good to know. Can I see a menu, please?
GINA: The menu's on the board, right outside the door.
NORA: Oh, I just saw it on my way in. A beer is eight dollars here?
EDDIE: That's exactly what I said! I said it last year, and I said it again this morning!
GINA: Do you really want a beer at eleven forty in the morning, on your first week?
NORA: No, no, I was just asking, because back home it's cheaper. Can I get a breakfast sandwich?
GINA: Sure. Bacon, egg, and cheese?
NORA: Yes, please. On a roll, if you have one.
GINA: That's nine fifty, the same as last year. Eggs are still really expensive, but we decided not to raise it.
NORA: Everything's expensive these days, especially here. Can I pay with a card?
TASHA: Sure you can. Here, just tap it right there.
NORA: Okay... wait a minute. It's asking for a tip. Twenty, twenty-five, or thirty percent?
TASHA: That's the machine, not me. Don't look at me, because I didn't program it.
NORA: Thirty percent? For a sandwich that I haven't even eaten yet?
GINA: You don't have to tip on a sandwich, honestly. Just press "No tip," and nobody here will be offended.
NORA: I'll do twenty percent. Is that normal around here, or is that weird?
TASHA: That's very normal. You're my favorite customer now.
EDDIE: Don't believe her. She says that to everybody. She said it to Jesse a year ago, and look at him now.
NORA: So how long has this bar been here? It looks like it's been here forever.
GINA: It's been twenty-one years. My parents opened it in two thousand six, and my mom named it.
NORA: Twenty-one years? That's a really long time. Is it still yours, with the rents in this city?
GINA: It's ours, and the building is ours too, because the people who live in it bought it in April. Look at the sign over the door next to us.
NORA: "Roode Hoek Co-op." I saw it this morning, and honestly, I thought it was a Dutch bakery.
EDDIE: It's a long story, but I'll tell you, and it's free. Tasha always says I tip in stories, and I'm a very generous tipper.

[Scene: 12:05 p.m. The door opens again. Mrs. Esposito, with her clipboard. Tasha has gone to the kitchen. Eddie tells Jesse about the lake at the end of the bar.]

MRS. ESPOSITO: Gina, excuse me. Your sign is on the sidewalk again.
GINA: Good morning to you too, Dolores.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Good morning. It's in the middle of the sidewalk, and people can't walk. Walter is going to write it in the minutes, you know.
GINA: It's a small sign, Dolores. It's only two feet wide, and it's been out there for twenty-one years.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Two feet is still two feet. Mrs. Lopez has a stroller, you know, and now she has a new tenant on her third floor, too.
NORA: That's me. Hi, I'm the new tenant on the third floor, and I promise I don't have a stroller.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Oh! Welcome to the block! I'm Dolores, in 2B, in the co-op right next door to you. If you need anything at all, knock twice.
NORA: I'll move the sign for you, since I'm the closest to the door. It's no problem.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Thank you, dear. You're a good girl.
NORA: I'm twenty-six, but thank you anyway.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And Gina? Eight dollars for a beer? Still? Shame on you. I know it didn't go up, but I'm complaining anyway, because somebody has to.
TOMMY: Morning, everybody! Don't worry, I'm not working, because it's Tuesday. I'm just walking by on my way to the pier.
LUCA: Nonno is taking me to the pier to go fishing! We're going to catch a fish, just like Diego did!
GINA: Dad, it's Tuesday, and you promised. Don't you dare come in.
TOMMY: I'm not in, sweetheart. I'm standing in the doorway, and everybody knows the doorway doesn't count.
GINA: A regular Tuesday, Dad. You finally have one. So how does it feel?
TOMMY: It's strange, and it's wonderful. It feels like the first day, twenty-one years ago, when your mother and I opened that door.
NORA: Is it always like this in here?
GINA: Every day. Every single day. Welcome to The Hook.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **1화 표현의 재낭독** — 같은 문장이 1년 뒤 다른 사람의 입에서 나온다.
  - *You're here early today. / Are you open right now? / Sit anywhere you like. / Just not on that stool, because that's Eddie's.*
  - *How ya doin' this morning? / Not bad, sweetheart. My knee hurts, but my heart still works.*
  - *Just coffee, please. It's a weekday.* — 에디의 1년 내내 변하지 않은 주문.
  - *Then keep the feeling.* — 마커스에서 루이스로 넘어간 농담.
  - *"Since when? Last week it was seven."* — 1화의 자기 대사를 타샤가 1년 뒤 스스로 인용. / *Some things in this neighborhood should never change.* — 1화와 같은 에디의 문장.
  - *Is it always like this in here?* — *Every day. Every single day. Welcome to The Hook.* — 토미에서 지나로.
- **1년의 변화를 담은 대비**
  - *It didn't go up, and that's good news* — 1화의 '또 올랐어'와 반대.
  - *It's the same as last year, and it'll be the same next year* — 안정된 임대료가 만든 안정된 가격.
  - *I don't need to be brave anymore* — 커피를 마시는 데 용기가 필요 없어졌다.
  - *Now they come in before something happens, and not after* — 사고 뒤가 아니라 사고 전에 오는 배달 기사들.
  - *"Not this time" is not "never."* — "이번엔 아니다"는 "절대 아니다"가 아니다.
- **The doorway doesn't count.** — "문간은 안 쳐." 화요일에 바에 들어오지 않겠다는 약속을 지키려는 토미의 변명.
- **I know it didn't go up, but I'm complaining anyway** — "안 오른 거 알아. 그래도 불평할 거야." 변하지 않는 부인.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 이 화는 1화를 옆에 두고 함께 읽는 화다. 같은 대사는 1화와 같은 리듬으로, 바뀐 단어(*Gina*, *Tasha*, *Luis*, *Nora*)에서 살짝 힘을 준다.
- 1화 A2~B1의 짧은 문장 위에, 1년 동안 쌓인 B2~C1의 문장(*Now they come in before something happens, and not after*)이 얹혀 있다. 두 층을 구분해 읽는다.
- 에디의 *Just coffee, please. It's a weekday.*는 1화와 똑같이. 90화 동안 변하지 않은 문장이다.
- 노라의 대사는 1화 제시처럼 조심스럽고 머뭇거리게.
- 마지막 지나의 *Every day. | Every single day. | Welcome to The Hook.* — 토미의 1화 마지막 대사를 딸이 그대로 받는다. 천천히, 미소 지으며. 시리즈의 마지막 문장.

지나: 올해는 월세가 안 올랐어요. 이제 7천으로 고정이거든요. 그러니까 맥주는 8달러 그대로예요. 사장으로서 제 결정이에요.

지나: 알아요. 그래도 그렇게 쓰면 더 친근해 보이잖아요. 아빠한테 배운 요령이에요. 거꾸로 쓴 거지만요.

지나: 에디! 딱 맞춰 오셨네요. 다른 날 아침처럼 11시 정각이에요.

타샤: 안녕하세요, 에디. 오늘 아침 어떠세요?

에디: 아. 그렇다면 우유는 하나도 넣지 말고 블랙으로. 운 좋은 남자처럼 블랙으로 마실게.
에디: 잠깐, 칠판에 이게 뭐야? 맥주 한 잔에 8달러? 1년이 지났는데 아직도?
지나: 제발 시작하지 마요, 에디. 안 올랐어요. 이번만큼은 좋은 소식이에요.
