# THE CORNER HOUSE — 5부 83화

## Stoop Sale Saturday (스툽 세일 토요일)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 5부 11화(통산 83화) · 시기: 2027년 5월 22일(토) 오전 9시~오후 5시 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 차량을 막은 밴 브런트 스트리트(파이어니어~비지테이션 플레이스) → 오전의 테이블들 → 오후의 바 앞 → 해 질 녘의 스툽
> - 등장인물: TOMMY, MRS. ESPOSITO, DIEGO, RAY, MR. HAYES, DAVID, TEO, JESSE, SAM, ZOE, GINA, EDDIE, MARISOL
> - 사건: 두 번째 스툽 세일. 토미가 사회를 보고, 부인은 마지막으로 회장 배지를 단다. 작년에 부인의 접시를 80달러에 사려던 윌리엄스버그의 빈티지 상인 레이가 다시 와서 살의 금전 등록기를 노린다. 헤이스 씨의 버스 지도는 통째로 팔린다. 작년에 운동화와 레고를 사 간 데이비드는 유리 건물 월세가 또 올라 협동조합에 대해 묻는다. 작년에 앤젤라의 램프를 사 간 조이는 고친 램프 이야기를 한다. 해 질 녘 총액 발표 뒤 부인이 후임을 발표한다. 회장은 월터 헤이스, '청소년 회장'은 디에고다.
> - 학습 포인트: 흥정 복습(How much is this? / Would you take…? / Can you do better? / That's my best price. / Meet me in the middle. / You've got a deal.), 가격 표현과 숫자 연음(twenty-five, a buck fifty, two for five), 13화와의 대비
> - 미국 현안: 동네 중고 거래와 기부, 치솟는 고급 신축 임대료, 지역 공동체의 세대교체, 협동조합에 대한 이웃의 관심
> - 연결: 13화 첫 스툽 세일(데이비드 22달러, 레이 80달러 제안 거절, 조이 램프 5달러, 디에고 41달러). 82화 준비(하버라인 의자, 버스 지도 95장, 금전 등록기, 샘의 액자 스프레드시트, 디에고 수익 절반은 달걀). 81화 부인 마지막 회장, 토미 사회자. 79화 헤이스(월터).

## 대본

[Scene: Saturday, May 22, 9:00 a.m. Van Brunt Street is closed to cars from Pioneer to Visitation Place. Eleven tables. Yellow stickers everywhere. Tommy stands on a milk crate with a lemon in his shirt pocket. Mrs. Esposito wears a badge that says "PRESIDENT."]

TOMMY: Good morning, Red Hook! Welcome to the second annual... well, actually, the forty-ninth annual... Dolores, how many years has it been?
MRS. ESPOSITO: It's been forty-nine years, Tommy, ever since nineteen seventy-eight, when Sal started it.
TOMMY: Welcome to the forty-ninth Van Brunt Street Stoop Sale! As Sal Cappello used to say, "Everything on this block has a price. Except the people." Now go! Buy stuff!
DIEGO: Ray! Ray is here! The vintage guy from last year, the one who wanted the plates!
RAY: Good morning, young man. And the lady with the plates, Mrs. Esposito. Did you change your mind? My offer of eighty dollars still stands.
MRS. ESPOSITO: The plates are not for sale, Mr. Ray. They served twelve people on a roof last August, so now they're retired. With honors.
RAY: That's a pity. Okay, what else have you got? I heard there's a cash register from nineteen seventy, cast iron.
DIEGO: It's not for sale! It's history! It goes in the bar!
RAY: Everything is for sale, kid, for the right price. I'll give you a hundred and fifty.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Not everything, Mr. Ray. Sal said so this morning, through Tommy, so it's official.
RAY: Two hundred, and that's my final offer.
TOMMY: Ray, I'm sorry, but it's not about money. Sal rang up every nail on this block with that machine, so it stays on the corner.
RAY: Fine, fine, you people are very sentimental. What about these bus maps, then? Who's selling these?
MR. HAYES: I am, sir. I'm Walter Hayes, retired B61 driver. It's ninety-five maps, from nineteen ninety-three to two thousand nineteen, and they're two dollars each.
RAY: Two dollars? These are great, and my customers love old New York. How much for the whole box?
MR. HAYES: Ninety-five maps at two dollars each, so that's a hundred and ninety.
RAY: Would you take a hundred?
MR. HAYES: A hundred dollars? That's barely a dollar a map. Can you do better?
RAY: A hundred and twenty. That's my best price, and I'm being generous.
MR. HAYES: Meet me in the middle, a hundred and fifty. And I keep the nineteen ninety-three one, because it has the stop at this corner.
RAY: A hundred and fifty, and you keep one. You've got a deal. You negotiate like a bus driver, you know that?
MR. HAYES: Twenty-six years of passengers saying, "I'll pay you tomorrow." After that long, you learn.
DIEGO: Mrs. Esposito, we made the first sale! A hundred and fifty dollars, and it's not even nine thirty!
MRS. ESPOSITO: Write it in the book, Diego, and write it neatly. Seller, item, price, and the time.
DIEGO: "Walter Hayes. Bus maps. One hundred fifty dollars. Nine twenty-two a.m."
MRS. ESPOSITO: That's very good. You write like a secretary, and that's the highest compliment I give.
RAY: Mrs. Esposito, can I ask you something? Why do you look so happy? Last year, you looked worried the whole day.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Last year, my building was being sold to strangers. This year, my building belongs to us, and that's the difference.
RAY: Well, congratulations to you. In Williamsburg, my building was sold three times in five years, and I don't even know the owner's name.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Then come to our meetings and learn how we did it. It's the first Monday of the month, and you should bring cookies.
RAY: You want cookies for a meeting? Why would I bring cookies?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Bribes, Mr. Ray. Everything has a price. Except the people.

[Scene: 11:30 a.m. The tables near the bar. Jesse runs a small table with his phone and a QR code. Sam stands next to a row of framed spreadsheets. David, in a nice jacket, holds the hand of his son Teo, now eight.]

DAVID: Hey, it's Jesse, right? From last year? You're the lemonade guy, aren't you?
JESSE: David, and Teo too! Look how big you are! Last year, you were a huge dinosaur fan.
TEO: I'm still a dinosaur fan. But now I like space too, because space is bigger.
JESSE: Space is awesome! Then you definitely need this, a rocket lamp. It's three dollars, and it works. Mostly.
DAVID: What exactly does "mostly" mean?
JESSE: It means it lights up, and it makes a little noise, like a spaceship. Or like a fridge, depending on the day.
DAVID: Would you take two dollars?
JESSE: Two fifty, final. It's for the pantry fund, because half of everything goes to eggs.
DAVID: Two fifty, deal, for eggs. Okay, and you're Sam, right? What are all these frames?
SAM: They're spreadsheets, the one we used to buy the building. Ten dollars each, and they're signed.
DAVID: Wait, you bought the building? This building, the one on the corner?
SAM: Five households and a land trust did. April twenty-ninth, at twelve twenty-three p.m.
DAVID: Wow, that's incredible. I read about the boiler in the news, but I didn't know it all worked out. Can I ask you something? Honestly?
SAM: Sure, go ahead and ask me anything.
DAVID: My building raised the rent again, the glass one by the water. My lease is up in August, and they want twelve percent more. That's four thousand five hundred for a one-bedroom.
SAM: Twelve percent? That's a lot, even for this neighborhood.
DAVID: Teo and I can't stay at that price. And I've been thinking. Is there any way... could a building like mine become a co-op?
SAM: A luxury glass building? Honestly, probably not. Those are owned by big investment funds, and they don't sell to tenants.
DAVID: Right, that's pretty much what I thought.
SAM: But our 3B has an open lottery next month, for people under the income limit. If you qualify, you can apply, just like anybody else.
DAVID: I work in finance, so I probably make too much.
SAM: Then you're exactly the kind of person who should buy one of these. It's ten dollars, and you should actually read it. Maybe you'll start a co-op somewhere else.
DAVID: Ha, okay, I'll buy one, for inspiration. Ten dollars, right?
SAM: For you, it's twelve, because you work in finance.
DAVID: That's not how pricing works!
SAM: It is at this table. And don't worry, the two dollars extra go to eggs.
TEO: Dad, can we live here? In the building with the cat?
DAVID: I don't know, buddy. Maybe not, but we can always come and visit.
SAM: There's a dog now too, Mango. And there's a stoop sale every year, so you'll have a reason to come back.
TEO: And Diego lives here! He sold me his sneakers last year, and they still fit. Almost.
JESSE: "Almost." That's just like my rocket lamp. "Mostly."
DAVID: Even the jokes are recycled around here. I really like this block.

[Scene: 2:30 p.m. In front of The Hook. Gina sorts cash in a box. Eddie sits on a folding chair in the shade of the green shed. A young woman with a tool bag stops by.]

ZOE: Excuse me, is Tommy here? I bought a lamp from him last year. It was Angela's lamp.
GINA: Wait, are you Zoe? You're the one who fixed Mom's lamp!
ZOE: That's me, the Ohio girl from the glass building with the lamp hobby. I saw the shed mural on social media, "Bad coffee, good people," and I knew I had to come back.
EDDIE: You fixed Angela's lamp, and now it's behind the bar, up on the shelf. It's the best light in the room.
ZOE: It was a nineteen seventy-eight lamp with a broken socket and a bad cord, so it only took twenty minutes. But I kept thinking about it. Five dollars, "as is," and Tommy almost cried when he sold it.
GINA: He cried even more when you brought it back.
TOMMY: Who's talking about me? Zoe, the lamp doctor herself! Come here, come here!
ZOE: Tommy! I heard you bought the building! Is it true?
TOMMY: Not me, all of us. Five households, a cat, a dog, and a land trust. But what are you doing here?
ZOE: I'm moving out of the glass building, because the rent went up twelve percent. I'm going to Bay Ridge with a roommate.
EDDIE: Twelve percent again? David said the exact same thing this morning.
ZOE: Everybody in my building is leaving. The lobby has a sign now that says, "We'll miss you," with a smiley face. And a new rent.
GINA: That's terrible, and the smiley face makes it even worse.
ZOE: Well, that's New York for you. Anyway, I brought something for the sale, six lamps. I found them on the curb and fixed them all. Can I sell them here, with half for your pantry?
TOMMY: Zoe, you're the most Red Hook person who never lived in Red Hook.
ZOE: Maybe someday I will, if there's ever a lottery.
GINA: Actually, there's one next month, for 3B.
ZOE: Really? Do you know what the income limits are?
GINA: Jesse has the link. He's at the table with the QR code, and he's applying too.
ZOE: Then we'll compete, fairly. With lamps, if we have to.
EDDIE: So you've got six lamps. How much are you asking for each one?
ZOE: Ten dollars, or fifteen for the big one. And they all work. Not "mostly." All of them.
EDDIE: Then I'll take the small one, for Walter. His room has a sewing machine, but it doesn't have a reading light.
ZOE: For Walter? Sorry, who's Walter?
EDDIE: He's the new man in 4C, a retired bus driver who moved in on Thursday. He reads bus maps in bed.
ZOE: Then he definitely needs a good light. Take it, please, for free.
EDDIE: No, ten dollars, because half goes to eggs. That's the rule today.
ZOE: Okay, ten dollars. For eggs, and for Walter.

[Scene: 5:15 p.m. The stoop. The street is open again. Empty tables. A donation truck from the church drives away with leftovers. Mrs. Esposito sits on the top step with the book. Tommy, Diego, Mr. Hayes, and Marisol stand around her.]

MRS. ESPOSITO: Okay, it's time for the final count. Diego, you read it, since you kept the book all day.
DIEGO: Total sales: two thousand, three hundred and forty dollars. That's more than last year, because last year was one thousand six hundred.
TOMMY: That's seven hundred more than last year, in just one day!
DIEGO: And half goes to the pantry for eggs, so that's one thousand one hundred and seventy dollars. Gloria said eggs are four dollars a dozen now. So that's... two hundred and ninety dozen. Almost.
MR. HAYES: Two hundred and ninety dozen eggs. In December, they only had three hundred dozen for the whole week.
MARISOL: So this almost doubles a week's eggs. Diego, that's amazing, honey.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And now it's time for my announcement, as the outgoing president of the Van Brunt Street Block Association, after twenty years.
TOMMY: Drumroll, please! Somebody give us a drumroll!
DIEGO: Brrrrrrrr...
MRS. ESPOSITO: Thank you, Diego. A president needs two things, time and energy. I have time, but I don't have the energy anymore. So I'm choosing two people for one presidency.
MARISOL: Two people? How can you have two people for one president?
MRS. ESPOSITO: The new president is Walter Hayes. He has time, because he's retired, and he drove the B61 for twenty-six years. He knows every stop, every face, and every name on this route.
MR. HAYES: Me? Dolores, I only moved in on Thursday!
MRS. ESPOSITO: And you've already sold a hundred and fifty dollars of maps, and you negotiated with Ray. Nobody negotiates with Ray.
TOMMY: Walter! Congratulations, my friend!
MR. HAYES: I don't know what to say. In my old building, I didn't know one single name, and now I'm the president?
MRS. ESPOSITO: And there's also a youth president, for energy and for the future. Diego Reyes.
DIEGO: Me? Youth president? Is that even a real thing?
MRS. ESPOSITO: It is now, because I just made it. It's in the bylaws of the block association, Article One: "The president shall be assisted by a youth president under the age of eighteen."
DIEGO: I'm a president! Mom! I'm a president!
MARISOL: Mijo... oh my God. Dolores, are you sure about this? He's only eleven.
MRS. ESPOSITO: He kept the book all day and wrote down every sale, and he calculated the eggs. He knows every name, every price, and every rule. He's ready.
TOMMY: Eddie called it last night, you know, because he said it would be Diego. But nobody saw Walter coming, not one of us.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Walter, Diego, here. This is the clipboard, the old one from nineteen ninety-five. I'm keeping the big one for the co-op minutes.
MR. HAYES: Thank you for the clipboard, Dolores. I'll take good care of it, I promise.
DIEGO: Can I hold it too, sometimes?
MR. HAYES: Every other week, so we'll share. It's like a bus route. I drive, you call the stops.
DIEGO: Deal. Next stop: Van Brunt and Pioneer!

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **흥정 표현 복습(13화)**
  - *How much for the whole box?* — "상자째로 얼마예요?"
  - *Would you take a hundred?* — "100에 주실래요?" 낮은 값을 제안할 때.
  - *Can you do better?* — "더 깎아 줄 수 있어요?" / "더 쳐줄 수 있어요?" (사는 쪽, 파는 쪽 모두 쓴다)
  - *That's my best price* — "그게 제 최선의 가격이에요."
  - *Meet me in the middle* — "중간에서 만나요(절충해요)."
  - *You've got a deal.* — "좋아요, 거래 성사."
  - *Final.* — "최종이에요."
- **가격 읽기** — *two fifty* = 2.50달러 / *a hundred and fifty* = 150 / *two thousand, three hundred and forty* = 2,340 / *four dollars a dozen* 한 판에 4달러
- **"Mostly."** — "대체로요." 제시의 정직한 판매 문구. 조이의 *Not "mostly." All of them.*과 대비된다.
- **Everything is for sale, kid, for the right price.** — "값만 맞으면 다 팔아." 레이의 철학. 살의 *Except the people*과 대비.
- **They're retired. With honors.** — "명예롭게 은퇴했어요." 접시를 두고 하는 부인의 말.
- **outgoing president** — 물러나는 회장. *youth president* 청소년 회장.
- **I drive, you call the stops.** — "내가 운전하고, 넌 정류장을 불러." 헤이스 씨의 협업 비유.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 토미의 사회는 크고 활기차게, 축제 시작처럼: *Good morning, Red Hook!*
- 흥정은 핑퐁처럼 빠르게. 숫자에 강세: *A HUNDRED dollars? … A hundred and TWENTY. … Meet me in the MIDDLE*
- 숫자 연음: *two fifty* [투피프티], *a hundred and fifty* [어헌드런피프티].
- 데이비드와 샘의 대화는 진지하게 바뀌는 순간이 있다. *Can I ask you something? Honestly?*에서 목소리를 낮춘다.
- 마지막 발표: 디에고의 드럼 소리, 부인의 *A president needs two things*는 천천히, 연설처럼.
- 디에고의 *Next stop: Van Brunt and Pioneer!*는 버스 안내 방송처럼 또박또박.
