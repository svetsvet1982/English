# THE CORNER HOUSE — 5부 83화

## Stoop Sale Saturday (스툽 세일 토요일)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 5부 11화(통산 83화) · 시기: 2027년 5월 22일(토) 오전 9시~오후 5시 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 차량을 막은 밴 브런트 스트리트(파이어니어~비지테이션 플레이스) → 오전의 테이블들 → 오후의 바 앞 → 해 질 녘의 스툽
> - 등장인물: TOMMY, MRS. ESPOSITO, DIEGO, RAY, MR. HAYES, DAVID, TEO, JESSE, SAM, ZOE, GINA, EDDIE, MARISOL
> - 사건: 두 번째 스툽 세일. 토미가 사회를 보고, 부인은 마지막으로 회장 배지를 단다. 작년에 부인의 접시를 80달러에 사려던 윌리엄스버그의 빈티지 상인 레이가 다시 와서 살의 금전 등록기를 노린다. 헤이스 씨의 버스 지도는 통째로 팔린다. 작년에 운동화와 레고를 사 간 데이비드는 유리 건물 월세가 또 올라 협동조합에 대해 묻는다. 작년에 앤젤라의 램프를 사 간 조이는 고친 램프 이야기를 한다. 해 질 녘 총액 발표 뒤 부인이 후임을 발표한다. 회장은 월터 헤이스, '청소년 회장'은 디에고다.
> - 학습 포인트: 흥정 복습(How much is this? / Would you take…? / Can you do better? / That's my best price. / Meet me in the middle. / You've got a deal.), 가격 표현과 숫자 연음(twenty-five, a buck fifty, two for five), 13화와의 대비
> - 미국 현안: 동네 중고 거래와 기부, 치솟는 고급 신축 임대료, 지역 공동체의 세대교체, 협동조합에 대한 이웃의 관심
> - 연결: 13화 첫 스툽 세일(데이비드 22달러, 레이 80달러 제안 거절, 조이 램프 5달러, 디에고 41달러). 82화 준비(하버라인 의자, 버스 지도 95장, 금전 등록기, 샘의 액자 스프레드시트, 디에고 수익 절반은 달걀). 81화 부인 마지막 회장, 토미 사회자. 79화 헤이스(월터).

## 대본

[Scene: Saturday, May 22, 9:00 a.m. Van Brunt Street is closed to cars from Pioneer to Visitation Place. Eleven tables. Yellow stickers everywhere. Tommy stands on a milk crate with a lemon in his shirt pocket. Mrs. Esposito wears a badge that says "PRESIDENT."]

TOMMY: Good morning, Red Hook! Welcome to the second annual... well, the forty-ninth annual... Dolores, how many years?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Forty-nine. Since nineteen seventy-eight. Sal started it.
TOMMY: Welcome to the forty-ninth Van Brunt Street Stoop Sale! As Sal Cappello used to say: "Everything on this block has a price. Except the people." Now go! Buy stuff!
DIEGO: Ray! Ray is here! The vintage guy! From last year!
RAY: Good morning, young man. And the lady with the plates. Mrs. Esposito. Did you change your mind? Eighty dollars still stands.
MRS. ESPOSITO: The plates are not for sale, Mr. Ray. They served twelve people on a roof last August. They're retired. With honors.
RAY: Pity. Okay. What else? I heard there's a cash register. From nineteen seventy. Cast iron.
DIEGO: It's not for sale! It's history! It goes in the bar!
RAY: Everything is for sale, kid. For the right price. A hundred and fifty.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Not everything, Mr. Ray. Sal said it this morning. Through Tommy.
RAY: Two hundred. Final.
TOMMY: Ray, I'm sorry. It's not about money. Sal rang up every nail on this block with that machine. It stays on the corner.
RAY: Fine. Fine. You people are very sentimental. What about these bus maps? Who's selling these?
MR. HAYES: I am. Walter Hayes. Retired B61 driver. Ninety-five maps. Nineteen ninety-three to twenty nineteen. Two dollars each.
RAY: Two dollars? These are great. My customers love old New York. How much for the whole box?
MR. HAYES: Ninety-five maps at two dollars. That's a hundred and ninety.
RAY: Would you take a hundred?
MR. HAYES: A hundred? That's barely a dollar a map. Can you do better?
RAY: A hundred and twenty. That's my best price.
MR. HAYES: Meet me in the middle. A hundred and fifty. And I keep the nineteen ninety-three one. With the stop at this corner.
RAY: A hundred and fifty, and you keep one. You've got a deal. You negotiate like a bus driver.
MR. HAYES: Twenty-six years of passengers saying "I'll pay you tomorrow." You learn.
DIEGO: Mrs. Esposito! The first sale! A hundred and fifty! Before nine thirty!
MRS. ESPOSITO: Write it in the book, Diego. Neatly. Seller, item, price. And the time.
DIEGO: "Walter Hayes. Bus maps. One hundred fifty dollars. Nine twenty-two a.m."
MRS. ESPOSITO: Good. You write like a secretary. That's the highest compliment I give.
RAY: Mrs. Esposito, can I ask? Why do you look happy? Last year, you looked worried.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Last year, my building was being sold to strangers. This year, my building belongs to us.
RAY: Congratulations. In Williamsburg, my building was sold three times in five years. I don't even know the owner's name.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Then come to our meetings. Learn how. First Monday of the month. Bring cookies.
RAY: Cookies? For a meeting?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Bribes, Mr. Ray. Everything has a price. Except the people.

[Scene: 11:30 a.m. The tables near the bar. Jesse runs a small table with his phone and a QR code. Sam stands next to a row of framed spreadsheets. David, in a nice jacket, holds the hand of his son Teo, now eight.]

DAVID: Hey! Jesse, right? From last year? The lemonade guy?
JESSE: David! And Teo! Look how big you are! Last year, you were a dinosaur fan.
TEO: I'm still a dinosaur fan. But now I like space too.
JESSE: Space! Then you need this. A rocket lamp. Three dollars. It works. Mostly.
DAVID: What does "mostly" mean?
JESSE: It means it lights up. And it makes a little noise. Like a spaceship. Or a fridge.
DAVID: Two dollars?
JESSE: Two fifty. Final. It's for the pantry fund. Half of everything goes to eggs.
DAVID: Two fifty. Deal. For eggs. Okay, Sam, right? What are these frames?
SAM: Spreadsheets. The one we used to buy the building. Ten dollars each. Signed.
DAVID: You bought the building? This building? The one on the corner?
SAM: Five households and a land trust. April twenty-ninth. Twelve twenty-three p.m.
DAVID: Wow. I read about the boiler. In the news. I didn't know it worked out. Can I ask you something? Honestly?
SAM: Sure.
DAVID: My building raised the rent again. The glass one. By the water. My lease is up in August. Twelve percent. Four thousand five hundred for a one-bedroom.
SAM: Twelve percent. That's a lot.
DAVID: Teo and I can't stay. And I've been thinking. Is there any way... could a building like mine become a co-op?
SAM: A luxury glass building? Honestly, probably not. Those are owned by big funds. They don't sell to tenants.
DAVID: Right. That's what I thought.
SAM: But our 3B has an open lottery next month. For people under the income limit. If you qualify, you can apply. Like anybody.
DAVID: I work in finance. I probably make too much.
SAM: Then you're the kind of person who should buy one of these. Ten dollars. And read it. Maybe you'll start a co-op somewhere else.
DAVID: Ha. Okay. I'll buy one. For inspiration. Ten dollars?
SAM: For you, twelve. You work in finance.
DAVID: That's not how pricing works!
SAM: It is at this table. Two dollars extra go to eggs.
TEO: Dad, can we live here? In the building with the cat?
DAVID: I don't know, buddy. Maybe not. But we can visit.
SAM: There's a dog too. Mango. And a stoop sale every year.
TEO: And Diego! He sold me his sneakers last year. They still fit. Almost.
JESSE: "Almost." Like my rocket lamp. "Mostly."
DAVID: Even the jokes are recycled here. I like this block.

[Scene: 2:30 p.m. In front of The Hook. Gina sorts cash in a box. Eddie sits on a folding chair in the shade of the green shed. A young woman with a tool bag stops by.]

ZOE: Excuse me. Is Tommy here? I bought a lamp from him last year. Angela's lamp.
GINA: Zoe? You're Zoe! You fixed Mom's lamp!
ZOE: That's me. Ohio girl. Glass building. Lamp hobby. I saw the shed mural on social media. "Bad coffee, good people." I had to come back.
EDDIE: You fixed Angela's lamp. It's behind the bar now. On the shelf. It's the best light in the room.
ZOE: It was a nineteen seventy-eight lamp. A broken socket. And a bad cord. Twenty minutes. But I kept thinking about it. Five dollars. "As is." Tommy almost cried when he sold it.
GINA: He cried when you brought it back. More.
TOMMY: Who's talking about me? Zoe! The lamp doctor! Come here!
ZOE: Tommy! I heard you bought the building!
TOMMY: Not me. All of us. Five households. A cat. A dog. And a land trust. What are you doing here?
ZOE: I'm moving. Out of the glass building. Rent went up twelve percent. I'm going to Bay Ridge. With a roommate.
EDDIE: Twelve percent. David said the same thing this morning.
ZOE: Everybody in my building is leaving. The lobby has a sign now. "We'll miss you." With a smiley face. And a new rent.
GINA: That's terrible.
ZOE: It's New York. Anyway. I brought something. For the sale. Lamps. Six of them. I fixed them all. From the curb. Can I sell them here? Half for your pantry?
TOMMY: Zoe, you're the most Red Hook person who never lived in Red Hook.
ZOE: Maybe someday. If there's ever a lottery.
GINA: There's one next month. 3B.
ZOE: Really? What are the income limits?
GINA: Jesse has the link. He's at the table with the QR code. He's applying too.
ZOE: Then we'll compete. Fairly. With lamps.
EDDIE: Six lamps. How much each?
ZOE: Ten dollars. Or fifteen for the big one. They all work. Not "mostly." All.
EDDIE: Then I'll take the small one. For Walter. His room has a sewing machine but no reading light.
ZOE: For Walter? Who's Walter?
EDDIE: The new man in 4C. Retired bus driver. Moved in Thursday. He reads bus maps in bed.
ZOE: Then he needs a good light. Take it. For free.
EDDIE: No. Ten dollars. Half goes to eggs. That's the rule today.
ZOE: Okay. Ten dollars. For eggs. And for Walter.

[Scene: 5:15 p.m. The stoop. The street is open again. Empty tables. A donation truck from the church drives away with leftovers. Mrs. Esposito sits on the top step with the book. Tommy, Diego, Mr. Hayes, and Marisol stand around her.]

MRS. ESPOSITO: Okay. Final count. Diego, read it. You kept the book.
DIEGO: Total sales: two thousand, three hundred and forty dollars. That's more than last year. Last year was one thousand six hundred.
TOMMY: Seven hundred more than last year! In one day!
DIEGO: And half goes to the pantry. For eggs. That's one thousand one hundred and seventy dollars. Gloria said eggs are four dollars a dozen now. That's... two hundred and ninety dozen. Almost.
MR. HAYES: Two hundred and ninety dozen eggs. In December, they had three hundred dozen for the whole week.
MARISOL: So this almost doubles a week. Diego, that's amazing.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And now. My announcement. As the outgoing president of the Van Brunt Street Block Association. For twenty years.
TOMMY: Drumroll. Somebody. A drumroll.
DIEGO: Brrrrrrrr...
MRS. ESPOSITO: Thank you, Diego. A president needs two things. Time. And energy. I have time. I don't have energy anymore. So I'm choosing two people. One president.
MARISOL: Two people? One president?
MRS. ESPOSITO: The new president is Walter Hayes. He has time. He's retired. He drove the B61 for twenty-six years. He knows every stop. Every face. Every name on this route.
MR. HAYES: Me? Dolores, I moved in on Thursday!
MRS. ESPOSITO: And you already sold a hundred and fifty dollars of maps. And you negotiated with Ray. Nobody negotiates with Ray.
TOMMY: Walter! Congratulations!
MR. HAYES: I don't know what to say. In my old building, I didn't know one name. Now I'm the president?
MRS. ESPOSITO: And the youth president. For energy. And for the future. Diego Reyes.
DIEGO: Me? Youth president? Is that real?
MRS. ESPOSITO: It is now. I just made it. In the bylaws of the block association. Article one. "The president shall be assisted by a youth president under the age of eighteen."
DIEGO: I'm a president! Mom! I'm a president!
MARISOL: Mijo... oh my God. Dolores, are you sure? He's ten.
MRS. ESPOSITO: He kept the book all day. He wrote every sale. He calculated the eggs. He knows every name. And every price. And every rule. He's ready.
TOMMY: Eddie called it. Last night. He said Diego. But Walter? Nobody saw that coming.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Walter, Diego. Here. The clipboard. The old one. From nineteen ninety-five. I'm keeping the big one. For the co-op minutes.
MR. HAYES: The clipboard. Thank you, Dolores. I'll take care of it.
DIEGO: Can I hold it too? Sometimes?
MR. HAYES: Every other week. We'll share. Like a bus route. I drive, you call the stops.
DIEGO: Deal. Next stop: Van Brunt and Pioneer!

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **흥정 표현 복습(13화)**
  - *How much for the whole box?* — "상자째로 얼마예요?"
  - *Would you take a hundred?* — "100에 주실래요?" 낮은 값을 제안할 때.
  - *Can you do better?* — "더 깎아 줄 수 있어요?" / "더 쳐줄 수 있어요?" (사는 쪽, 파는 쪽 모두 쓴다)
  - *That's my best price.* — "그게 제 최선의 가격이에요."
  - *Meet me in the middle.* — "중간에서 만나요(절충해요)."
  - *You've got a deal.* — "좋아요, 거래 성사."
  - *Final.* — "최종이에요."
- **가격 읽기** — *two fifty* = 2.50달러 / *a hundred and fifty* = 150 / *two thousand, three hundred and forty* = 2,340 / *four dollars a dozen* 한 판에 4달러
- **"Mostly."** — "대체로요." 제시의 정직한 판매 문구. 조이의 *Not "mostly." All.*과 대비된다.
- **Everything is for sale, for the right price.** — "값만 맞으면 다 팔아." 레이의 철학. 살의 *Except the people*과 대비.
- **They're retired. With honors.** — "명예롭게 은퇴했어요." 접시를 두고 하는 부인의 말.
- **outgoing president** — 물러나는 회장. *youth president* 청소년 회장.
- **I drive, you call the stops.** — "내가 운전하고, 넌 정류장을 불러." 헤이스 씨의 협업 비유.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 토미의 사회는 크고 활기차게, 축제 시작처럼: *Good morning, Red Hook!*
- 흥정은 핑퐁처럼 빠르게. 숫자에 강세: *A HUNDRED? … A hundred and TWENTY. … Meet me in the MIDDLE.*
- 숫자 연음: *two fifty* [투피프티], *a hundred and fifty* [어헌드런피프티].
- 데이비드와 샘의 대화는 진지하게 바뀌는 순간이 있다. *Can I ask you something? Honestly?*에서 목소리를 낮춘다.
- 마지막 발표: 디에고의 드럼 소리, 부인의 *A president needs two things.*는 천천히, 연설처럼.
- 디에고의 *Next stop: Van Brunt and Pioneer!*는 버스 안내 방송처럼 또박또박.
