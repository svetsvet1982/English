# THE CORNER HOUSE — 5부 85화

## A Wedding on the Pier (부두의 결혼식)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 5부 13화(통산 85화) · 시기: 2027년 5월 29일(토) 오후~밤 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 4B(신부 준비) → 발렌티노 부두(예식) → 더 훅(피로연) → 밤의 부두 끝
> - 등장인물: PRIYA, MRS. SHAH, MARISOL, TASHA, EDDIE, SAM, DIEGO, TOMMY, GINA, JESSE, TESS
> - 사건: 프리야와 샘의 결혼식 날. 하객은 80명으로 합의했지만 프리야 어머니의 사촌들이 더 와서 112명이 된다. 주례는 온라인으로 주례 자격을 받은 에디다. 반지는 망고의 목줄에 묶여 온다. 혼인 서약에서 샘은 스프레드시트 농담을, 프리야는 '같은 문, 다른 방향'을 말한다. 피로연은 더 훅에서 열린다. 사모사와 엠파나다, 깡통 크랜베리 소스가 함께 나온다. 토미가 축사를 하고, 프리야 어머니가 1990년 에디슨의 작은 약국 이야기를 들려준다. 밤에 제시와 테스가 3B 추첨 신청서를 냈다고 털어놓는다.
> - 학습 포인트: 결혼 어휘(vows, officiant, bride, groom, ring bearer, reception, toast, newlyweds), 축사 화법(I've known… for… / Here's to the happy couple. / May you always… / Please raise your glasses.), 감동 표현(I'm so happy for you. / You look beautiful. / I'm speechless.)
> - 미국 현안: 다문화 결혼(인도계 미국인과 오하이오 출신), 줄인 결혼식 비용, 온라인 주례 자격, 이민 1세대 부모의 희생
> - 연결: 26화 프리야 부모(1990년 구자라트→에디슨 약국). 55화 샘의 깡통 크랜베리. 59화 결혼식 예산 축소(60명·8~9천, 토미 케이터링). 69화 '같은 문, 다른 방향'. 73화 하객 80명 합의. 77화 망고 입양. 79화 제시 3B 신청 결심.

## 대본

[Scene: Saturday, May 29, 2:30 p.m. Apartment 4B. Priya sits in front of a mirror in a red and gold sari. Her mother, Mrs. Shah, fixes a pin in her hair. Marisol and Tasha help with the flowers. Mango wears a small bow tie.]

MRS. SHAH: Stay still, beta, because I need one more pin. Your grandmother wore this sari in Ahmedabad in nineteen sixty-three, so don't move.
PRIYA: I'm not moving, Mummy, I'm shaking. That's very different.
TASHA: You look beautiful, Priya. You look like the poster for one of those big movies where everybody dances at the end.
MARISOL: You look like a queen. Maybe a nervous queen, but still a queen.
PRIYA: How many people are down on the pier right now? Be honest with me, please.
TASHA: I counted on my way up, and there were about a hundred. More taxis were pulling up when I came inside.
PRIYA: A hundred? Mummy! We agreed on eighty! Sam negotiated eighty, and you said it was your "best and final" number!
MRS. SHAH: I invited eighty, and I kept my promise. But cousins bring cousins, and that's not my fault. That's family physics.
MARISOL: Family physics. I'm definitely using that at my next family party.
PRIYA: Sam is going to faint when he sees them. His spreadsheet said eighty chairs, and he color-coded every single one.
TASHA: Relax, Tommy brought extra chairs from the bar. He also brought Harborline's old office chairs from the stoop sale, since nobody bought them.
MRS. SHAH: Priya, look at me for a moment. Can I say something before we go down?
PRIYA: Okay.
MRS. SHAH: When you said you wanted to marry a boy from Ohio, I was worried. When you said you wanted a small wedding, I was angry. When you said "on a pier," I said, "What pier? We have a hall in Edison."
PRIYA: I remember that very well. You didn't talk to me for three whole days.
MRS. SHAH: It was four days. But then in March I came to your building for the party, when you bought it, and I saw the neighbors. There was the old man with the cane, the lady with the clipboard, and the boy president.
MRS. SHAH: And I thought, "This is a village. My daughter found a village in Brooklyn, like the one I left in Gujarat." That's why I'm not angry anymore.
PRIYA: Mummy, please don't make me cry right now. My makeup took an hour!
MRS. SHAH: So cry, and then fix it. That's what makeup is for.
MARISOL: It's three o'clock, and Eddie says the officiant is ready. Apparently he's been ready since noon.
TASHA: He's wearing his green sweater under a suit jacket. He says it's for luck.

[Scene: 3:30 p.m. Valentino Pier. Folding chairs of every kind in uneven rows. Sam paces in a blue suit. Tommy, Gina, and Jesse set out programs. Diego holds Mango's leash.]

SAM: There are a hundred and twelve people here, because I counted. We only have ninety-four chairs, which means eighteen people will have to stand.
TOMMY: Sam, breathe. Eighteen people can stand for half an hour. They're cousins, and cousins are flexible.
JESSE: I can stand in the back. I'll give my chair to a grandmother, since there seem to be a lot of them.
SAM: Where are my parents? They were supposed to be here at three, and it's already three-thirty.
GINA: Your parents are at the bar. Your dad is drinking "The Frankie," and your mom is asking Tasha for the empanada recipe.
SAM: My mom? My mom from Ohio is asking for an empanada recipe?
GINA: She said, "In Columbus, we don't have this." Now she's taking notes on a napkin, like it's a cooking class.
SAM: Okay, that's good. That's actually very good. And what about the officiant?
TOMMY: Eddie has been standing at the end of the pier since one o'clock, practicing. Right now he's giving his speech to the seagulls.
DIEGO: He said the seagulls are a tough audience, because they keep walking away in the middle.
SAM: And the rings? Diego, please tell me you have the rings.
DIEGO: Mango has the rings, on her collar. I checked them six times, and they're tied really tight.
SAM: Six times. Good, that's my kind of number.
JESSE: Sam, can I give you some advice? I'm speaking as a man who has never been married, so take it for what it's worth.
SAM: Is it good advice?
JESSE: No, but it's short. When she walks down the pier, look at her, and not at the chairs.
SAM: ...That's actually really good advice.
TOMMY: It happens about once a year, when Jesse says something smart. I guess today's the day.

[Scene: 4:00 p.m. Valentino Pier. The harbor sparkles. The Statue of Liberty stands in the distance. More than a hundred guests sit on folding chairs of every kind. Eddie stands at the end of the pier with a small book. Sam waits beside him. Diego holds Mango's leash. Priya walks down the pier with her mother.]

EDDIE: Okay, everybody, please sit. Or stand, if you're one of the extra cousins. Welcome to Valentino Pier.
EDDIE: My name is Eddie O'Neill, and I'm eighty-four years old. Last month, my granddaughter Kayla helped me become an officiant on the internet. It took eleven minutes, and I'm told it's legal.
SAM: It's legal, I promise. I checked twice.
EDDIE: Of course you did. Priya, Sam, I've known you both for six years, since you moved into 4B. I've heard you through the ceiling, arguing about dishes, laughing at two in the morning, and practicing your arguments for the bank.
EDDIE: This pier is where I worked. Pier Nine is a little north of here, and I was there for forty years. This harbor is also where my Peggy and I used to watch the fireworks. So if anybody is going to get married here, I'm glad it's you.
EDDIE: Sam, you wrote your own vows, so go ahead.
SAM: Okay, I'm nervous. Priya, I'm an insurance analyst, which means I calculate risk for a living. Before I met you, I avoided risk. Honestly, I avoided almost everything.
SAM: Then you moved me into a building with a leaking roof and a dying boiler. We had a landlord who wanted us out, and a cat who controls the stairs.
SAM: And somehow, it was the best risk I ever took. You made me the treasurer, then you made me a co-owner, and then you made me adopt a dog before the wedding.
SAM: I promise to love you on green days and on red days. I promise to check the numbers, and to stop checking them when you say "enough."
PRIYA: Enough.
SAM: See? I'm already stopping.
EDDIE: Priya, it's your turn.
PRIYA: Sam, in February, a lawyer taught me a sentence: "Same door, different directions." She was talking about a negotiation, but it's also about us.
PRIYA: You're careful, and I'm fast. You're Ohio, and I'm New Jersey. You like canned cranberry sauce, and I think it's a crime.
PRIYA: But every morning, we walk out the same door, and every night, we come back through it. That's the only direction that matters: home.
PRIYA: I promise to love you in Red Hook, in Edison, and even in Ohio if I have to. And I promise to let you buy the canned cranberry sauce, but only once a year.
SAM: Once a year. I'm warning you, that's a binding agreement.
EDDIE: Now the rings. Diego? Is our ring bearer ready?
DIEGO: Mango! Come! Sit! Good girl! The rings are on her collar, in a little bag tied with a bow.
EDDIE: Very professional. Thank you, Mango, and thank you, Diego. I see the youth president is also the ring handler.
DIEGO: Yeah, I'm very busy this week.
EDDIE: Sam, put the ring on Priya's finger and repeat after me. "With this ring, I choose you."
SAM: With this ring, I choose you.
EDDIE: Priya, now it's your turn. "With this ring, I choose you."
PRIYA: With this ring, I choose you. I'll choose you every day, even on the red days.
EDDIE: By the power given to me by the internet and the State of New York... I now pronounce you married. You may kiss.
MRS. SHAH: Kiss! Kiss! Finally! I've been waiting for this day for years!

[Scene: 6:30 p.m. The Hook. Tables pushed together with white cloths. Samosas, empanadas, lasagna, tamales, and a bowl of canned cranberry sauce with its ridges still showing. Gina taps a glass. Tommy stands up.]

GINA: Everybody, please, can I have your attention? The best man would like to give a toast!
TOMMY: I'm not the best man. Sam asked me to be the "best man for the food," and that's a completely different job.
GINA: It's close enough, Dad. Go!
TOMMY: Okay. I've known Sam for six years. When he first came in, he ordered a beer and asked me what the alcohol percentage was, to two decimal places.
TOMMY: I thought, "This guy will never fit in Red Hook." I was wrong. This year, he saved Red Hook with a spreadsheet, and it even had colors.
TOMMY: And Priya came in during her first week and told me my coffee could be used to clean pipes. She was right, because she's always right. Sam, you'd better get used to it.
TOMMY: In this building, we have a rule: "Never sign alone." Today, you two signed together, in front of a hundred and twelve people and one dog.
TOMMY: So please raise your glasses. To the happy couple. May your roof never leak. May your boiler never die. And may your spreadsheet always be green.
GINA: To Priya and Sam!
JESSE: To Priya and Sam! And to Mango, the best ring bearer in Brooklyn!
TOMMY: Mrs. Shah, would you like to say something to the happy couple?
MRS. SHAH: Yes, thank you, Tommy. By the way, your coffee is terrible. I tried a cup this morning, and I'm still awake.
TOMMY: Everybody says that, so I'm used to it. Please, go ahead.
MRS. SHAH: In nineteen ninety, my husband and I came from Gujarat with two suitcases, and we opened a small pharmacy in Edison. We worked twelve hours a day, seven days a week, for thirty years.
MRS. SHAH: We missed everything, like school plays and birthdays, because we were always behind the counter, counting pills and counting money. We did it so Priya could study, and so she could be a pharmacist in a big hospital, not behind a small counter.
MRS. SHAH: My husband passed away three years ago, and he would be so proud today. He always said, "Our daughter will find good people." Today I can see the good people, all hundred and twelve of them.
TESS: Oh, Mrs. Shah, that's beautiful. Priya, Sam, I'm so happy for you.
MRS. SHAH: Sam, you're a good boy from Ohio, even with your strange cranberries, so take care of my daughter. And Priya, take care of this building, because it took care of you.
GINA: To family! To every kind of family!
JESSE: To family! And to cranberries, straight from the can!
TOMMY: Okay, everybody, the cake comes out in ten minutes! Ramon made it, and it has three layers, and it's Mango-shaped.
GINA: Wait, do you mean Mango the dog or mango the fruit?
TOMMY: Both. It's a mango cake shaped like Mango. Don't ask, because it took him two days.
TESS: That might be the most Red Hook cake in history.
JESSE: Can I eat the tail first? Or is that rude at a wedding?

[Scene: 10:15 p.m. The end of Valentino Pier. The party continues faintly at the bar. Jesse and Tess sit on a bench with their shoes off. Eddie sits nearby with his cane, looking at the water. Priya and Sam walk up, still dressed.]

PRIYA: There you are! We've been looking everywhere for you, and the cake is about to come out.
TESS: We needed some air. Jesse cried at Tommy's toast, then at your mother's speech, and then at the cranberry sauce.
JESSE: The cranberry sauce was emotional! It still had the ridges from the can!
SAM: Eddie, thank you, really. You were a perfect officiant, and I'm not just saying that.
EDDIE: I forgot one line, you know. I skipped "Do you take this woman," and nobody noticed.
PRIYA: I noticed, but I didn't care. You said "I choose you," and that's better.
JESSE: Can we tell you something? It's a happy night, so maybe it's the right time.
TESS: Jesse, not tonight. It's their night, not ours.
PRIYA: Tell us, please. Good news makes good news better.
JESSE: Okay. Yesterday, we applied for 3B, in the lottery. We did it together, Tess and me.
SAM: Together? You two? In one apartment?
TESS: My lease above the bakery ends in August, and so does his. So we thought, why pay two rents?
EDDIE: That's the most romantic thing a New Yorker can say. "Why pay two rents?"
JESSE: We're under the income limit, but barely. Tess makes more than me, much more, and honestly, nurses should.
PRIYA: Jesse! That's amazing! You'd be on the third floor, right across from Eddie!
EDDIE: Then I'll hear you through the wall, arguing about dishes and laughing at two in the morning.
SAM: You know it's a lottery, right? There are already two hundred applications, and maybe more.
JESSE: I know. It's two hundred people and one apartment, so it's not a sure thing.
TESS: But it's a chance, and we decided together. That's the important part.
PRIYA: Never sign alone.
TESS: Never sign alone, even on a lottery form.
EDDIE: Look at the Statue, all lit up. Look at her. I've been looking at her from this pier for fifty-three years.
SAM: Eddie, are you okay? You've gotten very quiet.
EDDIE: I'm happy, son. That's my quiet. Peggy and I got married in a church with a small cake, on a Saturday in May, fifty-two years ago this week.
PRIYA: This week? Your anniversary is this week?
EDDIE: May thirty-first, which is Monday, Memorial Day. We picked it because Kelly's, the old bar on Coffey Street, had a free buffet that day, and we were broke.
TESS: Eddie, that's the best wedding story I've ever heard.
EDDIE: It's a good story, and today is a good story too. Come on, let's get the cake before Tommy cuts it with a bread knife.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **결혼 어휘**
  - *vows* 혼인 서약 / *officiant* 주례 / *bride* 신부 / *groom* 신랑 / *newlyweds* 신혼부부
  - *ring bearer* 반지 운반자 / *reception* 피로연 / *best man* 신랑 들러리
  - *I now pronounce you married.* — "이제 두 사람이 부부가 되었음을 선언합니다."
  - *With this ring, I choose you.* — 반지를 끼우며 하는 서약(이 화의 표현).
- **축사 화법**
  - *I've known Sam for six years.* — "샘을 안 지 6년이 됐어요." 관계로 시작한다.
  - *I thought… I was wrong.* — 첫인상과 반전.
  - *Please raise your glasses. To the happy couple.* — "잔을 들어 주세요. 행복한 두 사람을 위하여."
  - *May your roof never leak.* — *May + 주어 + 동사원형*으로 축복을 빈다.
- **감동 표현** — *You look beautiful. / I'm so happy for you. / That's beautiful.*
- **beta** — 구자라트어·힌디어로 '얘야'(자녀를 부르는 말). *Mummy* — 엄마.
- **family physics** — "가족 물리학." 사촌이 사촌을 데려오는 법칙. 샤 여사의 농담.
- **Why pay two rents?** — "왜 월세를 두 번 내?" 뉴요커의 가장 현실적인 로맨스.
- **binding agreement** — 구속력 있는 합의.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 혼인 서약은 천천히, 한 문장씩 숨을 쉬며. 샘은 떨리게, 프리야는 단단하게.
- *With this ring, | I choose you.* — 쉼표 뒤를 또렷하게.
- 에디의 *I now pronounce you married.*는 장엄하게 시작했다가 *You may kiss.*에서 미소 짓는다.
- 토미의 축사는 농담으로 시작해 *May your roof never leak…*의 축복 세 개에서 리듬을 맞춘다.
- 샤 여사의 이야기는 이민 1세대의 담담한 톤. *twelve hours a day, | seven days a week, | for thirty years.*
- 마지막 부두 장면은 밤의 작은 목소리. 에디의 *That's my quiet.*

## 한글 해석

샤 여사: 가만있어, 얘야, 핀 하나만 더 꽂아야 하니까. 네 할머니가 1963년에 아마다바드에서 이 사리를 입으셨어. 그러니까 움직이지 마.
프리야: 안 움직여요, 엄마, 떨리는 거예요. 그건 완전히 다른 거예요.
타샤: 정말 아름다워요, 프리야. 마지막에 다 같이 춤추는 그런 대작 영화 포스터 같아요.
마리솔: 여왕 같아요. 긴장한 여왕일지는 몰라도, 그래도 여왕이에요.
프리야: 지금 부두에 사람이 몇 명이나 있어요? 솔직하게 말해 줘요, 제발.
타샤: 올라오면서 세어 봤는데 100명쯤 있었어. 내가 들어올 때 택시가 더 서고 있었고.
프리야: 100명? 엄마! 80명으로 합의했잖아요! 샘이 80명으로 협상했고, 엄마가 그게 '최종 최선' 숫자라고 했잖아요!
샤 여사: 난 80명을 초대했고, 약속을 지켰어. 근데 사촌은 사촌을 데려오는 법이고, 그건 내 잘못이 아니야. 가족 물리학이야.
마리솔: 가족 물리학. 다음 가족 모임 때 꼭 써먹어야겠어요.
프리야: 샘이 사람들 보면 기절하겠어요. 스프레드시트에 의자 80개라고 해 놓고 하나하나 색깔까지 칠해 놨는데.
타샤: 진정해, 토미가 바에서 의자를 더 가져왔어. 스툽 세일 때 아무도 안 사 간 하버라인의 옛날 사무용 의자들도 가져왔고.
샤 여사: 프리야, 잠깐 날 봐. 내려가기 전에 하나 말해도 될까?
프리야: 네.
샤 여사: 네가 오하이오 남자애랑 결혼하고 싶다고 했을 때 걱정했어. 작은 결혼식을 원한다고 했을 땐 화가 났고. '부두에서'라고 했을 땐 "무슨 부두? 에디슨에 예식장이 있잖아"라고 했지.
프리야: 아주 잘 기억나요. 꼬박 사흘 동안 저랑 말 안 하셨잖아요.
샤 여사: 나흘이었어. 그러다 3월에 너희가 건물을 샀을 때 파티하러 네 건물에 갔고, 이웃들을 봤지. 지팡이 짚은 할아버지, 클립보드 든 부인, 그리고 꼬마 회장이 있었어.
샤 여사: 그리고 생각했어. "이건 마을이구나. 내 딸이 브루클린에서 마을을 찾았구나, 내가 구자라트에 두고 온 마을 같은." 그래서 이제 화 안 나.
프리야: 엄마, 제발 지금 울리지 마요. 화장하는 데 한 시간 걸렸단 말이에요!
샤 여사: 그럼 울고, 그다음에 고쳐. 화장은 그러라고 있는 거야.
마리솔: 3시예요, 에디가 주례 준비 다 됐대요. 듣자 하니 정오부터 준비하고 계셨대요.
타샤: 정장 재킷 밑에 초록 스웨터를 입으셨어요. 행운을 위해서래요.
샘: 여기 112명이 있어요, 제가 세어 봤거든요. 의자는 94개밖에 없으니까 18명은 서 있어야 해요.
토미: 샘, 숨 쉬어. 18명쯤은 30분 동안 서 있을 수 있어. 사촌들이잖아, 사촌은 유연해.
