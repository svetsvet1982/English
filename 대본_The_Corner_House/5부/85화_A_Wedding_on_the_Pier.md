# THE CORNER HOUSE — 5부 85화

## A Wedding on the Pier (부두의 결혼식)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 5부 13화(통산 85화) · 시기: 2027년 5월 29일(토) 오후~밤 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 4B(신부 준비) → 발렌티노 부두(예식) → 더 훅(피로연) → 밤의 부두 끝
> - 등장인물: PRIYA, MRS. SHAH, MARISOL, TASHA, EDDIE, SAM, DIEGO, TOMMY, GINA, JESSE, TESS
> - 사건: 프리야와 샘의 결혼식 날. 하객은 80명으로 합의했지만 프리야 어머니의 사촌들이 더 와서 112명이 된다. 주례는 온라인으로 주례 자격을 받은 에디다. 반지는 망고의 목줄에 묶여 온다. 혼인 서약에서 샘은 스프레드시트 농담을, 프리야는 '같은 문, 다른 방향'을 말한다. 피로연은 더 훅에서 열린다. 사모사와 엠파나다, 깡통 크랜베리 소스가 함께 나온다. 토미가 축사를 하고, 프리야 어머니가 1990년 에디슨의 작은 약국 이야기를 들려준다. 밤에 제시와 테스가 3B 추첨 신청서를 냈다고 털어놓는다.
> - 학습 포인트: 결혼 어휘(vows, officiant, bride, groom, ring bearer, reception, toast, newlyweds), 축사 화법(I've known… for… / Here's to the happy couple. / May you always… / Please raise your glasses.), 감동 표현(I'm so happy for you. / You look beautiful. / I'm speechless.)
> - 미국 현안: 다문화 결혼(인도계 미국인과 오하이오 출신), 줄인 결혼식 비용, 온라인 주례 자격, 이민 1세대 부모의 희생
> - 연결: 26화 프리야 부모(1990년 구자라트→에디슨 약국). 55화 샘의 깡통 크랜베리. 59화 결혼식 예산 축소(60명·8~9천, 토미 케이터링). 69화 '같은 문, 다른 방향'. 73화 하객 80명 합의. 77화 망고 입양. 79화 제시 3B 신청 결심.

## 대본

[Scene: Saturday, May 29, 2:30 p.m. Apartment 4B. Priya sits in front of a mirror in a red and gold sari. Her mother, Mrs. Shah, fixes a pin in her hair. Marisol and Tasha help with the flowers. Mango wears a small bow tie.]

MRS. SHAH: Stay still, beta. One more pin. Your grandmother wore this sari in nineteen sixty-three. In Ahmedabad. Don't move.
PRIYA: I'm not moving, Mummy. I'm shaking. That's different.
TASHA: You look beautiful, Priya. Like a movie poster.
MARISOL: Like a queen. A nervous queen.
PRIYA: How many people are on the pier right now? Be honest.
TASHA: I counted on my way up. About a hundred. And more taxis are coming.
PRIYA: A hundred? Mummy! We agreed eighty! Sam negotiated eighty! "Best and final"!
MRS. SHAH: I invited eighty. But cousins bring cousins. That's not my fault. That's family physics.
MARISOL: Family physics. I'm using that.
PRIYA: Sam is going to faint. His spreadsheet said eighty chairs.
TASHA: Tommy brought extra chairs. From the bar. And Harborline's office chairs. From the stoop sale. Nobody bought them.
MRS. SHAH: Priya. Look at me. Can I say something? Before we go down?
PRIYA: Okay.
MRS. SHAH: When you said you wanted to marry a boy from Ohio, I was worried. When you said you wanted a small wedding, I was angry. When you said "on a pier," I said, "What pier? We have a hall in Edison."
PRIYA: I remember. You didn't talk to me for three days.
MRS. SHAH: Four days. But then I came to your building. In March. For the party. When you bought it. I saw the neighbors. The old man with the cane. The lady with the clipboard. The boy president.
MRS. SHAH: And I thought, "This is a village. My daughter found a village in Brooklyn." Like the one I left in Gujarat. That's why I'm not angry anymore.
PRIYA: Mummy... don't make me cry. My makeup.
MRS. SHAH: Cry. Then fix it. That's what makeup is for.
MARISOL: It's three o'clock. Eddie says the officiant is ready. He's been ready since noon.
TASHA: He's wearing his green sweater. Under a suit jacket. He says it's for luck.

[Scene: 3:30 p.m. Valentino Pier. Folding chairs of every kind in uneven rows. Sam paces in a blue suit. Tommy, Gina, and Jesse set out programs. Diego holds Mango's leash.]

SAM: A hundred and twelve people. I counted. We have ninety-four chairs. Eighteen people will stand.
TOMMY: Sam. Breathe. Eighteen people can stand. They're cousins. Cousins are flexible.
JESSE: I can stand. In the back. I'll give my chair to a grandmother.
SAM: Where are my parents? They were supposed to be here at three.
GINA: Your parents are at the bar. Your dad is drinking "The Frankie." Your mom is asking Tasha for the empanada recipe.
SAM: My mom? From Ohio? Asking for an empanada recipe?
GINA: She said, "In Columbus, we don't have this." She's taking notes. On a napkin.
SAM: Okay. That's good. That's very good. And the officiant?
TOMMY: Eddie has been standing at the end of the pier since one o'clock. Practicing. He's talking to the seagulls.
DIEGO: He said the seagulls are a tough audience.
SAM: And the rings? Diego, you have the rings?
DIEGO: Mango has the rings. On her collar. I checked them six times.
SAM: Six times. Good. That's my kind of number.
JESSE: Sam, can I give you advice? As a man who has never been married?
SAM: Is it good advice?
JESSE: No. But it's short. When she walks down the pier, look at her. Not at the chairs.
SAM: ...That's actually good advice.
TOMMY: It happens once a year. Jesse says something smart. Today's the day.

[Scene: 4:00 p.m. Valentino Pier. The harbor sparkles. The Statue of Liberty stands in the distance. More than a hundred guests sit on folding chairs of every kind. Eddie stands at the end of the pier with a small book. Sam waits beside him. Diego holds Mango's leash. Priya walks down the pier with her mother.]

EDDIE: Okay. Everybody. Please sit. Or stand, if you're one of the extra cousins. Welcome to Valentino Pier.
EDDIE: My name is Eddie O'Neill. I'm eighty-four. Last month, my granddaughter Kayla helped me become an officiant. On the internet. It took eleven minutes. I'm told it's legal.
SAM: It's legal. I checked. Twice.
EDDIE: Of course you did. Priya, Sam. I've known you both for six years. Since you moved into 4B. I've heard you through the ceiling. Arguing about dishes. Laughing at two a.m. Practicing your arguments for the bank.
EDDIE: This pier is where I worked. Pier Nine, a little north. Forty years. This harbor is where my Peggy and I watched the fireworks. So if anybody is going to get married here, I'm glad it's you.
EDDIE: Sam. You wrote your own vows. Go ahead.
SAM: Okay. I'm nervous. Priya, I'm an insurance analyst. I calculate risk for a living. Before I met you, I avoided risk. I avoided everything.
SAM: Then you moved me to a building with a leaking roof. A dying boiler. A landlord who wanted us out. And a cat who controls the stairs.
SAM: And somehow, it was the best risk I ever took. You made me the treasurer. You made me a co-owner. You made me adopt a dog before the wedding.
SAM: I promise to love you. On green days and red days. I promise to check the numbers. And to stop checking them when you say "enough."
PRIYA: Enough.
SAM: See? I'm already stopping.
EDDIE: Priya. Your turn.
PRIYA: Sam. In February, a lawyer taught me a sentence. "Same door. Different directions." It was about a negotiation. But it's also about us.
PRIYA: You're careful. I'm fast. You're Ohio. I'm New Jersey. You like canned cranberry sauce. I think it's a crime.
PRIYA: But every morning, we walk out the same door. And every night, we come back through it. That's the only direction that matters. Home.
PRIYA: I promise to love you. In Red Hook. In Edison. In Ohio, if I have to. And I promise to let you buy the canned cranberry sauce. Once a year.
SAM: Once a year. That's a binding agreement.
EDDIE: The rings. Diego? Is our ring bearer ready?
DIEGO: Mango! Come! Sit! Good girl! The rings are on her collar. In a little bag. Tied with a bow.
EDDIE: Very professional. Thank you, Mango. And thank you, Diego. The youth president is also the ring handler.
DIEGO: I'm very busy this week.
EDDIE: Sam, put the ring on Priya's finger. Repeat after me. "With this ring, I choose you."
SAM: With this ring, I choose you.
EDDIE: Priya. "With this ring, I choose you."
PRIYA: With this ring, I choose you. Every day. Even on red days.
EDDIE: By the power given to me by the internet and the State of New York... I now pronounce you married. You may kiss.
MRS. SHAH: Kiss! Kiss! Finally!

[Scene: 6:30 p.m. The Hook. Tables pushed together with white cloths. Samosas, empanadas, lasagna, tamales, and a bowl of canned cranberry sauce with its ridges still showing. Gina taps a glass. Tommy stands up.]

GINA: Everybody! Please! The best man would like to give a toast!
TOMMY: I'm not the best man. Sam asked me to be the "best man for the food." That's a different job.
GINA: Close enough, Dad. Go.
TOMMY: Okay. I've known Sam for six years. When he first came in, he ordered a beer and asked me what the alcohol percentage was. To two decimal places.
TOMMY: I thought, "This guy will never fit in Red Hook." I was wrong. This year, he saved Red Hook. With a spreadsheet. With colors.
TOMMY: And Priya. Priya came in her first week and told me my coffee could be used to clean pipes. She was right. She's always right. Sam, get used to it.
TOMMY: In this building, we have a rule. "Never sign alone." Today, you signed together. In front of a hundred and twelve people. And one dog.
TOMMY: So please raise your glasses. To the happy couple. May your roof never leak. May your boiler never die. And may your spreadsheet always be green.
GINA: To Priya and Sam!
JESSE: To Priya and Sam! And to Mango!
TOMMY: Mrs. Shah, would you like to say something?
MRS. SHAH: Yes. Thank you, Tommy. Your coffee is terrible, by the way. I tried it this morning.
TOMMY: Everybody says that. Go ahead.
MRS. SHAH: In nineteen ninety, my husband and I came from Gujarat with two suitcases. We opened a small pharmacy in Edison. Twelve hours a day. Seven days a week. For thirty years.
MRS. SHAH: We missed everything. School plays. Birthdays. We were always behind the counter. Counting pills. Counting money. So Priya could study. So Priya could be a pharmacist in a big hospital. Not behind a small counter.
MRS. SHAH: My husband passed away three years ago. He would be so proud today. He always said, "Our daughter will find good people." Today, I see the good people. A hundred and twelve of them.
TESS: Oh, Mrs. Shah. That's beautiful.
MRS. SHAH: Sam, you're a good boy. From Ohio. With strange cranberries. Take care of my daughter. And Priya, take care of this building. It took care of you.
GINA: To family! Every kind!
JESSE: To family! And to cranberries! From a can!
TOMMY: Okay! Cake in ten minutes! Ramon made it. Three layers. Mango shaped.
GINA: Mango the dog or mango the fruit?
TOMMY: Both. It's a mango cake shaped like Mango. Don't ask. It took him two days.
TESS: That's the most Red Hook cake in history.
JESSE: Can I eat the tail first? Is that rude?

[Scene: 10:15 p.m. The end of Valentino Pier. The party continues faintly at the bar. Jesse and Tess sit on a bench with their shoes off. Eddie sits nearby with his cane, looking at the water. Priya and Sam walk up, still dressed.]

PRIYA: There you are! We've been looking for you. The cake is coming out.
TESS: We needed air. Jesse cried at Tommy's toast. And at your mother's speech. And at the cranberry sauce.
JESSE: The cranberry sauce was emotional! It had ridges!
SAM: Eddie, thank you. Really. You were a perfect officiant.
EDDIE: I forgot one line. "Do you take this woman." I skipped it. Nobody noticed.
PRIYA: I noticed. I didn't care. You said "I choose you." That's better.
JESSE: Can we tell you something? Since it's a happy night?
TESS: Jesse. Not tonight. It's their night.
PRIYA: Tell us. Please. Good news makes good news better.
JESSE: Okay. We applied. For 3B. The lottery. Together. Tess and me. Yesterday.
SAM: Together? You two? In one apartment?
TESS: My lease above the bakery ends in August. His ends in August. We thought... why pay two rents?
EDDIE: That's the most romantic thing a New Yorker can say. "Why pay two rents?"
JESSE: We're under the income limit. Barely. Tess makes more than me. Much more. Nurses should.
PRIYA: Jesse! That's amazing! You'd be on the third floor! Across from Eddie!
EDDIE: Then I'll hear you through the wall. Arguing about dishes. Laughing at two a.m.
SAM: It's a lottery. You know that. There are two hundred applications already. Maybe more.
JESSE: I know. Two hundred people. One apartment. It's not a sure thing.
TESS: But it's a chance. And we decided together. That's the important part.
PRIYA: Never sign alone.
TESS: Never sign alone. Even a lottery form.
EDDIE: Look. The Statue. All lit up. Look at her. Fifty-three years I've been looking at her from this pier.
SAM: Eddie, are you okay? You're quiet.
EDDIE: I'm happy, son. That's my quiet. Peggy and I got married in a church. With a small cake. On a Saturday in May. Fifty-two years ago. This week.
PRIYA: This week? Your anniversary?
EDDIE: May thirty-first. Monday. Memorial Day. We picked it because Kelly's... the old bar on Coffey Street... had a free buffet that day. We were broke.
TESS: Eddie, that's the best wedding story I've ever heard.
EDDIE: It's a good story. And today is a good story too. Come on. The cake. Before Tommy cuts it with a bread knife.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **결혼 어휘**
  - *vows* 혼인 서약 / *officiant* 주례 / *bride* 신부 / *groom* 신랑 / *newlyweds* 신혼부부
  - *ring bearer* 반지 운반자 / *reception* 피로연 / *best man* 신랑 들러리
  - *I now pronounce you married.* — "이제 두 사람이 부부가 되었음을 선언합니다."
  - *With this ring, I choose you.* — 반지를 끼우며 하는 서약(이 화의 표현).
- **축사 화법**
  - *I've known Sam for six years.* — "샘을 안 지 6년이 됐어요." 관계로 시작한다.
  - *I thought… I was wrong.* — 첫인상과 반전.
  - *Please raise your glasses. To the happy couple.* — "잔을 들어 주세요. 행복한 두 사람을 위하여."
  - *May your roof never leak.* — *May + 주어 + 동사원형*으로 축복을 빈다.
- **감동 표현** — *You look beautiful. / I'm so happy for you. / That's beautiful.*
- **beta** — 구자라트어·힌디어로 '얘야'(자녀를 부르는 말). *Mummy* — 엄마.
- **family physics** — "가족 물리학." 사촌이 사촌을 데려오는 법칙. 샤 여사의 농담.
- **Why pay two rents?** — "왜 월세를 두 번 내?" 뉴요커의 가장 현실적인 로맨스.
- **binding agreement** — 구속력 있는 합의.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 혼인 서약은 천천히, 한 문장씩 숨을 쉬며. 샘은 떨리게, 프리야는 단단하게.
- *With this ring, | I choose you.* — 쉼표 뒤를 또렷하게.
- 에디의 *I now pronounce you married.*는 장엄하게 시작했다가 *You may kiss.*에서 미소 짓는다.
- 토미의 축사는 농담으로 시작해 *May your roof never leak…*의 축복 세 개에서 리듬을 맞춘다.
- 샤 여사의 이야기는 이민 1세대의 담담한 톤. *Twelve hours a day. | Seven days a week. | For thirty years.*
- 마지막 부두 장면은 밤의 작은 목소리. 에디의 *That's my quiet.*

## 한글 해석

샤 여사: 가만있어, 얘야. 핀 하나만 더. 네 할머니가 1963년에 이 사리를 입으셨어. 아마다바드에서. 움직이지 마.
프리야: 안 움직여요, 엄마. 떨리는 거예요. 그건 달라요.
타샤: 정말 아름다워요, 프리야. 영화 포스터 같아요.
마리솔: 여왕 같아요. 긴장한 여왕이요.
프리야: 지금 부두에 사람이 몇 명이에요? 솔직하게요.
타샤: 올라오면서 세어 봤어. 100명쯤. 택시가 더 오고 있어.
프리야: 100명? 엄마! 80명으로 합의했잖아요! 샘이 80명으로 협상했어요! '최종 최선'으로요!
샤 여사: 난 80명을 초대했어. 근데 사촌은 사촌을 데려와. 그건 내 잘못이 아니야. 가족 물리학이야.
마리솔: 가족 물리학. 그 말 써먹을래요.
프리야: 샘이 기절하겠어요. 스프레드시트엔 의자 80개라고 되어 있는데.
타샤: 토미가 의자를 더 가져왔어. 바에서. 그리고 하버라인 사무용 의자들도. 스툽 세일 때 아무도 안 산 거.
샤 여사: 프리야. 날 봐. 하나 말해도 될까? 내려가기 전에?
프리야: 네.
샤 여사: 네가 오하이오 남자애랑 결혼하고 싶다고 했을 때 걱정했어. 작은 결혼식을 원한다고 했을 땐 화가 났고. '부두에서'라고 했을 땐 "무슨 부두? 에디슨에 예식장이 있잖아"라고 했지.
프리야: 기억나요. 사흘 동안 저랑 말 안 하셨잖아요.
샤 여사: 나흘이야. 그러다 3월에 네 건물에 갔지. 파티 때. 너희가 건물 샀을 때. 이웃들을 봤어. 지팡이 짚은 할아버지. 클립보드 든 부인. 꼬마 회장.
샤 여사: 그리고 생각했어. "이건 마을이구나. 내 딸이 브루클린에서 마을을 찾았구나." 내가 구자라트에 두고 온 마을처럼. 그래서 이제 화 안 나.
프리야: 엄마… 울리지 마요. 화장이요.
샤 여사: 울어. 그다음 고쳐. 화장은 그러라고 있는 거야.
마리솔: 3시예요. 에디가 주례 준비 다 됐대요. 정오부터 준비하고 계셨대요.
타샤: 초록 스웨터를 입으셨어요. 정장 재킷 밑에요. 행운을 위해서래요.
샘: 112명이요. 세어 봤어요. 의자는 94개예요. 18명은 서 있어야 해요.
토미: 샘. 숨 쉬어. 18명은 서 있을 수 있어. 사촌들이잖아. 사촌은 유연해.
제시: 전 서 있을 수 있어요. 뒤쪽에서요. 제 의자는 할머니께 드릴게요.
샘: 우리 부모님은 어디 계세요? 3시까지 오시기로 했는데.
지나: 부모님은 바에 계세요. 아버님은 '더 프랭키'를 드시고 계시고요. 어머님은 타샤한테 엠파나다 레시피를 물어보고 계세요.
샘: 우리 엄마가요? 오하이오에서 온? 엠파나다 레시피를요?
지나: "콜럼버스엔 이런 게 없어"라고 하셨어요. 메모하고 계세요. 냅킨에요.
샘: 좋아요. 그거 좋네요. 아주 좋아요. 그럼 주례는요?
토미: 에디는 1시부터 부두 끝에 서 있어. 연습 중이야. 갈매기한테 말하고 있어.
디에고: 갈매기들이 까다로운 관객이래요.
샘: 그럼 반지는? 디에고, 반지 있지?
디에고: 망고가 갖고 있어요. 목줄에요. 여섯 번 확인했어요.
샘: 여섯 번. 좋아. 내 취향의 숫자야.
제시: 샘, 조언 하나 해도 돼요? 결혼 한 번도 안 해 본 남자로서요?
샘: 좋은 조언이야?
제시: 아니요. 근데 짧아요. 프리야가 부두를 걸어올 때 프리야를 봐요. 의자 말고요.
샘: …그거 진짜 좋은 조언이네.
