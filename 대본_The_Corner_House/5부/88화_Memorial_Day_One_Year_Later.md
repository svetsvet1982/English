# THE CORNER HOUSE — 5부 88화

## Memorial Day, One Year Later (메모리얼 데이, 1년 뒤)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 5부 16화(통산 88화) · 시기: 2027년 5월 31일(월) 메모리얼 데이 오후 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 오후 2시 반의 더 훅 → 3시의 묵념 → 묵념 뒤의 바 → 해 질 녘 발렌티노 부두
> - 등장인물: GINA, TASHA, TOMMY, JESSE, EDDIE, MRS. ESPOSITO, MARCUS, MR. HAYES, DIEGO
> - 사건: 1년 전 15화와 같은 날, 같은 시각. 지나가 대표 동업자로서 처음 문을 연다. 제시는 1년 전 약속대로 넥타이를 매고 와서 에디 옆에 앉는다. 3시 정각, 대니 월시를 위한 빈 잔 옆에 올해는 잔이 몇 개 더 놓인다. 페기, 지미, 앤젤라, 카르미네, 그리고 마커스 삼촌의 전우들. 묵념이 끝나자 에디는 1년을 돌아본다. 사람들은 각자 잃은 사람을 이야기한다. 아무도 잃어 본 적 없는 디에고는 그래도 괜찮은지 묻는다. 해 질 녘 에디는 디에고와 제시를 데리고 부두로 간다. 대니의 마지막 편지에 적힌 대로 '좋은 지렁이'를 들고. 그리고 처음으로 고기가 잡힌다.
> - 학습 포인트: 현재완료 종합(I've come here every year since… / It's been a year since… / We've lost… / Have you ever…? / I haven't… yet. / I've never…), 회고와 노년의 어조, 15화와 대비 낭독
> - 미국 현안: 메모리얼 데이의 의미(전사자 추모), 베트남전과 이라크전 참전 세대, 지역 공동체의 애도 의식
> - 연결: 15화(2006년부터 3시 묵념, 빈 잔, 대니 월시 — 커피 스트리트·1944년생·편지마다 작은 물고기·마지막 편지 '부두에서 보자, 에디. 좋은 지렁이 가져와', 제시 '내년 3시에 에디 옆에'), 마커스 삼촌(이라크 2회, VA 병원, 전우 라미레스·콜). 53화 지미. 81화 카르미네. 85화 에디·페기 52주년. 86화 '내일부터: 지나를 찾아 주세요'. 84화 '다른 학교, 같은 부두'.

## 대본

[Scene: Monday, May 31, 2:30 p.m. The Hook. Quieter than usual. Small flags on the tables, the same as last year. Gina stands at the chalkboard with a new piece of chalk. Tasha polishes glasses. Tommy sits on a customer's stool for the first time in twenty-one years.]

TASHA: Gina, boss, managing partner, whatever we're calling you now. What are you writing over there?
GINA: The board. It's my first board, and I've been staring at it for twenty minutes. I've never been so nervous about a piece of chalk.
TOMMY: Just write the menu, and then put one line at the bottom. That's all it ever needs.
GINA: Dad, you're sitting on a customer stool. In twenty-one years, you've never sat on that side of the bar.
TOMMY: I've sat here once, in two thousand six, before we opened. Your mother made me sit here so that I could see what the customers see.
GINA: And what did you see?
TOMMY: I saw her, behind the bar, laughing at something. I thought, "Customers are going to love this place." I was right, because they loved her.
TASHA: And then they loved you, too, eventually. They loved you despite the coffee, which says a lot about you.
GINA: Okay, here it is. "Memorial Day. Hot dogs free after five. Lemonade by Diego. Moment of silence at three." And the bottom line says...
TOMMY: Go on. What's the bottom line?
GINA: "Today's coffee: still Tasha's. Some things change. Some don't."
TOMMY: That's good. That's very good, Gina. Your mother would have written exactly that.
JESSE: Hey, everybody. Am I early? I wanted to be here before Eddie, so is he here yet?
TASHA: Jesse! Look at you, you're wearing a tie!
JESSE: I promised, last year, right here. I said, "Next year, at three, I'll sit with you, Eddie." So I bought a tie on Saturday, and it's my first one since my cousin's wedding.
GINA: It's crooked, you know.
JESSE: It's a first attempt, so give me some credit. Technically, it's still a tie.
TOMMY: Sit down, Jesse, right next to Eddie's stool. He'll be here at two fifty-eight, because he's always here at two fifty-eight.
JESSE: Why two fifty-eight? Why not three o'clock, like everybody else?
TOMMY: It takes him two minutes to walk from the door to the stool with the cane. He's done it every year since two thousand six, and he's never been late once.
TASHA: Last year, Jesse, you didn't even know what Memorial Day was for. You thought it was just a sale weekend.
JESSE: I thought it was about mattress sales and barbecues. I'm from Scranton, and we had a parade, but I was always at the mattress store with my dad.
GINA: And now you're wearing a tie for it. That's a big change in one year.
JESSE: Now I know the names, like Danny's. When you know the names, that changes things.

[Scene: 2:58 p.m. The door opens slowly. Eddie, in a jacket and tie, walks in with his cane. In his other hand, he holds a small envelope. Mrs. Esposito follows him with a paper bag.]

EDDIE: Good afternoon, everybody. Tommy, Gina, Tasha... and Jesse. Well, look at that, you wore a tie.
JESSE: I promised you last year that I would. Remember?
EDDIE: I remember. I just didn't believe you, because people promise a lot of things at three o'clock. Thank you, son.
TOMMY: Eddie, your glass is ready for Danny, in the same spot as every year.
EDDIE: Thank you, Tommy. And today... could we have more glasses? I've thought about it all night, and I couldn't sleep.
TOMMY: Of course we can. How many do you want?
EDDIE: Danny's. And Peggy's, because today would have been fifty-two years. And Jimmy's. And Angela's.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And Carmine's, if that's okay. He never served, but he drove the B61 for eighteen years. That's a kind of service.
EDDIE: Carmine's too, Dolores. Of course he gets a glass.
MARCUS: And two more, for my uncle's friends, Ramirez and Cole. They died in Fallujah in two thousand four. My uncle can't come, because he's working at the VA today. He always works today.
TOMMY: That's seven glasses. Tasha, seven glasses, all full, even though nobody drinks them.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And I brought these lemons, one for each glass. Angela started that, so now it's ours.
EDDIE: A lemon next to every glass. Look at that, a whole bar full of lemons. Peggy would laugh at that.
TOMMY: Angela used to pour Danny's glass every year, right there behind the bar. For eight years now, I've poured it for her.
EDDIE: Here's Danny's letter, the last one he sent. And here's Peggy's picture, which is the only one I have left. The water took the rest in twenty twelve.
JESSE: Can I see the fish again, the one on the letter?
EDDIE: Here, at the bottom, the little fish. "See you at the pier, Eddie. Bring good worms."
JESSE: It's still a good fish. He really had a steady hand.
EDDIE: He drew better than he fished. I've said that every year, and it's still true.
TOMMY: It's three o'clock. Everybody. One minute.

[Scene: 3:01 p.m. The silence is over. Seven glasses stand on the bar with seven lemons. People breathe again. Diego and Mr. Hayes have come in quietly during the minute.]

EDDIE: Thank you, all of you. That was the fullest silence in twenty-one years.
MR. HAYES: I hope it's okay that I came in during the minute. I tried not to open the door loudly.
EDDIE: Walter, you opened it like a bus driver, nice and smooth, so nobody heard a thing. Sit down.
DIEGO: Eddie, can I ask you something? Is it okay to talk now, or should we stay quiet?
EDDIE: It's okay, because the minute is over. Now we talk, and that's the second part. It's the more important part.
DIEGO: It's been a year since the last Memorial Day. What's changed for you since then?
EDDIE: A lot, so let me think. I've learned to use a smartphone. I've skated on ice. For the first time in fifty years. And I've married two people on the internet.
MRS. ESPOSITO: You've also become a homeowner, Eddie. You hold share number one.
EDDIE: I've become a homeowner at eighty-four. I've never owned anything bigger than a toolbox, and now I own one fifth of a building.
MARCUS: And you've lost Jimmy this year.
EDDIE: I've lost Jimmy. I lost him in November, and I still miss him every single day. Every year, you gain something and you lose something. That's the deal.
MR. HAYES: Can I ask you all a question? Have you all lost someone? Is that the reason you come here every year?
MRS. ESPOSITO: I've lost Carmine. Thirty-three years ago. But I've never sat in this bar on this day until today, because I always stayed upstairs.
MARCUS: I've never lost anyone close, not like that. But my uncle has lost too many friends, so I come for him, because he can't.
MR. HAYES: I've lost my mother, in two thousand twelve. And I lost my brother in Vietnam in nineteen sixty-nine, when he was only nineteen.
EDDIE: Walter, you never said anything. You had a brother?
MR. HAYES: Raymond, but everybody called him Ray. He was ten years older than me, so I was only nine. I don't remember his face well, but I remember his laugh, and his shoes, which were always polished.
EDDIE: Nineteen sixty-nine. I was already home by then, working at the port. I might have passed him when they shipped out.
MR. HAYES: Maybe you did. I like to think that maybe you did.
EDDIE: Then we need one more glass, for Ray Hayes. Marcus, can you pour it?
MARCUS: That's eight glasses. I'm pouring this one for Ray.
DIEGO: Eddie... can I say something? It might be the wrong thing to say.
EDDIE: Nothing is wrong at this bar today, so go ahead and say it.
DIEGO: I haven't lost anybody. Ever. My grandparents are alive, and so is my mom, and so is everybody. Is it okay that I'm here if I haven't lost anyone?
EDDIE: Diego, come here. That's the most important question anybody has asked today.
EDDIE: You're here so that you can remember for us. Someday I won't be here, and Dolores won't be here, and you'll pour the first glass for Danny, even though you never met him.
DIEGO: For Danny? The man I never met?
EDDIE: For Danny, who drew little fish. And for Peggy, who skated backward, and for Jimmy, who said, "Then lift." You'll know them because you heard about them. That's what remembering is.
DIEGO: Okay, I'll remember, I promise. I'm good at remembering, and besides, I'm the youth president.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And he keeps the book now. Nobody forgets anything that goes in the book.
MRS. ESPOSITO: So tonight, Diego, you write their names in it, all eight of them, in your neatest handwriting.

[Scene: 5:30 p.m. The backyard behind The Hook. Hot dogs on the old grill from 1998. Ramon works the tongs. Diego runs a lemonade table. Gina, Tasha, Tommy, and Marisol stand by the fence.]

DIEGO: Lemonade! One dollar! Half goes to the pantry! And the other half also goes to the pantry!
MARISOL: Mijo, if both halves go to the pantry, then that's all of it.
DIEGO: I know. It's a marketing trick, and Jesse taught it to me.
TASHA: So, Gina, how's your first day going? As the boss, I mean?
GINA: I've made three decisions so far. The hot dogs are free, like every year. Tasha makes the coffee. And Dad isn't allowed to touch the grill.
TOMMY: I can't touch the grill? It's my grill! I've had it since nineteen ninety-eight!
GINA: It's a holiday, Dad, so the grill is Ramon's today. You're a customer, remember?
TOMMY: A customer, on Memorial Day, in my own backyard. I can't believe this.
TASHA: So how does it feel, being a customer for once?
TOMMY: It's strange, but it's good. It's like taking a vacation in a place I already know.
MARISOL: Gina, about the lottery for 3B, they finally announced the date. It's June fifteenth, online, and there are two hundred and forty applications.
GINA: Two hundred and forty? So Jesse and Tess have a one-in-two-forty chance.
DIEGO: That's about zero point four percent. I calculated it on a napkin.
MARISOL: The lottery is fair, and that's the whole point. We accept the result, whatever happens.
GINA: And whatever happens, the first dinner is free for whoever moves in. I promised that.

[Scene: 7:40 p.m. Valentino Pier. The sun is low over the harbor. Eddie sits on a folding stool at the edge with a fishing rod. Diego holds a small cup. Jesse, still in his crooked tie, holds a second rod. Tommy brings a cooler.]

DIEGO: Eddie, I bought the worms at the bait shop on Van Brunt. The man said they're the best worms in Brooklyn.
EDDIE: Good worms, just like Danny said. "Bring good worms." I'm fifty-nine years late, but I brought them.
JESSE: Have you ever fished here, Eddie? I mean, since Danny?
EDDIE: Never, not once in fifty-nine years. I've walked past this pier ten thousand times, but I've never put a line in the water, because it felt wrong without him.
TOMMY: You taught me to fish when I was a kid, Eddie. But it wasn't here. We always went to Sheepshead Bay.
EDDIE: Not here, never here, because this was Danny's pier. But today I want to try, with the youth president and the man in the tie.
DIEGO: How do I put the worm on the hook? It keeps moving, and it's really squishy.
EDDIE: Gently, like this, right through the middle. Don't be afraid, because the worm is brave. Be brave like the worm.
DIEGO: Be brave like the worm. Okay. Gross, gross, gross. Okay, it's done.
EDDIE: Now cast it, but not too hard. Just let the line go, like you're letting out a breath.
JESSE: Mine only went three feet. Is that good?
EDDIE: It's a beginning. Danny's first cast went two feet, and it landed in my hair.
JESSE: In your hair? How is that even possible?
EDDIE: He cast backward, the same way Peggy skated. Some people only know one direction.
TOMMY: Eddie, I've never asked you this. Have you ever caught anything good here, you and Danny?
EDDIE: Never, not once in twenty-two years. We caught old shoes, a bicycle wheel, and a crab that bit Danny's thumb. But we never caught a real fish.
DIEGO: Eddie! Eddie! Something's pulling! My line is pulling really hard!
EDDIE: Hold it! Hold on tight, and don't pull too fast! Reel it in slowly! Slowly!
JESSE: It's a fish! It's a real fish! Diego, you've got a fish!
TOMMY: Look at that! It's a striper, a striped bass, right here in Red Hook!
DIEGO: I caught it! I caught a fish on Danny's pier! Eddie, look at it!
EDDIE: You caught it, on your very first try. Fifty-nine years, and the first fish on this pier... is yours.
DIEGO: Is it too small? Do we get to keep it?
EDDIE: It's too small, so we let it go, because that's the rule. But first, look at it and remember it. The little fish.
DIEGO: The little fish, just like the one on Danny's letter.
EDDIE: Exactly like the one on Danny's letter. Okay, now let it go, gently, back to the harbor.
DIEGO: Bye, fish. Tell Danny we brought good worms this time.
JESSE: That's... I'm crying. I'm crying in a tie, on a pier, over a fish.
TOMMY: Everybody cries on this pier, Jesse. It's that kind of pier.
EDDIE: Danny, did you see that? The kid caught one. Finally, after all these years, one of us caught one.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **현재완료 종합**
  - 계속: *He's done it every year since two thousand six, and he's never been late once.* / *I've lost Carmine. Thirty-three years ago.* (상실이 지금까지 이어진다는 느낌)
  - 경험: *Have you ever fished here, Eddie?* / *I've never sat in this bar on this day until today* / *I've skated on ice. For the first time in fifty years.*
  - 결과: *I've become a homeowner at eighty-four.* / *I've learned to use a smartphone.*
  - 기간: *It's been a year since the last Memorial Day.*
  - 아직: *I haven't lost anybody. Ever.*
  - ※ 과거 시점이 분명하면 과거형: *I lost him in November* / 지금과의 연결을 강조하면 현재완료: *I've lost Jimmy.*
- **애도·회고 표현**
  - *That's the deal.* — "그게 삶의 조건이야." 얻고 잃는 것.
  - *That's a kind of service.* — "그것도 일종의 복무예요." 버스 기사의 공적인 일을 기린다.
  - *That's what remembering is.* — "그게 기억한다는 거야."
- **낚시 어휘** — *fishing rod* 낚싯대 / *bait shop* 미끼 가게 / *worms* 지렁이 / *cast* 던지다 / *reel* 감다 / *striped bass (striper)* 줄무늬농어 / *let it go* 놓아주다
- **Be brave like the worm.** — "지렁이처럼 용감해져." 에디의 농담 섞인 격려.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 15화와 같은 장면(2시 58분, 넥타이, 빈 잔, 물고기 그림)을 같은 리듬으로 읽되, 사람이 늘어난 만큼 따뜻하게.
- 잔을 하나씩 늘려 가는 장면은 이름마다 쉼: *Danny's. | And Peggy's. | And Jimmy's. | And Angela's.*
- *It's three o'clock. Everybody. One minute.* 다음엔 실제로 침묵을 둔다.
- 현재완료 문장은 *I've*를 [아이브]로 약하게, 과거분사를 강하게: *I've LOST Jimmy.*
- 낚시 장면은 갑자기 빨라지고 커진다. *Something's pulling!*에서 숨이 차게.
- 마지막 에디의 *One of us caught one.*은 하늘을 보듯, 아주 조용하게.

## 한글 해석

타샤: 지나, 사장님, 대표 동업자님, 이제 뭐라고 부르든 간에요. 거기서 뭐 써요?
지나: 칠판이요. 내 첫 칠판인데 20분째 쳐다보고만 있어요. 분필 한 자루 때문에 이렇게 긴장한 적은 처음이에요.
토미: 그냥 메뉴 쓰고, 맨 아래에 한 줄 넣어. 늘 그거면 충분해.
지나: 아빠, 손님 스툴에 앉아 계시네요. 21년 동안 바의 이쪽에 앉은 적은 한 번도 없잖아요.
토미: 한 번 앉아 봤지. 2006년에, 문 열기 전에. 손님들이 뭘 보는지 보라고 네 엄마가 여기 앉혔어.
지나: 그래서 뭘 보셨는데요?
토미: 엄마를 봤지. 바 뒤에서 뭔가에 웃고 있는. 생각했어. "손님들이 여길 좋아하겠다." 맞았지. 다들 엄마를 좋아했으니까.
타샤: 그다음엔 토미도 좋아했죠, 결국엔요. 커피에도 불구하고 좋아했으니, 그게 토미에 대해 많은 걸 말해 주죠.
지나: 좋아요, 됐다. "메모리얼 데이. 5시 이후 핫도그 공짜. 레모네이드는 디에고가. 3시 묵념." 그리고 맨 아래 줄은…
토미: 계속해. 맨 아래 줄은 뭐야?
지나: "오늘의 커피: 여전히 타샤 것. 어떤 건 변하고. 어떤 건 안 변한다."
토미: 좋다. 아주 좋아, 지나. 네 엄마가 딱 그렇게 썼을 거야.
제시: 안녕하세요, 여러분. 제가 일찍 왔어요? 에디보다 먼저 오고 싶었는데, 에디 아직 안 왔어요?
타샤: 제시! 이것 좀 봐, 넥타이 맸네!
제시: 약속했잖아요. 작년에, 바로 여기서요. "내년 3시엔 에디 옆에 앉을게요"라고요. 그래서 토요일에 넥타이를 샀어요. 사촌 결혼식 이후 처음이에요.
지나: 그거 삐뚤어졌어, 알지?
제시: 첫 시도니까 좀 봐줘요. 엄밀히 말하면 그래도 넥타이예요.
토미: 앉아, 제시. 에디 스툴 바로 옆에. 2시 58분에 올 거야. 늘 2시 58분에 오거든.
제시: 왜 2시 58분이에요? 왜 다른 사람들처럼 3시가 아니고요?
토미: 지팡이 짚고 문에서 스툴까지 걸어오는 데 2분 걸려. 2006년부터 매년 그렇게 했고, 한 번도 안 늦었어.
타샤: 작년엔, 제시, 메모리얼 데이가 뭐 하는 날인지도 몰랐잖아. 그냥 할인 행사 주말인 줄 알았지.
제시: 매트리스 할인이랑 바비큐 하는 날인 줄 알았어요. 스크랜턴 출신인데, 퍼레이드도 있었지만 전 늘 아빠랑 매트리스 가게에 있었거든요.
지나: 그런데 지금은 그날을 위해 넥타이를 맸네. 1년 만에 큰 변화다.
제시: 이제 이름을 알잖아요. 대니 같은. 이름을 알면, 그게 많은 걸 바꿔요.
에디: 안녕하세요, 여러분. 토미, 지나, 타샤… 그리고 제시. 이것 봐라, 넥타이를 맸구나.
제시: 작년에 맨다고 약속했잖아요. 기억나요?
