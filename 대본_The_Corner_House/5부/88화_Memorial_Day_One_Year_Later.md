# THE CORNER HOUSE — 5부 88화

## Memorial Day, One Year Later (메모리얼 데이, 1년 뒤)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 5부 16화(통산 88화) · 시기: 2027년 5월 31일(월) 메모리얼 데이 오후 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 오후 2시 반의 더 훅 → 3시의 묵념 → 묵념 뒤의 바 → 해 질 녘 발렌티노 부두
> - 등장인물: GINA, TASHA, TOMMY, JESSE, EDDIE, MRS. ESPOSITO, MARCUS, MR. HAYES, DIEGO
> - 사건: 1년 전 15화와 같은 날, 같은 시각. 지나가 대표 동업자로서 처음 문을 연다. 제시는 1년 전 약속대로 넥타이를 매고 와서 에디 옆에 앉는다. 3시 정각, 대니 월시를 위한 빈 잔 옆에 올해는 잔이 몇 개 더 놓인다. 페기, 지미, 앤젤라, 카르미네, 그리고 마커스 삼촌의 전우들. 묵념이 끝나자 에디는 1년을 돌아본다. 사람들은 각자 잃은 사람을 이야기한다. 아무도 잃어 본 적 없는 디에고는 그래도 괜찮은지 묻는다. 해 질 녘 에디는 디에고와 제시를 데리고 부두로 간다. 대니의 마지막 편지에 적힌 대로 '좋은 지렁이'를 들고. 그리고 처음으로 고기가 잡힌다.
> - 학습 포인트: 현재완료 종합(I've come here every year since… / It's been a year since… / We've lost… / Have you ever…? / I haven't… yet. / I've never…), 회고와 노년의 어조, 15화와 대비 낭독
> - 미국 현안: 메모리얼 데이의 의미(전사자 추모), 베트남전과 이라크전 참전 세대, 지역 공동체의 애도 의식
> - 연결: 15화(2006년부터 3시 묵념, 빈 잔, 대니 월시 — 커피 스트리트·1944년생·편지마다 작은 물고기·마지막 편지 '부두에서 보자, 에디. 좋은 지렁이 가져와', 제시 '내년 3시에 에디 옆에'), 마커스 삼촌(이라크 2회, VA 병원, 전우 라미레스·콜). 53화 지미. 81화 카르미네. 85화 에디·페기 52주년. 86화 '내일부터: 지나를 찾아 주세요'. 84화 '다른 학교, 같은 부두'.

## 대본

[Scene: Monday, May 31, 2:30 p.m. The Hook. Quieter than usual. Small flags on the tables, the same as last year. Gina stands at the chalkboard with a new piece of chalk. Tasha polishes glasses. Tommy sits on a customer's stool for the first time in twenty-one years.]

TASHA: Gina. Boss. Managing partner. What are you writing?
GINA: The board. My first board. I've been staring at it for twenty minutes. I've never been so nervous about chalk.
TOMMY: Write the menu. Then one line at the bottom. That's all.
GINA: Dad, you're sitting on a customer stool. You've never sat on that side of the bar.
TOMMY: I've sat here once. In two thousand six. Before we opened. Your mother made me sit here. To see what the customers see.
GINA: What did you see?
TOMMY: I saw her. Behind the bar. Laughing. I thought, "Customers are going to love this place." I was right. They loved her.
TASHA: And then they loved you. Eventually. Despite the coffee.
GINA: Okay. Here. "Memorial Day. Hot dogs free after five. Lemonade by Diego. Moment of silence at three." And the bottom line...
TOMMY: What's the bottom line?
GINA: "Today's coffee: still Tasha's. Some things change. Some don't."
TOMMY: That's good. That's very good. Your mother would have written that.
JESSE: Hey. Am I early? Is Eddie here yet?
TASHA: Jesse! You're wearing a tie!
JESSE: I promised. Last year. Right here. I said, "Next year, at three, I'll sit with you, Eddie." So I bought a tie. On Saturday. It's my first tie since my cousin's wedding.
GINA: It's crooked.
JESSE: It's a first attempt. Technically, it's still a tie.
TOMMY: Sit, Jesse. Next to the stool. Eddie's stool. He'll be here at two fifty-eight. He's always here at two fifty-eight.
JESSE: Why two fifty-eight?
TOMMY: Two minutes to walk from the door to the stool. With the cane. He's done it every year since two thousand six. He's never been late.
TASHA: Last year, Jesse, you didn't even know what Memorial Day was for. You thought it was a sale weekend.
JESSE: I thought it was mattress sales. And barbecues. I'm from Scranton. We had a parade, but I was always at the mattress store with my dad.
GINA: And now you're wearing a tie for it.
JESSE: Now I know the names. Danny. That changes things.

[Scene: 2:58 p.m. The door opens slowly. Eddie, in a jacket and tie, walks in with his cane. In his other hand, he holds a small envelope. Mrs. Esposito follows him with a paper bag.]

EDDIE: Good afternoon, everybody. Tommy. Gina. Tasha. Jesse... you wore a tie.
JESSE: I promised you. Last year. Remember?
EDDIE: I remember. I just didn't believe you. People promise a lot of things at three o'clock. Thank you, son.
TOMMY: Eddie. Your glass is ready. For Danny. Same as every year.
EDDIE: Thank you, Tommy. And today... can we have more glasses? I've thought about it all night.
TOMMY: Of course. How many?
EDDIE: Danny's. And Peggy's. Today is fifty-two years. And Jimmy's. And Angela's.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And Carmine's. If that's okay. He never served. But he drove the B61 for eighteen years. That's a kind of service.
EDDIE: Carmine's too. Of course.
MARCUS: And two more. For my uncle's friends. Ramirez and Cole. Fallujah. Two thousand four. My uncle can't come. He's working at the VA today. He always works today.
TOMMY: Seven glasses. Tasha, seven glasses. Full. Nobody drinks them.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And I brought this. Lemons. One for each glass. Angela started it. Now it's ours.
EDDIE: A lemon next to every glass. Look at that. A bar full of lemons. Peggy would laugh.
TOMMY: Angela used to pour Danny's glass. Every year. Right there. Behind the bar. For eight years now, I've poured it for her.
EDDIE: Here. Danny's letter. The last one. And Peggy. Her picture. The only one left. The water took the rest. In twenty twelve.
JESSE: Can I see the fish again? On the letter?
EDDIE: Here. The little fish. At the bottom. "See you at the pier, Eddie. Bring good worms."
JESSE: It's still a good fish.
EDDIE: He drew better than he fished. I've said that every year. It's still true.
TOMMY: It's three o'clock. Everybody. One minute.

[Scene: 3:01 p.m. The silence is over. Seven glasses stand on the bar with seven lemons. People breathe again. Diego and Mr. Hayes have come in quietly during the minute.]

EDDIE: Thank you. All of you. That was the fullest silence in twenty-one years.
MR. HAYES: I hope it's okay that I came in. During the minute. I didn't want to open the door loudly.
EDDIE: Walter, you opened it like a bus driver. Smooth. Nobody heard. Sit.
DIEGO: Eddie, can I ask something? Is it okay to talk now?
EDDIE: It's okay. The minute is over. Now we talk. That's the second part. The more important part.
DIEGO: It's been a year since the last Memorial Day. What's changed? For you?
EDDIE: A lot. Let me think. I've learned to use a smartphone. I've skated on ice. For the first time in fifty years. I've married two people. On the internet.
MRS. ESPOSITO: You've also become a homeowner. Share number one.
EDDIE: I've become a homeowner. At eighty-four. I've never owned anything bigger than a toolbox. Now I own one fifth of a building.
MARCUS: And you've lost Jimmy.
EDDIE: I've lost Jimmy. In November. Every year, you gain something. And you lose something. That's the deal.
MR. HAYES: Can I ask you all a question? Have you all lost someone? Is that why you come?
MRS. ESPOSITO: I've lost Carmine. Thirty-three years ago. I've never sat in this bar on this day. Until today. I always stayed upstairs.
MARCUS: I've never lost anyone close. Not like that. But my uncle has. He's lost too many friends. I come for him. Because he can't.
MR. HAYES: I've lost my mother. In two thousand twelve. And my brother. In Vietnam. Nineteen sixty-nine. He was nineteen.
EDDIE: Walter. You never said. Your brother?
MR. HAYES: Raymond. Ray. He was older than me. Ten years. I was nine. I don't remember his face well. I remember his laugh. And his shoes. Always polished.
EDDIE: Nineteen sixty-nine. I was home by then. I might have passed him. At the port. When they shipped out.
MR. HAYES: Maybe. Maybe you did.
EDDIE: Then one more glass. For Ray Hayes. Marcus, can you pour it?
MARCUS: Eight glasses. Pouring. For Ray.
DIEGO: Eddie... can I say something? It might be wrong.
EDDIE: Nothing is wrong at this bar today. Say it.
DIEGO: I haven't lost anybody. Ever. My grandparents are alive. My mom. Everybody. Is it okay that I'm here? If I haven't lost anyone?
EDDIE: Diego. Come here. That's the most important question anybody has asked today.
EDDIE: You're here so that you remember for us. Someday, I won't be here. And Dolores won't be here. And you'll pour the first glass for Danny. Even though you never met him.
DIEGO: For Danny? Who I never met?
EDDIE: For Danny. Who drew little fish. And for Peggy. Who skated backward. And for Jimmy. Who said, "Then lift." You'll know them. Because you heard. That's what remembering is.
DIEGO: Okay. I'll remember. I promise. I'm good at remembering. I'm the youth president.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And he keeps the book. Nobody forgets anything in the book.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And he keeps the book. Nobody forgets anything in the book.

[Scene: 5:30 p.m. The backyard behind The Hook. Hot dogs on the old grill from 1998. Ramon works the tongs. Diego runs a lemonade table. Gina, Tasha, Tommy, and Marisol stand by the fence.]

DIEGO: Lemonade! One dollar! Half for the pantry! The other half also for the pantry!
MARISOL: Mijo, that's all for the pantry.
DIEGO: I know. It's a marketing trick. Jesse taught me.
TASHA: Gina, how's your first day? As boss?
GINA: I've made three decisions. Free hot dogs, like every year. Tasha makes the coffee. And Dad can't touch the grill.
TOMMY: Can't touch the grill? It's my grill! From nineteen ninety-eight!
GINA: It's a holiday, Dad. The grill is Ramon's today. You're a customer.
TOMMY: A customer. On Memorial Day. In my own backyard.
TASHA: How does it feel? Being a customer?
TOMMY: Strange. Good. Like a vacation. In a place I already know.
MARISOL: Gina, the lottery. For 3B. They announced the date. June fifteenth. Online. Two hundred and forty applications.
GINA: Two hundred and forty. Jesse and Tess have a one-in-two-forty chance.
DIEGO: That's about zero point four percent. I calculated it.
MARISOL: The lottery is fair. That's the point. Whatever happens.
GINA: Whatever happens, the first dinner is free. I promised.

[Scene: 7:40 p.m. Valentino Pier. The sun is low over the harbor. Eddie sits on a folding stool at the edge with a fishing rod. Diego holds a small cup. Jesse, still in his crooked tie, holds a second rod. Tommy brings a cooler.]

DIEGO: Eddie, I bought the worms. At the bait shop on Van Brunt. The man said they're the best worms in Brooklyn.
EDDIE: Good worms. Like Danny said. "Bring good worms." Fifty-nine years late. But I brought them.
JESSE: Have you ever fished here? Since Danny?
EDDIE: Never. Not once. Fifty-nine years. I've walked past this pier ten thousand times. I've never put a line in the water. It felt wrong. Without him.
TOMMY: You taught me to fish, Eddie. When I was a kid. But not here. In Sheepshead Bay.
EDDIE: Not here. Never here. This was Danny's pier. Today, I want to try. With the youth president. And the man in the tie.
DIEGO: How do I put the worm on? It's moving. It's squishy.
EDDIE: Gently. Like this. Through the middle. Don't be afraid. The worm is brave. Be brave like the worm.
DIEGO: Be brave like the worm. Okay. Gross. Okay. Done.
EDDIE: Now cast. Not too hard. Let the line go. Like letting out a breath.
JESSE: Mine went three feet. Is that good?
EDDIE: It's a beginning. Danny's first cast went two feet. Into my hair.
JESSE: Into your hair? How?
EDDIE: He cast backward. Like Peggy skated. Some people only know one direction.
TOMMY: Eddie, I've never asked. Have you ever caught anything good here? You and Danny?
EDDIE: Never. Not once. In twenty-two years. We caught old shoes. A bicycle wheel. A crab that bit Danny's thumb. Never a real fish.
DIEGO: Eddie! Eddie! Something's pulling! My line is pulling!
EDDIE: Hold it! Hold it! Don't pull too fast! Reel slowly! Slowly!
JESSE: It's a fish! It's a real fish! Diego, you've got a fish!
TOMMY: Look at that! A striper! A striped bass! In Red Hook!
DIEGO: I caught it! I caught a fish! On Danny's pier! Eddie, look!
EDDIE: You caught it. On the first try. Fifty-nine years, and the first fish on this pier... is yours.
DIEGO: Is it too small? Do we keep it?
EDDIE: It's too small. We let it go. That's the rule. But first, look at it. Remember it. The little fish.
DIEGO: The little fish. Like on Danny's letter.
EDDIE: Exactly like on Danny's letter. Okay. Let it go. Gently. Back to the harbor.
DIEGO: Bye, fish. Tell Danny we brought good worms.
JESSE: That's... I'm crying. In a tie. On a pier.
TOMMY: Everybody cries on this pier, Jesse. It's that kind of pier.
EDDIE: Danny. Did you see that? The kid caught one. Finally. One of us caught one.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **현재완료 종합**
  - 계속: *He's done it every year since two thousand six.* / *I've lost Carmine. Thirty-three years ago.* (상실이 지금까지 이어진다는 느낌)
  - 경험: *Have you ever fished here?* / *I've never sat in this bar on this day.* / *I've skated on ice. For the first time in fifty years.*
  - 결과: *I've become a homeowner.* / *I've learned to use a smartphone.*
  - 기간: *It's been a year since the last Memorial Day.*
  - 아직: *I haven't lost anybody. Ever.*
  - ※ 과거 시점이 분명하면 과거형: *I lost Jimmy in November.* / 지금과의 연결을 강조하면 현재완료: *I've lost Jimmy.*
- **애도·회고 표현**
  - *That's the deal.* — "그게 삶의 조건이야." 얻고 잃는 것.
  - *That's a kind of service.* — "그것도 일종의 복무예요." 버스 기사의 공적인 일을 기린다.
  - *That's what remembering is.* — "그게 기억한다는 거야."
- **낚시 어휘** — *fishing rod* 낚싯대 / *bait shop* 미끼 가게 / *worms* 지렁이 / *cast* 던지다 / *reel* 감다 / *striped bass (striper)* 줄무늬농어 / *let it go* 놓아주다
- **Be brave like the worm.** — "지렁이처럼 용감해져." 에디의 농담 섞인 격려.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 15화와 같은 장면(2시 58분, 넥타이, 빈 잔, 물고기 그림)을 같은 리듬으로 읽되, 사람이 늘어난 만큼 따뜻하게.
- 잔을 하나씩 늘려 가는 장면은 이름마다 쉼: *Danny's. | And Peggy's. | And Jimmy's. | And Angela's.*
- *It's three o'clock. Everybody. One minute.* 다음엔 실제로 침묵을 둔다.
- 현재완료 문장은 *I've*를 [아이브]로 약하게, 과거분사를 강하게: *I've LOST Jimmy.*
- 낚시 장면은 갑자기 빨라지고 커진다. *Something's pulling!*에서 숨이 차게.
- 마지막 에디의 *One of us caught one.*은 하늘을 보듯, 아주 조용하게.

## 한글 해석

타샤: 지나. 사장님. 대표 동업자님. 뭐 써요?
지나: 칠판이요. 내 첫 칠판. 20분째 쳐다보고 있어요. 분필 때문에 이렇게 긴장한 적은 처음이에요.
토미: 메뉴 써. 그다음 맨 아래 한 줄. 그게 다야.
지나: 아빠, 손님 스툴에 앉아 계시네요. 바의 이쪽에 앉은 적 한 번도 없잖아요.
토미: 한 번 앉아 봤지. 2006년에. 문 열기 전에. 네 엄마가 여기 앉혔어. 손님들이 뭘 보는지 보라고.
지나: 뭘 보셨는데요?
토미: 엄마를 봤지. 바 뒤에서. 웃고 있는. 생각했어. "손님들이 여길 좋아하겠다." 맞았지. 다들 엄마를 좋아했어.
타샤: 그다음엔 토미를 좋아했죠. 결국엔. 커피에도 불구하고요.
지나: 좋아요. 여기요. "메모리얼 데이. 5시 이후 핫도그 공짜. 레모네이드는 디에고가. 3시 묵념." 그리고 맨 아래 줄은…
토미: 맨 아래 줄은 뭐야?
지나: "오늘의 커피: 여전히 타샤 것. 어떤 건 변하고. 어떤 건 안 변한다."
토미: 좋다. 아주 좋아. 네 엄마가 썼을 법한 말이야.
제시: 안녕하세요. 제가 일찍 왔어요? 에디 아직 안 왔어요?
타샤: 제시! 넥타이 맸네!
제시: 약속했잖아요. 작년에요. 바로 여기서요. "내년 3시엔 에디 옆에 앉을게요"라고요. 그래서 넥타이를 샀어요. 토요일에요. 사촌 결혼식 이후 처음 매는 넥타이예요.
지나: 삐뚤어졌어.
제시: 첫 시도라서요. 엄밀히 말하면 그래도 넥타이예요.
토미: 앉아, 제시. 스툴 옆에. 에디 스툴. 2시 58분에 올 거야. 늘 2시 58분에 와.
제시: 왜 2시 58분이에요?
토미: 문에서 스툴까지 2분. 지팡이 짚고. 2006년부터 매년 그렇게 했어. 한 번도 안 늦었어.
타샤: 작년엔, 제시, 메모리얼 데이가 뭐 하는 날인지도 몰랐잖아. 할인 행사 주말인 줄 알았지.
제시: 매트리스 할인인 줄 알았어요. 그리고 바비큐요. 스크랜턴 출신이잖아요. 퍼레이드도 있었는데 전 늘 아빠랑 매트리스 가게에 있었어요.
지나: 그런데 지금은 그날을 위해 넥타이를 맸네.
제시: 이제 이름을 알잖아요. 대니요. 그게 많은 걸 바꿔요.
에디: 안녕하세요, 여러분. 토미. 지나. 타샤. 제시… 넥타이를 맸구나.
제시: 약속했잖아요. 작년에요. 기억나요?
에디: 기억나지. 그냥 안 믿었을 뿐이야. 사람들은 3시에 약속을 많이 하거든. 고맙다, 젊은이.
토미: 에디. 잔 준비됐어요. 대니 몫이요. 매년처럼요.
에디: 고마워, 토미. 그리고 오늘은… 잔을 더 놓아도 될까? 밤새 생각했어.
토미: 물론이죠. 몇 개요?
에디: 대니 거. 그리고 페기 거. 오늘이 52년이야. 그리고 지미 거. 그리고 앤젤라 거.
에스포지토 부인: 그리고 카르미네 거도요. 괜찮다면요. 군대엔 안 갔어요. 그래도 B61을 18년 동안 몰았어요. 그것도 일종의 복무예요.
에디: 카르미네 것도. 당연하지.
마커스: 그리고 두 개 더요. 삼촌 전우들 거요. 라미레스랑 콜이요. 팔루자요. 2004년이요. 삼촌은 못 와요. 오늘 VA 병원에서 일해요. 오늘은 늘 일해요.
토미: 잔 일곱 개. 타샤, 일곱 잔. 가득. 아무도 안 마셔.
에스포지토 부인: 그리고 이거 가져왔어요. 레몬이요. 잔마다 하나씩요. 앤젤라가 시작했죠. 이제 우리 거예요.
