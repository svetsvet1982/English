# THE CORNER HOUSE — 5부 75화

## The Closing (거래 마감)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 5부 3화(통산 75화) · 시기: 2027년 4월 29일(목) 오전~오후 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 맨해튼 다운타운의 권원 보험 회사 회의실 → 서명 테이블 → 송금 확인 → 오후의 건물 앞과 지하실
> - 등장인물: MS. BROOKS, MARISOL, SAM, NADIA, MS. LIN, MS. HARGROVE, WHITLOCK, TOMMY, EDDIE, MRS. ESPOSITO, DIEGO
> - 사건: 거래 마감 날. 권원 보험 회사의 마감 담당자 린 씨가 서류를 한 장씩 넘긴다. 양도 증서, 권원 보험, 에스크로에 맡긴 계약금, 랜드 트러스트의 99년 토지 임대 계약, 브루클린 네이버후드 펀드 담보 대출, 시의 융자 서류, 긴급 수리 청구서 정산, 보일러 공제, 일할 계산한 4월 임대료. 마리솔과 샘이 수십 장에 서명하고, 나디아가 땅을 받는다. 정오가 지나 송금이 확인되자 휘틀록이 열쇠 꾸러미를 건넨다. 오후에 레드훅으로 돌아온 마리솔은 지하실 열쇠를 에디에게 준다.
> - 학습 포인트: 계약 어휘(deed, title, title insurance, escrow, closing, wire transfer, payoff, proration, lien, recorded), 확인 질문(Just to confirm… / So that means… / Can you walk us through…? / Is that correct?), 격식 확인 질문의 상승 억양
> - 미국 현안: 부동산 거래 마감 절차, 권원 보험과 에스크로, 주민 소유 전환의 최종 단계, 공공·비영리 자금의 결합
> - 연결: 73화 수락(335만, 4/30까지 마감). 74화 3/15 매매 계약 서명. 69화 계약금 3만·보일러 공제 4.5만·HPD 긴급 수리 청구서 2.2만. 59화 재원 구조. 61·73화 아르투로 3/18 시민권 선서식. 72화 코왈스키·커먼 그라운드 5/1 관리 시작. 54화 토미 계약 5/31 만료.

## 대본

[Scene: Thursday, April 29, 9:30 a.m. A conference room in Lower Manhattan. A long table with stacks of paper, each with colored "sign here" tabs. Danielle Brooks, Marisol, Sam, and Nadia sit together, early.]

MS. BROOKS: Okay, today you'll sign about a hundred and twenty pages, maybe more. Don't panic, because most of them say the same thing in different words.
SAM: A hundred and twenty pages? Then it's a good thing I brought three pens, in case two of them die.
MARISOL: I only brought one pen, but it's Papi's pen from his citizenship ceremony. He lent it to me for today.
NADIA: How was the ceremony, by the way? I'm sorry I missed it, because I really wanted to be there.
MARISOL: It was beautiful. On March eighteenth, there were two hundred people from sixty countries, and Diego held the flag. Papi cried when they said, "Welcome, fellow Americans."
SAM: And what about Héctor? Was he on time for once?
MARISOL: He was forty minutes early, for the first time in his life. He took the R train at five a.m., just like he promised.
MS. BROOKS: That's a good sign for today. Okay, there's one rule: if you don't understand something, ask out loud. Say, "Just to confirm," because closers love that phrase.
SAM: "Just to confirm." Got it. I'll probably say it fifty times.
MS. BROOKS: And don't sign anything until I nod at you. I'll nod, like this.
NADIA: That's a very small nod. I'm not sure I'd even notice it.
MS. BROOKS: Lawyers have small nods. It's part of our training.

[Scene: 10:05 a.m. The same room. Ms. Lin, the closer from the title company, sits at the head of the table. Ms. Hargrove sits quietly across from Marisol and Sam, initialing pages. Whitlock has not arrived yet.]

MS. LIN: Good morning, everyone. I'm Grace Lin from the title company, and I'll be running the closing today. We'll go document by document, so please stop me anytime you have a question.
MS. LIN: Ms. Hargrove tells me that Mr. Whitlock will join us at noon for the keys. He already signed his documents in advance.
MS. LIN: So let's begin. Document one is the deed, which transfers ownership of the building from Harborline Red Hook LLC to the Corner House Housing Development Fund Corporation.
MARISOL: Just to confirm. That's us? The co-op?
MS. LIN: That's you, yes, because it's the co-op's legal name. And a separate deed transfers the land to the Harbor Commons Land Trust, which Ms. Okafor signs.
NADIA: That's me, signing for the land, which is one point four million. I have my board's blessing, and also a lot of their nervous texts.
MS. LIN: Document two is the title insurance. We've searched the property's history all the way back to nineteen hundred and twelve, and there were no surprises, except for one lien: the HPD emergency repair bill.
SAM: That's the twenty-two thousand dollars, for the boiler repair back in January.
MS. LIN: Correct. It will be paid off from the seller's proceeds today, before the deed is recorded. That way, the co-op receives the building with no liens on it.
SAM: Just to confirm, Harborline pays the twenty-two thousand? Not us?
MS. LIN: Harborline pays it, because it's in the contract, in section nine. Ms. Hargrove confirmed it again this morning.
MS. BROOKS: That's correct. It's section nine, and I checked it myself.
MS. LIN: Document three is the closing statement, which means the money. Can I walk you through it?
MARISOL: Yes, please, and slowly, because Sam will want to check every single number.
SAM: Actually, I've already checked every number twice, last night. But please, go ahead anyway.
MS. LIN: The purchase price for the building is one point nine five million. Minus the boiler credit, that's forty-five thousand, and minus the deposit already held in escrow, that's thirty thousand.
SAM: So that means the co-op pays one million eight hundred seventy-five thousand today, at closing.
MS. LIN: Correct, plus the closing costs, which are title insurance, recording fees, and transfer taxes. That's about thirty-eight thousand, and those were in your budget.
SAM: That's line forty, where we estimated thirty-nine thousand. So we're a thousand under, which makes it green.
MS. LIN: Then there's the proration, which is the rent for April. The tenants paid Harborline for the whole month, but the co-op owns the building from today. So Harborline credits the co-op for one day, April thirtieth.
MARISOL: One day of rent, for five households and two stores? How much does that come to?
MS. LIN: It comes to six hundred and twelve dollars and forty cents.
SAM: Can I keep the forty cents, as a souvenir?
MS. LIN: I'm afraid it goes into the co-op's account. But I'll print you a copy of the statement, and you can frame that instead.
MS. LIN: Document four is the mortgage, from the Brooklyn Neighborhood Fund. It's one point one million, for thirty years, at six point five percent, fixed.
SAM: Six point five, fixed. I've been saying that in my sleep for two months, according to Priya.
MS. LIN: Document five is the city's loan, which is eight hundred thousand at one percent, with payments deferred for five years. It comes with the regulatory agreement, which says the building stays affordable, with income limits for future buyers.
MARISOL: Is that the one that says the building stays affordable forever?
MS. BROOKS: That one says forty years, but the land trust's ground lease says ninety-nine. Together, they say "a very long time." Close enough to forever.
MS. LIN: Document six is the ground lease, between the co-op and the land trust. Ms. Okafor and Ms. Reyes both sign it, on pages fourteen to thirty-one.
NADIA: Are you ready, Marisol?
MARISOL: I'm ready, and I've got Papi's pen.
MS. BROOKS: Nod.
MARISOL: Okay, she nodded, so I'm signing. "Marisol Reyes. President." Page fourteen, fifteen, sixteen...
SAM: Wow, you're signing so fast! Slow down, or you'll sign the table!
MARISOL: Look, my hand is not shaking. For the first time in this whole process, it's not shaking.

[Scene: 12:20 p.m. The same room. The paper stacks have moved from one side of the table to the other. Ms. Lin looks at her laptop. Whitlock enters, holding a small paper bag.]

MS. LIN: Okay, I'm checking the wires now. The lender's funds... received. The city's funds... received. The co-op's funds... received. The land trust's funds... received.
SAM: All four of them? All four are in?
MS. LIN: All four are in. Harborline's loan payoff has been sent, and the lien is paid. I'm sending the deed to be recorded with the city right now.
MS. LIN: As of twelve twenty-three p.m., the Corner House Housing Development Fund Corporation owns the building at Van Brunt and Pioneer.
MARISOL: ...
NADIA: Marisol, breathe. Just breathe for a second.
MARISOL: I'm breathing, I'm breathing. It's real. It's recorded. It's real.
WHITLOCK: Congratulations, everyone. I'm sorry I'm late, but I wanted to bring these in person.
MARISOL: Mr. Whitlock. I didn't think you'd come yourself.
WHITLOCK: These are the keys, all of them: the front door, the basement, the roof, the boiler room, and the storage room behind the bar. And then there's this one.
NADIA: What's that one? It looks really old.
WHITLOCK: Honestly, I don't know. It was in Sal Cappello's desk, in the basement office, and it has a tag on it that says, "4C — Sal's."
MARISOL: 4C? But that's the empty apartment.
WHITLOCK: Nobody at Harborline ever found the lock it opens. Maybe you will.
SAM: But why would Sal have a special key to 4C, of all apartments?
WHITLOCK: I have no idea, and that's your mystery now, not mine.
MARISOL: Mr. Whitlock, can I ask you something? What's in the paper bag?
WHITLOCK: Oh, this. It's coffee beans from a roaster in Red Hook, for Mr. Russo. Tell him to throw away whatever he's using now.
SAM: That's the nicest insult Tommy will ever receive.
WHITLOCK: And Ms. Reyes? The Community Board voted on Pier Seven last week: "No, unless conditions are met." There are twelve conditions, and two of them are Mrs. Esposito's.
MARISOL: She'll be very happy when she hears that. She'll probably laminate them.
WHITLOCK: I believe it. Goodbye, Ms. Reyes, and good luck with your building.
MARISOL: Goodbye, Mr. Whitlock, and thank you, for reading the letter and for sending back the drawing.
WHITLOCK: The drawing belongs where it is now. Goodbye, everyone.
MS. LIN: In thirty years of closings, I've never seen that before, a seller bringing coffee.
NADIA: That's because in thirty years, you've never seen a buyer like this one.

[Scene: 3:30 p.m. Van Brunt Street. The corner building in spring sun. Tommy, Eddie, Mrs. Esposito, and Diego wait on the stoop. Marisol walks up from the bus stop holding up the ring of keys.]

DIEGO: Mom! Mom! Is it done? Is it really ours now?
MARISOL: It's done, and it's recorded. As of twelve twenty-three p.m., it's ours.
TOMMY: Twelve twenty-three. I'm going to get that tattooed on my arm.
MRS. ESPOSITO: You will not, Tommy. You'll write it on the chalkboard instead, like a normal person.
EDDIE: Marisol, are those the keys you're holding?
MARISOL: These are the keys, all of them. Whitlock brought them himself, along with coffee beans for Tommy.
TOMMY: Coffee beans? For me? Wait, was that supposed to be an insult?
MARISOL: It was the nicest insult you'll ever receive. Sam said so.
TOMMY: Then I'll accept it, and I'll grind them tomorrow. But I'll still find a way to make them terrible.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And what about the Community Board? Did Whitlock say anything about the vote?
MARISOL: They voted "no, unless conditions are met," and there are twelve conditions. Two of them are yours, Dolores.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Two of mine? Which two did they take?
MARISOL: Enforceable flood-wall commitments, and penalties for missed repairs.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Then I'm going to laminate them, both of them.
MARISOL: Eddie, this one is for you.
EDDIE: Which one? There are a lot of keys on that ring.
MARISOL: It's the basement key, for the boiler room. For fifty-two years, you've wanted to see the boiler without asking anybody's permission.
EDDIE: The basement key. Sal never let anybody have it, not even me.
MARISOL: Well, now you don't need permission, because it's your building. Go and see it.
EDDIE: Dolores, come with me. Diego, you come too, and hold my arm on the stairs.
DIEGO: Is it scary down there?
EDDIE: It's the same old basement, but today it's our basement, and that's different.

[Scene: 3:45 p.m. The basement. A bare bulb. The old 1992 boiler, patched with the city's used part. A new boiler waits in a crate against the wall, with a delivery tag dated May 3. Eddie unlocks the door himself.]

EDDIE: Look at that, the old one. It's still running, but barely. Just like me.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And there's the new one, still in the box. They're putting it in next Monday, and Brian says it will be installed in one day.
DIEGO: The new one is huge! It looks like a robot!
EDDIE: Put your hand on the old one, Diego, and feel it. It's warm.
DIEGO: It's warm, and it's shaking a little bit.
EDDIE: That's because it's tired. It worked for thirty-five years, and it kept Peggy warm, and me, and your mother, when she was pregnant with you.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And it also froze you in January, if you remember.
EDDIE: Nobody's perfect, Dolores, not even boilers. Thank you, old boiler. You can finally rest next week.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Will you miss it? Tell me honestly.
EDDIE: A little, yes. It made a noise every morning at six, clank, clank, like an old man getting out of bed.
DIEGO: Like you!
EDDIE: Like me, exactly. The new one will be quiet, so I'll need a new alarm clock.
MRS. ESPOSITO: But you already have one. Eight o'clock, two knocks.
EDDIE: That's not an alarm clock, Dolores. That's a friendship.
DIEGO: Eddie, what's that on the wall, behind the pipe?
EDDIE: Where? Oh. It's a little door, a metal box built into the wall. I've never seen that before.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Neither have I, not in thirty-one years. Is it locked?
DIEGO: Yes, it's locked. There's a keyhole, but it's a small one.
EDDIE: Marisol said there was an old key with a tag, "4C — Sal's." Maybe it's not for 4C at all.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Eddie, don't touch it. We should wait for Marisol, and a lawyer, and possibly a priest.
EDDIE: Dolores, it's our building now, and it's our basement. Diego, run upstairs and ask your mother for the old key, the one with the tag.
DIEGO: I'm the runner, and I'm fast! I'll be back in one minute!
MRS. ESPOSITO: Eddie O'Neill. It's been fifty-two years, and you're still looking for trouble.
EDDIE: It's not trouble, Dolores, it's a story. There's always one more story in an old building.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **확인 질문**
  - *Just to confirm. That's us?* — "확인차 여쭤보면, 그게 저희인가요?"
  - *So that means the co-op pays…* — "그러니까 조합이 ~을 내는 거네요."
  - *Can I walk you through it?* — "차근차근 설명해 드려도 될까요?"
  - *Correct. / That's correct.* — "맞습니다."
- **계약 어휘**
  - *deed* 양도 증서 / *title* 소유권, 권원 / *title insurance* 권원 보험 / *recorded* (시에) 등기된
  - *escrow* 제3자 예치 / *closing statement* 정산서 / *closing costs* 마감 비용 / *transfer taxes* 양도세
  - *proration* 일할 계산 / *payoff* 대출 상환 / *lien* 유치권 / *wire* 송금
  - *regulatory agreement* (시와 맺는) 규제 약정 / *ground lease* 토지 임대 계약
- **As of twelve twenty-three p.m., the co-op owns the building.** — "오후 12시 23분부로 조합이 건물을 소유합니다."
- **Close enough to forever.** — "영원이나 다름없죠."
- **That's the nicest insult you'll ever receive.** — "평생 받을 욕 중에 제일 친절한 욕이네요."
- **possibly a priest** — "어쩌면 신부님까지." 부인의 과장된 걱정.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 확인 질문은 끝을 올린다: *Just to confirm. | That's us?↗* / *Harborline pays the twenty-two thousand?↗*
- 린 씨는 마감 전문가답게 차분하고 일정하게. 서류 번호(*Document one… Document two…*)에서 매번 짧게 쉰다.
- 숫자: *one million eight hundred seventy-five thousand* / *six hundred and twelve dollars and forty cents*.
- 송금 확인 장면은 한 줄씩 느리게: *received. | received. | received.* — 마지막에 *All four are in.*
- 마리솔의 *It's real. It's recorded. It's real.* — 두 번째 *real*은 울음 직전.
- 지하실 장면은 다시 작고 신비롭게. 에디의 *There's always one more story in an old building.*
