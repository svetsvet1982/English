# THE CORNER HOUSE — 5부 75화

## The Closing (거래 마감)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 5부 3화(통산 75화) · 시기: 2027년 4월 29일(목) 오전~오후 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 맨해튼 다운타운의 권원 보험 회사 회의실 → 서명 테이블 → 송금 확인 → 오후의 건물 앞과 지하실
> - 등장인물: MS. BROOKS, MARISOL, SAM, NADIA, MS. LIN, MS. HARGROVE, WHITLOCK, TOMMY, EDDIE, MRS. ESPOSITO, DIEGO
> - 사건: 거래 마감 날. 권원 보험 회사의 마감 담당자 린 씨가 서류를 한 장씩 넘긴다. 양도 증서, 권원 보험, 에스크로에 맡긴 계약금, 랜드 트러스트의 99년 토지 임대 계약, 브루클린 네이버후드 펀드 담보 대출, 시의 융자 서류, 긴급 수리 청구서 정산, 보일러 공제, 일할 계산한 4월 임대료. 마리솔과 샘이 수십 장에 서명하고, 나디아가 땅을 받는다. 정오가 지나 송금이 확인되자 휘틀록이 열쇠 꾸러미를 건넨다. 오후에 레드훅으로 돌아온 마리솔은 지하실 열쇠를 에디에게 준다.
> - 학습 포인트: 계약 어휘(deed, title, title insurance, escrow, closing, wire transfer, payoff, proration, lien, recorded), 확인 질문(Just to confirm… / So that means… / Can you walk us through…? / Is that correct?), 격식 확인 질문의 상승 억양
> - 미국 현안: 부동산 거래 마감 절차, 권원 보험과 에스크로, 주민 소유 전환의 최종 단계, 공공·비영리 자금의 결합
> - 연결: 73화 수락(335만, 4/30까지 마감). 74화 3/15 매매 계약 서명. 69화 계약금 3만·보일러 공제 4.5만·HPD 긴급 수리 청구서 2.2만. 59화 재원 구조. 61·73화 아르투로 3/18 시민권 선서식. 72화 코왈스키·커먼 그라운드 5/1 관리 시작. 54화 토미 계약 5/31 만료.

## 대본

[Scene: Thursday, April 29, 9:30 a.m. A conference room in Lower Manhattan. A long table with stacks of paper, each with colored "sign here" tabs. Danielle Brooks, Marisol, Sam, and Nadia sit together, early.]

MS. BROOKS: Okay. Today, you'll sign about a hundred and twenty pages. Maybe more. Don't panic. Most of them say the same thing in different words.
SAM: A hundred and twenty pages. I brought three pens. In case two die.
MARISOL: I brought one pen. Papi's pen. From his citizenship ceremony. He lent it to me.
NADIA: How was the ceremony? I'm sorry I missed it.
MARISOL: It was beautiful. March eighteenth. Two hundred people from sixty countries. Diego held the flag. Papi cried when they said, "Welcome, fellow Americans."
SAM: And Héctor? Was he on time?
MARISOL: He was forty minutes early. For the first time in his life. He took the R train at five a.m. Like he promised.
MS. BROOKS: That's a good sign for today. Okay. One rule. If you don't understand something, ask. Out loud. "Just to confirm." Closers love that phrase.
SAM: "Just to confirm." Got it.
MS. BROOKS: And don't sign anything until I nod. I'll nod. Like this.
NADIA: That's a very small nod.
MS. BROOKS: Lawyers have small nods. It's our training.

[Scene: 10:05 a.m. The same room. Ms. Lin, the closer from the title company, sits at the head of the table. Ms. Hargrove sits quietly across from Marisol and Sam, initialing pages. Whitlock has not arrived yet.]

MS. LIN: Good morning, everyone. I'm Grace Lin from the title company. I'll be running the closing today. We'll go document by document. Please stop me anytime.
MS. LIN: Ms. Hargrove tells me Mr. Whitlock will join us at noon. For the keys. He signed his documents in advance.
MS. LIN: Let's begin. Document one: the deed. This transfers ownership of the building from Harborline Red Hook LLC to the Corner House Housing Development Fund Corporation.
MARISOL: Just to confirm. That's us? The co-op?
MS. LIN: That's you. The co-op's legal name. And a separate deed transfers the land to the Harbor Commons Land Trust. Ms. Okafor signs that one.
NADIA: I'm signing for the land. One point four million. With my board's blessing. And their nervous texts.
MS. LIN: Document two: title insurance. We've searched the property's history back to nineteen hundred and twelve. No surprises. Except one lien. The HPD emergency repair bill.
SAM: Twenty-two thousand dollars. For the boiler. In January.
MS. LIN: Correct. It will be paid off from the seller's proceeds. Today. Before the deed is recorded. So the co-op receives the building with no liens.
SAM: Just to confirm. Harborline pays the twenty-two thousand? Not us?
MS. LIN: Harborline pays it. It's in the contract. Section nine. Ms. Hargrove confirmed it this morning.
MS. BROOKS: That's correct. Section nine.
MS. LIN: Document three: the closing statement. The money. Can I walk you through it?
MARISOL: Please. Slowly. Sam will want to check every number.
SAM: I've already checked every number. Twice. Last night. But please. Go ahead.
MS. LIN: The purchase price for the building: one point nine five million. Minus the boiler credit: forty-five thousand. Minus the deposit already held in escrow: thirty thousand.
SAM: So that means the co-op pays one million eight hundred seventy-five thousand today. At closing.
MS. LIN: Correct. Plus closing costs. Title insurance, recording fees, transfer taxes. About thirty-eight thousand. Those were in your budget.
SAM: Line forty. Thirty-nine thousand estimated. We're a thousand under. That's green.
MS. LIN: Then there's the proration. Rent for April. The tenants paid Harborline for the whole month. But the co-op owns the building from today. So Harborline credits the co-op for one day. April thirtieth.
MARISOL: One day of rent. For five households and two stores. How much is that?
MS. LIN: Six hundred and twelve dollars and forty cents.
SAM: Can I keep the forty cents? As a souvenir?
MS. LIN: It goes into the co-op's account. But I'll print you a copy of the statement. You can frame that.
MS. LIN: Document four: the mortgage. From the Brooklyn Neighborhood Fund. One point one million. Thirty years. Six point five percent. Fixed.
SAM: Six point five. Fixed. I said that in my sleep for two months.
MS. LIN: Document five: the city's loan. Eight hundred thousand. One percent. Payments deferred for five years. And the regulatory agreement. It says the building stays affordable. With income limits for future buyers.
MARISOL: Is that the one that says forever?
MS. BROOKS: That one says forty years. The land trust's ground lease says ninety-nine. Together, they say "a very long time." Close enough to forever.
MS. LIN: Document six: the ground lease. Between the co-op and the land trust. Ms. Okafor and Ms. Reyes both sign. Pages fourteen to thirty-one.
NADIA: Ready, Marisol?
MARISOL: Ready. With Papi's pen.
MS. BROOKS: Nod.
MARISOL: She nodded. Okay. Signing. "Marisol Reyes. President." Page fourteen. Fifteen. Sixteen...
SAM: You're signing so fast!
MARISOL: My hand is not shaking. Look. For the first time. It's not shaking.

[Scene: 12:20 p.m. The same room. The paper stacks have moved from one side of the table to the other. Ms. Lin looks at her laptop. Whitlock enters, holding a small paper bag.]

MS. LIN: Okay. I'm checking the wires. The lender's funds... received. The city's funds... received. The co-op's funds... received. The land trust's funds... received.
SAM: All four? All four are in?
MS. LIN: All four are in. Harborline's loan payoff has been sent. The lien is paid. I'm sending the deed to be recorded with the city now.
MS. LIN: As of twelve twenty-three p.m., the Corner House Housing Development Fund Corporation owns the building at Van Brunt and Pioneer.
MARISOL: ...
NADIA: Marisol. Breathe.
MARISOL: I'm breathing. I'm breathing. It's real. It's recorded. It's real.
WHITLOCK: Congratulations. I'm sorry I'm late. I wanted to bring these in person.
MARISOL: Mr. Whitlock.
WHITLOCK: The keys. All of them. The front door. The basement. The roof. The boiler room. The storage room behind the bar. And this one.
NADIA: What's that one? It's old.
WHITLOCK: I don't know. It was in Sal Cappello's desk. In the basement office. It has a tag. It says, "4C — Sal's."
MARISOL: 4C. The empty apartment.
WHITLOCK: Nobody at Harborline ever found the lock it opens. Maybe you will.
SAM: Why would Sal have a special key to 4C?
WHITLOCK: I have no idea. That's your mystery now. Not mine.
MARISOL: Mr. Whitlock. Can I ask you something? Why the paper bag?
WHITLOCK: Oh. This. It's coffee beans. From a roaster in Red Hook. For Mr. Russo. Tell him to throw away whatever he's using now.
SAM: That's the nicest insult Tommy will ever receive.
WHITLOCK: And Ms. Reyes? The Community Board voted on Pier Seven last week. "No, unless conditions are met." Twelve conditions. Two of them are Mrs. Esposito's.
MARISOL: She'll be very happy. She'll laminate them.
WHITLOCK: I believe it. Goodbye, Ms. Reyes. Good luck with your building.
MARISOL: Goodbye, Mr. Whitlock. And thank you. For reading the letter. And for the drawing.
WHITLOCK: The drawing belongs where it is. Goodbye.
MS. LIN: In thirty years of closings, I've never seen that. A seller bringing coffee.
NADIA: In thirty years, you've never seen a buyer like this one.

[Scene: 3:30 p.m. Van Brunt Street. The corner building in spring sun. Tommy, Eddie, Mrs. Esposito, and Diego wait on the stoop. Marisol walks up from the bus stop holding up the ring of keys.]

DIEGO: Mom! Mom! Is it done? Is it ours?
MARISOL: It's done. It's recorded. As of twelve twenty-three p.m. It's ours.
TOMMY: Twelve twenty-three. I'm going to tattoo that on my arm.
MRS. ESPOSITO: You will not, Tommy. Write it on the chalkboard instead.
EDDIE: Marisol. Are those the keys?
MARISOL: These are the keys. All of them. Whitlock brought them himself. And coffee beans for Tommy.
TOMMY: Coffee beans? For me? Was that an insult?
MARISOL: It was the nicest insult you'll ever receive. Sam said so.
TOMMY: I'll accept it. I'll grind them tomorrow. I'll still make them terrible.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And the Community Board? Did Whitlock say anything?
MARISOL: They voted "no, unless conditions are met." Twelve conditions. Two of them are yours, Dolores.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Two of mine? Which two?
MARISOL: Enforceable flood-wall commitments. And penalties for missed repairs.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I'm going to laminate them. Both.
MARISOL: Eddie. This one is for you.
EDDIE: Which one?
MARISOL: The basement. The boiler room. You've been asking for fifty-two years to see the boiler without asking permission.
EDDIE: The basement key. Sal never let anybody have it. Not even me.
MARISOL: Now you don't need permission. It's your building. Go see it.
EDDIE: Dolores. Come with me. Diego, you too. Hold my arm.
DIEGO: Is it scary down there?
EDDIE: It's the same basement. But today, it's our basement. That's different.

[Scene: 3:45 p.m. The basement. A bare bulb. The old 1992 boiler, patched with the city's used part. A new boiler waits in a crate against the wall, with a delivery tag dated May 3. Eddie unlocks the door himself.]

EDDIE: Look at that. The old one. Still running. Barely. Like me.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And the new one. In the box. It comes next Monday. Brian says it will be installed in one day.
DIEGO: The new one is huge! It's like a robot!
EDDIE: Put your hand on the old one, Diego. Feel it. It's warm.
DIEGO: It's warm. And it's shaking a little.
EDDIE: It's tired. It worked for thirty-five years. It kept Peggy warm. And me. And your mother, when she was pregnant with you.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And it froze you in January.
EDDIE: Nobody's perfect, Dolores. Not even boilers. Thank you, old boiler. You can rest next week.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Will you miss it? Honestly?
EDDIE: A little. It made a noise every morning at six. Clank, clank. Like an old man getting up.
DIEGO: Like you!
EDDIE: Like me. Exactly. The new one will be quiet. I'll need a new alarm clock.
MRS. ESPOSITO: You have one. Eight o'clock. Two knocks.
EDDIE: That's not an alarm clock, Dolores. That's a friendship.
DIEGO: Eddie, what's that? On the wall? Behind the pipe?
EDDIE: Where? Oh. A little door. A metal box. In the wall. I've never seen that.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Neither have I. In thirty-one years. Is it locked?
DIEGO: It's locked. There's a keyhole. A small one.
EDDIE: Marisol said there was an old key. "4C — Sal's." Maybe it's not for 4C at all.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Eddie, don't. We should wait for Marisol. And a lawyer. And possibly a priest.
EDDIE: Dolores, it's our building now. And it's our basement. Diego. Run upstairs. Ask your mother for the old key. The one with the tag.
DIEGO: I'm the runner! I'm fast! I'll be back in one minute!
MRS. ESPOSITO: Eddie O'Neill. Fifty-two years, and you're still looking for trouble.
EDDIE: Not trouble, Dolores. A story. There's always one more story in an old building.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **확인 질문**
  - *Just to confirm. That's us?* — "확인차 여쭤보면, 그게 저희인가요?"
  - *So that means the co-op pays…* — "그러니까 조합이 ~을 내는 거네요."
  - *Can I walk you through it?* — "차근차근 설명해 드려도 될까요?"
  - *Correct. / That's correct.* — "맞습니다."
- **계약 어휘**
  - *deed* 양도 증서 / *title* 소유권, 권원 / *title insurance* 권원 보험 / *recorded* (시에) 등기된
  - *escrow* 제3자 예치 / *closing statement* 정산서 / *closing costs* 마감 비용 / *transfer taxes* 양도세
  - *proration* 일할 계산 / *payoff* 대출 상환 / *lien* 유치권 / *wire* 송금
  - *regulatory agreement* (시와 맺는) 규제 약정 / *ground lease* 토지 임대 계약
- **As of twelve twenty-three p.m., the co-op owns the building.** — "오후 12시 23분부로 조합이 건물을 소유합니다."
- **Close enough to forever.** — "영원이나 다름없죠."
- **That's the nicest insult you'll ever receive.** — "평생 받을 욕 중에 제일 친절한 욕이네요."
- **possibly a priest** — "어쩌면 신부님까지." 부인의 과장된 걱정.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 확인 질문은 끝을 올린다: *Just to confirm. | That's us?↗* / *Harborline pays the twenty-two thousand?↗*
- 린 씨는 마감 전문가답게 차분하고 일정하게. 서류 번호(*Document one… Document two…*)에서 매번 짧게 쉰다.
- 숫자: *one million eight hundred seventy-five thousand* / *six hundred and twelve dollars and forty cents*.
- 송금 확인 장면은 한 줄씩 느리게: *received. | received. | received.* — 마지막에 *All four are in.*
- 마리솔의 *It's real. It's recorded. It's real.* — 두 번째 *real*은 울음 직전.
- 지하실 장면은 다시 작고 신비롭게. 에디의 *There's always one more story in an old building.*
