# THE CORNER HOUSE — 5부 86화

## The Handover (넘겨주기)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 5부 14화(통산 86화) · 시기: 2027년 5월 30일(일) 아침 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 개점 전 더 훅 → 같은 테이블(새 임대 계약 서명) → 바 뒤, 칠판 앞
> - 등장인물: TOMMY, GINA, LUCA, MARISOL, KOWALSKI, EDDIE, TASHA
> - 사건: 하버라인과 맺었던 토미의 계약은 내일 끝난다. 조합과 맺는 새 계약은 6월 1일부터 10년, 월 7천 달러다. 서명하는 날 아침, 토미가 지나에게 계획을 털어놓는다. 새 계약은 '더 훅 LLC' 이름으로 하고, 지분 51퍼센트를 지나에게 넘기겠다고. 자기는 일주일에 나흘만 맥주를 따르고, 화요일과 금요일은 루카의 할아버지로 살겠다고. 지나는 오픈 블록을 주 3일로 줄이고 바의 장부와 운영을 맡는다. 서명에는 마리솔과 코왈스키가 조합 쪽으로, 에디가 증인으로 참석한다. 서명이 끝나자 토미는 열쇠 꾸러미와 앤젤라의 레몬 앞치마, 칠판 분필을 지나에게 건넨다.
> - 학습 포인트: 승계 어휘(ownership, stake, majority, partner, take over, step back, hand over, lease, guarantor), 감정 서술과 짧은 문장, 울먹임과 쉼(I... I don't know what to say. / Give me a minute. / This is hard to say.)
> - 미국 현안: 자영업 승계와 세대교체, 소상공인의 장기 임대 안정, 일과 돌봄의 균형(조부모의 손주 돌봄), 여성의 가업 승계
> - 연결: 1화 개점 시간 오전 11시·칠판 메뉴. 17화 개업일 2006년 5월 28일. 47화 지나의 '폐업 후 내 집으로' 계획과 앤젤라의 유언 '계속 열어 둬, 내 바이기도 해'. 52화 지나 퇴사. 54화 '갈고리는 놓지 않는다'. 59화 바 월 7천. 60화 레몬 앞치마. 69화 계약 종료 뒤 월 단위 잔류 합의.

## 대본

[Scene: Sunday, May 30, 7:30 a.m. The Hook, closed. Morning light through the window. Chairs on the tables. A folder sits on the bar. Tommy makes coffee. Gina sits at the bar with Luca, now five, who eats a pancake with his hands.]

LUCA: Nonno, why is it so early? The bar is closed.
TOMMY: Because today is a special day, buddy. Nonno is signing a paper. A big paper.
LUCA: Like a drawing?
TOMMY: Like a promise. A ten-year promise. That this bar stays open. Until you're fifteen.
LUCA: Fifteen! That's so old!
GINA: Dad, Marisol and Brian come at nine. With the lease. Why did you want me here at seven thirty?
TOMMY: Because I have something to say first. Before the lease. Before anybody else.
GINA: Okay. You're scaring me a little. Is it your heart? Your blood pressure?
TOMMY: My heart is fine. Dr. Levine says I'm "a healthy man with an unhealthy coffee." It's not that.
GINA: Then what?
TOMMY: Gina. This is hard to say. So I'm going to say it fast. Like ripping off a bandage.
GINA: Okay.
TOMMY: I don't want to sign the new lease alone. I want to sign it with you. As partners. "The Hook LLC." Fifty-one percent you. Forty-nine percent me.
GINA: ...What?
TOMMY: I want to give you the bar, Gina. Not all at once. But the bigger part. The deciding part.
GINA: Dad. I... I don't know what to say.
TOMMY: Don't say anything yet. Let me finish. I'm fifty-nine in July. I've worked seventy hours a week for twenty-one years. Since your mother and I opened the door. May twenty-eighth, two thousand six.
GINA: I know. I was fourteen. I painted the sign.
TOMMY: You painted the sign. Badly. The "K" was crooked.
GINA: It's still crooked.
TOMMY: It's still crooked. I never fixed it. I like it crooked.
LUCA: Mommy painted the sign?
GINA: I did, baby. A long time ago. Before you. Before your dad. Before everything.
TOMMY: Gina, I want to step back. Not out. Back. I'll still pour beer. Four days a week. But Tuesdays and Fridays, I want to be Nonno. Only Nonno. Pick up Luca. Take him to the pier. Teach him the Mets. Badly.
LUCA: The Mets! Nonno, the Mets lost yesterday.
TOMMY: The Mets always lose, buddy. That's the first lesson.
LUCA: What's the second lesson?
TOMMY: The second lesson is you love them anyway.
GINA: That's not about the Mets, Dad. Is it?
TOMMY: It's about everything, sweetheart. Bars. Buildings. Daughters who paint crooked signs.
LUCA: And grandsons!

[Scene: 8:15 a.m. The same bar. Gina walks back and forth. Tommy sits. Luca draws on a napkin.]

GINA: Dad. I have a job. At Open Block. I love that job. I just started in December.
TOMMY: I know. I'm not asking you to quit. I'm asking you to cut it to three days. And give the bar two. Friday and Saturday. Plus the books. You already do the books.
GINA: I do the books on Fridays. For free.
TOMMY: Not for free anymore. Partners get paid. From the profit. With a lease at seven thousand, there's profit. Sam showed me. In green.
GINA: Dad... why me? Why not Tasha? She's been here sixteen years. She knows the bar better than anyone.
TOMMY: Tasha is going to be a nurse. In two years. Maybe three. She told me on New Year's. She'll still work weekends. But her future is in a hospital. Not behind a bar.
GINA: And mine is behind a bar?
TOMMY: I don't know. That's the question. That's why I'm asking. Not telling.
GINA: Remember October? In your kitchen? I said, "Close the bar in May. Move in with me." I wanted you to quit.
TOMMY: I remember. You called it "a prison with free tacos."
GINA: I was wrong. It's not a prison. It's... it's Mom. It's you. It's Eddie's stool. And Walter's map. And Sal's cash register. It's everybody.
TOMMY: Then help me keep it. Not for me. For them. And for Luca.
GINA: Give me a minute. Please. Just a minute.
TOMMY: Take two. Take ten. Marisol isn't here yet.
LUCA: Mommy, are you sad?
GINA: No, baby. I'm thinking. Grown-ups make that face when they think.
LUCA: Nonno makes that face when the Mets lose.
GINA: That's a different face. That's the hopeless face.
GINA: Okay. Dad. Here's my answer. Yes. But with conditions.
TOMMY: Conditions? Like Harborline?
GINA: Like a co-op. Condition one: we keep Mom's rules. No hats at the bar. No sad songs before nine. A lemon in every bag.
TOMMY: Done.
GINA: Condition two: you stop making the coffee. Forever. Tasha makes it. Or I do.
TOMMY: That's... that's very harsh. But fine. Done.
GINA: Condition three: Tuesdays and Fridays, you really take them off. No checking the cooler. No just one hour. If I see you behind the bar on a Tuesday, I'm calling Dolores.
TOMMY: You'd call Dolores? That's a war crime.
GINA: Then don't come in. Deal?
TOMMY: Deal. Fifty-one percent. And you're already giving orders. Your mother would be so proud.

[Scene: 9:05 a.m. The same table. Marisol and Kowalski sit across from Tommy and Gina. Eddie sits at the end as a witness, in his green sweater. The lease lies open.]

MARISOL: Good morning. Okay. This is the commercial lease. Between the Corner House Co-op, as landlord, and the tenant. Ten years. Starting June first. Seven thousand a month. Three percent a year.
KOWALSKI: And the tenant name, Tommy? On the first page? You said you had a change.
TOMMY: Yes. Not "Thomas Russo." "The Hook LLC." Gina and me. Partners.
MARISOL: Partners? Gina, really?
GINA: Really. Fifty-one, forty-nine. He told me an hour ago. I'm still in shock. But I said yes.
EDDIE: Fifty-one percent. Gina, you're the boss now. Of the last real bar in Brooklyn.
GINA: Co-boss, Eddie. With conditions.
KOWALSKI: We'll need the LLC papers. Do you have them?
GINA: I filed them online. At eight forty. On my phone. While Dad was crying into the pancakes.
TOMMY: I wasn't crying. There was syrup in my eye.
MARISOL: Then let's update the lease. Brian, can you change the first page?
KOWALSKI: Already changed. I brought two versions. Just in case. I learned that from Sam.
EDDIE: Everybody learns from Sam. It's a disease.
MARISOL: Okay. Page one. Tenant: The Hook LLC. Page four. Rent. Page nine. The co-op's promises. Repairs within forty-eight hours. Heat. Water. No surprises.
TOMMY: Repairs within forty-eight hours. For twenty years, Sal took forty-eight days. Harborline took forty-eight weeks.
KOWALSKI: And now you have Common Ground. And me. I'll take forty-eight hours. Or less. With working brakes.
MARISOL: Page twelve. Signatures. Landlord first. I sign as president of the co-op. "Marisol Reyes." Done.
KOWALSKI: And I sign as managing agent. Done.
MARISOL: Now the tenant. Both partners.
GINA: Dad. You first. It's your bar.
TOMMY: No. You first. Fifty-one percent goes first. That's the rule now.
GINA: Okay. "Gina Russo. Managing partner." Done.
TOMMY: "Thomas Russo. Partner." And Angela Russo. In spirit. I'm writing it. Small. In the margin.
MARISOL: Tommy, you can't write a deceased person on a lease.
TOMMY: I know. It's in pencil. Brian can erase it later. If he wants.
KOWALSKI: I'm not erasing that, Tommy. Never.
EDDIE: Witness. Edward O'Neill. Eighty-four. Third floor. I've been coming to this corner since nineteen seventy-four. I saw the hardware store. Rizzo's. Then the empty years. Then this bar. And now this. I witness it.
MARISOL: It's signed. Ten years. The Hook stays on the corner. Until twenty thirty-seven.
EDDIE: Twenty thirty-seven. I'll be ninety-four. I'll come back to witness the renewal.
TOMMY: You'd better, Eddie. Your stool isn't going anywhere.

[Scene: 10:30 a.m. Behind the bar. Marisol and Kowalski have left. Tasha has arrived for the Sunday shift. Tommy stands in front of the chalkboard with Gina. Eddie and Luca watch from the stools.]

TASHA: Okay. Is it true? Marisol told me on the stairs. Gina is the boss now?
GINA: Managing partner. Fifty-one percent. Tasha, I'm sorry. I should have told you first. It happened in two hours.
TASHA: Sorry? Gina, I'm happy! Now I have a boss who knows how to read a spreadsheet. And who doesn't make coffee.
TOMMY: That's condition number two. I'm banned. From my own coffee machine.
TASHA: Best news I've had all year. After my chemistry grade. B plus.
GINA: B plus! Tasha!
TOMMY: Okay. Before we open. One more thing. Gina, come here.
GINA: What?
TOMMY: These. The keys. All of them. The front door. The cooler. The register. The basement. And Angela's key.
GINA: Mom's key? Which one is Mom's?
TOMMY: The green one. With the nail polish on it. She painted it in two thousand six. So she could find it in the dark. It still works. The back door.
GINA: The green one. I remember her painting it. On the bar. With her pink nail polish.
TOMMY: And this. Her apron. With the lemons. You already have one. From Christmas. But this is the one she wore on the last day. Before the hospital. Before she couldn't come down anymore.
GINA: Dad...
TOMMY: And this. A piece of chalk. For the board. Every day for twenty-one years, I wrote the menu. And one line at the bottom. A joke. Or a warning. "Today's coffee: survivable."
EDDIE: My favorite was "Today's coffee: ask a lawyer."
TOMMY: Tomorrow is Memorial Day. We open at eleven. Three p.m., the moment of silence. Like every year. Today is the last day I write the board.
GINA: Then write it, Dad. The last one. Write something good.
TOMMY: Okay. Okay. "Sunday. Lasagna. Empanadas. Tamales from Doña Elena. Fries free for presidents under eighteen."
LUCA: What's at the bottom, Nonno? Write the bottom!
TOMMY: The bottom. Okay. "Today's coffee: made by Tasha. Finally."
TASHA: Ha! It's official! It's on the board!
TOMMY: And one more line. Under that. Small.
GINA: What does it say?
TOMMY: "Thanks for twenty-one years. — T. Starting tomorrow: Ask for Gina."
GINA: Dad. Come here. Give me a hug. Before I start crying on the menu.
TOMMY: You're already crying on the menu. The chalk is running.
GINA: Then rewrite it. Tomorrow. That's my job now.
EDDIE: Tommy. Pour me a coffee. Not yours. Tasha's. It's Sunday. A day of rest. You're resting already.
TOMMY: I'm not resting, Eddie. I'm handing over. That's harder.
EDDIE: Harder than resting. Easier than leaving. You picked the middle. Good man.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **승계 어휘**
  - *partners / managing partner* 동업자 / 대표 동업자
  - *stake / fifty-one percent* 지분 / 51퍼센트(과반 = 결정권)
  - *step back, not out* — "물러서는 거지, 나가는 게 아니야."
  - *hand over / take over* 넘겨주다 / 넘겨받다
  - *LLC (limited liability company)* 유한 책임 회사 / *managing agent* 관리 대리인 / *witness* 증인
- **감정의 짧은 문장**
  - *This is hard to say. So I'm going to say it fast.* — "말하기 어려워. 그래서 빨리 말할게."
  - *I... I don't know what to say.* — 말이 막힌다.
  - *Give me a minute.* — "잠깐만 시간을 줘."
  - *Take two. Take ten.* — "2분이든 10분이든."
- **조건부 수락** — *Yes. But with conditions.* / *Condition one: … / Done.*
- **a healthy man with an unhealthy coffee** — 레빈 박사의 진단. "건강한 남자, 건강하지 못한 커피."
- **a war crime** — "전쟁 범죄." 부인에게 이르겠다는 협박에 대한 과장된 농담.
- **Harder than resting. Easier than leaving. You picked the middle.** — 에디의 말. "쉬는 것보다 어렵고, 떠나는 것보다 쉽지. 넌 중간을 골랐어."

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 토미의 고백은 정말로 빠르게. *Fifty-one percent you. | Forty-nine percent me.*에서만 끊는다.
- 지나의 *...What?*와 *I... I don't know what to say.*는 숨이 막힌 듯.
- 조건 세 개는 회의처럼 또박또박: *Condition one: … — Done.*의 핑퐁 리듬.
- 서명 장면의 에디 증언은 연설처럼 천천히. *I witness it.*에 무게를 둔다.
- 마지막 칠판 장면: 토미가 글자를 쓰며 읽는 속도로. *"Starting tomorrow: | Ask for Gina."*
- 에디의 마지막 세 문장은 리듬을 맞춘다: *Harder than resting. | Easier than leaving. | You picked the middle.*

## 한글 해석

루카: 논노, 왜 이렇게 일찍이에요? 바 문 닫았잖아요.
토미: 오늘은 특별한 날이거든, 꼬마야. 논노가 종이에 서명하는 날이야. 큰 종이에.
루카: 그림처럼요?
토미: 약속처럼. 10년짜리 약속. 이 바가 계속 문을 연다는 약속. 네가 열다섯 살이 될 때까지.
루카: 열다섯! 엄청 늙었다!
지나: 아빠, 마리솔이랑 브라이언은 9시에 와요. 계약서 들고요. 왜 저를 7시 반에 오라고 하셨어요?
토미: 먼저 할 말이 있어서. 계약 전에. 다른 누구보다 먼저.
지나: 좋아요. 좀 무섭네요. 심장 때문이에요? 혈압이요?
토미: 심장은 괜찮아. 레빈 박사 말로는 내가 "건강한 남자인데 커피가 건강하지 못하다"래. 그런 거 아니야.
지나: 그럼 뭔데요?
토미: 지나. 이건 말하기 어려워. 그래서 빨리 말할게. 반창고 떼듯이.
지나: 네.
토미: 새 계약에 혼자 서명하고 싶지 않아. 너랑 같이 서명하고 싶어. 동업자로. '더 훅 LLC'. 51퍼센트는 너. 49퍼센트는 나.
지나: …네?
토미: 너한테 바를 주고 싶어, 지나. 한 번에 다는 아니고. 그래도 더 큰 부분을. 결정하는 부분을.
지나: 아빠. 저… 뭐라고 해야 할지 모르겠어요.
토미: 아직 아무 말도 하지 마. 끝까지 말하게 해 줘. 7월이면 쉰아홉이야. 21년 동안 주 70시간을 일했어. 네 엄마랑 내가 문을 연 날부터. 2006년 5월 28일.
지나: 알아요. 전 열네 살이었어요. 간판을 칠했죠.
토미: 네가 간판을 칠했지. 엉망으로. 'K'가 삐뚤어졌어.
지나: 아직도 삐뚤어져 있어요.
토미: 아직도 삐뚤어져 있지. 한 번도 안 고쳤어. 삐뚤어진 게 좋아.
루카: 엄마가 간판을 칠했어요?
지나: 그랬지, 얘야. 오래전에. 너 태어나기 전에. 아빠 만나기 전에. 모든 것보다 전에.
토미: 지나, 난 한 발 물러서고 싶어. 나가는 게 아니라. 물러서는 거야. 맥주는 계속 따를 거야. 일주일에 나흘. 그래도 화요일이랑 금요일엔 논노가 되고 싶어. 논노만. 루카 데리러 가고. 부두에 데려가고. 메츠를 가르치고. 엉망으로.
루카: 메츠! 논노, 메츠 어제 졌어요.
토미: 메츠는 늘 져, 꼬마야. 그게 첫 번째 교훈이야.
루카: 두 번째 교훈은요?
토미: 두 번째 교훈은 그래도 사랑한다는 거야.
지나: 그거 메츠 얘기 아니죠, 아빠?
토미: 모든 것에 대한 얘기야, 얘야. 바. 건물. 간판을 삐뚤게 칠한 딸.
루카: 그리고 손자도요!
지나: 아빠. 저 직장 있어요. 오픈 블록이요. 그 일 좋아해요. 12월에 막 시작했잖아요.
토미: 알아. 그만두라는 게 아니야. 사흘로 줄이라는 거야. 그리고 바에 이틀을 주고. 금요일이랑 토요일. 장부까지. 장부는 이미 네가 하고 있잖아.
지나: 장부는 금요일마다 해요. 공짜로요.
토미: 이제 공짜 아니야. 동업자는 돈을 받아. 이익에서. 월 7천짜리 계약이면 이익이 나. 샘이 보여 줬어. 초록색으로.
지나: 아빠… 왜 저예요? 타샤는요? 여기서 16년이나 일했잖아요. 누구보다 바를 잘 알아요.
토미: 타샤는 간호사가 될 거야. 2년 뒤에. 3년일 수도 있고. 새해에 나한테 말했어. 주말엔 계속 일하겠지. 그래도 걔 미래는 병원에 있어. 바 뒤가 아니라.
지나: 그럼 제 미래는 바 뒤에 있어요?
토미: 모르겠어. 그게 질문이야. 그래서 묻는 거야. 시키는 게 아니라.
지나: 10월 기억나요? 아빠 부엌에서요? 제가 "5월에 바 닫고 우리 집으로 들어와요"라고 했잖아요. 아빠가 그만두길 원했어요.
토미: 기억나. '공짜 타코가 있는 감옥'이라고 했지.
지나: 제가 틀렸어요. 감옥이 아니에요. 이건… 엄마예요. 아빠고요. 에디의 스툴이고. 월터의 지도고. 살의 금전 등록기고요. 모두예요.
