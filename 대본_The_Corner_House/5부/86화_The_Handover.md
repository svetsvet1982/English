# THE CORNER HOUSE — 5부 86화

## The Handover (넘겨주기)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 5부 14화(통산 86화) · 시기: 2027년 5월 30일(일) 아침 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 개점 전 더 훅 → 같은 테이블(새 임대 계약 서명) → 바 뒤, 칠판 앞
> - 등장인물: TOMMY, GINA, LUCA, MARISOL, KOWALSKI, EDDIE, TASHA
> - 사건: 하버라인과 맺었던 토미의 계약은 내일 끝난다. 조합과 맺는 새 계약은 6월 1일부터 10년, 월 7천 달러다. 서명하는 날 아침, 토미가 지나에게 계획을 털어놓는다. 새 계약은 '더 훅 LLC' 이름으로 하고, 지분 51퍼센트를 지나에게 넘기겠다고. 자기는 일주일에 나흘만 맥주를 따르고, 화요일과 금요일은 루카의 할아버지로 살겠다고. 지나는 오픈 블록을 주 3일로 줄이고 바의 장부와 운영을 맡는다. 서명에는 마리솔과 코왈스키가 조합 쪽으로, 에디가 증인으로 참석한다. 서명이 끝나자 토미는 열쇠 꾸러미와 앤젤라의 레몬 앞치마, 칠판 분필을 지나에게 건넨다.
> - 학습 포인트: 승계 어휘(ownership, stake, majority, partner, take over, step back, hand over, lease, guarantor), 감정 서술과 짧은 문장, 울먹임과 쉼(I... I don't know what to say. / Give me a minute. / This is hard to say.)
> - 미국 현안: 자영업 승계와 세대교체, 소상공인의 장기 임대 안정, 일과 돌봄의 균형(조부모의 손주 돌봄), 여성의 가업 승계
> - 연결: 1화 개점 시간 오전 11시·칠판 메뉴. 17화 개업일 2006년 5월 28일. 47화 지나의 '폐업 후 내 집으로' 계획과 앤젤라의 유언 '계속 열어 둬, 내 바이기도 해'. 52화 지나 퇴사. 54화 '갈고리는 놓지 않는다'. 59화 바 월 7천. 60화 레몬 앞치마. 69화 계약 종료 뒤 월 단위 잔류 합의.

## 대본

[Scene: Sunday, May 30, 7:30 a.m. The Hook, closed. Morning light through the window. Chairs on the tables. A folder sits on the bar. Tommy makes coffee. Gina sits at the bar with Luca, now five, who eats a pancake with his hands.]

LUCA: Nonno, why are we here so early? The bar is closed, and nobody's here except us.
TOMMY: Because today is a special day, buddy. Nonno is signing a paper, and it's a really big paper.
LUCA: Is it like a drawing? I can help with drawings, because I'm really good at them.
TOMMY: It's more like a promise. It's a ten-year promise that this bar stays open until you're fifteen.
LUCA: Fifteen! That's so old! That's older than Diego!
GINA: Dad, Marisol and Brian are coming at nine with the lease. So why did you want me here at seven thirty?
TOMMY: Because I have something to say first, before the lease and before anybody else hears it.
GINA: Okay, now you're scaring me a little. Is it your heart? Is it your blood pressure?
TOMMY: My heart is fine. Dr. Levine says I'm "a healthy man with an unhealthy coffee," so it's not that.
GINA: Then what is it? Just tell me, Dad, because you're making me nervous.
TOMMY: Gina, this is hard to say. So I'm going to say it fast. I'll do it like ripping off a bandage.
GINA: Okay. I'm listening, so go ahead.
TOMMY: I don't want to sign the new lease alone. I want to sign it with you, as partners, under the name "The Hook LLC." Fifty-one percent you. Forty-nine percent me.
GINA: ...What?
TOMMY: I want to give you the bar, Gina. Not all at once, but the bigger part, which is the part that makes the decisions.
GINA: Dad. I... I don't know what to say.
TOMMY: Don't say anything yet, and let me finish. I'll be fifty-nine in July, and I've worked seventy hours a week for twenty-one years. That's since your mother and I opened the door on May twenty-eighth, two thousand six.
GINA: I know, because I was there. I was fourteen, and I painted the sign.
TOMMY: You painted the sign, and you painted it badly. The "K" was crooked.
GINA: It's still crooked, you know. I see it every time I walk in.
TOMMY: It's still crooked, because I never fixed it. I like it crooked.
LUCA: Mommy painted the sign? The big one outside, over the door?
GINA: I did, baby, a long time ago. That was before you, before your dad, before everything.
TOMMY: Gina, I want to step back, not out. I'll still pour beer four days a week. But on Tuesdays and Fridays, I want to be Nonno, only Nonno. I'll pick up Luca, take him to the pier, and teach him the Mets, badly.
LUCA: The Mets! Nonno, the Mets lost yesterday, and you yelled at the TV.
TOMMY: The Mets always lose, buddy. That's the first lesson.
LUCA: What's the second lesson? Is it about hot dogs?
TOMMY: The second lesson is that you love them anyway, even when they break your heart.
GINA: That's not really about the Mets, Dad, is it?
TOMMY: It's about everything, sweetheart. It's about bars, and buildings, and daughters who paint crooked signs.
LUCA: And grandsons! Don't forget the grandsons!

[Scene: 8:15 a.m. The same bar. Gina walks back and forth. Tommy sits. Luca draws on a napkin.]

GINA: Dad, I have a job at Open Block, and I love that job. I only started in December.
TOMMY: I know, and I'm not asking you to quit. I'm asking you to cut it to three days and give the bar two, Friday and Saturday. Plus the books, but you already do the books.
GINA: I do the books on Fridays, and I do them for free.
TOMMY: Not for free anymore, because partners get paid from the profit. With a lease at seven thousand, there's profit. Sam showed me, in green.
GINA: Dad, why me? Why not Tasha? She's been here sixteen years, and she knows the bar better than anyone.
TOMMY: Tasha is going to be a nurse in two years, maybe three, because she told me on New Year's. She'll still work weekends, but her future is in a hospital, not behind a bar.
GINA: And you think mine is behind a bar?
TOMMY: I don't know, and that's the question. That's why I'm asking you, not telling you.
GINA: Remember October, in your kitchen? I said, "Close the bar in May and move in with me." I wanted you to quit.
TOMMY: I remember it very well. You called this place "a prison with free tacos," and I didn't sleep that night.
GINA: I was wrong, because it's not a prison. It's Mom, and it's you. It's Eddie's stool, and Walter's map, and Sal's cash register. It's everybody.
TOMMY: Then help me keep it. Don't do it for me, do it for them, and for Luca.
GINA: Give me a minute. Please, just a minute.
TOMMY: Take two. Take ten. Marisol isn't here yet, so we have time.
LUCA: Mommy, are you sad? Your face looks funny.
GINA: No, baby, I'm thinking. Grown-ups make that face when they think about something important.
LUCA: Nonno makes that face when the Mets lose, and then he eats a whole bag of chips.
GINA: That's a different face, sweetie. That's the hopeless face.
GINA: Okay, Dad, here's my answer. Yes. But with conditions.
TOMMY: Conditions? You mean like Harborline?
GINA: Like a co-op. Condition one: we keep Mom's rules. No hats at the bar, no sad songs before nine, and a lemon in every bag.
TOMMY: Done.
GINA: Condition two: you stop making the coffee, forever. From now on, Tasha makes it, or I do, and nobody else touches the machine.
TOMMY: That's... that's very harsh. But fine. Done.
GINA: Condition three: on Tuesdays and Fridays, you really take them off. No checking the cooler, and no "just one hour." If I see you behind the bar on a Tuesday, I'm calling Dolores.
TOMMY: You'd call Dolores? That's a war crime, and you know it.
GINA: Then don't come in. Do we have a deal?
TOMMY: Deal. Fifty-one percent, and you're already giving orders. Your mother would be so proud of you.

[Scene: 9:05 a.m. The same table. Marisol and Kowalski sit across from Tommy and Gina. Eddie sits at the end as a witness, in his green sweater. The lease lies open.]

MARISOL: Good morning, everybody. Okay, this is the commercial lease between the Corner House Co-op, as landlord, and the tenant. It's ten years, starting June first, at seven thousand a month, with three percent a year.
KOWALSKI: And what about the tenant name on the first page, Tommy? You said on the phone that you had a change.
TOMMY: Yes. It's not "Thomas Russo" anymore. It's "The Hook LLC," which means Gina and me, as partners.
MARISOL: Partners? Gina, really? Nobody told me anything about this!
GINA: Really, fifty-one, forty-nine. He told me an hour ago, and I'm still in shock. But I said yes.
EDDIE: Fifty-one percent. Gina, that means you're the boss now, of the last real bar in Brooklyn.
GINA: I'm the co-boss, Eddie. And it comes with conditions, which my dad already agreed to.
KOWALSKI: We'll need the LLC papers. Do you have them with you?
GINA: I filed them online at eight forty, on my phone, while Dad was crying into the pancakes.
TOMMY: I wasn't crying. There was syrup in my eye.
MARISOL: Then let's update the lease. Brian, can you change the first page?
KOWALSKI: It's already changed, because I brought two versions just in case. I learned that from Sam.
EDDIE: Everybody learns from Sam. It's like a disease in this building.
MARISOL: Okay. Page one is the tenant: The Hook LLC. Page four is the rent. Page nine is the co-op's promises: repairs within forty-eight hours, heat, water, and no surprises.
TOMMY: Repairs within forty-eight hours. For twenty years, Sal took forty-eight days, and Harborline took forty-eight weeks.
KOWALSKI: And now you have Common Ground, and you have me. I'll take forty-eight hours or less, and I'll show up in a van with working brakes.
MARISOL: Page twelve is the signatures, and the landlord signs first. I sign as president of the co-op. "Marisol Reyes." Done.
KOWALSKI: And I sign as managing agent for Common Ground. Done.
MARISOL: Now it's the tenant's turn, and we need both partners.
GINA: Dad, you go first. It's your bar.
TOMMY: No, you go first. Fifty-one percent goes first, because that's the rule now.
GINA: Okay. "Gina Russo, managing partner." Done.
TOMMY: "Thomas Russo, partner." And Angela Russo, in spirit. I'm writing her name small, in the margin.
MARISOL: Tommy, you can't put a deceased person on a lease. Legally, it doesn't count.
TOMMY: I know. That's why it's in pencil, so Brian can erase it later if he wants.
KOWALSKI: I'm not erasing that, Tommy. Never, not in a hundred years.
EDDIE: Witness: Edward O'Neill, eighty-four, third floor. I've been coming to this corner since nineteen seventy-four. I saw the hardware store, Rizzo's, and then the empty years, and then this bar. And now I see this. I witness it.
MARISOL: It's signed, for ten years. The Hook stays on the corner until twenty thirty-seven.
EDDIE: Twenty thirty-seven. I'll be ninety-four by then, but I'll come back to witness the renewal.
TOMMY: You'd better, Eddie, because your stool isn't going anywhere.

[Scene: 10:30 a.m. Behind the bar. Marisol and Kowalski have left. Tasha has arrived for the Sunday shift. Tommy stands in front of the chalkboard with Gina. Eddie and Luca watch from the stools.]

TASHA: Okay, is it true? Marisol told me on the stairs that Gina is the boss now.
GINA: I'm the managing partner, with fifty-one percent. Tasha, I'm sorry, I should have told you first. It all happened in two hours.
TASHA: Sorry? Gina, I'm happy! Now I have a boss who knows how to read a spreadsheet, and who doesn't make coffee.
TOMMY: That's condition number two. I'm banned from my own coffee machine.
TASHA: That's the best news I've had all year, after my chemistry grade. I got a B plus.
GINA: A B plus! Tasha, that's amazing!
TOMMY: Okay, before we open, there's one more thing. Gina, come here.
GINA: What is it? Please don't tell me there's another surprise.
TOMMY: These are the keys, all of them. There's the front door, the cooler, the register, the basement, and Angela's key.
GINA: Mom's key? I didn't know she had her own. Which one is Mom's?
TOMMY: The green one, with the pink nail polish on top. She painted it in two thousand six so she could find it in the dark. It still opens the back door.
GINA: The green one. I remember her painting it on the bar, with her pink nail polish.
TOMMY: And this is her apron, the one with the lemons. You already have one from Christmas. But this is the one she wore on the last day, before the hospital, before she couldn't come down anymore.
GINA: Dad...
TOMMY: And this is a piece of chalk for the board. Every day for twenty-one years, I wrote the menu, and one line at the bottom, either a joke or a warning. "Today's coffee: survivable."
EDDIE: My favorite was "Today's coffee: ask a lawyer." I laughed about that one for a week.
TOMMY: Tomorrow is Memorial Day, and we open at eleven. At three p.m. we have the moment of silence, like every year. So today is the last day I write the board.
GINA: Then write it, Dad. It's the last one, so make it something good.
TOMMY: Okay. Okay. "Sunday: lasagna, empanadas, and tamales from Doña Elena. Fries are free for presidents under eighteen."
LUCA: What's at the bottom, Nonno? Write the bottom!
TOMMY: The bottom. Okay, here it is. "Today's coffee: made by Tasha. Finally."
TASHA: Ha! It's official now, because it's on the board!
TOMMY: And there's one more line under that, written small.
GINA: What does it say? I can't read it from here.
TOMMY: "Thanks for twenty-one years. — T. Starting tomorrow: Ask for Gina."
GINA: Dad, come here and give me a hug, before I start crying on the menu.
TOMMY: You're already crying on the menu. Look, the chalk is running.
GINA: Then I'll rewrite it tomorrow, because that's my job now.
EDDIE: Tommy, pour me a coffee. Not yours, Tasha's. It's Sunday, a day of rest, and you're already resting.
TOMMY: I'm not resting, Eddie, I'm handing over. That's harder.
EDDIE: Harder than resting. Easier than leaving. You picked the middle. Good man.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **승계 어휘**
  - *partners / managing partner* 동업자 / 대표 동업자
  - *stake / fifty-one percent* 지분 / 51퍼센트(과반 = 결정권)
  - *step back, not out* — "물러서는 거지, 나가는 게 아니야."
  - *hand over / take over* 넘겨주다 / 넘겨받다
  - *LLC (limited liability company)* 유한 책임 회사 / *managing agent* 관리 대리인 / *witness* 증인
- **감정의 짧은 문장**
  - *This is hard to say. So I'm going to say it fast.* — "말하기 어려워. 그래서 빨리 말할게."
  - *I... I don't know what to say.* — 말이 막힌다.
  - *Give me a minute.* — "잠깐만 시간을 줘."
  - *Take two. Take ten.* — "2분이든 10분이든."
- **조건부 수락** — *Yes. But with conditions.* / *Condition one: … / Done.*
- **a healthy man with an unhealthy coffee** — 레빈 박사의 진단. "건강한 남자, 건강하지 못한 커피."
- **a war crime** — "전쟁 범죄." 부인에게 이르겠다는 협박에 대한 과장된 농담.
- **Harder than resting. Easier than leaving. You picked the middle.** — 에디의 말. "쉬는 것보다 어렵고, 떠나는 것보다 쉽지. 넌 중간을 골랐어."

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 토미의 고백은 정말로 빠르게. *Fifty-one percent you. | Forty-nine percent me.*에서만 끊는다.
- 지나의 *...What?*와 *I... I don't know what to say.*는 숨이 막힌 듯.
- 조건 세 개는 회의처럼 또박또박: *Condition one: … — Done.*의 핑퐁 리듬.
- 서명 장면의 에디 증언은 연설처럼 천천히. *I witness it.*에 무게를 둔다.
- 마지막 칠판 장면: 토미가 글자를 쓰며 읽는 속도로. *"Starting tomorrow: | Ask for Gina."*
- 에디의 마지막 세 문장은 리듬을 맞춘다: *Harder than resting. | Easier than leaving. | You picked the middle.*

## 한글 해석

루카: 논노, 우리 왜 이렇게 일찍 왔어요? 바 문 닫았고, 우리 말고 아무도 없잖아요.
토미: 오늘은 특별한 날이거든, 꼬마야. 논노가 종이에 서명하는데, 아주 큰 종이야.
루카: 그림 같은 거예요? 그림이면 도와줄 수 있어요, 저 그림 진짜 잘 그리거든요.
토미: 그보다는 약속에 가까워. 네가 열다섯 살이 될 때까지 이 바가 계속 문을 연다는 10년짜리 약속이야.
루카: 열다섯! 엄청 늙었다! 디에고 형보다 더 늙었어요!
지나: 아빠, 마리솔이랑 브라이언은 9시에 계약서 들고 와요. 그런데 왜 저를 7시 반에 오라고 하셨어요?
토미: 먼저 할 말이 있어서. 계약 전에, 다른 누구보다 먼저 말하고 싶었어.
지나: 좋아요, 이제 좀 무섭네요. 심장 때문이에요? 혈압이에요?
토미: 심장은 괜찮아. 레빈 박사 말로는 내가 "건강한 남자인데 커피가 건강하지 못하다"래. 그러니까 그런 거 아니야.
지나: 그럼 뭔데요? 그냥 말해요, 아빠, 저 긴장되잖아요.
토미: 지나, 이건 말하기 어려워. 그래서 빨리 말할게. 반창고 떼듯이 할게.
지나: 네. 듣고 있어요, 말해요.
토미: 새 계약에 혼자 서명하고 싶지 않아. '더 훅 LLC'라는 이름으로, 동업자로서 너랑 같이 서명하고 싶어. 51퍼센트는 너. 49퍼센트는 나.
지나: …네?
토미: 너한테 바를 주고 싶어, 지나. 한 번에 다는 아니고, 더 큰 부분을. 결정을 내리는 부분 말이야.
지나: 아빠. 저… 뭐라고 해야 할지 모르겠어요.
토미: 아직 아무 말도 하지 말고 끝까지 듣기만 해. 7월이면 쉰아홉이고, 21년 동안 주 70시간을 일했어. 네 엄마랑 내가 2006년 5월 28일에 문을 연 날부터.
지나: 알아요, 저도 거기 있었으니까요. 열네 살이었고, 간판을 칠했죠.
토미: 네가 간판을 칠했지, 그것도 엉망으로. 'K'가 삐뚤어졌어.
지나: 아직도 삐뚤어져 있잖아요. 들어올 때마다 보여요.
토미: 아직도 삐뚤어져 있지, 한 번도 안 고쳤으니까. 삐뚤어진 게 좋아.
루카: 엄마가 간판을 칠했어요? 밖에 있는 큰 거, 문 위에 있는 거요?
지나: 그랬지, 얘야, 오래전에. 너 태어나기 전, 아빠 만나기 전, 모든 것보다 전에.
토미: 지나, 난 한 발 물러서고 싶어, 나가는 게 아니라. 맥주는 일주일에 나흘 계속 따를 거야. 그래도 화요일이랑 금요일엔 논노가, 논노만 되고 싶어. 루카 데리러 가고, 부두에 데려가고, 메츠를 엉망으로 가르치고.
루카: 메츠! 논노, 메츠 어제 졌어요, 그리고 논노가 TV한테 소리 질렀잖아요.
토미: 메츠는 늘 져, 꼬마야. 그게 첫 번째 교훈이야.
루카: 두 번째 교훈은요? 핫도그에 관한 거예요?
토미: 두 번째 교훈은 그래도 사랑한다는 거야, 가슴이 찢어지게 해도.
지나: 그거 사실 메츠 얘기 아니죠, 아빠?
토미: 모든 것에 대한 얘기야, 얘야. 바, 건물, 그리고 간판을 삐뚤게 칠한 딸.
루카: 그리고 손자도요! 손자 빼먹지 마요!
지나: 아빠, 저 오픈 블록에 직장 있어요, 그 일 좋아하고요. 12월에 막 시작했잖아요.
