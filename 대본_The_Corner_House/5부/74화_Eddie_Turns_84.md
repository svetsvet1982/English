# THE CORNER HOUSE — 5부 74화

## Eddie Turns 84 (에디, 여든넷이 되다)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 5부 2화(통산 74화) · 시기: 2027년 3월 17일(수) 성 패트릭 데이 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 오전의 더 훅 → 오후의 바(깜짝 방문) → 저녁 생일 파티 → 밤의 계단
> - 등장인물: EDDIE, TOMMY, TASHA, MRS. ESPOSITO, MICHAEL, KAYLA, DIEGO, MARISOL, GINA, JESSE
> - 사건: 1년 전 6화와 똑같이 에디가 초록 스웨터를 입고 바에 들어온다. "그냥 커피. 평일이잖아." 토미와 타샤는 "작년 이맘때"를 하나씩 꺼낸다. 피처폰, 파란 자전거, 하버라인, 보일러, 그리고 매매 계약. 오후에 플로리다에서 아들 마이클과 손녀 케일라가 몰래 찾아온다. 저녁 파티에서는 디에고가 그 그림의 액자를, 제시가 '200만 조회 수' 인증서를, 부인이 코팅한 '두 번 두드리기' 카드를 선물한다. 작년 소원 "이 건물에서 1년 더"는 이루어졌다. 밤에 계단에서 마이클은 처음으로 이 계단이 왜 아버지의 세상인지 이해한다.
> - 학습 포인트: 회고 표현(This time last year… / A year ago, I'd never… / Since then… / Looking back… / Who would have thought…?), 생일 표현 복습(6화 재사용: Many happy returns. / Make a wish. / Blow out the candles.), 노년의 어조
> - 미국 현안: 고령 부모와 멀리 사는 자녀, '에이징 인 플레이스'(살던 곳에서 늙어 가기), 세대 간 기억
> - 연결: 6화 83세 생일(초록 스웨터, 부인의 1974년 사진, 소원 '이 건물에서 1년 더'+토미 커피, 'Danny Boy', 마이클의 스마트폰). 11화 스마트폰·케일라 사칭 사기. 64화 스케이트. 68화 마이클 '7만 5천 받으세요'. 70화 마이클 2천 기부. 73화 매매 수락. 3월 15일 매매 계약 서명.

## 대본

[Scene: Wednesday, March 17, 11:00 a.m. Eddie comes into The Hook wearing a bright green sweater. The same one as last year. Tommy is behind the bar. Tasha is cutting lemons.]

EDDIE: Morning, Tommy. Just coffee. It's a weekday.
TOMMY: Just coffee? On your birthday, and on Saint Patrick's Day, again? You did this exact thing last year.
EDDIE: Again, yes. Some things change and some things don't, and my coffee order is one of the things that doesn't.
TASHA: Happy birthday, Eddie! You're eighty-four today, and you look exactly the same as last year!
EDDIE: Same sweater. Different man.
TOMMY: Different how? You look like the same guy to me, just a little greener.
EDDIE: Let me think about it. This time last year, I had a flip phone from two thousand nine. Now I have a phone that sends little hearts to Dolores.
TASHA: And now you're internet famous, with over two million views.
EDDIE: A year ago, I'd never heard the word "viral." When I first heard it, I thought it was a disease.
TOMMY: Well, it kind of is. Okay, my turn. This time last year, I was worried about the price of eggs. Remember? They were seven dollars a dozen.
TASHA: And I was worried about my tips, but now I'm worried about a chemistry exam on Friday. Night classes are a lot harder than I expected.
EDDIE: Looking back, what was the biggest thing that happened this whole year?
TOMMY: That's easy. It was the note under my door on May thirty-first, which said, "The building is being sold." I almost threw up when I read it.
EDDIE: And since then? What happened after that?
TOMMY: Since then... Harborline, Bridgewater, buyout letters, a flood, a boiler, Brian Kowalski, and a co-op. And on Monday, we signed the purchase agreement, which was forty pages long. I signed my name fourteen times.
TASHA: It was fifteen, because I counted. You signed one page twice by mistake.
TOMMY: Who would have thought? A year ago, I didn't even know what a quorum was.
EDDIE: A year ago, nobody around here knew what a quorum was, except Dolores, of course.
TASHA: Eddie, do you remember your wish from last year, when you blew out the candles?
EDDIE: Of course I remember. "One more year in this building." And a better cup of coffee, too.
TOMMY: So that's one out of two. The building, you got. The coffee, never.
EDDIE: One out of two at my age is a perfect score.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Happy birthday, Eddie! I brought you something, but you can't open it until tonight, at the party.
EDDIE: Thank you, Dolores. And the knock this morning was very loud, a lot louder than usual.
MRS. ESPOSITO: That's because it was a birthday knock. It was three knocks, with one extra for the birthday.
EDDIE: And I knocked back four, with one extra for the building.
TASHA: You two have a secret language now, with knocks, like whales talking under the ocean.
MRS. ESPOSITO: It's not secret, Tasha, because everybody in the building can hear it. That's the whole point.

[Scene: 2:30 p.m. The Hook. Eddie sits at his stool reading the newspaper. The door opens. A man in his fifties and a young woman with a backpack walk in.]

MICHAEL: Excuse me, is this the last real bar in Brooklyn? I'm looking for a famous skater who lives around here.
EDDIE: Michael? Michael! What... what are you doing here?
MICHAEL: Happy birthday, Dad. Surprise! We wanted to see your face when we walked in.
KAYLA: Happy birthday, Grandpa! We flew in this morning, on the seven a.m. flight from Tampa!
EDDIE: Kayla! Kayla, look at you, all grown up! Come here, both of you, come here!
TOMMY: Michael O'Neill, I haven't seen you in ten years. You got gray, my friend.
MICHAEL: And you got loud, Tommy Russo. Well, louder than before.
TOMMY: I was always loud, but now I'm a homeowner, and homeowners are louder.
EDDIE: Why didn't you tell me you were coming? I would have cleaned the apartment, and I would have bought a cake.
MICHAEL: Because Tommy told us not to tell you, and so did Mrs. Esposito. She planned everything, with a clipboard.
EDDIE: Dolores planned this? That woman. That sneaky, wonderful woman.
KAYLA: She emailed me a schedule, Grandpa, with times on it. "Two thirty, arrive. Two thirty-five, Eddie cries."
EDDIE: I'm not crying.
TOMMY: It's two thirty-four, so he's actually ahead of schedule.
MICHAEL: Dad, I watched the skating video forty times. My boss watched it, my wife cried, and Kayla's whole dorm watched it too.
KAYLA: My roommate even made it her phone background, so you're her phone background now, Grandpa.
EDDIE: Her phone background? What does that mean? Am I living inside her phone now?
KAYLA: Kind of, yes. It's hard to explain.
MICHAEL: And then I read about the boiler, and about the offers. So I called you and told you to take the money and come to Florida.
EDDIE: I remember that call, and I remember that you were angry.
MICHAEL: I wasn't angry, I was scared, and that's different. I was scared you'd be alone on the third floor at fifty-one degrees, while I was a thousand miles away.
EDDIE: But I wasn't alone, son, and that's what I tried to tell you. Dolores came up with the key, Tess came with a thermometer, and Tommy opened the bar.
MICHAEL: I know that now, and I can see it now. That's why I came, so I could see it with my own eyes.
TOMMY: Then you have to stay for the party tonight. It starts at six o'clock, and the whole building is coming, so you'll see everything.
KAYLA: We're staying until Sunday, because Mrs. Esposito already booked us a room. It's at a bed-and-breakfast in Carroll Gardens, and Gina's friend runs it.
EDDIE: Of course she did. Michael, Kayla, sit down here with me. Tommy, two more coffees, please. Not your coffee, though. Tasha's coffee.
TOMMY: Tasha's coffee? On your birthday? Wait, have you been hiding Tasha's coffee from me?
EDDIE: Everybody has, Tommy, for about a year now. It's behind the cereal.

[Scene: 6:30 p.m. Green balloons over the bar. Neighbors crowd in. Diego holds a flat package. Jesse holds a rolled-up paper. Marisol and Gina stand near the cake.]

GINA: Okay, everybody, listen up! We do gifts first, then cake, and then the song!
DIEGO: Me first! Eddie, open it, I made it! Well, I drew it, and Mom paid for the frame.
EDDIE: What is it? It's heavy, whatever it is. Oh. Oh, Diego.
DIEGO: It's the drawing from the bid, the real one. Mr. Whitlock sent it back to us with a note.
MARISOL: He mailed it on Monday, together with the signed contract. His note says, "This belongs to the building, not to Harborline."
EDDIE: Look at that. That's me in the window with my radio, and I'm young, like it's nineteen seventy.
DIEGO: And Peggy is there too, look! She's on the roof next to Nadia, in a red coat, skating backward.
EDDIE: You... you put Peggy up there on the roof?
DIEGO: She needed to be there, because she's part of the building. That's what you always say.
EDDIE: Many happy returns, Diego. That's the best gift anybody has given me since the day at the lake.
JESSE: Okay, that's a hard act to follow, but here you go. I made a certificate, and it's official: "Edward O'Neill. Two million, three hundred forty-one thousand views. Most famous man in Red Hook."
EDDIE: Two million three hundred! It went up again!
JESSE: It goes up every day. A school in Japan even used it in an English class, as a reading exercise.
EDDIE: Japan? You mean children in Japan are listening to me say "Push and glide"?
JESSE: Yes, but with subtitles.
GINA: And this one is from the building, from the co-op. It's not official yet, because the closing is April thirtieth, but we made a sample. It's share certificate number one.
EDDIE: Number one? Why do I get number one?
MARISOL: Because you've lived here the longest, for fifty-two years, so you get share number one. We voted on it, five to zero.
EDDIE: Five to zero, again? This building agrees with itself way too much lately.
GINA: And this last one is from Dolores. She's in the kitchen right now, guarding the cake, so open it.
EDDIE: It's a card, and it's laminated. "Eight a.m. Two knocks. Reply: two knocks. If no reply: come up. Key: Dolores. Backup key: Marisol. Backup backup: Tommy."
GINA: She said it's for your fridge, next to Peggy's picture, so everybody knows the system. Even when she's gone.
EDDIE: Dolores is not going anywhere, and you can tell her I said so.
GINA: She said you'd say that, and her answer was, "Neither is he. That's why it's laminated. It's built to last."
GINA: Okay, now it's time for cake! Jesse, get the lights! Everybody, sing!
JESSE: The lights are off, and we're ready!
GINA: Happy birthday to you... happy birthday to you...
DIEGO: Happy birthday, dear Eddie...
GINA: Happy birthday to you! Now make a wish, Eddie!
EDDIE: Okay, a wish. Let me think about it. Last year, I wished for one more year in this building.
DIEGO: And you got it, didn't you?
EDDIE: I got it. So this year... I wish for the same thing, one more year in this building, in my building. And then next year, I'll make the same wish again.
JESSE: So that's a renewable wish. It's just like a lease.
EDDIE: Exactly, like a lease. A good lease, one that never runs out. Okay, now I blow out the candles.
DIEGO: Eighty-four candles, and you got all of them! In one breath!
EDDIE: Eighty-four candles. There was a time when I'd blow out one match and get dizzy, so I must be getting stronger.
DIEGO: Eddie, now the song! Sing "Danny Boy," like last year!
EDDIE: My voice is even worse than it was last year, you know.
MARISOL: Your voice is the tradition, Eddie, so please sing.
EDDIE: "Oh, Danny boy, the pipes, the pipes are calling..."

[Scene: 11:00 p.m. The staircase between the second and third floors. Eddie sits on his step. Michael sits one step below him with two cups of tea that Mrs. Esposito sent up.]

MICHAEL: So this is the famous step. The halfway house, you call it?
EDDIE: The halfway house. It was Dr. Levine's idea, and I rest here every time I go up. There are forty-two steps total, and this is number twenty-one.
MICHAEL: I grew up on these stairs, Dad, and I forgot that. I used to jump down the last five steps, and Mom yelled at me every time.
EDDIE: Every single time. "Michael Patrick O'Neill, you'll break your neck!" Then you'd land like a cat, and she'd laugh.
MICHAEL: I'd forgotten that too. Her laugh, echoing on the stairs.
EDDIE: The stairs remember, though. That's the thing about old buildings, because they remember everything. Every footstep, every fight, every Christmas.
MICHAEL: Dad, I owe you an apology for the phone call in February, when I said, "Take the seventy-five thousand."
EDDIE: You don't owe me anything. You were scared, and you're my son, so you're allowed to be scared for me.
MICHAEL: But I didn't understand. I thought you were staying because you're stubborn, like Grandpa was. I thought it was just pride.
EDDIE: It's a little pride, I won't lie to you.
MICHAEL: But it's not only pride, and I saw that tonight. Dolores with her laminated card, Diego with the drawing, Tommy crying into the cake, Sam fixing the candle holder. Nobody in Florida knows my name like that, not even after twenty years.
EDDIE: Well, it took fifty-two years, so it doesn't happen fast. It happens one knock at a time.
MICHAEL: Can I say something, if you promise you won't get mad?
EDDIE: People always say that, and then I always get mad. But go ahead.
MICHAEL: When you can't do the stairs anymore, and that day will come, Dad, will you think about Florida? Will you at least think about it?
EDDIE: ...I'll think about it when that day comes, I promise. But I also want you to know something.
MICHAEL: What is it?
EDDIE: The co-op already has a plan for that day. There's a ground-floor apartment behind the bar, which is storage right now. Sam put it in the five-year budget as an "accessible unit," with no stairs at all.
MICHAEL: Sam planned for that? He did that for you?
EDDIE: Sam plans for everything, and that's his curse.
MICHAEL: Then I'm not worried anymore. Okay, maybe a little, but a lot less than before.
EDDIE: Less is good, and less is enough. Now help me up, because there are twenty-one more steps, and then I sleep.
MICHAEL: Twenty-one more, then. Lean on me, Dad.
EDDIE: Happy birthday to me. This time last year, I climbed these stairs alone. This year, I have my son.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **회고 표현**
  - *This time last year, I had a flip phone from two thousand nine.* — "작년 이맘때는 폴더폰을 썼지."
  - *A year ago, I'd never heard the word "viral."* — 과거완료로 '그때까지 한 번도'.
  - *Since then, …* — "그 뒤로…"
  - *Looking back, what was the biggest thing that happened this whole year?* — "돌아보면 가장 큰 일은 뭐였어?"
  - *Who would have thought?* — "누가 생각이나 했겠어?"
- **생일 표현 복습(6화)**
  - *Many happy returns.* — "생일 축하해(이런 날이 여러 번 돌아오길)." 격식 있고 오래된 표현.
  - *Make a wish. / Blow out the candles.* — "소원 빌어. / 촛불 꺼."
- **Same sweater. Different man.** — "스웨터는 같고, 사람은 달라졌지."
- **One out of two at my age is a perfect score.** — "내 나이에 둘 중 하나면 만점이야."
- **a renewable wish. Like a lease.** — 갱신 가능한 소원. 임대 계약처럼.
- **halfway house** — 원래는 '사회 복귀 시설'. 에디는 계단 중간 쉼터를 농담으로 이렇게 부른다.
- **accessible unit** — 장애인·노인이 쓰기 쉬운(계단 없는) 집.
- **It happens one knock at a time.** — "하나의 노크씩 쌓여 이루어지는 거야."

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 6화와 같은 첫 대사 *Just coffee. It's a weekday.*를 똑같은 리듬으로. 듣는 사람이 1년 전을 떠올리게 한다.
- *This time last year*를 말할 때마다 약간 멈추고 그때를 떠올리듯.
- 마이클의 첫 등장은 장난스럽게, 에디의 *Michael? Michael!*은 두 번째에 크게 터진다.
- 생일 노래는 다 같이 부르듯, 'Danny Boy'는 에디 혼자 느리게.
- 마지막 계단 장면은 아버지와 아들의 낮은 대화. *There are forty-two steps total* 숫자는 담담하게.
- 마지막 문장 *This year, I have my son.* — 3월의 새벽처럼 조용하게.

## 한글 해석

에디: 좋은 아침이야, 토미. 그냥 커피. 평일이잖아.
토미: 그냥 커피요? 생일에, 그것도 성 패트릭 데이에, 또요? 작년에도 똑같이 그러셨잖아요.
에디: 그래, 또. 바뀌는 것도 있고 안 바뀌는 것도 있는데, 내 커피 주문은 안 바뀌는 쪽이야.
타샤: 생일 축하해요, 에디! 오늘로 여든넷인데, 작년이랑 완전 똑같아 보여요!
에디: 스웨터는 같고. 사람은 달라졌지.
토미: 뭐가 달라졌는데요? 제 눈엔 같은 사람인데요. 좀 더 초록색이 됐을 뿐.
에디: 생각 좀 해 보자. 작년 이맘때는 2009년산 폴더폰을 썼어. 지금은 돌로레스한테 작은 하트를 보내는 폰이 있지.
타샤: 그리고 이제 인터넷 유명인이시죠. 조회 수가 200만이 넘어요.
에디: 1년 전만 해도 '바이럴'이라는 말을 들어 본 적이 없었어. 처음 들었을 땐 병 이름인 줄 알았지.
토미: 뭐, 사실 좀 그렇긴 하죠. 좋아요, 제 차례. 작년 이맘때 난 달걀값을 걱정했어요. 기억나요? 한 판에 7달러였잖아요.
타샤: 그리고 난 팁을 걱정했는데, 지금은 금요일에 있는 화학 시험을 걱정해요. 야간 수업이 생각보다 훨씬 어려워요.
에디: 돌아보면 올 한 해 동안 일어난 일 중에 뭐가 제일 컸어?
토미: 쉽죠. 5월 31일 문 밑에 있던 쪽지요. "건물이 팔리고 있음"이라고 써 있었죠. 읽자마자 토할 뻔했어요.
에디: 그 뒤로는? 그다음엔 무슨 일이 있었지?
토미: 그 뒤로는… 하버라인, 브리지워터, 바이아웃 편지, 홍수, 보일러, 브라이언 코왈스키, 그리고 협동조합. 그리고 월요일에 매매 계약에 서명했죠. 40쪽짜리였어요. 이름을 열네 번 썼어요.
타샤: 열다섯 번이에요, 제가 세어 봤거든요. 한 장에 실수로 두 번 했어요.
토미: 누가 생각이나 했겠어요? 1년 전엔 정족수가 뭔지도 몰랐는데.
에디: 1년 전엔 이 동네 아무도 정족수가 뭔지 몰랐어. 물론 돌로레스 빼고.
타샤: 에디, 작년에 촛불 끌 때 빌었던 소원 기억나요?
에디: 당연히 기억하지. "이 건물에서 1년 더." 그리고 더 나은 커피 한 잔도.
토미: 그럼 둘 중 하나네요. 건물은 얻으셨고. 커피는 절대 안 되죠.
에디: 내 나이에 둘 중 하나면 만점이야.
에스포지토 부인: 생일 축하해요, 에디! 선물 가져왔어요. 근데 오늘 밤 파티 전까지는 열면 안 돼요.
에디: 고마워, 돌로레스. 그리고 오늘 아침 노크 아주 컸어. 평소보다 훨씬 컸어.
에스포지토 부인: 생일 노크라서 그래요. 세 번이었죠. 생일이라 한 번 더.
에디: 그리고 난 네 번으로 답했지. 건물 몫으로 한 번 더.
타샤: 두 분 이제 비밀 언어가 있네요. 노크로요. 바닷속에서 대화하는 고래처럼요.
에스포지토 부인: 비밀 아니야, 타샤. 건물 사람들 다 들리거든. 그게 요점이야.
마이클: 실례합니다, 여기가 브루클린의 마지막 진짜 바인가요? 이 근처에 사는 유명한 스케이터를 찾는데요.
