# THE CORNER HOUSE — 5부 89화

## A New Sign Over the Door (문 위의 새 현판)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 5부 17화(통산 89화) · 시기: 2027년 6월 1일(화) 오전~밤 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 오전의 더 훅(이름과 디자인 확정) → 오후 건물 현관 앞(설치) → 저녁 6시 제막식 → 밤의 인도
> - 등장인물: MARISOL, LAUREN, GINA, EDDIE, SAM, MARCUS, LUIS, TOMMY, DIEGO, TERESA, MR. HAYES, MRS. ESPOSITO, NADIA
> - 사건: 조합의 새 계약이 시작되는 날. 이사회는 건물 현관 위에 걸 현판 이름을 정한다. 'The Corner House Co-op'과 에디가 제안한 'Roode Hoek Co-op'이 맞붙는다. 지나의 옛 동료이자 지금 오픈 블록의 파트타임 디자이너인 로런이 시안을 가져온다. 오후에는 첫 출근한 루이스와 마커스가 현판 받침대를 단다. 저녁 제막식에서 에디가 레드훅의 옛 네덜란드 이름 '로더 훅'의 뜻을 풀어 준다. '로더'는 붉은 흙, '훅'은 튀어나온 땅이자 모퉁이다. 앤젤라가 '사람들이 이 모퉁이에 낚이니까'라며 지은 바 이름 '더 훅'이 우연히도 400년 전 이름과 겹친다는 것이다.
> - 학습 포인트: 상징 서술(It stands for… / It represents… / What it means to us is…), 어원 이야기(The name comes from… / Originally, it meant… / Over time, it became…), 'Roode Hoek' 발음과 의미 강조, 감정 서술
> - 미국 현안: 뉴욕의 네덜란드 식민지 시대 지명(브루클린=브뢰컬런, 할렘=하를럼), 원주민 레나페의 땅, 지역 정체성과 이름 짓기, 주민 소유를 상징하는 현판
> - 연결: 47화 앤젤라가 지은 '더 훅'(사람들이 이 모퉁이에 낚이니까). 86화 지나가 칠한 삐뚤어진 'K'. 52화 로런(봄에 오픈 블록 파트타임 추천). 80화 루이스 6/1 첫 출근. 53화 지미의 갈고리. 75화 4/29 소유. 76화 '규칙이 책임자'.

## 대본

[Scene: Tuesday, June 1, 9:00 a.m. The Hook, before opening. Marisol, Sam, Gina, and Eddie sit at a table. Lauren, with a laptop and a portfolio, sets up three printed designs.]

MARISOL: Okay, this is a quick board meeting with only one item: the sign over the front door, which means the building's name. Lauren, thank you for coming.
LAUREN: Thank you for having me. And thank you, Gina, for the job at Open Block, two days a week. My commute is eighteen minutes now, and I cried on the subway. They were happy tears.
GINA: You were the best designer at Brightline. The agents never really replaced you, because they just made worse mockups.
LAUREN: I heard. They hired two humans back in April, and then Craig called me. I told him no.
SAM: Good for you. Okay, there are two names on the table. Option one is "The Corner House Co-op." Option two is Eddie's idea.
EDDIE: "Roode Hoek Co-op." That's R-O-O-D-E, and then H-O-E-K.
MARISOL: Eddie, nobody can pronounce that. The mail carrier is going to think it's a typo.
EDDIE: Then the mail carrier will learn something new. That's what signs are for.
SAM: "The Corner House" is clear, and everybody already calls it that. Even the news article called it that.
GINA: That's the argument for A. People already know the name.
EDDIE: People know it, but nobody chose it. A reporter named it last January, in a headline.
MARISOL: That's true. "Boiler War at the Corner House." That was the very first article about us.
EDDIE: I don't want our name to come from trouble. I want it to come from the ground, from the soil itself.
SAM: From the soil. Okay, Eddie, you have my attention.
SAM: So what does it actually stand for? Before we vote, I need some data.
EDDIE: I'll tell you tonight at the unveiling, if it wins. If it loses, I'll tell you anyway, but it'll be a sadder story.
LAUREN: Let me show you the designs first, and then you can decide. Design A is "The Corner House Co-op." It's simple and clean, with black letters on brass.
GINA: It's nice, and it's clear. Everybody will know what it means.
LAUREN: Design B is "Roode Hoek Co-op," on the same brass, but with a small drawing of a hook of land sticking into the harbor. It's based on a map from the sixteen hundreds.
MARISOL: Oh, that's beautiful. Is that the real shape of Red Hook?
LAUREN: Eddie gave me an old map from the historical society. The land used to curve like that, like a hook, before the piers were built and before they filled in the marshes.
SAM: And what about Design C?
LAUREN: Design C has both names. "Roode Hoek Co-op" is on top, and underneath, in smaller letters: "The Corner House. Owned by the people who live in it."
EDDIE: "Owned by the people who live in it." That's from Sal's letter.
LAUREN: Gina told me about it, and I thought those words belonged on the door.
SAM: Okay, I'm convinced, data or no data. I vote C.
GINA: C. Absolutely C, no question.
MARISOL: I vote C too. Eddie?
EDDIE: C, with both names: the old one and the true one. Lauren, you're a good designer, and you're an even better listener.
MARISOL: Dolores and Priya voted C by text this morning, so it's unanimous. We're going with Design C.
LAUREN: Then I'll send the file to the engraver right now. He promised it by two o'clock, in brass, with the little hook of land.

[Scene: 2:30 p.m. The front door of the building, next to The Hook. Marcus stands on a ladder with a drill. Luis, in a brand-new Bell Bikes T-shirt, holds the ladder. Tommy and Diego watch. The new brass sign leans against the wall, covered with a cloth.]

LUIS: Marcus, move it a little to the left. No, your left, which is my right. Okay, stop. That's perfect.
MARCUS: Is it level? Diego, check the level for me, please.
DIEGO: The bubble is right in the middle! It's level! It's perfectly level!
TOMMY: Unlike the "K" on my sign. Gina painted that in two thousand six, and it's been crooked for twenty-one years.
MARCUS: Do you want me to fix it while I'm up here?
TOMMY: Touch that K and you're fired from the building.
MARCUS: You can't fire me. You're only forty-nine percent of a bar and one fifth of a building.
TOMMY: Then I'll ask Gina to fire you, because she has fifty-one percent.
LUIS: Marcus, it's my first day. Am I doing okay so far?
MARCUS: Luis, you've done six battery checks and two brake repairs, and now you're holding a ladder for a historic sign. It's only two thirty, so you're doing great.
LUIS: Six battery checks, and one of them was bad, really swollen. I put it in the fire cabinet with sand, just like you showed me.
DIEGO: Did you smell it? Like you said in the interview?
LUIS: I smelled it from the sidewalk, like burnt plastic but sweet. The delivery guy asked, "How did you know?" And I said, "Experience."
TOMMY: And this morning, was it strange? Getting a paycheck instead of working for an app?
LUIS: I haven't gotten a paycheck yet, because that's on Friday. But this morning my daughter asked me, "Papi, what time do you finish?" I said, "Six o'clock." I knew the answer. For the first time in six years.
MARCUS: Six o'clock, and no rain delays. Okay, the bracket is in, with two screws, and it's strong.
TOMMY: So what's under the cloth? Can I take a little peek?
DIEGO: No! Nobody peeks! Those are Mrs. Esposito's orders, because the unveiling is at six!
TOMMY: Wow, the youth president is very strict today.
DIEGO: I learned from the best, and also from the bylaws.

[Scene: 5:40 p.m. The sidewalk before the unveiling. Folding chairs. Teresa Cappello arrives carrying a long, flat package wrapped in brown paper. Marisol, Sam, Mr. Hayes, and Diego set up chairs.]

TERESA: Marisol! I made it, but the traffic on the Verrazzano was terrible, as always.
MARISOL: Teresa, you came! What's that you're carrying?
TERESA: It's a gift from Dad. He had a good morning, and he said, "Take the old sign from your garage, and give it to the corner."
SAM: What old sign? I didn't know there was one.
TERESA: Look. "CAPPELLO HARDWARE. Est. 1970." It's the original, and it hung over this door before the bar, before Rizzo rented the space. Dad kept it in my garage for twenty years.
MR. HAYES: I remember that sign! I saw it from the B61 in nineteen ninety-three. It was faded, but it was still there, over the corner.
DIEGO: Before The Hook? There was a hardware store here?
TERESA: Your building had a hardware store downstairs, with nails, paint, and hammers. My dad sold them, and he knew every customer's name.
MARISOL: Like a president does. Like Walter does now.
MR. HAYES: Or like a bus driver. When you see the same people every day, you learn their names.
SAM: So where do we put it? The new sign is going over the door.
TERESA: Somewhere inside. Dad said, "Not outside. My name doesn't belong outside anymore. Theirs does."
DIEGO: "Theirs does." That means us, right?
TERESA: That means you, Diego. All of you.

[Scene: 6:00 p.m. The sidewalk in front of the building. About fifty neighbors gather. The sign hangs over the front door, still covered with the cloth. Mrs. Esposito holds the cord. Nadia stands beside her. Eddie stands in front of the door, leaning on his cane.]

MRS. ESPOSITO: Everybody, quiet please! Thank you! As secretary of the co-op, I've been asked to say one sentence, so here it is. On June first, we hang our own name on our own door.
NADIA: And as a member of the land trust, I'll say one sentence too. This land is safe now, for ninety-nine years, under this sign.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And now, Eddie, our oldest member and holder of share number one, will explain the name. Eddie?
EDDIE: Thank you, Dolores. Can everybody hear me in the back? My voice is old, but it still works.
EDDIE: Before I explain, let me ask you something. Who here knows what Red Hook was called four hundred years ago?
MRS. ESPOSITO: The Dutch named it, in the sixteen hundreds. Diego told me about it all week, because Ms. Park taught them.
EDDIE: Ms. Park is right. But before the Dutch, the Lenape people lived here for thousands of years. This was their land first, so let's remember that.
EDDIE: Then, in sixteen thirty-six, the Dutch came, and they had a name for this place. The name comes from two Dutch words: "Roode Hoek."
NADIA: Roode Hoek. How do you say it correctly?
EDDIE: "ROH-duh HOOK." The first word, "roode," means red. That's because of the soil, since the clay here was red. It was red earth.
EDDIE: The second word, "hoek," means a point, a piece of land that sticks out into the water, like a hook. But in Dutch, "hoek" also means something else.
NADIA: Something else? What else does it mean?
EDDIE: A corner. "Hoek" means corner.
MRS. ESPOSITO: A corner. So originally, it meant Red Corner.
EDDIE: Red Corner. Over the years, the English speakers heard "Roode Hoek" and said "Red Hook." The name stayed. The meaning was lost, and nobody remembered that it meant corner.
EDDIE: And now, Tommy and Gina, come up here. In two thousand six, Angela Russo named a bar on this corner "The Hook," because, she said, "People get hooked on this corner."
GINA: That's true. She said it to everybody who walked in.
EDDIE: Angela didn't know Dutch, and she never read an old map. But four hundred years later, she named this place by its oldest name, "hoek," the corner. And she did it by accident.
TOMMY: ...By accident. She did it by accident.
EDDIE: Maybe it was an accident, and maybe it wasn't. Peggy always said, "Angela knows things she doesn't know she knows."
GINA: That's... that's exactly Mom. That's exactly who she was.
EDDIE: So this building is the Corner House, and the bar is the Hook, and the Hook means corner. What it means to us is simple: the corner is ours. Dolores, pull the cord.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I'm pulling the cord now. One, two, three!
NADIA: Look! It's beautiful! "Roode Hoek Co-op," and there's the little hook of land!
MRS. ESPOSITO: And there's more underneath. Read it, Eddie. Read it out loud for everybody.
EDDIE: "The Corner House. Owned by the people who live in it."
NADIA: That's the best sign in Brooklyn, and believe me, I've seen a lot of signs.

[Scene: 9:30 p.m. The sidewalk. The crowd has gone. The streetlight shines on the brass sign. Next to it, the old wooden sign of The Hook, with its crooked K. Tommy and Gina stand together, looking up.]

TOMMY: Look at them, side by side. The new one and the old one, right next to each other.
GINA: The new one is brass, and it's professional and level. Lauren is a genius.
TOMMY: And the old one is wood, crooked and peeling. It was painted by a fourteen-year-old who wanted to go to the mall.
GINA: I really did want to go to the mall, but Mom made me paint the sign first. She said, "You'll thank me in twenty years."
TOMMY: Well, it's been twenty-one years. Do you thank her?
GINA: I thank her every day, and especially today. Dad, did you know about "hoek" before tonight?
TOMMY: No, never. I thought it was just a joke, that people get hooked, like fish. Eddie's always had the old maps, but he never told me.
GINA: Maybe he was waiting for the right day, and for a sign to explain.
TOMMY: Eddie waits for everything. He waited fifty-nine years to fish on that pier.
GINA: And what about Sal's old sign, the one Teresa brought? Where do we hang it?
TOMMY: Inside, over the register, next to Sal's cash register and Mom's calamari card, right under Walter's bus map.
GINA: So the bar is a museum now. Dolores was right about that.
TOMMY: It's a museum where you can drink, which is the best kind.
GINA: And the coffee's finally good, thanks to Tasha.
TOMMY: Don't push it.
GINA: And the first fish went to Diego, the same way the corner went to us.
TOMMY: Gina, can I ask you something as your partner? As your forty-nine percent?
GINA: Go ahead and ask.
TOMMY: Should we fix the K on the old sign, now that there's a perfect new sign right next to it?
GINA: Never. The crooked K stays, because it's the only thing on this corner that's never changed.
TOMMY: Except your mother's lemon rule. And Eddie's coffee order. And Dolores's clipboard.
GINA: Okay, fine, four things. Four things never change, and the rest can.
TOMMY: Four things is a good number. It's like rule number four, which is never sign alone.
GINA: Never sign alone. Never fix the K.
TOMMY: Goodnight, partner. Tomorrow's Wednesday, so I'm working, and you're at Open Block.
GINA: And next Tuesday? You're off, with Luca, on the pier.
TOMMY: A regular Tuesday. I haven't had a regular Tuesday in twenty-one years, so I don't even know what to do with one.
GINA: Then it's time you found out. Goodnight, Dad.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **상징 서술**
  - *It stands for… / It represents…* — "~을 상징한다."
  - *What it means to us is…* — "우리에게 그것이 의미하는 건…"
  - *On June first, we hang our own name on our own door.* — 부인의 한 문장. 소유의 상징.
- **어원 이야기**
  - *The name comes from…* — "그 이름은 ~에서 왔다."
  - *Originally, it meant…* — "원래는 ~라는 뜻이었다."
  - *Over the years, … The name stayed. The meaning was lost, …* — "세월이 흐르며… 이름은 남고 뜻은 잊혔다."
  - *Roode Hoek* [로더 훅] — 네덜란드어 '붉은 곶·붉은 모퉁이'. *roode* = red(붉은), *hoek* = point / corner(곶, 모퉁이).
  - 뉴욕의 네덜란드 지명: *Brooklyn* ← Breukelen / *Harlem* ← Haarlem.
- **Lenape** — 레나페. 네덜란드인 이전부터 이 땅에 살던 원주민.
- **Angela knows things she doesn't know she knows.** — "앤젤라는 자기가 안다는 걸 모르는 것들을 알아." 페기의 말.
- **Touch that K and you're fired from the building.** — 토미식 협박 농담.
- **I knew the answer. For the first time in six years.** — 루이스. 퇴근 시간을 알게 된 기쁨.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- **Roode Hoek**는 에디처럼 천천히: [ROH-duh] [HOOK]. 첫 단어의 *ROH*에 강세.
- 에디의 어원 설명은 수업처럼 한 단계씩: *The first word, | "roode," | means red.* / *The second word, | "hoek," | means a point.*
- *A corner. | "Hoek" means corner.* — 이 화의 반전. 멈추고 천천히.
- 토미의 *...By accident.*는 거의 속삭임.
- 제막 순간의 *One, two, three!*와 디에고의 환호는 크게. 현판 문구는 에디가 또박또박: *"Owned by the people who live in it."*
- 마지막 부녀의 대화는 밤의 낮은 목소리. *Never sign alone. | Never fix the K.*
