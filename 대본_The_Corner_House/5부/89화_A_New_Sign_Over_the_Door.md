# THE CORNER HOUSE — 5부 89화

## A New Sign Over the Door (문 위의 새 현판)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 5부 17화(통산 89화) · 시기: 2027년 6월 1일(화) 오전~밤 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 오전의 더 훅(이름과 디자인 확정) → 오후 건물 현관 앞(설치) → 저녁 6시 제막식 → 밤의 인도
> - 등장인물: MARISOL, LAUREN, GINA, EDDIE, SAM, MARCUS, LUIS, TOMMY, DIEGO, TERESA, MR. HAYES, MRS. ESPOSITO, NADIA
> - 사건: 조합의 새 계약이 시작되는 날. 이사회는 건물 현관 위에 걸 현판 이름을 정한다. 'The Corner House Co-op'과 에디가 제안한 'Roode Hoek Co-op'이 맞붙는다. 지나의 옛 동료이자 지금 오픈 블록의 파트타임 디자이너인 로런이 시안을 가져온다. 오후에는 첫 출근한 루이스와 마커스가 현판 받침대를 단다. 저녁 제막식에서 에디가 레드훅의 옛 네덜란드 이름 '로더 훅'의 뜻을 풀어 준다. '로더'는 붉은 흙, '훅'은 튀어나온 땅이자 모퉁이다. 앤젤라가 '사람들이 이 모퉁이에 낚이니까'라며 지은 바 이름 '더 훅'이 우연히도 400년 전 이름과 겹친다는 것이다.
> - 학습 포인트: 상징 서술(It stands for… / It represents… / What it means to us is…), 어원 이야기(The name comes from… / Originally, it meant… / Over time, it became…), 'Roode Hoek' 발음과 의미 강조, 감정 서술
> - 미국 현안: 뉴욕의 네덜란드 식민지 시대 지명(브루클린=브뢰컬런, 할렘=하를럼), 원주민 레나페의 땅, 지역 정체성과 이름 짓기, 주민 소유를 상징하는 현판
> - 연결: 47화 앤젤라가 지은 '더 훅'(사람들이 이 모퉁이에 낚이니까). 86화 지나가 칠한 삐뚤어진 'K'. 52화 로런(봄에 오픈 블록 파트타임 추천). 80화 루이스 6/1 첫 출근. 53화 지미의 갈고리. 75화 4/29 소유. 76화 '규칙이 책임자'.

## 대본

[Scene: Tuesday, June 1, 9:00 a.m. The Hook, before opening. Marisol, Sam, Gina, and Eddie sit at a table. Lauren, with a laptop and a portfolio, sets up three printed designs.]

MARISOL: Okay. Quick board meeting. One item. The sign over the front door. The building's name. Lauren, thank you for coming.
LAUREN: Thank you for having me. And thank you, Gina. For the job. Open Block. Two days a week. My commute is eighteen minutes now. I cried on the subway. Happy tears.
GINA: You were the best designer at Brightline. The agents never replaced you. They just made worse mockups.
LAUREN: I heard. They hired two humans back in April. Craig called me. I said no.
SAM: Good for you. Okay. The two names. On the table. Option one: "The Corner House Co-op." Option two: Eddie's idea.
EDDIE: "Roode Hoek Co-op." R-O-O-D-E. H-O-E-K.
MARISOL: Eddie, nobody can pronounce that. The mail carrier will think it's a typo.
EDDIE: Then the mail carrier will learn something. That's what signs are for.
SAM: "The Corner House" is clear. Everybody already calls it that. Even the news article.
GINA: That's the argument for A. People know it.
EDDIE: People know it. But nobody chose it. A reporter named it. Last January. In a headline.
MARISOL: That's true. "Boiler War at the Corner House." That was the first article.
EDDIE: I don't want our name to come from trouble. I want it to come from the ground. From the soil.
SAM: From the soil. Okay. You have my attention.
SAM: What does it mean? Before we vote. I need data.
EDDIE: I'll tell you tonight. At the unveiling. If it wins. If it loses, I'll tell you anyway. But sadder.
LAUREN: Let me show you the designs first. Then decide. Design A. "The Corner House Co-op." Simple. Clean. Black letters on brass.
GINA: It's nice. It's clear. Everybody knows what it means.
LAUREN: Design B. "Roode Hoek Co-op." Same brass. But with a small drawing. A hook of land. Sticking into the harbor. Like the map from sixteen hundred.
MARISOL: Oh. That's beautiful. Is that the real shape? Of Red Hook?
LAUREN: Eddie gave me an old map. From the historical society. The land used to curve like that. Like a hook. Before the piers. Before they filled the marshes.
SAM: Design C?
LAUREN: Design C. Both names. "Roode Hoek Co-op" on top. And smaller, underneath: "The Corner House. Owned by the people who live in it."
EDDIE: "Owned by the people who live in it." That's Sal's letter.
LAUREN: Gina told me. I thought it belonged on the door.
SAM: Okay. I'm convinced. Data or no data. I vote C.
GINA: C. Absolutely C.
MARISOL: C. Eddie?
EDDIE: C. Both names. The old one and the true one. Lauren, you're a good designer. And a better listener.
MARISOL: Dolores and Priya voted C by text this morning. So it's unanimous. Design C.
LAUREN: Then I'll send the file to the engraver. He promised by two o'clock. Brass. With the little hook of land.

[Scene: 2:30 p.m. The front door of the building, next to The Hook. Marcus stands on a ladder with a drill. Luis, in a brand-new Bell Bikes T-shirt, holds the ladder. Tommy and Diego watch. The new brass sign leans against the wall, covered with a cloth.]

LUIS: Marcus, a little to the left. No. Your left. My right. Okay. Stop. Perfect.
MARCUS: Is it level? Diego, check the level.
DIEGO: The bubble is in the middle! It's level! Perfectly level!
TOMMY: Unlike the "K" on my sign. Gina painted that in two thousand six. Twenty-one years crooked.
MARCUS: Want me to fix it? While I'm up here?
TOMMY: Touch that K and you're fired from the building.
MARCUS: You can't fire me. You're forty-nine percent of a bar. And one fifth of a building.
TOMMY: Then I'll ask Gina to fire you. She has fifty-one percent.
LUIS: Marcus, first day. Am I doing okay?
MARCUS: Luis, you've done six battery checks, two brake repairs, and now you're holding a ladder for a historic sign. It's two thirty. You're doing great.
LUIS: Six battery checks. One of them was bad. Swollen. I put it in the fire cabinet. With sand.
DIEGO: You smelled it? Like you said in the interview?
LUIS: I smelled it from the sidewalk. Burnt plastic. And sweet. The delivery guy said, "How did you know?" I said, "Experience."
TOMMY: And this morning? Was it strange? Getting a paycheck instead of an app?
LUIS: I haven't gotten a paycheck yet. That's Friday. But this morning, my daughter asked me something. She said, "Papi, what time do you finish?" I said, "Six o'clock." I knew the answer. For the first time in six years.
MARCUS: Six o'clock. And no rain delays. Okay. The bracket is in. Two screws. Strong.
TOMMY: What's under the cloth? Can I peek?
DIEGO: No! Nobody peeks! Mrs. Esposito's orders! The unveiling is at six!
TOMMY: The youth president is very strict.
DIEGO: I learned from the best. And from the bylaws.

[Scene: 5:40 p.m. The sidewalk before the unveiling. Folding chairs. Teresa Cappello arrives carrying a long, flat package wrapped in brown paper. Marisol, Sam, Mr. Hayes, and Diego set up chairs.]

TERESA: Marisol! I made it. Traffic on the Verrazzano. As always.
MARISOL: Teresa! You came! What's that?
TERESA: A gift. From Dad. He had a good morning. He said, "Take the old sign. From your garage. Give it to the corner."
SAM: What old sign?
TERESA: Look. "CAPPELLO HARDWARE. Est. 1970." The original. It hung over this door before the bar. Before Rizzo rented it. Dad kept it in my garage. For twenty years.
MR. HAYES: I remember that sign! From the B61! Nineteen ninety-three. It was faded, but still there. Over the corner.
DIEGO: Before The Hook? There was a hardware store here?
TERESA: Your building was a hardware store. Downstairs. Nails, paint, hammers. My dad sold them. He knew every customer's name.
MARISOL: Like a president. Like Walter.
MR. HAYES: Like a bus driver. You learn the names.
SAM: Where do we put it? The new sign is going over the door.
TERESA: Inside. Somewhere. Dad said, "Not outside. My name doesn't belong outside anymore. Theirs does."
DIEGO: "Theirs does." That means us.
TERESA: That means you. All of you.

[Scene: 6:00 p.m. The sidewalk in front of the building. About fifty neighbors gather. The sign hangs over the front door, still covered with the cloth. Mrs. Esposito holds the cord. Nadia stands beside her. Eddie stands in front of the door, leaning on his cane.]

MRS. ESPOSITO: Everybody, quiet please! Thank you! As secretary of the co-op, I've been asked to say one sentence. Here it is. On June first, we hang our own name on our own door.
NADIA: And as a member of the land trust, I'll say one sentence too. This land is safe now. For ninety-nine years. Under this sign.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And now, Eddie. Our oldest member. Share number one. He'll explain the name. Eddie?
EDDIE: Thank you, Dolores. Can everybody hear me? My voice is old. But it still works.
EDDIE: Before I explain, let me ask. Who here knows what Red Hook was called four hundred years ago?
MRS. ESPOSITO: The Dutch. Diego told me all week. Ms. Park taught them. In the sixteen hundreds.
EDDIE: Ms. Park is right. But before the Dutch, the Lenape people lived here. For thousands of years. This was their land first. Let's remember that.
EDDIE: Then, in sixteen thirty-six, the Dutch came. They had a word for this place. Two words. "Roode Hoek."
NADIA: Roode Hoek. How do you say it correctly?
EDDIE: "ROH-duh HOOK." The first word, "roode," means red. Because of the soil. The clay here was red. Red earth.
EDDIE: The second word, "hoek," means a point. A piece of land that sticks out into the water. A hook. But in Dutch, "hoek" also means something else.
NADIA: What else?
EDDIE: A corner. "Hoek" means corner.
MRS. ESPOSITO: A corner. Red Corner.
EDDIE: Red Corner. Over the years, the English speakers heard "Roode Hoek" and said "Red Hook." The name stayed. The meaning was lost. Nobody remembered it meant corner.
EDDIE: And now. Tommy, Gina, come here. In two thousand six, Angela Russo named a bar on this corner. "The Hook." Because, she said, "People get hooked on this corner."
GINA: That's true. She said it to everybody.
EDDIE: Angela didn't know Dutch. She never read an old map. But she named this place by its oldest name. Hoek. The corner. Four hundred years later. By accident.
TOMMY: ...By accident. She did it by accident.
EDDIE: Maybe by accident. Maybe not. Peggy always said, "Angela knows things she doesn't know she knows."
GINA: That's... that's exactly Mom. Exactly.
EDDIE: So. This building is the Corner House. And the bar is the Hook. And the Hook means corner. And the corner is ours. Dolores, pull the cord.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Pulling the cord. One, two, three!
NADIA: Look! It's beautiful! "Roode Hoek Co-op." And the little hook of land!
MRS. ESPOSITO: And underneath. Read it, Eddie. Read it out loud.
EDDIE: "The Corner House. Owned by the people who live in it."
NADIA: That's the best sign in Brooklyn. And I've seen a lot of signs.

[Scene: 9:30 p.m. The sidewalk. The crowd has gone. The streetlight shines on the brass sign. Next to it, the old wooden sign of The Hook, with its crooked K. Tommy and Gina stand together, looking up.]

TOMMY: Look at them. Side by side. The new one and the old one.
GINA: The new one is brass. Professional. Level. Lauren is a genius.
TOMMY: And the old one is wood. Crooked. Peeling. Painted by a fourteen-year-old who wanted to go to the mall.
GINA: I did want to go to the mall. Mom made me paint the sign first. She said, "You'll thank me in twenty years."
TOMMY: Twenty-one years. Do you thank her?
GINA: I thank her. Every day. Especially today. Dad, did you know? About "hoek"? Before tonight?
TOMMY: No. Never. I thought it was just a joke. People get hooked. Like fish. Eddie's always had the old maps. He never told me.
GINA: Maybe he was waiting. For the right day. For a sign to explain.
TOMMY: Eddie waits for everything. He waited fifty-nine years to fish on that pier.
GINA: And Sal's old sign. From Teresa. Where do we hang it?
TOMMY: Inside. Over the register. Next to Sal's cash register. Next to Mom's calamari card. Under Walter's bus map.
GINA: The bar is a museum now. Dolores was right.
TOMMY: A museum where you can drink. The best kind.
GINA: And the coffee's finally good.
TOMMY: Don't push it.
GINA: And the first fish went to Diego. Like the corner went to us.
TOMMY: Gina. Can I ask you something? As your partner? Forty-nine percent?
GINA: Ask.
TOMMY: Should we fix the K? On the old sign? Now that there's a perfect new sign next to it?
GINA: Never. The crooked K stays. It's the only thing on this corner that's never changed.
TOMMY: Except your mother's lemon rule. And Eddie's coffee order. And Dolores's clipboard.
GINA: Okay. Four things. Four things never change. The rest can.
TOMMY: Four things. That's a good number. It's like rule number four. Never sign alone.
GINA: Never sign alone. Never fix the K.
TOMMY: Goodnight, partner. Tomorrow's Wednesday. I'm working. You're at Open Block.
GINA: And next Tuesday? You're off. With Luca. On the pier.
TOMMY: A regular Tuesday. I haven't had a regular Tuesday in twenty-one years.
GINA: Then it's time. Goodnight, Dad.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **상징 서술**
  - *It stands for… / It represents…* — "~을 상징한다."
  - *What it means to us is…* — "우리에게 그것이 의미하는 건…"
  - *On June first, we hang our own name on our own door.* — 부인의 한 문장. 소유의 상징.
- **어원 이야기**
  - *The name comes from…* — "그 이름은 ~에서 왔다."
  - *Originally, it meant…* — "원래는 ~라는 뜻이었다."
  - *Over the years, … The name stayed. The meaning was lost.* — "세월이 흐르며… 이름은 남고 뜻은 잊혔다."
  - *Roode Hoek* [로더 훅] — 네덜란드어 '붉은 곶·붉은 모퉁이'. *roode* = red(붉은), *hoek* = point / corner(곶, 모퉁이).
  - 뉴욕의 네덜란드 지명: *Brooklyn* ← Breukelen / *Harlem* ← Haarlem.
- **Lenape** — 레나페. 네덜란드인 이전부터 이 땅에 살던 원주민.
- **Angela knows things she doesn't know she knows.** — "앤젤라는 자기가 안다는 걸 모르는 것들을 알아." 페기의 말.
- **Touch that K and you're fired from the building.** — 토미식 협박 농담.
- **I knew the answer. For the first time in six years.** — 루이스. 퇴근 시간을 알게 된 기쁨.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- **Roode Hoek**는 에디처럼 천천히: [ROH-duh] [HOOK]. 첫 단어의 *ROH*에 강세.
- 에디의 어원 설명은 수업처럼 한 단계씩: *The first word, | "roode," | means red.* / *The second word, | "hoek," | means a point.*
- *A corner. | "Hoek" means corner.* — 이 화의 반전. 멈추고 천천히.
- 토미의 *...By accident.*는 거의 속삭임.
- 제막 순간의 *One, two, three!*와 디에고의 환호는 크게. 현판 문구는 에디가 또박또박: *"Owned by the people who live in it."*
- 마지막 부녀의 대화는 밤의 낮은 목소리. *Never sign alone. | Never fix the K.*
