# THE CORNER HOUSE — 5부 82화

## The Night Before the Stoop Sale (스툽 세일 전날 밤)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 5부 10화(통산 82화) · 시기: 2027년 5월 21일(금) 저녁~밤 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 지하실(물건 정리) → 더 훅(가격표 붙이기) → 밤의 스툽
> - 등장인물: MRS. ESPOSITO, DIEGO, TOMMY, MR. HAYES, MARISOL, GINA, TASHA, JESSE, EDDIE
> - 사건: 두 번째 스툽 세일 전날. 1년 전 12화와 똑같은 노란 가격 스티커가 등장한다. 그런데 이번에는 지하실에 하버라인 시절의 잔해와 살의 오래된 물건이 가득하다. 낡은 보일러 부품, 리치가 버리고 간 가구, 살의 금전 등록기. 헤이스 씨는 중복된 버스 지도를 내놓고, 토미는 이번엔 아무것도 팔지 않겠다고 한다. 작년 판매 수익 절반은 디에고의 자전거 저금이었다. 올해 디에고는 수익 절반을 글로리아의 팬트리 달걀값에 보태겠다고 한다. 밤 스툽에서 디에고는 화요일에 올 중학교 결과 때문에 잠들지 못한다. 에디가 작년 이맘때 이야기를 들려준다.
> - 학습 포인트: 비교 표현(Last year, we… This year, we… / more than / less than / as… as… / not as… as… / the same as… / different from…), 1부 회상, 설렘과 긴장의 표현
> - 미국 현안: 지역 중고 거래와 재사용 문화, 공동체 기부, 중학교 배정 발표를 기다리는 아이
> - 연결: 12·13화 첫 스툽 세일(9~5시, 파이어니어~비지테이션 통제, 노란 스티커, 디에고 자전거 저금, 앤젤라 램프 5달러를 조이가 사 감, 부인 접시는 팔지 않고 옥상 만찬). 81화 부인 마지막 회장, 토미 사회자. 79화 헤이스 버스 지도 수집. 58화 팬트리 달걀 부족. 84화 결과 발표(화요일).

## 대본

[Scene: Friday, May 21, 6:00 p.m. The basement. Boxes, old chairs, a dusty cash register. The new boiler hums quietly. Mrs. Esposito sits at a folding table with a roll of yellow stickers. Diego, Tommy, Mr. Hayes, and Marisol carry things into the light.]

MRS. ESPOSITO: Okay. Same rules as last year. Yellow sticker. Price in black marker. Round numbers only. No cents. Nobody has cents anymore.
DIEGO: Same stickers! The exact same yellow! Mrs. Esposito, are these from last year?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Same roll. I bought a thousand stickers in two thousand nineteen. I've used about four hundred. They'll outlive me.
TOMMY: Don't say that, Dolores. Not the night before your last sale.
MRS. ESPOSITO: It's my last sale as president, Tommy. Not my last sale as a human. I'll still sell things. I'll just complain less officially.
MARISOL: Okay. What do we have? Last year it was mostly closets. This year it's mostly the basement.
TOMMY: This year, it's mostly Harborline's garbage. Look. Three office chairs. From the construction trailer. They left them.
MARISOL: Are they ours now?
TOMMY: Brian says yes. Anything left in the building at the closing belongs to the co-op. It's in the contract.
DIEGO: Then we sell Harborline's chairs! Five dollars each!
MRS. ESPOSITO: Ten. They're better chairs than mine. Harborline had money. Before it didn't.
MR. HAYES: And here's my box. Bus maps. Duplicates. Ninety-five maps. From nineteen ninety-three to twenty nineteen. I have two of each.
DIEGO: Who would buy an old bus map?
MR. HAYES: You'd be surprised, young man. Collectors. Artists. People who grew up here and moved away. They want to see their old route.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Two dollars each. Or five for three dollars? No. Too complicated. Two dollars each.
MARISOL: And this? This big metal thing? It's so heavy.
TOMMY: That's Sal's cash register. From the hardware store days. Nineteen seventy. Before my bar. Before Rizzo's. It was in a corner. Under a tarp.
MR. HAYES: Does it work?
TOMMY: Let me try. Push this. Pull that... Ding!
DIEGO: It dinged! It still works! It's like a robot from the past!
MRS. ESPOSITO: We don't sell that. That's history. That goes in the bar.
TOMMY: In the bar? Where?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Next to Angela's calamari card. Under Walter's bus map. The bar is becoming a museum, Tommy. Accept it.
TOMMY: A museum with bad coffee. Okay. The register stays.
MARISOL: Tommy, are you selling anything this year? Last year you sold Angela's lamp.
TOMMY: For five dollars. "As is." It didn't work. Zoe bought it. Then she fixed it and brought it back. It's on the shelf behind the bar now. It works better than I do.
MARISOL: So this year?
TOMMY: This year, nothing. Last year, I was getting ready to lose everything. So I was letting things go. This year, I'm keeping everything. Even the jukebox stain on the floor.
MRS. ESPOSITO: That's growth, Tommy. Or hoarding. One of those.
MARISOL: Diego, and you? Last year, half your money went to the bike fund. This year?
DIEGO: This year, half goes to Gloria. For eggs. At the pantry.
MRS. ESPOSITO: For eggs? Not for you?
DIEGO: I have a bike. It works. Marcus checks it every Saturday. The pantry had no eggs in December. I counted the people. Forty-two.
MR. HAYES: I was one of those people. In that line.
DIEGO: I know. You got whole wheat. I remember.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Diego Reyes. That's the most presidential thing anyone has said in this basement.

[Scene: 8:00 p.m. The Hook. Tables covered with things to sell. Gina, Tasha, and Jesse put stickers on items. Eddie supervises from his stool. Tommy pours drinks.]

GINA: Okay. Sticker check. Jesse, why does this teapot say "priceless"?
JESSE: Because it's a joke. Technically, it has no price.
GINA: Then nobody can buy it. Write a number.
JESSE: Fine. Three dollars. It's a three-dollar teapot with a priceless personality.
TASHA: Last year, you had a lemonade stand. Remember? Thirty-two cups. Sixty-four dollars.
JESSE: And a profit of about thirty dollars. I was so proud. I was unemployed. Well, I was employed. But I felt unemployed.
TASHA: And this year?
JESSE: This year, I'm employed. Really employed. Open Block. Six months. I'm too busy for lemonade. I'm doing a text-message system. For the sale. "Your table is ready. Your bargain is waiting."
GINA: You built a system for a stoop sale?
JESSE: It took twenty minutes. I'm not as nervous as I was last year. And I'm a lot more useful.
EDDIE: Last year, you were a lot more confused too. You sold my old radio for four dollars. Without asking.
JESSE: I did! I'm sorry! I thought it was trash!
EDDIE: It was trash. But it was my trash. Mrs. Esposito bought it back for me. For five dollars. She made a profit.
TASHA: Dolores, the businesswoman.
GINA: Okay. Comparisons. Let's do it properly. Last year, how many tables did we have?
EDDIE: Six. Six tables. On the sidewalk. And Diego's sneaker table.
GINA: This year, we have eleven. Walter's maps. Harborline's chairs. Marcus's old tires. Priya's mother's sari collection. And Sam's spreadsheets.
TASHA: Sam is selling spreadsheets?
GINA: Printed ones. Framed. "The spreadsheet that bought a building." Ten dollars each. Signed.
TOMMY: Who would buy that?
JESSE: Me. I'd buy that. It's a historical document.
EDDIE: Last year, nobody here knew what a co-op was. This year, people want to frame a spreadsheet. The world is different.
TASHA: Is it better? Or just different?
EDDIE: Both. It's better than last year. And it's not as scary as last year. Last year, the sale was the day before the note. Remember? The note under Tommy's door. May thirty-first.
TOMMY: I remember. I found it the morning after the party. Not after the sale. But close. I threw up in the sink.
GINA: And this year?
TOMMY: This year, there's a note on my door too. From Dolores. It says, "MC, seven a.m. Bring a lemon."
TASHA: That's a much better note.
TOMMY: It's the same handwriting as the minutes. But a much better note.
GINA: Dad, what will you say tomorrow? As MC? Did you write a speech?
TOMMY: A speech? I'm going to say, "Good morning, Red Hook. Buy stuff." That's the speech.
TASHA: That's not a speech. That's a sign.
JESSE: I can write you something. With jokes. Hidden jokes.
TOMMY: No hidden jokes. Dolores will cut them.
EDDIE: Tommy, say what Sal used to say. At the first sale. In nineteen seventy-eight.
TOMMY: What did Sal say?
EDDIE: "Everything on this block has a price. Except the people."

[Scene: 10:30 p.m. The stoop. The street is quiet. Tables are stacked under the green shed with the kids' mural. Diego sits on the top step in a hoodie. Eddie comes out slowly with his cane.]

EDDIE: Diego. It's ten thirty. Your mother is going to kill both of us.
DIEGO: I can't sleep. I'm thinking about Tuesday.
EDDIE: The letter. The middle school.
DIEGO: The robot school. Near the canal. Malik already knows his. His came early. He got his first choice.
EDDIE: Good for Malik. And yours comes Tuesday.
DIEGO: What if I don't get it? Ms. Park says it's very competitive. Two hundred kids applied. For one hundred places.
EDDIE: Then you go to your second choice. The soccer school. Two buses. You'll be very fit.
DIEGO: Eddie! That's not funny!
EDDIE: It's a little funny. Okay. Listen. Can I tell you something? About last year?
DIEGO: About the stoop sale?
EDDIE: About this exact night. One year ago. You sat on this exact step. In the same hoodie. You were worried about your bike fund.
DIEGO: I had forty-one dollars. I needed seventy. For the chain and the tires.
EDDIE: You were sure you'd never get there. "Eddie, I'll never fix the bike." You said it three times.
DIEGO: And then I sold my lucky sneakers. And you paid me eleven dollars for listening to your stories.
EDDIE: Best eleven dollars I ever spent. And then?
DIEGO: Then I fixed the bike. With Marcus. And rang the bell in front of your window. The biggest bell.
EDDIE: Exactly. Last year, you were as worried as you are now. Maybe more. And it worked out.
DIEGO: But a bike is not the same as a school. A school is bigger.
EDDIE: A school is bigger. You're bigger too. You're not the same kid as last year. Last year, you didn't know what a quorum was.
DIEGO: Nobody knew what a quorum was.
EDDIE: True. But you know now. You know what a land trust is. You know the price of a boiler. You signed a cover letter. Small. "Treasurer. In the future."
DIEGO: That's true. I'm more experienced now.
EDDIE: Much more. And whatever the letter says on Tuesday, you'll still be the same Diego. The runner. The one who counted forty-two people in the pantry line.
DIEGO: Eddie, what school did you go to? When you were eleven?
EDDIE: Saint Stephen's. On Summit Street. The nuns. Sister Agnes. I told you.
DIEGO: Did you like it?
EDDIE: I hated it. Until sixth grade. Then I had a teacher. Mr. Kelly. He taught me maps. Ships. Where coffee comes from. That's why I wanted to work on the docks.
DIEGO: So a teacher made you a longshoreman?
EDDIE: A teacher made me curious. The docks made me a longshoreman. The curious part is the important part.
DIEGO: Ms. Park makes me curious.
EDDIE: Then you already won, kid. Whatever school. You already have the curious part.
DIEGO: Okay. That helps. A little.
EDDIE: A little is good. A little is enough for one night.
DIEGO: Eddie, can I ask you something? Mrs. Esposito said she's choosing a new block president. A "he." Do you know who?
EDDIE: I have a guess. But she'll tell everyone tomorrow. After the sale.
DIEGO: Is it Tommy?
EDDIE: Tommy is the MC. Not the president.
DIEGO: Is it you?
EDDIE: I'm eighty-four. My only job is knocking on a floor.
DIEGO: Then who?
EDDIE: Somebody who knows every name. And every price. And every rule. Go to bed, Diego. You'll find out.
DIEGO: Eddie... do you think it's...
EDDIE: Bed. Now. Seven a.m. is early. Mrs. Esposito doesn't wait for anybody. Not even presidents.
DIEGO: Okay. Goodnight, Eddie. Thank you. For last year. And for this year.
EDDIE: Goodnight, kid. And Diego? This year, no sneakers. You keep those.
DIEGO: I already sold them. Last year. These are new.
EDDIE: Then keep the new ones. You'll need them. Presidents walk a lot.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **비교 표현**
  - *Last year, we had six tables. This year, we have eleven.* — 같은 날의 작년과 올해를 나란히 놓는 기본 비교.
  - *more than / less than* — *This year, it's more Harborline garbage than closets.*
  - *as worried as* — *Last year, you were as worried as you are now.* "지금만큼 걱정했어."
  - *not as nervous as* — *I'm not as nervous as I was last year.* "작년만큼 긴장하지 않아."
  - *the same as / not the same as* — *A bike is not the same as a school.*
  - *different from* — *You're not the same kid as last year.* / *The world is different.*
- **stoop sale 어휘** — *sticker* 스티커 / *price in black marker* 검은 마커로 쓴 가격 / *round numbers* 끝자리 없는 숫자 / *as is* 현 상태 그대로 / *bargain* 싼 물건, 득템
- **They'll outlive me.** — "나보다 오래 살 거야." 스티커가 너무 많다는 농담.
- **That's growth. Or hoarding. One of those.** — "그건 성장이야. 아니면 저장 강박이거나. 둘 중 하나."
- **a three-dollar teapot with a priceless personality** — "성격만큼은 값을 매길 수 없는 3달러짜리 찻주전자." 제시식 농담.
- **Presidents walk a lot.** — "회장은 많이 걸어." 에디의 힌트.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 12화와 대비해 읽는다. *Last year, … This year, …*의 대구에서 *last*와 *this*에 강세를 준다.
- 금전 등록기의 *Ding!*은 효과음처럼, 디에고의 반응은 크게.
- 바 장면은 일하면서 나누는 가벼운 대화. 리듬 빠르게.
- 마지막 스툽 장면은 밤의 속삭임. 에디의 *About this exact night. One year ago.*에서 속도를 늦춘다.
- 디에고의 *Eddie... do you think it's...* — 말을 다 하지 못하고 끊긴다. 에디는 바로 *Bed. Now.*로 덮는다.

에스포지토 부인: 좋아요. 작년이랑 같은 규칙이에요. 노란 스티커. 검은 마커로 가격. 딱 떨어지는 숫자만. 센트 없음. 요즘 아무도 센트 없어요.
디에고: 같은 스티커다! 똑같은 노란색! 에스포지토 할머니, 이거 작년 거예요?
에스포지토 부인: 같은 롤이야. 2019년에 스티커 천 장을 샀어. 400장쯤 썼고. 나보다 오래 살 거야.
토미: 그런 말 하지 마요, 돌로레스. 마지막 세일 전날 밤에요.
에스포지토 부인: 회장으로서 마지막 세일이에요, 토미. 인간으로서 마지막 세일이 아니고요. 계속 팔 거예요. 덜 공식적으로 불평할 뿐이죠.
마리솔: 좋아요. 뭐가 있어요? 작년엔 거의 옷장 물건이었잖아요. 올해는 거의 지하실 물건이네요.
토미: 올해는 거의 하버라인 쓰레기야. 봐. 사무용 의자 세 개. 공사 컨테이너에서 나온 거. 두고 갔어.
마리솔: 그게 이제 우리 거예요?
토미: 브라이언 말로는 그래. 거래 마감 때 건물에 남아 있던 건 다 조합 거래. 계약서에 있대.
디에고: 그럼 하버라인 의자를 팔아요! 하나에 5달러!
에스포지토 부인: 10달러. 내 의자보다 좋은 의자야. 하버라인은 돈이 있었거든. 없어지기 전까지는.
헤이스 씨: 그리고 이건 제 상자예요. 버스 지도요. 중복된 거요. 지도 95장이요. 1993년부터 2019년까지요. 다 두 장씩 있어요.
디에고: 누가 옛날 버스 지도를 사요?
헤이스 씨: 놀랄걸, 꼬마 신사. 수집가들. 예술가들. 여기서 자라서 이사 간 사람들. 옛날 노선을 보고 싶어 하지.
에스포지토 부인: 한 장에 2달러. 아니면 다섯 장에 3달러? 아니다. 너무 복잡해. 한 장에 2달러.
마리솔: 그럼 이건요? 이 커다란 쇳덩이요? 엄청 무거워요.
토미: 그건 살의 금전 등록기야. 철물점 시절 거. 1970년. 내 바 전에. 리조네 전에. 구석에 있었어. 방수포 밑에.
헤이스 씨: 작동해요?
토미: 해 볼게. 이걸 누르고. 저걸 당기고… 딩!
디에고: 딩 했어요! 아직 작동해요! 과거에서 온 로봇 같아요!
에스포지토 부인: 그건 안 팔아요. 그건 역사예요. 그건 바로 가요.
토미: 바로? 어디에?
에스포지토 부인: 앤젤라 칼라마리 카드 옆에요. 월터 버스 지도 밑에요. 바가 박물관이 되고 있어요, 토미. 받아들여요.
토미: 커피 맛없는 박물관. 좋아요. 등록기는 남겨 두죠.
마리솔: 토미, 올해 파는 거 있어요? 작년엔 앤젤라 램프를 팔았잖아요.
토미: 5달러에. '있는 그대로'. 안 켜졌어. 조이가 샀지. 그러고는 고쳐서 다시 가져왔어. 지금 바 뒤 선반에 있어. 나보다 잘 돌아가.
마리솔: 그럼 올해는요?
토미: 올해는 아무것도. 작년엔 다 잃을 준비를 하고 있었어. 그래서 물건을 놓아주고 있었지. 올해는 다 지킬 거야. 바닥의 주크박스 자국까지.
에스포지토 부인: 그건 성장이네요, 토미. 아니면 저장 강박이거나요. 둘 중 하나예요.
마리솔: 디에고, 너는? 작년엔 번 돈 절반이 자전거 저금으로 갔잖아. 올해는?
디에고: 올해는 절반을 글로리아한테 줄 거예요. 달걀 사라고요. 팬트리에요.
에스포지토 부인: 달걀? 너 쓰려는 게 아니고?
디에고: 전 자전거 있어요. 잘 굴러가요. 마커스 형이 토요일마다 봐 줘요. 12월에 팬트리에 달걀이 없었잖아요. 사람들 세어 봤어요. 마흔두 명이요.

에디: 작년엔 훨씬 정신도 없었지. 내 옛날 라디오를 4달러에 팔았잖아. 묻지도 않고.
제시: 그랬죠! 죄송해요! 쓰레기인 줄 알았어요!
에디: 쓰레기였지. 그래도 내 쓰레기였어. 에스포지토 부인이 5달러에 도로 사다 줬어. 이익을 남겼지.

지나: 그럼 올해는요?
토미: 올해도 내 문에 쪽지가 있어. 돌로레스한테서. "사회자, 아침 7시. 레몬 지참."
타샤: 훨씬 좋은 쪽지네요.
토미: 회의록이랑 같은 글씨야. 그래도 훨씬 좋은 쪽지지.
지나: 아빠, 내일 뭐라고 할 거예요? 사회자로서요? 연설문 썼어요?
토미: 연설문? "좋은 아침입니다, 레드훅. 물건 사세요." 그게 연설이야.
타샤: 그건 연설이 아니라 간판이죠.
제시: 제가 써 드릴 수 있어요. 농담 넣어서요. 숨은 농담이요.
토미: 숨은 농담 금지. 돌로레스가 다 자를 거야.
에디: 토미, 살이 하던 말을 해. 첫 세일 때. 1978년에.
토미: 살이 뭐라고 했는데요?
에디: "이 블록의 모든 것엔 값이 있다. 사람만 빼고."
에디: 디에고. 10시 반이야. 네 엄마가 우리 둘 다 죽일 거야.
디에고: 잠이 안 와요. 화요일 생각을 하고 있어요.
에디: 편지. 중학교.
디에고: 로봇 학교요. 운하 근처요. 말릭은 벌써 알아요. 일찍 왔대요. 1지망 붙었대요.
에디: 잘됐네, 말릭. 그리고 네 건 화요일에 오고.
디에고: 안 되면 어떡해요? 박 선생님이 경쟁이 엄청 세대요. 100자리에 200명이 지원했대요.
에디: 그럼 2지망 가는 거지. 축구 학교. 버스 두 번. 몸이 아주 튼튼해질 거야.
디에고: 에디! 안 웃겨요!
에디: 조금은 웃기지. 좋아. 들어 봐. 하나 얘기해 줘도 돼? 작년 얘기?
디에고: 스툽 세일 얘기요?
에디: 바로 이날 밤 얘기. 1년 전. 넌 바로 이 계단에 앉아 있었어. 같은 후드 티를 입고. 자전거 저금을 걱정하고 있었지.
디에고: 41달러 있었어요. 70달러가 필요했어요. 체인이랑 타이어 때문에요.

디에고: 그러다 행운의 운동화를 팔았어요. 그리고 에디가 이야기 들어 준 값으로 11달러 줬고요.

디에고: 그다음엔 자전거를 고쳤어요. 마커스 형이랑요. 그리고 에디 창문 앞에서 벨을 울렸어요. 제일 큰 벨이요.
