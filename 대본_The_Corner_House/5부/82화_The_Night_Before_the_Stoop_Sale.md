# THE CORNER HOUSE — 5부 82화

## The Night Before the Stoop Sale (스툽 세일 전날 밤)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 5부 10화(통산 82화) · 시기: 2027년 5월 21일(금) 저녁~밤 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 지하실(물건 정리) → 더 훅(가격표 붙이기) → 밤의 스툽
> - 등장인물: MRS. ESPOSITO, DIEGO, TOMMY, MR. HAYES, MARISOL, GINA, TASHA, JESSE, EDDIE
> - 사건: 두 번째 스툽 세일 전날. 1년 전 12화와 똑같은 노란 가격 스티커가 등장한다. 그런데 이번에는 지하실에 하버라인 시절의 잔해와 살의 오래된 물건이 가득하다. 낡은 보일러 부품, 리치가 버리고 간 가구, 살의 금전 등록기. 헤이스 씨는 중복된 버스 지도를 내놓고, 토미는 이번엔 아무것도 팔지 않겠다고 한다. 작년 판매 수익 절반은 디에고의 자전거 저금이었다. 올해 디에고는 수익 절반을 글로리아의 팬트리 달걀값에 보태겠다고 한다. 밤 스툽에서 디에고는 화요일에 올 중학교 결과 때문에 잠들지 못한다. 에디가 작년 이맘때 이야기를 들려준다.
> - 학습 포인트: 비교 표현(Last year, we… This year, we… / more than / less than / as… as… / not as… as… / the same as… / different from…), 1부 회상, 설렘과 긴장의 표현
> - 미국 현안: 지역 중고 거래와 재사용 문화, 공동체 기부, 중학교 배정 발표를 기다리는 아이
> - 연결: 12·13화 첫 스툽 세일(9~5시, 파이어니어~비지테이션 통제, 노란 스티커, 디에고 자전거 저금, 앤젤라 램프 5달러를 조이가 사 감, 부인 접시는 팔지 않고 옥상 만찬). 81화 부인 마지막 회장, 토미 사회자. 79화 헤이스 버스 지도 수집. 58화 팬트리 달걀 부족. 84화 결과 발표(화요일).

## 대본

[Scene: Friday, May 21, 6:00 p.m. The basement. Boxes, old chairs, a dusty cash register. The new boiler hums quietly. Mrs. Esposito sits at a folding table with a roll of yellow stickers. Diego, Tommy, Mr. Hayes, and Marisol carry things into the light.]

MRS. ESPOSITO: Okay, same rules as last year. Yellow sticker, price in black marker, and round numbers only. No cents, because nobody carries cents anymore.
DIEGO: Same stickers! The exact same yellow! Mrs. Esposito, are these really the ones from last year?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Same roll. I bought a thousand stickers in two thousand nineteen, and I've used about four hundred. They'll outlive me.
TOMMY: Don't say that, Dolores. Not tonight, not the night before your last sale.
MRS. ESPOSITO: It's my last sale as president, Tommy, not my last sale as a human. I'll still sell things. I'll just complain less officially.
MARISOL: Okay, so what do we have? Last year it was mostly stuff from our closets, but this year it's mostly the basement.
TOMMY: This year, it's mostly Harborline's garbage. Look at this, three office chairs from the construction trailer. They just left them here.
MARISOL: Are they ours now? Can we really just sell them?
TOMMY: Brian says yes. Anything that was left in the building at the closing belongs to the co-op, and it's right there in the contract.
DIEGO: Then we sell Harborline's chairs! Five dollars each! That's fifteen dollars for eggs already!
MRS. ESPOSITO: Ten. They're better chairs than mine, because Harborline had money. Before it didn't.
MR. HAYES: And here's my box. Bus maps, all duplicates, ninety-five of them, from nineteen ninety-three to two thousand nineteen. I have two of each, so these are the extras.
DIEGO: Who would buy an old bus map, though?
MR. HAYES: You'd be surprised, young man. Collectors, artists, and people who grew up here and moved away. They want to see their old route again, the one that took them to school.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Two dollars each. Or five for three dollars? No, that's too complicated, and somebody will argue. Two dollars each.
MARISOL: And what about this big metal thing? I can barely lift it, it's so heavy.
TOMMY: That's Sal's cash register, from the hardware store days, nineteen seventy. That's before my bar and before Rizzo's. It was sitting in a corner under a tarp.
MR. HAYES: Does it still work, after all these years?
TOMMY: Let me try. You push this, and you pull that... Ding!
DIEGO: It dinged! It still works! It's like a robot from the past!
MRS. ESPOSITO: We don't sell that, because that's history. That goes in the bar.
TOMMY: In the bar? Where am I supposed to put it?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Next to Angela's calamari card, under Walter's bus map. The bar is becoming a museum, Tommy, so you might as well accept it.
TOMMY: A museum with bad coffee. Okay, fine, the register stays, but I'm not dusting it.
MARISOL: Tommy, are you selling anything this year? I remember that last year you sold Angela's lamp.
TOMMY: For five dollars, "as is," because it didn't work. Zoe bought it, and then she fixed it and brought it back. Now it's on the shelf behind the bar, and it works better than I do.
MARISOL: So what about this year? Anything at all?
TOMMY: This year, nothing. Last year, I was getting ready to lose everything, so I was letting things go. This year, I'm keeping everything, even the jukebox stain on the floor.
MRS. ESPOSITO: That's growth, Tommy. Or hoarding. One of those.
MARISOL: Diego, what about you? Last year, half your money went to the bike fund. What's it going to be this year?
DIEGO: This year, half goes to Gloria, so she can buy eggs for the pantry.
MRS. ESPOSITO: For eggs? Not for you? You're sure about that?
DIEGO: I already have a bike, and it works, because Marcus checks it every Saturday. But the pantry had no eggs in December. I counted the people in line, and there were forty-two.
MR. HAYES: I was one of those people, standing in that line.
DIEGO: I know. You got the whole wheat bread instead. I remember because you said thank you twice.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Diego Reyes, that is the most presidential thing anyone has ever said in this basement.

[Scene: 8:00 p.m. The Hook. Tables covered with things to sell. Gina, Tasha, and Jesse put stickers on items. Eddie supervises from his stool. Tommy pours drinks.]

GINA: Okay, sticker check. Jesse, why does this teapot say "priceless"?
JESSE: Because it's a joke. Technically, it has no price, so it's priceless.
GINA: Then nobody can buy it, can they? Write a real number.
JESSE: Fine, three dollars. It's a three-dollar teapot with a priceless personality.
TASHA: Last year, you had a lemonade stand, remember? You sold thirty-two cups for sixty-four dollars.
JESSE: And I made a profit of about thirty dollars, which made me so proud. I was unemployed back then. Well, I was employed, but I felt unemployed.
TASHA: And what about this year?
JESSE: This year, I'm employed, really employed, at Open Block for six months now. I'm too busy for lemonade, so I'm doing a text-message system for the sale. "Your table is ready. Your bargain is waiting."
GINA: Wait, you built a whole system for a stoop sale?
JESSE: It took twenty minutes, honestly. I'm not as nervous as I was last year, and I'm a lot more useful.
EDDIE: Last year, you were a lot more confused too. You sold my old radio for four dollars, and you didn't even ask me first.
JESSE: I did! I'm so sorry! I really thought it was trash!
EDDIE: It was trash, but it was my trash. Mrs. Esposito bought it back for me for five dollars, so she made a profit.
TASHA: Dolores, the businesswoman. Of course she made a profit.
GINA: Okay, comparisons, and let's do it properly. Last year, how many tables did we have?
EDDIE: Six tables on the sidewalk, plus Diego's sneaker table.
GINA: This year, we have eleven. There's Walter's maps, Harborline's chairs, Marcus's old tires, Priya's mother's sari collection, and Sam's spreadsheets.
TASHA: Sam is selling spreadsheets? Who does that?
GINA: Printed ones, in frames. It's called "The spreadsheet that bought a building," and they're ten dollars each, signed.
TOMMY: Who would actually pay money for that?
JESSE: Me. I'd buy that, because it's a historical document.
EDDIE: Last year, nobody here even knew what a co-op was. This year, people want to frame a spreadsheet. The world is different now.
TASHA: But is it better? Or is it just different?
EDDIE: Both. It's better than last year, and it's not as scary as last year. Last year, the sale was right before the note, remember? The note under Tommy's door, on May thirty-first.
TOMMY: I remember. I found it the morning after the party, not after the sale, but it was close. I threw up in the sink.
GINA: And what about this year?
TOMMY: This year, there's a note on my door too, from Dolores. It says, "MC, seven a.m. Bring a lemon."
TASHA: Well, that's a much better note.
TOMMY: It's the same handwriting as the minutes, but yeah, it's a much better note.
GINA: Dad, what are you going to say tomorrow as MC? Did you actually write a speech?
TOMMY: A speech? I'm going to say, "Good morning, Red Hook. Buy stuff." That's the whole speech.
TASHA: That's not a speech, Tommy. That's a sign.
JESSE: I can write you something, with jokes in it. Hidden jokes, so only the smart people laugh.
TOMMY: No hidden jokes, because Dolores will cut them all anyway.
EDDIE: Tommy, just say what Sal used to say at the very first sale, back in nineteen seventy-eight.
TOMMY: Why, what did Sal say?
EDDIE: "Everything on this block has a price. Except the people."

[Scene: 10:30 p.m. The stoop. The street is quiet. Tables are stacked under the green shed with the kids' mural. Diego sits on the top step in a hoodie. Eddie comes out slowly with his cane.]

EDDIE: Diego, it's ten thirty. If your mother finds you out here, she's going to kill both of us.
DIEGO: I can't sleep, because I keep thinking about Tuesday.
EDDIE: The letter, right? The middle school?
DIEGO: The robot school near the canal. Malik already knows where he's going, because his came early. He got his first choice.
EDDIE: Good for Malik. And yours comes on Tuesday, so that's only four more days.
DIEGO: But what if I don't get it? Ms. Park says it's very competitive, and two hundred kids applied for one hundred places.
EDDIE: Then you go to your second choice, the soccer school. It's two buses, so you'll be very fit.
DIEGO: Eddie! That's not funny!
EDDIE: It's a little funny. Okay, listen. Can I tell you something about last year?
DIEGO: About the stoop sale?
EDDIE: About this exact night, one year ago. You sat on this exact step, in the same hoodie, and you were worried about your bike fund.
DIEGO: I had forty-one dollars, but I needed seventy for the chain and the tires.
EDDIE: You were sure you'd never get there. "Eddie, I'll never fix the bike." You said it three times, and every time it got sadder.
DIEGO: And then I sold my lucky sneakers. And you paid me eleven dollars for listening to your stories.
EDDIE: Best eleven dollars I ever spent. And then what happened?
DIEGO: Then I fixed the bike with Marcus, and I rang the bell in front of your window. It was the biggest bell.
EDDIE: Exactly. Last year, you were as worried as you are now, maybe even more. And it worked out anyway.
DIEGO: But a bike is not the same as a school. A school is way bigger.
EDDIE: A school is bigger, sure, but you're bigger too. You're not the same kid as last year. Last year, you didn't even know what a quorum was.
DIEGO: Nobody knew what a quorum was. Even Tommy had to look it up.
EDDIE: True, but you know now. You know what a land trust is, and you know the price of a boiler. You even signed a cover letter, small, at the bottom. "Treasurer. In the future."
DIEGO: That's true. I guess I'm more experienced now.
EDDIE: Much more. And whatever the letter says on Tuesday, you'll still be the same Diego. The runner, the one who counted forty-two people in the pantry line.
DIEGO: Eddie, what school did you go to when you were eleven?
EDDIE: Saint Stephen's, on Summit Street, with the nuns. Sister Agnes was the scary one. I told you about her.
DIEGO: Did you like it there?
EDDIE: I hated it, until sixth grade. Then I had a teacher, Mr. Kelly, and he taught me maps, ships, and where coffee comes from. That's why I wanted to work on the docks.
DIEGO: So a teacher made you a longshoreman?
EDDIE: A teacher made me curious, and the docks made me a longshoreman. The curious part is the important part, kid.
DIEGO: Ms. Park makes me curious. She makes everything sound like a puzzle.
EDDIE: Then you already won, kid, whatever school it is. You already have the curious part, and nobody can take that away.
DIEGO: Okay. That helps. A little.
EDDIE: A little is good. A little is enough for one night.
DIEGO: Eddie, can I ask you something else? Mrs. Esposito said she's choosing a new block president, and it's a "he." Do you know who it is?
EDDIE: I have a guess. But she'll tell everyone tomorrow, after the sale, so you'll have to wait like the rest of us.
DIEGO: Is it Tommy?
EDDIE: Tommy is the MC, not the president. He's got enough jobs already.
DIEGO: Is it you, then?
EDDIE: I'm eighty-four years old. My only job around here is knocking on a floor.
DIEGO: Then who is it?
EDDIE: Somebody who knows every name, and every price, and every rule. Go to bed, Diego. You'll find out.
DIEGO: Eddie... do you think it's...
EDDIE: Bed. Now. Seven a.m. comes early, and Mrs. Esposito doesn't wait for anybody. Not even presidents.
DIEGO: Okay. Goodnight, Eddie, and thank you. For last year, and for this year too.
EDDIE: Goodnight, kid. And Diego? This year, no selling your sneakers. You keep those.
DIEGO: I already sold the lucky ones last year. These are new.
EDDIE: Then keep the new ones, because you'll need them. Presidents walk a lot.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **비교 표현**
  - *Last year, how many tables did we have?* / *This year, we have eleven.* — 같은 날의 작년과 올해를 나란히 놓는 기본 비교.
  - *more than / -er than* — *They're better chairs than mine* / *I'm a lot more useful.*
  - *as worried as* — *Last year, you were as worried as you are now, maybe even more.* "지금만큼 걱정했어."
  - *not as nervous as* — *I'm not as nervous as I was last year* "작년만큼 긴장하지 않아."
  - *the same as / not the same as* — *A bike is not the same as a school.*
  - *different from* — *You're not the same kid as last year.* / *The world is different now.*
- **stoop sale 어휘** — *sticker* 스티커 / *price in black marker* 검은 마커로 쓴 가격 / *round numbers* 끝자리 없는 숫자 / *as is* 현 상태 그대로 / *bargain* 싼 물건, 득템
- **They'll outlive me.** — "나보다 오래 살 거야." 스티커가 너무 많다는 농담.
- **That's growth. Or hoarding. One of those.** — "그건 성장이야. 아니면 저장 강박이거나. 둘 중 하나."
- **a three-dollar teapot with a priceless personality** — "성격만큼은 값을 매길 수 없는 3달러짜리 찻주전자." 제시식 농담.
- **Presidents walk a lot.** — "회장은 많이 걸어." 에디의 힌트.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 12화와 대비해 읽는다. *Last year, … This year, …*의 대구에서 *last*와 *this*에 강세를 준다.
- 금전 등록기의 *Ding!*은 효과음처럼, 디에고의 반응은 크게.
- 바 장면은 일하면서 나누는 가벼운 대화. 리듬 빠르게.
- 마지막 스툽 장면은 밤의 속삭임. 에디의 *About this exact night, one year ago.*에서 속도를 늦춘다.
- 디에고의 *Eddie... do you think it's...* — 말을 다 하지 못하고 끊긴다. 에디는 바로 *Bed. Now.*로 덮는다.

## 한글 해석

에스포지토 부인: 좋아요, 작년이랑 같은 규칙이에요. 노란 스티커, 검은 마커로 가격, 그리고 딱 떨어지는 숫자만요. 센트는 없어요. 요즘 아무도 센트를 안 갖고 다니니까요.
디에고: 같은 스티커다! 똑같은 노란색! 에스포지토 할머니, 이거 진짜 작년 그거예요?
에스포지토 부인: 같은 롤이야. 2019년에 스티커 천 장을 샀는데, 400장쯤 썼어. 나보다 오래 살 거야.
토미: 그런 말 하지 마요, 돌로레스. 오늘 밤은 안 돼요, 마지막 세일 전날 밤에는요.
에스포지토 부인: 회장으로서 마지막 세일이에요, 토미. 인간으로서 마지막 세일이 아니고요. 계속 팔 거예요. 덜 공식적으로 불평할 뿐이죠.
마리솔: 좋아요, 그럼 뭐가 있어요? 작년엔 거의 우리 옷장에서 나온 물건이었는데, 올해는 거의 지하실 물건이네요.
토미: 올해는 거의 하버라인 쓰레기야. 이거 봐, 공사 컨테이너에서 나온 사무용 의자 세 개. 그냥 여기 두고 갔어.
마리솔: 그게 이제 우리 거예요? 정말 그냥 팔아도 돼요?
토미: 브라이언 말로는 그래. 거래 마감 때 건물에 남아 있던 건 다 조합 거고, 계약서에 바로 나와 있대.
디에고: 그럼 하버라인 의자를 팔아요! 하나에 5달러! 벌써 달걀값 15달러예요!
에스포지토 부인: 10달러. 내 의자보다 좋은 의자야. 하버라인은 돈이 있었거든. 없어지기 전까지는.
헤이스 씨: 그리고 이건 제 상자예요. 버스 지도요, 전부 중복된 거고, 95장이에요. 1993년부터 2019년까지요. 다 두 장씩 있어서 이건 남는 거예요.
디에고: 근데 누가 옛날 버스 지도를 사요?
헤이스 씨: 놀랄걸, 꼬마 신사. 수집가들, 예술가들, 그리고 여기서 자라서 이사 간 사람들. 옛날 노선을 다시 보고 싶어 하지. 자기를 학교에 데려다주던 그 노선 말이야.
에스포지토 부인: 한 장에 2달러. 아니면 다섯 장에 3달러? 아니다, 너무 복잡해. 누군가 따질 거야. 한 장에 2달러.
마리솔: 그럼 이 커다란 쇳덩이는요? 너무 무거워서 겨우 들겠어요.
토미: 그건 살의 금전 등록기야. 철물점 시절 거, 1970년. 내 바보다도, 리조네보다도 전이지. 구석에 방수포 덮인 채로 있었어.
헤이스 씨: 그게 아직 작동해요? 이렇게 오래됐는데?
토미: 해 볼게. 이걸 누르고, 저걸 당기면… 딩!
디에고: 딩 했어요! 아직 작동해요! 과거에서 온 로봇 같아요!
에스포지토 부인: 그건 안 팔아요. 그건 역사니까요. 그건 바로 가요.
토미: 바로? 그걸 어디에 두라고요?
에스포지토 부인: 앤젤라 칼라마리 카드 옆에요, 월터 버스 지도 밑에요. 바가 박물관이 되고 있어요, 토미. 그러니 그냥 받아들여요.
토미: 커피 맛없는 박물관. 좋아요, 알았어요. 등록기는 남겨 두죠. 근데 먼지는 안 털 거예요.
마리솔: 토미, 올해 파는 거 있어요? 작년엔 앤젤라 램프를 판 걸로 기억하는데요.
토미: 5달러에, '있는 그대로'. 안 켜졌거든. 조이가 샀는데, 그러고는 고쳐서 다시 가져왔어. 지금 바 뒤 선반에 있는데, 나보다 잘 돌아가.
마리솔: 그럼 올해는요? 뭐라도요?
토미: 올해는 아무것도. 작년엔 다 잃을 준비를 하고 있어서 물건을 놓아주고 있었지. 올해는 다 지킬 거야. 바닥의 주크박스 자국까지.
