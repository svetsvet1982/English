# THE CORNER HOUSE — 5부 81화

## A Letter from Mrs. Esposito (에스포지토 부인의 편지)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 5부 9화(통산 81화) · 시기: 2027년 5월 20일(목) 밤 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 루이스 가족이 떠난 뒤의 더 훅 → 바의 끝자리 → 2B 문 앞
> - 등장인물: MRS. ESPOSITO, GINA, TOMMY, EDDIE, TASHA
> - 사건: 열흘 전 첫 이사회에서 부인은 토미에게 "리치가 한 짓처럼 보인다"고 말했다. 그 뒤로 둘은 어색하다. 부인은 타자기로 편지를 쳤지만 직접 읽을 용기가 없다. 그래서 지나에게 봉투를 건넨다. "네 아버지에게 읽어 줘." 지나가 바에서 소리 내어 읽는다. 1994년에 세상을 떠난 남편 카르미네, 혼자 이 건물에 들어온 1995년, 클립보드와 규칙이 그녀에게 무엇이었는지, 그리고 2020년 봄 토미가 매일 문 앞에 두고 간 장바구니 이야기가 나온다. 편지 끝에는 부탁이 하나 있다. 내일 스툽 세일의 사회를 맡아 달라는 것이다. 이번이 그녀가 주최하는 마지막 스툽 세일이다.
> - 학습 포인트: 편지체(I am writing to… / I owe you an apology. / I was wrong to say… / I will never forget… / Please accept… / With gratitude, / Yours truly,), 사과·감사 표현, 편지 낭독 톤
> - 미국 현안: 혼자 사는 노년, 팬데믹의 기억(2020년 봉쇄와 이웃의 돌봄), 이웃 간의 화해, 지역 공동체 리더십의 세대교체
> - 연결: 76화 '리치가 한 짓처럼 보일 거예요'. 12·13화 첫 스툽 세일(디에고 자전거 저금). 35화 부인의 옥상 만찬. 58화 부인 MTA 급여과 1978~2008. 53화 '두 번 두드리기'. 80화 루이스 가족 방문.

## 대본

[Scene: Thursday, May 20, 9:30 p.m. The Hook. Luis's family has just gone home. Tamale wrappers on the tables. Tasha stacks glasses. Tommy wipes the bar. Eddie sits on his stool. Mrs. Esposito comes in holding a white envelope and goes straight to Gina at the end of the bar.]

MRS. ESPOSITO: Gina, can I ask you a favor? It's a strange one, so you can say no.
GINA: Of course, Dolores. You know I'll do anything for you.
MRS. ESPOSITO: This is a letter for your father. I typed it on my typewriter, and it took me three drafts.
GINA: For Dad? Then why don't you just give it to him? He's right there, wiping the bar.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Because I can't read it out loud. My voice will shake, and I'll stop, and then I'll say something sharp to cover it. That's what I always do.
GINA: So you want me to read it to him, out loud?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Yes, please, right here. I'll sit next to Eddie and listen, and for once in my life, I won't interrupt.
EDDIE: That I'd like to see. Dolores Esposito, not interrupting anybody.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Quiet, Eddie. I'm practicing.
EDDIE: So what's in it? Can you give me a little preview, just between us?
MRS. ESPOSITO: No previews. You'll hear it at the same time as everybody else.
EDDIE: Is it about me?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Not everything is about you, Eddie. Only some things, and this isn't one of them.
GINA: Dad, can you come over here? Dolores wrote you a letter, and she wants me to read it.
TOMMY: A letter, to me? Am I in trouble? Is this about the shed again?
MRS. ESPOSITO: It's not about the shed, Tommy.
TASHA: Should I leave? It sounds private, and I can finish the glasses in the back.
MRS. ESPOSITO: No, Tasha, please stay. Everybody heard what I said at the meeting, so everybody should hear this too.
TOMMY: Okay, okay, I'm sitting down. Go ahead, Gina.

[Scene: 9:40 p.m. The same place. Gina unfolds two typed pages. Tommy sits on a stool facing her. Mrs. Esposito sits beside Eddie with her hands folded. Tasha leans on the bar.]

GINA: "Dear Tommy. I am writing to say something that I could not say at the board meeting. I owe you an apology."
GINA: "Ten days ago, you asked the board to give 3B to Gina and Luca. I said it would look like what Richie did, with family first and rules later."
GINA: "I was wrong to say that. I was not wrong about the rules, because the rules were right. But I was wrong to compare you to Richie. You are nothing like Richie."
TOMMY: ...
GINA: "Richie sold this building to strangers to pay his debts, while you fought for it with everything you had, even when you had nothing. You wanted to give your daughter a home. That is not corruption. That is love. I should have said that first."
TOMMY: Dolores...
MRS. ESPOSITO: Shh. Let her finish, because there's more. There are two whole pages.
GINA: "You may wonder why I care so much about rules, and why I carry a clipboard and count everything. I have never told anyone in this building the reason, not even Eddie."
EDDIE: That's true, she hasn't. I asked her once, back in two thousand five, and she changed the subject to the trash schedule.
GINA: "In nineteen ninety-four, my husband, Carmine, died very suddenly. He had a heart attack on the subway, on his way home from work. He was fifty-one."
TASHA: Oh, Dolores. I had no idea.
GINA: "We had an apartment in Bensonhurst, but the lease was in his name only. After he died, the landlord gave me thirty days and said, 'The lease is not yours.' I had a job at the MTA, but I had no home. I was forty-four."
GINA: "A woman from church told me about a building in Red Hook and a landlord named Sal. I came here in the rain in March nineteen ninety-five, with one suitcase and Carmine's watch."
GINA: "Sal looked at me for a long time, and then he said, 'You need a place, and I have a place. Two B. Pay me when you can.' I paid him on the first of every month for thirty-one years, and I was never late, not once."
EDDIE: Never late, that's right. Sal told me so himself. He said, "The widow in 2B pays before the bank opens."
GINA: "After that, I made a promise that nobody would ever tell me again that something was not mine. So I wrote everything down: every rule, every date, and every receipt. The clipboard was my armor, and it still is."
GINA: "Then, in March twenty twenty, the city closed, and everyone stayed inside. I was seventy, and I was alone with a cat. I was afraid to go to the store, because the news said people my age were dying."
GINA: "Every morning for three months, there was a bag at my door with eggs, milk, bread, cat food, and one lemon. Always one lemon. There was never a note, and I never saw who left it."
TOMMY: ...You knew?
GINA: "I found out in June, when I opened the door early and saw you running down the stairs. You had a mask on, and you were carrying four more bags: one for Eddie, one for Sal, one for the family in 4A, and one for yourself."
EDDIE: Tommy, that was you? The lemons? All this time, I thought it was Michael, ordering online from Florida.
TOMMY: Michael couldn't order a pizza online. It was me. Angela used to put a lemon in every bag for luck, so I just kept doing it.
GINA: "Tommy, I never thanked you, because I was too proud. I am thanking you now. I will never forget those bags. And I will never forget the lemon."
GINA: "Please accept my apology. Please also accept a favor. Tomorrow evening we set up the stoop sale, and Saturday is the sale itself. It will be my last one as president of the block association, because twenty years is enough."
TASHA: Your last one? Dolores, no!
GINA: "I would like you to be the master of ceremonies. You have a loud voice and a big heart, and those are the only two things the job needs."
GINA: "With gratitude, and with respect. Yours truly, Dolores Esposito, 2B. P.S. Bring a lemon."

[Scene: 9:55 p.m. The same place. Gina folds the letter. Nobody speaks for a moment. Then Tommy stands up and walks to the end of the bar.]

TOMMY: Dolores.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Please don't say anything sharp, because then I'll cry.
TOMMY: I'm not going to say anything sharp. I'm going to say thank you, and I'm going to say I'm sorry too.
MRS. ESPOSITO: You? What on earth are you sorry for?
TOMMY: For getting angry at the meeting, because you were right about the rules and about how it looked. I was being a father that night, not a board member.
MRS. ESPOSITO: You were being both, and that's allowed. It's just hard.
TOMMY: And I'm sorry about Carmine. It's been thirty-three years, and I never knew, because you never said a word.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I never said anything because nobody asked. And I never asked anyone to ask, so that's my fault.
EDDIE: It's nobody's fault, Dolores. You were busy running the block, writing the minutes, and saving all of us.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I wasn't saving anybody, Eddie. I was saving myself, with paper.
EDDIE: In the end, it's the same thing. You saved yourself, and the rest of us just came along.
TASHA: Dolores, can I ask you something? Carmine's watch, do you still have it?
MRS. ESPOSITO: It's in my nightstand. It stopped in two thousand three, and I never fixed it, so it says four seventeen forever.
TOMMY: Marcus could fix it, you know. He can fix anything with gears in it.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Maybe someday, but not yet. I like that it stopped, because it's honest.
GINA: Dolores, why is this your last stoop sale? You love it.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I do love it, and that's why I'm stopping while I still love it. I'm seventy-six, and my knees count the steps now, like Eddie's.
EDDIE: Welcome to the club. It's forty-two steps, and then you rest.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Besides, someone younger should run the block. It should be someone with a phone that isn't laminated.
TASHA: Who, Marisol? She's already running the whole building.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Not Marisol, because she's busy enough. I'm thinking of someone else, someone who knows every name, every price, and every rule.
GINA: Who is it?
MRS. ESPOSITO: I'll tell you on Saturday, after the sale, if he says yes.
TOMMY: "He"? Who's "he"? Come on, Dolores, tell me.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Saturday, Tommy, so be patient. You'll be the MC, which means you'll be the first to know.
TOMMY: Okay, then my answer is yes. I'll be the MC, with a loud voice and a lemon.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Thank you. Now I'm going home before I say something kind again, because it's exhausting.

[Scene: 10:10 p.m. The bar, after Mrs. Esposito has gone home. Tommy, Gina, Eddie, and Tasha sit together at one end.]

TASHA: Okay, so who is "he"? The new block president? I need to know, because I can't sleep without gossip.
EDDIE: It's not me. I'm eighty-four, and I already have one job, which is knocking on the floor.
TASHA: Walter, then? But he just moved in today, so that would be fast.
GINA: What about Marcus? He knows every delivery worker in Red Hook.
TOMMY: Marcus has a new employee and a city contract now, so he has no time.
TASHA: Jesse? He doesn't even live here yet.
EDDIE: "Someone who knows every name, every price, and every rule." Think about it. Who counts everything? Who ran forty-one times at the pantry?
GINA: Oh no. Diego? He's eleven!
EDDIE: He turned eleven in February, and he's the runner. He's also our future treasurer, if you ask him.
TOMMY: An eleven-year-old block association president? Is that even legal?
TASHA: Is anything on this block legal?
GINA: Dad, can I say something about the lemons? You never told me about them either.
TOMMY: You were in Carroll Gardens with Luca, and he was a baby. You had enough to worry about.
GINA: Dad, no secrets, remember? That's the rule from the napkin.
TOMMY: The napkin rule started in October, and the lemons were in twenty twenty. Old secrets don't count.
GINA: Old secrets count double. That's Mom's rule.
TOMMY: That's not your mother's rule, and you know it.
GINA: Well, it is now, because I just made it up. Just like last time.
EDDIE: She's getting good at that, Tommy, making up your wife's rules.
TOMMY: She learned from the best, because Angela made up rules all the time. "No hats at the bar." "No sad songs before nine." "One lemon in every bag."
TASHA: "One lemon in every bag." That's a good rule, and I'm stealing it for my nursing bag.

[Scene: 10:30 p.m. The second-floor landing. Mrs. Esposito unlocks the door of 2B. Tommy comes up the stairs behind her with a small paper bag.]

TOMMY: Dolores, wait. Give me one second.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Tommy? What is it? It's late, and I'm already in my slippers.
TOMMY: Here, this is from the bar: eggs, milk, bread, cat food, and one lemon.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Oh, Tommy. You didn't have to do that.
TOMMY: I know, and that's why I did it. And this time, I'm not running down the stairs. I'm standing right here, so you can see me.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I see you. I've always seen you, Tommy, even with the mask.
TOMMY: Dolores, can I ask you something? Who's the new president, the "he"? Eddie thinks it's Diego.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Eddie thinks too much, for a man who goes to sleep at nine.
TOMMY: So it's not Diego?
MRS. ESPOSITO: I didn't say that. I said Saturday.
TOMMY: You're really enjoying this, aren't you?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Tommy, I'm seventy-six, and secrets are my last hobby. Let me have one, a good one.
TOMMY: But you never said anything about the bags, not in seven years.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Some things you say in letters, not on stairs. Stairs are for knocking.
TOMMY: Then knock on my door sometimes, not only on Eddie's ceiling. I'm on the second floor too, right across the hall.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Two knocks?
TOMMY: Two knocks, and I'll knock back, just like Eddie does.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Then I'll need a bigger system, with a second card. Laminated, of course.
TOMMY: Of course you will. Goodnight, Dolores.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Goodnight, Tommy, and thank you for the lemon. Carmine would have liked you, because he was loud too.
TOMMY: What did Carmine do for work? You never told us.
MRS. ESPOSITO: He drove a bus, the B61, for eighteen years.
TOMMY: The B61? Just like Walter?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Just like Walter. That's why I fixed his appeal so fast. When I saw him in the pantry line in his old MTA jacket, I thought, "Carmine would want me to help this man."
TOMMY: Dolores, you've been taking care of this whole neighborhood quietly for thirty years.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Not quietly, Tommy. With a clipboard. Very loudly. Goodnight.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **편지체 표현**
  - *Dear Tommy. I am writing to say…* — "~을 말하려고 이 편지를 씁니다." 편지의 정석 시작.
  - *I owe you an apology.* — "당신에게 사과할 것이 있습니다."
  - *I was wrong to compare you to Richie.* — "당신을 리치에게 비교한 것은 잘못이었습니다." *I was wrong to + 동사*.
  - *I should have said that first.* — "그 말을 먼저 했어야 했습니다."
  - *I will never forget those bags.* — "그 장바구니들을 절대 잊지 못할 겁니다."
  - *Please accept my apology.* — "제 사과를 받아 주세요."
  - *With gratitude, and with respect. Yours truly,* — 감사와 존경을 담은 맺음말.
  - *P.S.* — 추신. 부인의 편지에는 늘 있다.
- **The clipboard was my armor.** — "클립보드는 내 갑옷이었다."
- **That is not corruption. That is love.** — "그건 부패가 아니라 사랑이다."
- **Pay me when you can.** — 살이 1995년에 한 말. "낼 수 있을 때 내요."
- **I'm stopping while I love it.** — "좋아할 때 그만두는 거야."
- **Stairs are for knocking.** — "계단은 두드리라고 있는 거야."
- **master of ceremonies (MC)** — 행사 사회자.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 편지는 지나가 대신 읽는다. 편지체 낭독 톤: 대화보다 느리고, 문장 끝을 분명히 내린다. 따옴표 안의 다른 사람 말(*'The lease is not yours.'*)은 목소리를 살짝 바꾼다.
- 단락이 바뀔 때(지나의 다음 대사로 넘어갈 때) 숨을 한 번 쉰다.
- *"Always one lemon."*에서 멈춘다. 이 화의 열쇠다.
- 토미의 *...You knew?*는 작게, 놀라서.
- 부인은 감정을 숨기려고 건조하게 말한다. 마지막 *Very loudly. | Goodnight.*에서 미소가 느껴지게.

## 한글 해석

에스포지토 부인: 지나, 부탁 하나 해도 될까? 좀 이상한 부탁이라 싫으면 거절해도 돼.
지나: 물론이죠, 돌로레스. 제가 뭐든 해 드리는 거 아시잖아요.
에스포지토 부인: 이건 네 아버지한테 쓴 편지야. 타자기로 쳤는데, 초안을 세 번이나 썼어.
지나: 아빠한테요? 그럼 그냥 직접 주시지 그래요? 바로 저기서 바 닦고 있잖아요.
에스포지토 부인: 소리 내서 못 읽겠으니까. 목소리가 떨릴 거고, 멈추게 될 거고, 그러면 그걸 감추려고 날카로운 말을 하겠지. 내가 늘 그래.
지나: 그러니까 제가 아빠한테 소리 내서 읽어 드리면 되는 거예요?
에스포지토 부인: 그래, 부탁해. 바로 여기서. 난 에디 옆에 앉아서 들을게. 그리고 평생 처음으로 끼어들지 않을게.
에디: 그건 꼭 보고 싶네. 돌로레스 에스포지토가 아무한테도 안 끼어드는 거.
에스포지토 부인: 조용히 해요, 에디. 연습 중이니까.
에디: 그래서 뭐가 들었어? 우리끼리 살짝 미리 알려 줄 수 있어?
에스포지토 부인: 미리 보기 없어요. 다른 사람들이랑 같이 들어요.
에디: 내 얘기야?
에스포지토 부인: 세상 모든 게 당신 얘기는 아니에요, 에디. 어떤 것만 그렇고, 이건 아니에요.
지나: 아빠, 이리 좀 와 볼래요? 돌로레스가 아빠한테 편지를 썼는데, 저보고 읽어 달래요.
토미: 편지? 나한테? 나 뭐 잘못했어? 또 가설 구조물 얘기야?
에스포지토 부인: 가설 구조물 얘기 아니에요, 토미.
타샤: 저 나가 있을까요? 사적인 얘기 같은데, 잔은 뒤에서 마저 닦을게요.
에스포지토 부인: 아니, 타샤, 있어 줘. 회의에서 내가 한 말을 다들 들었으니까, 이것도 다들 들어야지.
토미: 알았어요, 알았어요, 앉을게요. 읽어, 지나.
지나: "토미에게. 이사회 회의에서 하지 못한 말을 하려고 이 편지를 씁니다. 당신에게 사과할 것이 있습니다."
지나: "열흘 전, 당신은 이사회에 3B를 지나와 루카에게 주자고 했지요. 나는 그것이 리치가 한 짓처럼, 가족 먼저, 규칙은 나중처럼 보일 거라고 말했습니다."
지나: "그렇게 말한 것은 잘못이었습니다. 규칙에 대해서는 틀리지 않았어요. 규칙은 옳았으니까요. 하지만 당신을 리치에게 비교한 것은 잘못이었습니다. 당신은 리치와 전혀 다릅니다."
토미: …
지나: "리치는 빚을 갚으려고 이 건물을 낯선 사람들에게 팔았지만, 당신은 가진 것을 다 걸고, 아무것도 없을 때조차 이 건물을 위해 싸웠습니다. 당신은 딸에게 집을 주고 싶었던 거예요. 그건 부패가 아닙니다. 그건 사랑입니다. 그 말을 먼저 했어야 했습니다."
토미: 돌로레스…
에스포지토 부인: 쉿. 끝까지 읽게 둬요. 더 있으니까. 두 장이나 돼요.
지나: "내가 왜 그렇게 규칙에 신경 쓰는지, 왜 클립보드를 들고 다니며 모든 걸 세는지 궁금할지도 모르겠네요. 이 건물 누구에게도 그 이유를 말한 적이 없습니다. 에디에게조차요."
에디: 맞아, 안 했어. 2005년에 한 번 물어봤는데, 쓰레기 배출 일정으로 화제를 돌리더라고.
