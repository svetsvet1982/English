# THE CORNER HOUSE — 5부 79화

## Moving Day (이삿날)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 5부 7화(통산 79화) · 시기: 2027년 5월 20일(목) 아침~저녁 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 건물 앞 인도(이삿짐 밴) → 계단 → 4C → 저녁의 스툽
> - 등장인물: MR. HAYES, JESSE, MARCUS, TOMMY, DIEGO, MRS. ESPOSITO, EDDIE, MARISOL
> - 사건: 수요일 밤 시의 긴급 배정 승인이 났다. 목요일 아침 헤이스 씨가 커내시에서 빌린 밴을 몰고 온다. 짐은 많지 않다. 상자 열한 개, 매트리스, 의자 하나, 그리고 어머니가 조지아에서 쓰던 페달 재봉틀. 무게가 40킬로그램이 넘는다. 제시, 마커스, 토미가 "셋 하면!"을 외치며 4층까지 나른다. 중간에 비스킷이 계단을 막는다. 4C에서 에디가 루치아의 바느질 상자를 건네고, 헤이스 씨는 그것을 어머니의 재봉틀 옆 창턱에 둔다. 저녁에는 이웃들이 음식을 들고 올라온다. 스툽에서 두 노인이 나란히 앉는다.
> - 학습 포인트: 이사 어휘(boxes, dolly, mattress, tape, label, landing, pivot), 지시와 도움 요청(Can you grab…? / Could you hold the door? / Watch your fingers! / On three. One, two, three. / Lift with your legs.), 숨찬 짧은 발화, 환영 표현(Welcome to the building. / Make yourself at home. / If you need anything, knock.)
> - 미국 현안: 적정 임대료 주택 입주, 노년의 이사, 긴급 주거 배정, 이웃 공동체로의 편입
> - 연결: 78화 헤이스 씨의 4C 방문·루치아의 방·남향 창. 76화 비스킷 조항(계단 우선 통행). 53화 '다리가 일하게 해'. 58화 헤이스 씨와 부인의 MTA 인연. 46·62화 제시(이사 경험 많음).

## 대본

[Scene: Thursday, May 20, 8:15 a.m. Van Brunt Street. A small rental van is double-parked in front of the green shed with the kids' mural. Mr. Hayes climbs out. Jesse, Marcus, Tommy, and Diego wait on the sidewalk with work gloves.]

MR. HAYES: Good morning! I didn't sleep a single minute, because the city called at nine last night and said, "Approved, you can move in tomorrow."
TOMMY: Welcome to the building, Mr. Hayes, and welcome home!
MR. HAYES: Home, I like the sound of that. I haven't said that word since February, when I started sleeping on my sister's couch.
JESSE: Okay, moving crew reporting for duty! I've moved six times in four years. I'm basically a professional.
MARCUS: He's basically a disaster, and he breaks at least one lamp every time. But he's strong, so we keep him.
DIEGO: I'm the runner, so I can carry the small boxes, and I can open doors!
MR. HAYES: Perfect, because there isn't much: eleven boxes, a mattress, one chair, and my clothes. And then... there's one heavy thing.
TOMMY: How heavy are we talking?
MR. HAYES: It's my mother's sewing machine, the old kind made of cast iron, with a foot pedal. It's from Georgia, and it weighs about ninety pounds.
JESSE: Ninety pounds, up four floors, with no elevator. Okay, I'm suddenly feeling a lot less professional.
MARCUS: We've carried worse than that. Remember the jukebox, Tommy, the one you bought in two thousand eight?
TOMMY: Don't talk about the jukebox, because that jukebox is a sad story. Okay, here's the plan: boxes first, and the machine last. Diego, could you hold the door?
DIEGO: Holding the door! Go, go, go, because nobody gets locked out while I'm on duty!
JESSE: Mr. Hayes, can you grab the small boxes? They're the ones marked "kitchen," and you can leave the heavy ones to us.
MR. HAYES: I can carry boxes, son. I drove a bus for twenty-six years, and I lifted a lot of strollers.
MARCUS: Then lift with your legs, not your back. You told us yourself that your back is bad.
MR. HAYES: My back is terrible, that's true. Okay, then I'll lift with my legs, like a young man.
TOMMY: Like Jimmy Doyle used to say. "Let your legs do the work."
MR. HAYES: Who's Jimmy Doyle?
TOMMY: He was a friend, a good one. Eddie will tell you about him, because he tells everybody, and it's a good story.
JESSE: Box number one says "Kitchen, fragile," so I'll carry it like a baby. Going up!
DIEGO: Box number two says "Books," and wow, it's heavy! What kind of books are in here?
MR. HAYES: They're bus schedules. I've kept every map of every route since nineteen ninety-three. I'm a collector.
DIEGO: That's the weirdest collection I've ever heard of. I love it.

[Scene: 9:30 a.m. The staircase between the third and fourth floors. Jesse, Marcus, and Tommy carry the black cast-iron sewing machine. Mr. Hayes walks behind them. Mrs. Esposito stands at the third-floor landing.]

TOMMY: Okay. On three. One, two, three. Lift!
JESSE: Oh my God, ninety pounds? This isn't ninety pounds, it's a hundred and ninety pounds!
MARCUS: Watch your fingers! The rail is right there, Jesse, and it's closer than you think!
JESSE: My fingers are fine! My soul is not, but my fingers are fine!
TOMMY: Step. Step. Turn. Pivot! Pivot!
MARCUS: Why does everybody say "pivot" when they move furniture? It doesn't help anybody, and it just makes everybody yell.
JESSE: Because of a TV show! Everybody saw that episode, so now it's basically a law!
MRS. ESPOSITO: Stop! Stop right there! Don't move!
TOMMY: What is it now? We're holding a hundred pounds of iron here, Dolores!
MRS. ESPOSITO: It's Biscuit, on the step right in front of you. Under our pet rules, she has the right of way on the stairs.
JESSE: Right now? The cat has the right of way now, while we're halfway up the stairs with a sewing machine?
MRS. ESPOSITO: The rules don't say "except during sewing machines." I typed those rules myself, so I would know.
MARCUS: Biscuit, please move. We love you, we really do, but you have to move.
MR. HAYES: Let me try something. Here, kitty, here, kitty. Look what I have for you, a crumb of my sandwich.
MRS. ESPOSITO: She's looking at it, and she's thinking about it. And now... now she's going up, slowly, to 4B, to see the dog.
TOMMY: Thank you, Biscuit, you're a real lady. Okay, everybody, one more time. One, two, three, lift!
JESSE: Last step, last step, and... landing! We made it to the fourth floor, and nobody died!
MARCUS: Now put it down, gently, gently, because it's older than all of us.
MR. HAYES: Thank you, all of you. My mother would cry. She carried this machine up three floors in Harlem in nineteen sixty-one, by herself.
JESSE: By herself? She carried this machine alone? I can barely feel my arms after one flight!
MR. HAYES: Well, my uncle helped a little, but it was mostly her. She said, "This machine is our future, because it feeds us."
MRS. ESPOSITO: Mr. Hayes, welcome to the building. I'm on the second floor, in 2B, so if you need anything, knock.
MR. HAYES: Dolores, from the pantry and from the MTA. You're the one who fixed my appeal, and I still haven't thanked you.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And now I'll fix your mail. Your mailbox says 4C, and I've already put your name on it. I typed it, because handwriting fades.

[Scene: 11:00 a.m. Apartment 4C. Boxes everywhere. Sunlight from the south window. The sewing machine stands under the window. Eddie climbs the last step slowly with a wooden box under his arm. Marisol follows with a set of keys. Diego carries a small plant.]

EDDIE: Okay, I made it up here. That's forty-two steps, plus nine more to the fourth floor, which makes it a record for May.
MR. HAYES: Mr. O'Neill, you climbed all the way up here for me?
EDDIE: Call me Eddie, because everybody does. And yes, I did. I had to bring this up myself, because it belongs here.
MR. HAYES: Is that... is that Lucia's box?
EDDIE: It's Lucia's sewing box, with her needles, her thread, and her little scissors. She'd want it back in her room, with somebody who sews.
MR. HAYES: I don't sew, I'm afraid. My mother did, almost every night of her life.
EDDIE: Then it can stay with somebody who remembers sewing. That's enough, believe me.
MR. HAYES: Here, on the windowsill, next to my mother's machine. That way, they're both facing the harbor.
MARISOL: Two women who sewed, in one window, in Red Hook. I think they'd have liked each other.
DIEGO: Mr. Hayes, this plant is from me. It's basil from Mrs. Esposito's roof boxes, and she grew it from seeds.
MR. HAYES: Basil, thank you, young man, it smells wonderful. What's your name again? I've met a lot of people today.
DIEGO: My name's Diego, and I live on this floor too, in 4A. I'm loud, and it's written in the official book, so it's a fact.
MR. HAYES: Diego, okay, that's the first name I'll remember in this building, and the basil will help me.
MR. HAYES: And what's the second name I should learn?
DIEGO: Eddie first, then Mrs. Esposito, and then Biscuit, who's a cat, but she's also the one in charge.
EDDIE: It's true, she outranks all of us, including the board.
MR. HAYES: Then I'll bring her a crumb every morning. That's called diplomacy, and I learned it from twenty-six years of difficult passengers.
MARISOL: And these are your keys: front door, mailbox, your apartment, and the roof. You're allowed on the roof any time.
MR. HAYES: The roof? I can go on the roof? In my old building, the roof door had a lock and an alarm.
EDDIE: There's a chair up there, in the southwest corner, and that one's mine. But there's room for two, as long as you don't talk during the sunset.
MR. HAYES: Then I'll bring my own chair. I don't want to steal an old man's corner on my very first day.
EDDIE: Good man, you learn fast, a lot faster than Tommy did.
MARISOL: Mr. Hayes, one more thing. The co-op meets once a month, and you can come. You'll have a vote, because it's one household, one vote.
MR. HAYES: A vote? I've rented apartments for forty years, and I never had a vote. Not once, not about anything.
MARISOL: Well, now you do, because you're not a tenant here. You're a member.
MR. HAYES: A member. Can you say it again? Slowly?
MARISOL: You're a full member, with the same vote as everybody else in this building.
MR. HAYES: Please, call me Walter.
MR. HAYES: That was my name behind the wheel of the B61 for twenty-six years, but I'm retired now. Here, I'd like to just be Walter.

[Scene: 1:00 p.m. The Hook. Lunch break. Sandwiches on the bar. Jesse, Marcus, Diego, and Mr. Hayes sit on stools. Tommy stands behind the bar.]

JESSE: Okay, sandwich break, everybody! There's an old moving rule: the person who moves buys lunch for the whole crew.
MR. HAYES: Then I'm buying, and I won't argue about it. Tommy, how much for five sandwiches?
MARCUS: Careful, Walter, because Tommy's prices change with his mood, and his mood changes with the weather.
TOMMY: Today the price is zero. Moving day is free, and that's a bar rule. I just made it up, but I'm an owner now, so I'm allowed.
DIEGO: Jesse, you said you moved six times. Which one was the worst?
JESSE: The worst one was probably number four, a fifth-floor walk-up in Bushwick. It was August, it was ninety-five degrees, and we had a fridge.
MARCUS: You moved a fridge up five floors by yourselves?
JESSE: Me and my friend Kyle. We named it "The Beast," and we dropped it on the third floor. It still worked, but it hummed in a minor key after that.
MR. HAYES: And where do you live now, Jesse?
JESSE: I'm in Gowanus now, in a room with a guy named Leo and a fish named Gerald. My lease ends in August.
TOMMY: Then apply for 3B, Jesse. The lottery opens next month, and I think you're under the income limit, probably.
JESSE: Me? In this building? With all of you? Every single day?
DIEGO: You'd be across the hall from Eddie! You could hear his radio all day long, even through the wall!
JESSE: Tess would have to approve, though. She's a nurse, so she approves things very carefully.
MR. HAYES: Young man, take it from someone who waited forty years: apply. The worst they can say is "not this time," and you can live with that.
JESSE: "Not this time" doesn't sound so bad, so okay, I'll apply. But if I win, you all have to carry my boxes.
MARCUS: Only if you don't own a fridge named The Beast. I'm not carrying another monster up those stairs.

[Scene: 7:30 p.m. The stoop. The sky over the harbor is orange. Neighbors have brought food all afternoon. Mr. Hayes and Eddie sit side by side on the top step with plates on their knees. Tommy brings two glasses.]

TOMMY: Here are two root beers, because there's nothing stronger on moving day, doctor's orders. Well, Tess's orders, which are even stricter.
MR. HAYES: Thank you, Tommy, but I can't eat anything else. Look at this plate: lasagna, empanadas, samosas, mole, and something called "The Frankie."
EDDIE: The Frankie is a drink, club soda and lime. Whatever you do, don't eat it.
MR. HAYES: The whole building came up, all afternoon. Knock, knock: "Welcome." Knock, knock: "Make yourself at home." Knock, knock: "Here's a lasagna."
TOMMY: That's Dolores's lasagna, and the recipe goes back to nineteen sixty-two. If she gave you a lasagna, you're officially family now.
MR. HAYES: In my old building, I lived eleven years. When I left, nobody noticed, and the super didn't even say goodbye.
EDDIE: That won't happen here. Here, if you leave, Dolores writes it in the minutes, with the date and the reason.
MR. HAYES: Eddie, can I ask you something? Why do you all do this for a stranger?
EDDIE: You're not a stranger. You drove the B61 for twenty-six years, and you stopped in front of this bar every morning. You've been part of this corner longer than Jesse has.
TOMMY: Longer than Marcus, too. Longer than half the people on this block.
MR. HAYES: I never thought of it that way. I always thought I was just passing through, on my route.
EDDIE: Everybody's just passing through, Walter. Some people just stop longer.
TOMMY: Walter, Diego told me you have a bus schedule collection that goes all the way back to nineteen ninety-three.
MR. HAYES: Every route, every year, and the maps too. My sister calls it clutter, but I call it history.
TOMMY: Would you hang one in the bar? The old B61 map, over the booth by the window?
MR. HAYES: The nineteen ninety-three map, with the stop right in front of your door. It would be an honor, Tommy.
EDDIE: Then the bus finally stops here for good.
MR. HAYES: Then I'd like to stop too, for a while, if that's okay with everybody.
EDDIE: It's more than okay. There's a rule here, rule number four: "Never sign alone." You'll see it on every fridge.
MR. HAYES: Never sign alone, I like that rule, because I've signed too many things alone in my life.
TOMMY: And there's rule number five, which is unofficial: "Nobody eats alone on their first night."
MR. HAYES: Is that a real rule, or did you just make it up?
TOMMY: It's real now, because I just made it. I'm the oldest owner of a bar in this building, so I'm allowed.
EDDIE: You're the only owner of a bar in this building, Tommy.
TOMMY: Then I'm the oldest by default. Drink your root beer, Walter. Welcome home.
MR. HAYES: Home. That's twice today. I could get used to that word.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **지시와 도움 요청**
  - *Can you grab the small boxes?* — "작은 상자들 좀 들어 줄래요?" *grab*은 가볍게 집어 드는 느낌.
  - *Could you hold the door?* — "문 좀 잡아 줄래요?"
  - *Watch your fingers!* — "손가락 조심!"
  - *On three. One, two, three. Lift!* — "셋 하면 들어. 하나, 둘, 셋!"
  - *Lift with your legs, not your back.* — "허리 말고 다리로 들어."
  - *Pivot!* — "돌려!" 좁은 계단에서 가구를 돌릴 때. 유명한 시트콤 장면 때문에 농담처럼 쓰인다.
- **이사 어휘** — *double-parked* 이중 주차된 / *fragile* 깨지기 쉬운 / *landing* 계단참 / *cast iron* 주철 / *mailbox* 우편함 / *super* 건물 관리인(superintendent)
- **환영 표현**
  - *Welcome to the building. / Welcome home.*
  - *Make yourself at home.* — "편하게 지내세요."
  - *If you need anything, knock.* — "필요한 거 있으면 문 두드려요."
- **You're not a tenant here. You're a member.** — "여기선 세입자가 아니라 회원이에요."
- **Everybody's just passing through. Some people just stop longer.** — 에디의 말. "다들 지나가는 중이야. 어떤 사람은 좀 더 오래 머물 뿐이지."
- **by default** — 다른 사람이 없어서 자동으로.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 구령은 리듬 있게: *On three. | One, | two, | three. | LIFT!*
- 계단 장면은 숨차게. 문장이 짧고 끊긴다. *Step. Step. Turn. Pivot! Pivot!*
- 부인의 *Stop! Stop right there! Don't move!*는 갑자기 크게. 그 뒤 비스킷 대목은 다시 진지하게.
- 4C 장면의 헤이스 씨는 감동을 누르는 목소리로. *A member. | Can you say it again? | Slowly?*
- 마지막 스툽 장면은 해 질 녘의 느린 대화. *Home. | That's twice today.*

## 한글 해석

헤이스 씨: 좋은 아침이에요! 한숨도 못 잤어요. 어젯밤 9시에 시에서 전화가 와서 "승인됐습니다, 내일 입주하셔도 됩니다"라고 했거든요.
토미: 건물에 오신 걸 환영해요, 헤이스 씨, 그리고 집에 오신 걸 환영해요!
헤이스 씨: 집, 그 말 좋네요. 2월에 여동생네 소파에서 자기 시작한 뒤로 그 단어를 한 번도 안 썼어요.
제시: 좋아요, 이사 팀 출동 보고합니다! 4년 동안 여섯 번 이사했어요. 거의 전문가예요.
마커스: 거의 재앙이지. 이사할 때마다 램프를 적어도 하나는 깨요. 그래도 힘이 세서 데리고 다녀요.
디에고: 전 운반 담당이에요. 그러니까 작은 상자 들 수 있고, 문도 열 수 있어요!
헤이스 씨: 완벽하네. 짐이 많지 않거든요. 상자 열한 개, 매트리스, 의자 하나, 그리고 옷. 그리고… 무거운 게 하나 있어요.
토미: 얼마나 무거운 건데요?
헤이스 씨: 우리 어머니 재봉틀이에요. 주철로 된 옛날 거요, 발 페달 달린. 조지아에서 온 건데, 90파운드쯤 나가요.
제시: 90파운드, 4층까지, 엘리베이터도 없이. 좋아요, 갑자기 전문가 느낌이 확 줄었어요.
마커스: 더한 것도 날랐잖아. 주크박스 기억나요, 토미? 2008년에 샀던 거요.
토미: 주크박스 얘기는 하지 마. 그 주크박스는 슬픈 이야기니까. 좋아, 계획은 이래. 상자 먼저, 재봉틀은 마지막. 디에고, 문 좀 잡아 줄래?
디에고: 문 잡았어요! 가요, 가요, 가요. 제가 당번인 동안엔 아무도 밖에 갇히지 않아요!
제시: 헤이스 씨, 작은 상자들 좀 들어 주실래요? '주방'이라고 써 있는 거요. 무거운 건 저희한테 맡기시고요.
헤이스 씨: 상자는 들 수 있어, 젊은이. 26년 동안 버스를 몰았고, 유모차를 엄청 들어 올렸거든.
마커스: 그럼 허리 말고 다리로 드세요. 허리 안 좋다고 직접 말씀하셨잖아요.
헤이스 씨: 허리가 엉망인 건 사실이죠. 좋아요, 그럼 젊은 사람처럼 다리로 들게요.
토미: 지미 도일이 늘 하던 말처럼요. "다리가 일하게 해."
헤이스 씨: 지미 도일이 누구예요?
토미: 친구였어요, 좋은 친구. 에디가 얘기해 줄 거예요. 모두한테 얘기하거든요. 좋은 이야기예요.
제시: 상자 1번에 "주방, 깨지기 쉬움"이라고 써 있으니까 아기처럼 안고 갈게요. 올라가요!
디에고: 상자 2번은 "책"이라고 써 있는데, 와, 무거워요! 안에 무슨 책이 들어 있어요?
헤이스 씨: 버스 시간표야. 1993년부터 모든 노선의 지도를 다 모아 뒀거든. 수집가란다.
디에고: 제가 들어 본 것 중에 제일 이상한 수집이에요. 완전 좋아요.
토미: 좋아. 셋 하면. 하나, 둘, 셋. 들어!
제시: 세상에, 90파운드라고요? 이건 90파운드가 아니라 190파운드예요!
마커스: 손가락 조심! 난간이 바로 거기 있어, 제시, 생각보다 가까워!
제시: 손가락은 괜찮아요! 영혼은 안 괜찮지만, 손가락은 괜찮아요!
