# THE CORNER HOUSE — 5부 79화

## Moving Day (이삿날)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 5부 7화(통산 79화) · 시기: 2027년 5월 20일(목) 아침~저녁 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 건물 앞 인도(이삿짐 밴) → 계단 → 4C → 저녁의 스툽
> - 등장인물: MR. HAYES, JESSE, MARCUS, TOMMY, DIEGO, MRS. ESPOSITO, EDDIE, MARISOL
> - 사건: 수요일 밤 시의 긴급 배정 승인이 났다. 목요일 아침 헤이스 씨가 커내시에서 빌린 밴을 몰고 온다. 짐은 많지 않다. 상자 열한 개, 매트리스, 의자 하나, 그리고 어머니가 조지아에서 쓰던 페달 재봉틀. 무게가 40킬로그램이 넘는다. 제시, 마커스, 토미가 "셋 하면!"을 외치며 4층까지 나른다. 중간에 비스킷이 계단을 막는다. 4C에서 에디가 루치아의 바느질 상자를 건네고, 헤이스 씨는 그것을 어머니의 재봉틀 옆 창턱에 둔다. 저녁에는 이웃들이 음식을 들고 올라온다. 스툽에서 두 노인이 나란히 앉는다.
> - 학습 포인트: 이사 어휘(boxes, dolly, mattress, tape, label, landing, pivot), 지시와 도움 요청(Can you grab…? / Could you hold the door? / Watch your fingers! / On three. One, two, three. / Lift with your legs.), 숨찬 짧은 발화, 환영 표현(Welcome to the building. / Make yourself at home. / If you need anything, knock.)
> - 미국 현안: 적정 임대료 주택 입주, 노년의 이사, 긴급 주거 배정, 이웃 공동체로의 편입
> - 연결: 78화 헤이스 씨의 4C 방문·루치아의 방·남향 창. 76화 비스킷 조항(계단 우선 통행). 53화 '다리가 일하게 해'. 58화 헤이스 씨와 부인의 MTA 인연. 46·62화 제시(이사 경험 많음).

## 대본

[Scene: Thursday, May 20, 8:15 a.m. Van Brunt Street. A small rental van is double-parked in front of the green shed with the kids' mural. Mr. Hayes climbs out. Jesse, Marcus, Tommy, and Diego wait on the sidewalk with work gloves.]

MR. HAYES: Good morning! I didn't sleep. Not one minute. The city called at nine last night. "Approved. You can move in tomorrow."
TOMMY: Welcome to the building, Mr. Hayes! Welcome home!
MR. HAYES: Home. I like the sound of that. I haven't said that word since February.
JESSE: Okay! Moving crew reporting for duty. I've moved six times in four years. I'm basically a professional.
MARCUS: He's basically a disaster. But he's strong.
DIEGO: I'm the runner! I can carry small boxes! And open doors!
MR. HAYES: Perfect. There isn't much. Eleven boxes. A mattress. One chair. My clothes. And... one heavy thing.
TOMMY: How heavy?
MR. HAYES: My mother's sewing machine. The old kind. Cast iron. With a pedal. From Georgia. It weighs about ninety pounds.
JESSE: Ninety pounds. Up four floors. No elevator.
MARCUS: We've carried worse. Remember the jukebox, Tommy? When you bought it in two thousand eight?
TOMMY: Don't talk about the jukebox. That jukebox is a sad story. Okay. Boxes first. The machine last. Diego, hold the door.
DIEGO: Holding the door! Go, go, go!
JESSE: Mr. Hayes, can you grab the small boxes? The ones marked "kitchen"? Leave the heavy ones to us.
MR. HAYES: I can carry boxes. I drove a bus for twenty-six years. I lifted a lot of strollers.
MARCUS: Lift with your legs, not your back. You told us your back is bad.
MR. HAYES: My back is terrible. Okay. Legs. I'll lift with my legs.
TOMMY: Like Jimmy Doyle used to say. "Let your legs do the work."
MR. HAYES: Who's Jimmy Doyle?
TOMMY: A friend. Eddie will tell you. He tells everybody. It's a good story.
JESSE: Box number one! "Kitchen. Fragile." Got it. Going up.
DIEGO: Box number two! "Books." It's heavy! What kind of books?
MR. HAYES: Bus schedules. Every map of every route since nineteen ninety-three. I'm a collector.
DIEGO: That's the weirdest collection I've ever heard of. I love it.

[Scene: 9:30 a.m. The staircase between the third and fourth floors. Jesse, Marcus, and Tommy carry the black cast-iron sewing machine. Mr. Hayes walks behind them. Mrs. Esposito stands at the third-floor landing.]

TOMMY: Okay. On three. One, two, three. Lift!
JESSE: Oh my God. Ninety pounds? This is a hundred and ninety pounds!
MARCUS: Watch your fingers on the rail! Jesse, your fingers!
JESSE: My fingers are fine! My soul is not!
TOMMY: Step. Step. Turn. Pivot! Pivot!
MARCUS: Why does everybody say "pivot" when they move furniture?
JESSE: Because of a TV show! Everybody saw it! It's a law now!
MRS. ESPOSITO: Stop! Stop right there! Don't move!
TOMMY: What? What is it? We're holding a hundred pounds of iron, Dolores!
MRS. ESPOSITO: Biscuit. On the step. Right in front of you. She has the right of way.
JESSE: Now? The cat has the right of way now? We're halfway up the stairs!
MRS. ESPOSITO: The rules don't say "except during sewing machines."
MARCUS: Biscuit. Please. Move. We love you. Move.
MR. HAYES: Let me try. Here, kitty. Here, kitty. Look. I have a crumb of my sandwich.
MRS. ESPOSITO: She's looking. She's thinking. She's... she's going up. Slowly. To 4B. To see the dog.
TOMMY: Thank you, Biscuit. Okay. Again. One, two, three. Lift!
JESSE: Last step. Last step. And... landing! We made it! Fourth floor!
MARCUS: Put it down. Gently. Gently. Okay. Down.
MR. HAYES: Thank you. Thank you, all of you. My mother would cry. She carried this machine up three floors in Harlem. In nineteen sixty-one. By herself.
JESSE: By herself? This machine?
MR. HAYES: With my uncle's help. A little. But mostly by herself. She said, "This machine is our future. It feeds us."
MRS. ESPOSITO: Mr. Hayes. Welcome to the building. I'm on the second floor. If you need anything, knock.
MR. HAYES: Dolores. From the pantry. From the MTA. You fixed my appeal.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And now I'll fix your mail. The mailbox. 4C. I've already put your name on it. Typed.

[Scene: 11:00 a.m. Apartment 4C. Boxes everywhere. Sunlight from the south window. The sewing machine stands under the window. Eddie climbs the last step slowly with a wooden box under his arm. Marisol follows with a set of keys. Diego carries a small plant.]

EDDIE: Okay. I made it. Forty-two steps. Plus nine more to the fourth floor. That's a record for May.
MR. HAYES: Mr. O'Neill. You climbed up here? For me?
EDDIE: Call me Eddie. Everybody does. And yes. I had to bring this. It belongs here.
MR. HAYES: Is that... Lucia's box?
EDDIE: Lucia's sewing box. Her needles, her thread, her little scissors. She'd want it back in her room. With somebody who sews.
MR. HAYES: I don't sew. My mother did.
EDDIE: Then with somebody who remembers sewing. That's enough.
MR. HAYES: Here. On the windowsill. Next to my mother's machine. Facing the harbor.
MARISOL: Two women who sewed. In one window. In Red Hook.
DIEGO: Mr. Hayes, this plant is from me. It's a basil. From Mrs. Esposito's roof boxes. She grew it from seeds.
MR. HAYES: Basil. Thank you, young man. What's your name again?
DIEGO: Diego. I live right below you. I'm loud. It's in the official book.
MR. HAYES: Diego. Okay. That's the first name I'll remember in this building.
MR. HAYES: And what's the second name?
DIEGO: Eddie. Then Mrs. Esposito. Then Biscuit. She's a cat. She's in charge.
EDDIE: It's true. She outranks all of us.
MR. HAYES: Then I'll bring her a crumb every morning. Diplomacy.
MARISOL: And these are your keys. Front door. Mailbox. Your apartment. And the roof. You're allowed on the roof. Any time.
MR. HAYES: The roof? I can go on the roof?
EDDIE: There's a chair up there. In the southwest corner. That's mine. But there's room for two.
MR. HAYES: I'll bring my own chair. I don't want to steal an old man's corner.
EDDIE: Good man. You learn fast.
MARISOL: Mr. Hayes, one more thing. The co-op meets once a month. You can come. You'll have a vote. One household, one vote.
MR. HAYES: A vote? I've rented apartments for forty years. I never had a vote. Not once.
MARISOL: Now you do. You're not a tenant here. You're a member.
MR. HAYES: A member. Can you say it again? Slowly?
MARISOL: You're a member, Mr. Hayes.
MR. HAYES: Please. Call me Walter.
MR. HAYES: Please. Call me Walter.

[Scene: 1:00 p.m. The Hook. Lunch break. Sandwiches on the bar. Jesse, Marcus, Diego, and Mr. Hayes sit on stools. Tommy stands behind the bar.]

JESSE: Okay. Sandwich break. Moving rule: the person who moves buys lunch.
MR. HAYES: I'm buying. Tommy, how much for five sandwiches?
MARCUS: Careful, Walter. Tommy's prices change with his mood.
TOMMY: Today, zero. Moving day is free. That's a bar rule. I just made it. I'm an owner now.
DIEGO: Jesse, you said you moved six times. Which was the worst?
JESSE: The worst? Probably number four. A fifth-floor walk-up in Bushwick. In August. With a fridge.
MARCUS: You moved a fridge? Yourself?
JESSE: Me and my friend Kyle. We named it "The Beast." We dropped it on the third floor. It still worked. But it hummed in a minor key after that.
MR. HAYES: And now? Where do you live now?
JESSE: Gowanus. A room. With a guy named Leo and a fish named Gerald. My lease ends in August.
TOMMY: Then apply for 3B, Jesse. The lottery opens next month. You're under the income limit. Probably.
JESSE: Me? In this building? With all of you? Every day?
DIEGO: You'd be across the hall from Eddie! You could hear his radio!
JESSE: Tess would have to approve. She's a nurse. She approves things carefully.
MR. HAYES: Young man, take it from someone who waited forty years. Apply. The worst they can say is "not this time."
JESSE: "Not this time." Okay. I'll apply. If I win, you all carry my boxes.
MARCUS: Only if you don't own a fridge named The Beast.

[Scene: 7:30 p.m. The stoop. The sky over the harbor is orange. Neighbors have brought food all afternoon. Mr. Hayes and Eddie sit side by side on the top step with plates on their knees. Tommy brings two glasses.]

TOMMY: Two root beers. Nothing stronger on moving day. Doctor's orders. Well, Tess's orders.
MR. HAYES: Thank you, Tommy. I can't eat anything else. Look at this plate. Lasagna. Empanadas. Samosas. Mole. And something called "The Frankie."
EDDIE: The Frankie is a drink. Club soda and lime. Don't eat it.
MR. HAYES: The whole building came up. All afternoon. Knock, knock. "Welcome." Knock, knock. "Make yourself at home." Knock, knock. "Here's a lasagna."
TOMMY: That's Dolores's lasagna. From nineteen sixty-two. You're officially family now.
MR. HAYES: In my old building, I lived eleven years. When I left, nobody noticed. The super didn't even say goodbye.
EDDIE: That won't happen here. Here, if you leave, Dolores writes it in the minutes. With a reason.
MR. HAYES: Eddie, can I ask you something? Why do you all do this? For a stranger?
EDDIE: You're not a stranger. You drove the B61 for twenty-six years. You stopped in front of this bar every morning. You've been part of this corner longer than Jesse.
TOMMY: Longer than Marcus. Longer than half the people on this block.
MR. HAYES: I never thought of it that way. I thought I was just passing through. On my route.
EDDIE: Everybody's just passing through, Walter. Some people just stop longer.
TOMMY: Walter, you have a bus schedule collection. Since nineteen ninety-three. Diego told me.
MR. HAYES: Every route. Every year. Maps too.
TOMMY: Would you hang one in the bar? The old B61 map? Over the booth by the window?
MR. HAYES: The nineteen ninety-three map. With the stop right in front of your door. It would be an honor.
EDDIE: Then the bus finally stops here for good.
MR. HAYES: Then I'd like to stop. For a while. If that's okay.
EDDIE: It's more than okay. There's a rule here. Rule number four. "Never sign alone." You'll see it on every fridge.
MR. HAYES: Never sign alone. I like that rule.
TOMMY: And rule number five. Unofficial. "Nobody eats alone on their first night."
MR. HAYES: Is that a real rule?
TOMMY: It is now. I just made it. I'm the oldest owner of a bar in this building. I'm allowed.
EDDIE: You're the only owner of a bar in this building, Tommy.
TOMMY: Then I'm the oldest by default. Drink your root beer, Walter. Welcome home.
MR. HAYES: Home. That's twice today. I could get used to that word.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **지시와 도움 요청**
  - *Can you grab the small boxes?* — "작은 상자들 좀 들어 줄래요?" *grab*은 가볍게 집어 드는 느낌.
  - *Could you hold the door?* — "문 좀 잡아 줄래요?"
  - *Watch your fingers!* — "손가락 조심!"
  - *On three. One, two, three. Lift!* — "셋 하면 들어. 하나, 둘, 셋!"
  - *Lift with your legs, not your back.* — "허리 말고 다리로 들어."
  - *Pivot!* — "돌려!" 좁은 계단에서 가구를 돌릴 때. 유명한 시트콤 장면 때문에 농담처럼 쓰인다.
- **이사 어휘** — *double-parked* 이중 주차된 / *fragile* 깨지기 쉬운 / *landing* 계단참 / *cast iron* 주철 / *mailbox* 우편함 / *super* 건물 관리인(superintendent)
- **환영 표현**
  - *Welcome to the building. / Welcome home.*
  - *Make yourself at home.* — "편하게 지내세요."
  - *If you need anything, knock.* — "필요한 거 있으면 문 두드려요."
- **You're not a tenant here. You're a member.** — "여기선 세입자가 아니라 회원이에요."
- **Everybody's just passing through. Some people just stop longer.** — 에디의 말. "다들 지나가는 중이야. 어떤 사람은 좀 더 오래 머물 뿐이지."
- **by default** — 다른 사람이 없어서 자동으로.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 구령은 리듬 있게: *On three. | One, | two, | three. | LIFT!*
- 계단 장면은 숨차게. 문장이 짧고 끊긴다. *Step. Step. Turn. Pivot! Pivot!*
- 부인의 *Stop! Stop right there! Don't move!*는 갑자기 크게. 그 뒤 비스킷 대목은 다시 진지하게.
- 4C 장면의 헤이스 씨는 감동을 누르는 목소리로. *A member. | Can you say it again? | Slowly?*
- 마지막 스툽 장면은 해 질 녘의 느린 대화. *Home. | That's twice today.*
