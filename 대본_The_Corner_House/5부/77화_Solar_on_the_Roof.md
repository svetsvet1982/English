# THE CORNER HOUSE — 5부 77화

## Solar on the Roof (옥상의 태양광)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 5부 5화(통산 77화) · 시기: 2027년 5월 15일(토) ~ 6월 12일(토) · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 보수를 막 마친 옥상 → 화요일 밤 회원 총회(더 훅) → 다시 옥상, 해 질 녘
> - 등장인물: KOWALSKI, SAM, MARCUS, DIEGO, MRS. ESPOSITO, PRIYA, EDDIE, MARISOL, TOMMY
> - 사건: 북쪽 지붕 보수가 끝난 날, 코왈스키가 태양광 업체 견적을 들고 옥상에 오른다. 패널 24장, 약 4만 8천 달러다. 연방 주택 태양광 세액 공제는 작년 말에 끝났지만, 주의 지원금과 지역 태양광 프로그램이 남아 있다. 마커스와 디에고는 찬성한다. 부인은 이 옥상에 토마토 정원을 만들고 싶어 하고, 에디는 이곳에서 페기와 독립기념일 불꽃놀이를 봤다. 총회에서는 비용과 효과, '그때 했더라면'과 '지금 안 하면'이 부딪친다. 히트펌프는 5년 뒤로 미룬다. 결론은 남쪽 절반에 패널, 북쪽 절반에 정원과 에디의 의자다.
> - 학습 포인트: 환경·에너지 어휘(solar panels, inverter, payback period, kilowatt-hour, heat pump, emissions, incentive, tax credit), 조건문 종합(2형: If we installed panels, we would save… / 3형: If we had done this last summer, we would have saved… / 혼합: If the boiler hadn't died, we wouldn't be talking about this now.), 기술 용어 강세
> - 미국 현안: 건물 탄소 배출 규제(로컬 법 97은 대형 건물 대상), 연방 세액 공제 축소, 주·시의 지원금, 전기 요금 상승, 작은 건물의 기후 대응 비용
> - 연결: 31화 폭염과 콘에드 요금(바 2,400). 65화 보일러. 75화 새 보일러 5/3. 76화 지붕 보수 2만 회원 표결. 68화 부인 언니의 스태튼 아일랜드 정원과 토마토. 26화 독립기념일 불꽃놀이. 40·54화 마커스 충전소. 41화 디에고 로봇 학교 지원.

## 대본

[Scene: Saturday, May 15, 10:00 a.m. The roof. Fresh black patches on the north side. Sun on the tar. The harbor shines in the distance. Kowalski holds a folder. Sam, Marcus, Diego, and Mrs. Esposito stand around him.]

KOWALSKI: Okay. The roof is fixed. North side, patched. It should hold for five years. Maybe more. Twenty thousand, as approved.
SAM: Nineteen thousand four hundred, actually. I saw the invoice. Six hundred under.
KOWALSKI: Of course you saw it. Now, while we're up here. I have something else. A quote. From a solar company.
MARCUS: Solar! Finally! I've been waiting for somebody to say that word.
KOWALSKI: Twenty-four panels. On the south side. Where the sun hits all day. About forty-eight thousand dollars installed.
SAM: Forty-eight thousand. That's a lot of red.
KOWALSKI: Wait. There's more. The state has incentives for small buildings. About twelve thousand. And there's a city program for affordable co-ops. Another eight, maybe.
SAM: So we pay twenty-eight thousand. What about the federal tax credit? Thirty percent?
KOWALSKI: For homes, that credit ended last year. December twenty twenty-five. Congress cut it. For buildings like ours, it's complicated. I wouldn't count on it.
MARCUS: Typical. The year we finally own a roof, they take away the money.
DIEGO: How much electricity does a panel make? Ms. Park said the sun is a giant battery.
KOWALSKI: Good question. All twenty-four together make about twelve thousand kilowatt-hours a year. That's enough for the hallways, the basement, the laundry, and maybe a third of the bar.
DIEGO: What's a kilowatt-hour?
MARCUS: It's how you measure electricity. Like a gallon for gas. One kilowatt-hour is about one hour of a microwave.
DIEGO: So twelve thousand hours of microwave. That's a lot of popcorn.
SAM: How much money does it save? Per year?
KOWALSKI: About three thousand dollars a year. At today's prices. Con Ed keeps raising prices. So probably more.
SAM: Twenty-eight thousand divided by three thousand. That's a payback period of about nine years. After that, it's free electricity.
MARCUS: And if the power goes out? Like during Sandy? Do the panels keep working?
KOWALSKI: Not without a battery. For safety, they shut off when the grid goes down. A battery is another fifteen thousand.
MARCUS: Then next time. A battery. For the hallways. And my chargers.
DIEGO: And the elevator!
SAM: We don't have an elevator, Diego.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Nine years. I'll be eighty-five. Fine. I can wait. But I have a question. The whole south side? Where would my garden go?
KOWALSKI: Your garden?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Tomatoes. Basil. Peppers. In pots. I've wanted a roof garden for thirty-one years. Sal always said no. "Dolores, the roof is not a farm."
MARCUS: Dolores, tomatoes need sun. Solar panels need sun. You're competing with science.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Then science can share. My tomatoes are older than science. My mother grew them in Bensonhurst.

[Scene: Tuesday, May 18, 8:00 p.m. The Hook. A members' meeting. Marisol leads. Priya, Eddie, Tommy, and Sam sit at the table. Sam has slides, as usual.]

MARISOL: Okay. Members' meeting. One item. The roof. Solar panels, yes or no. And if yes, how much of the roof.
SAM: Slide one. The numbers. Twenty-eight thousand after incentives. Three thousand saved per year. Nine years to pay back. After that, about three thousand a year, forever. Or until the panels die. Twenty-five years.
PRIYA: I'll say it. I'm for it. Completely. If we installed panels, we'd use less electricity from gas plants. We'd cut our emissions. We'd save money.
TOMMY: And the bar? My Con Ed bill was twenty-four hundred dollars last July. The heat wave. The old air conditioner.
SAM: If the panels had been there last summer, your bill would have been lower. Maybe five or six hundred lower that month.
TOMMY: If we had done this last summer, I would have saved six hundred dollars. And I could have fixed the ice machine.
EDDIE: If, if, if. Last summer, we didn't own the roof. Harborline owned it. And Harborline didn't even fix the leak.
MARISOL: That's true. If Harborline had owned it longer, there would be no panels. There would be a penthouse.
PRIYA: Exactly. So now is the time. If we don't do it now, we'll keep saying "next year."
EDDIE: Can I say something? As the only person here who remembers when the roof was black tar and nothing else?
MARISOL: Go ahead, Eddie.
EDDIE: Peggy and I watched the fireworks from that roof. Every Fourth of July. From nineteen seventy-four. A blanket, two beers, Michael asleep on her lap.
TOMMY: I didn't know that.
EDDIE: Nobody knows. It was ours. The south corner. Where you can see the harbor and the Statue. If you fill it with panels, where do I sit? I'm not against the sun. I'm against losing my corner.
PRIYA: Eddie... I didn't think about that.
EDDIE: Why would you? It's an old man's corner. But it's the only corner I have left with Peggy in it.
SAM: Okay. Let me check something. One second. The south side has thirty-two spots for panels. We only need twenty-four. Eight spots are empty anyway.
MARISOL: Where are the eight empty spots?
SAM: The southwest corner. By the harbor side. Because of the chimney. It casts a shadow in the afternoon. Panels there would be useless anyway.
EDDIE: The southwest corner. That's... that's the corner. That's where the blanket was.
SAM: Then the chimney saved your corner, Eddie. Physics is on your side.
TOMMY: And Dolores? Her tomatoes?
MARISOL: She's not here. She's at bingo. But she sent a note. Priya, read it.
PRIYA: "If the panels take the south, the tomatoes take the north. The north gets four hours of sun. Enough for basil. Not enough for tomatoes. I want a fair trade."
SAM: What does "fair trade" mean?
PRIYA: There's a second line. "Sam builds me two raised boxes on wheels. So I can move them into the sun. Signed, Dolores. P.S. I have a list."
SAM: Boxes on wheels. Okay. That's thirty dollars of wood. And one Saturday. I can live with that.
MARISOL: And heat pumps? Kowalski said that's the next step. Replacing gas heat. Before the city requires it.
SAM: Heat pumps are expensive. About ninety thousand for the whole building. We just bought a new gas boiler. If we had known in January, maybe we would have chosen differently. But the boiler was dead. People were freezing.
EDDIE: If the boiler hadn't died, we wouldn't be talking about any of this. We wouldn't own the building.
TOMMY: That's true. That boiler is the reason we're here. Rest in peace, old boiler.
MARISOL: So heat pumps go in the five-year plan. Not now. Solar now. Twenty-four panels. South side. Except the southwest corner. That's Eddie's.
PRIYA: And the tomato boxes on wheels.
MARISOL: And the tomato boxes on wheels. Okay. Let's vote. All in favor?
TOMMY: Aye. For my Con Ed bill.
PRIYA: Aye. For the planet.
EDDIE: Aye. For the sun. As long as it stays out of my corner.
SAM: Aye. With a payback period of nine years.
MARISOL: And Dolores voted aye by note. With the tomato condition. The motion carries. Five to zero.

[Scene: Wednesday, June 2, 9:00 a.m. The stoop and the staircase. Installers wait at the bottom with panels. Mrs. Esposito stands on the stairs. Priya stands on the sidewalk holding a small brown dog on a leash. Sam and Tommy watch from the door of the bar.]

KOWALSKI: Okay, everybody. The installers are carrying twenty-four panels up four floors. Please keep the stairs clear.
MRS. ESPOSITO: The stairs are not clear, Brian. Biscuit is on the stairs. She has the right of way. It's in the rules.
KOWALSKI: The cat has the right of way over solar panels?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Over everything. Pet rule, version one. The Biscuit clause.
TOMMY: It's true, Brian. We voted. Five to zero.
KOWALSKI: Okay. Can someone please carry the cat?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Nobody carries Biscuit. Biscuit decides.
PRIYA: Dolores, while we wait... can I introduce someone? Here on the sidewalk. Neutral territory.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Is that... the dog?
PRIYA: This is Mango. A rescue. Three years old. Very calm. Sam and I picked her up on Sunday.
SAM: She was supposed to wait until after the wedding. She did not wait.
PRIYA: Someone else wanted to adopt her! I had to move fast! Best and final!
TOMMY: She learned from the negotiation. Look at her.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Okay. Slowly. I'll bring Biscuit down. To the bottom step. Mango stays on the sidewalk.
PRIYA: Mango, sit. Good girl. Look, Biscuit. A friend.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Biscuit is looking. She's thinking. She's... she's sniffing.
SAM: The dog is wagging. That's a good sign. Right?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Biscuit just walked past her. Up the stairs. Without hissing. For Biscuit, that's a hug.
KOWALSKI: Wonderful. Now that the cat has gone upstairs, can my installers use the stairs?
MRS. ESPOSITO: No. She's on the third floor now. Wait five minutes.
TOMMY: Brian, welcome to cooperative living. Everything takes five more minutes.
KOWALSKI: At Bridgewater, I managed forty buildings. Now I manage one building, and a cat is in charge.
SAM: That's not true. The rules are in charge. And the rules say the cat is in charge.

[Scene: Saturday, June 12, 7:45 p.m. The roof, at sunset. Twenty-four new panels shine on the south side. Two wooden boxes on wheels hold small tomato plants. In the southwest corner, an old folding chair faces the harbor. Diego, Marcus, and Eddie stand together. Mrs. Esposito waters her basil.]

DIEGO: Marcus, look at the app! The panels made forty-two kilowatt-hours today! That's forty-two hours of microwave!
MARCUS: Forty-two. On a sunny day in June. That's good. Very good.
DIEGO: Ms. Park says I can do my science fair project on it. "How much sun does a building eat?"
MARCUS: Great title. Better than mine. In sixth grade, I did "Why is my bike slow?" The answer was "because you're lazy."
MRS. ESPOSITO: Eddie, your chair is in the right spot? Facing the right way?
EDDIE: Facing the harbor. The Statue on the left. Governors Island on the right. Exactly like nineteen seventy-four.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Sam put it there this morning. With a level. He measured it. Of course.
EDDIE: Of course. Diego, come here. Sit on the chair. One minute.
DIEGO: Me? It's your chair.
EDDIE: Sit. Look. That's the view Peggy and I had. Every Fourth of July. For thirty-eight years.
DIEGO: It's beautiful. You can see the whole harbor. And the boats. And the ferry going to Rockaway.
EDDIE: Someday, when I'm not here, this chair is yours. You and whoever you love. On the Fourth of July. With a blanket.
DIEGO: Eddie, you always say "when I'm not here."
EDDIE: Because it's true, kid. But not this year. This year, I'm here. And on the Fourth, I'm sitting in this chair. And you're bringing me a beer. A root beer for you.
MARCUS: What about the panels, Eddie? Do they ruin the view?
EDDIE: Honestly? They're okay. They look like a field of blue water. Facing the sun. They're working. Like everybody else in this building.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And my tomatoes are working too. Look. Three small green ones. I'll roll them into the sun tomorrow at ten.
DIEGO: Eddie, did you meet Mango? Priya's dog?
EDDIE: I met Mango. She licked my cane. Biscuit watched from the window. Very suspicious.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Biscuit is not suspicious. Biscuit is supervising.
MARCUS: In this building, everybody supervises.
EDDIE: That's why it works. Nobody gets away with anything.
DIEGO: Not even the sun. It has to make forty-two kilowatt-hours. Or we'll send it a letter.
DIEGO: Mrs. Esposito, are the tomatoes part of the science fair too?
MRS. ESPOSITO: The tomatoes are not science, Diego. The tomatoes are family. My mother's seeds. From Bensonhurst. Nineteen sixty.
MARCUS: Sixty-seven-year-old seeds?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Saved every year. From the best tomato. Every summer. That's how you keep something alive. You save the best part. And plant it again.
EDDIE: Like this building. We saved the best part. And planted it again.
DIEGO: That's going in my science project. As the conclusion.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Then spell "Bensonhurst" correctly. Ms. Park will check.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **조건문 종합**
  - 2형(현재·미래의 가정): *If we installed panels, we'd use less electricity from gas plants.*
  - 3형(과거에 대한 가정): *If the panels had been there last summer, your bill would have been lower.* / *If we had done this last summer, I would have saved six hundred dollars.*
  - 혼합형(과거 → 현재 결과): *If the boiler hadn't died, we wouldn't be talking about any of this.*
  - 1형(현실적 미래): *If we don't do it now, we'll keep saying "next year."*
- **에너지 어휘**
  - *solar panels* 태양광 패널 / *kilowatt-hour (kWh)* 킬로와트시 / *payback period* 투자금 회수 기간
  - *incentive* 지원금, 장려금 / *tax credit* 세액 공제 / *emissions* 배출량 / *heat pump* 히트펌프
  - *casts a shadow* 그림자를 드리우다 / *raised boxes on wheels* 바퀴 달린 높은 화분 상자
- **You're competing with science.** — "과학이랑 경쟁하는 거예요."
- **Physics is on your side.** — "물리학이 당신 편이에요." 굴뚝 그림자 덕분에 에디의 자리가 남았다.
- **I'm not against the sun. I'm against losing my corner.** — 반대의 대상을 좁히는 화법(66화의 *I'm not against housing*과 같은 구조).
- **You save the best part. And plant it again.** — 부인의 씨앗 이야기이자 이 화의 주제.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 기술 용어 강세: *KIL-o-watt-HOUR*, *PAY-back PER-iod*, *IN-cen-tive*, *E-MIS-sions*.
- 3형 조건문은 끊어 읽는다: *If we had done this last summer, | I would have saved | six hundred dollars.* — *had done*과 *would have saved*를 또렷하게.
- 에디의 페기 이야기는 회의의 속도를 갑자기 늦춘다. *A blanket, | two beers, | Michael asleep on her lap.*
- 표결의 *Aye*마다 각자의 이유를 붙이는 리듬을 살린다.
- 마지막 옥상 장면은 해 질 녘의 부드러운 톤. *You save the best part. | And plant it again.*
