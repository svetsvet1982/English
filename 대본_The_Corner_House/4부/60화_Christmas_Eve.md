# THE CORNER HOUSE — 4부 60화

## Christmas Eve (크리스마스이브)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 4부 6화(통산 60화) · 시기: 2026년 12월 24일(목) 오후~밤 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 2A 토미의 부엌 → 2A 거실의 긴 식탁 → 건물 앞 계단(스툽)
> - 등장인물: TOMMY, GINA, LUCA, AUNT CONNIE, FRANKIE, EDDIE
> - 사건: 앤젤라가 세상을 떠난 2019년 이후 토미는 '일곱 가지 생선' 만찬을 한 번도 차리지 않았다. 올해 지나가 엄마의 손글씨 레시피 카드를 들고 와서 다시 하자고 한다. 바칼라는 사흘 동안 물에 담가 뒀다. 밤에는 토미의 고모 코니(84)와 사촌 프랭키, 에디가 식탁에 앉는다. 프랭키는 엄마에게 베팅 빚을 털어놓는다. 자정 무렵 스툽에서 토미와 지나가 오래 묵은 이야기를 꺼낸다. 토미가 놓친 딸의 학예회들, 그리고 서로에게 주는 선물.
> - 학습 포인트: 가족 화법과 화해 표현(I owe you an apology. / Let's put it behind us. / I should have been there. / You did your best.), 과거 습관 회상(She used to… / Every year, we would…), 이탈리아계 음식 이름 발음
> - 미국 현안: 이탈리아계 미국인의 명절 전통, 오르는 해산물값, 스포츠 베팅 빚과 가족, 혼자 보내는 노인의 명절
> - 연결: 47화 앤젤라 이야기와 49화 '비밀 금지' 냅킨. 42화 프랭키의 베팅과 '엄마한텐 말하지 마'. 26화 토미 집안의 1910년 나폴리→컬럼비아 스트리트 이주. 53화 지미의 갈고리. 54화 '갈고리는 놓지 않는다'.

## 대본

[Scene: Thursday, 3:00 p.m. Apartment 2A. A small kitchen full of steam. A bowl of salt cod soaks in the sink. Gina spreads old index cards on the counter. Luca, four, stands on a chair in a dinosaur apron.]

GINA: Okay. Seven cards. Seven fishes. All in Mom's handwriting. Look, Dad. She even wrote "Tommy, DON'T touch" on the calamari one.
TOMMY: She wrote that in two thousand three. I burned the calamari once. Once. And she never let me forget it.
GINA: Then don't touch the calamari. You're on the baccalà. It's been soaking since Monday.
TOMMY: Three days. Changing the water twice a day. Like a baby. A very salty baby.
LUCA: Nonno, why is the fish in the bath?
TOMMY: Because it's salty, buddy. Very, very salty. The water takes the salt out. Slowly.
LUCA: Does the fish like the bath?
TOMMY: The fish doesn't like anything anymore. The fish is retired.
GINA: Dad. Don't traumatize him. Luca, the fish is sleeping. Okay?
LUCA: Okay. Goodnight, fish.
TOMMY: Gina, can I say something? Before we start?
GINA: Of course.
TOMMY: I didn't want to do this. When you called last week, I almost said no. Again.
GINA: I know. You said no in twenty twenty. And twenty twenty-one. And every year since.
TOMMY: Because it was her night. Your mother used to start on the twenty-first. Three days of cooking. She used to sing. Terrible singing. Dean Martin. Every song wrong.
GINA: "That's Amore." She sang it to the clams. Every single year.
TOMMY: And every year, we would fight about the eel. Your grandmother wanted eel. Your mother hated eel. I just wanted to watch the game.
GINA: And every year you lost the fight.
TOMMY: Every year. Happily. After she died, I couldn't come into this kitchen on Christmas Eve. It was too quiet.
GINA: That's why I'm here. To make it loud again. With a four-year-old. He's very loud.
LUCA: I'm VERY loud!
TOMMY: You are. You're the loudest Russo since your great-grandfather. He sold fish on Columbia Street. In nineteen twenty. He yelled prices. People heard him in New Jersey.
GINA: Okay. Fish one: baccalà. Fish two: fried calamari. Fish three: shrimp scampi. Four: linguine with clams. Five: fried smelts. Six: octopus salad. Seven: stuffed clams.
TOMMY: No eel?
GINA: No eel. Mom won that one permanently.
TOMMY: Good. Eel is forty dollars a pound now. Everything is crazy. The shrimp was twenty-two. The clams, eighteen a dozen.
GINA: I know. I paid. Don't look at the receipt. It's my Christmas present to you. And to Mom.
TOMMY: Gina... You don't have to pay. You make less money now.
GINA: I make less money. And I have more time. That's the trade. And tonight, the time is for this.
LUCA: Nonno, can I put the flour on the squid?
TOMMY: Yes. Gently. Like snow. Not like a snowball.
LUCA: Like snow!
GINA: Dad, he's covered in flour. Completely.
LUCA: Nonno, did Nonna like fish?
TOMMY: Nonna loved fish. She loved clams the most. She called them "little treasure boxes."
LUCA: Treasure boxes! Can I open one?
TOMMY: Later. With a grown-up. Clams fight back.

[Scene: 7:30 p.m. The living room of 2A. Two tables pushed together, covered with a red cloth. Candles. Aunt Connie, eighty-four, sits at the head of the table in a fur hat she refuses to take off. Frankie sits next to her. Eddie sits across.]

AUNT CONNIE: Tommy. Come here. Let me look at you. You got fat.
TOMMY: Merry Christmas to you too, Zia Connie.
AUNT CONNIE: It's a compliment. Skinny men can't be trusted. Your uncle was skinny. He was a crook.
FRANKIE: Ma! That was Dad!
AUNT CONNIE: I know who it was. I was married to him for fifty-one years. I'm allowed.
EDDIE: Mrs. Russo, I'm Eddie O'Neill. Third floor. Thank you for letting an Irishman sit at an Italian table.
AUNT CONNIE: An Irishman. At the Feast of the Seven Fishes. Are you Catholic?
EDDIE: Since nineteen forty-three. Visitation Church. Every Sunday. Almost.
AUNT CONNIE: Then you're half Italian already. Sit. Eat. You're too thin too.
GINA: Okay, everybody. Fish number one. Baccalà. Dad made it. Three days of work.
AUNT CONNIE: Let me taste. Hm. Hmm. It's good. It's not Angela's. But it's good.
TOMMY: Thank you, Zia. That's the nicest thing you've ever said to me.
AUNT CONNIE: Don't get used to it. Angela was a better cook than all of us. God rest her soul. She used to make me take home three containers.
EDDIE: She made me take home four. Every Christmas. I lived on her scampi until January sixth.
GINA: She did? I didn't know that.
EDDIE: She'd knock on my door. December twenty-sixth. "Eddie, you're alone. Take this." I never said no. Nobody said no to Angela.
FRANKIE: Can I... can I say something? Before the calamari?
AUNT CONNIE: Before the calamari? It must be serious. Nobody interrupts calamari.
FRANKIE: Ma. I have to tell you something. I lost money. A lot of money. Betting. On my phone.
AUNT CONNIE: Betting? Like your uncle Vinny? With the horses?
FRANKIE: Worse. The phone is faster than horses. I lost about six thousand dollars this year. Tommy knows. He told me to stop. I deleted the app in October.
AUNT CONNIE: Six thousand dollars. Francesco. Look at me.
FRANKIE: I'm sorry, Ma. I didn't want you to know. I was ashamed.
AUNT CONNIE: Why did you tell me now? On Christmas Eve? In front of the Irishman?
FRANKIE: Because Gina has a rule. With Tommy. "No secrets." It's on a napkin. On his fridge. I saw it when I came in.
TOMMY: It's true. It's right there. Next to the Mets schedule.
AUNT CONNIE: Hmph. And are you still betting? Tell me the truth. On your father's memory.
FRANKIE: No. Two months. Not one bet. I go to a meeting. On Tuesdays. In a church basement. In Bay Ridge.
AUNT CONNIE: A meeting. Good. Your father never went to a meeting. He went to the track instead.
FRANKIE: So you're not angry?
AUNT CONNIE: I'm furious. I'm very angry. And I'm proud. Both. An Italian mother can be both at the same time. It's our talent.
EDDIE: Irish mothers too. My mother was furious and proud for forty years. Mostly furious.
AUNT CONNIE: Francesco, come here. Give me a kiss. And next time, you tell me first. Not after. I'm eighty-four. I don't have time for "after."
FRANKIE: I owe you an apology, Ma. A big one.
AUNT CONNIE: You owe me six thousand dollars of cannoli. But we'll put it behind us. Now pass the calamari before it gets cold.
TOMMY: Okay. The calamari. Gina made it. I didn't touch it. I swear on Angela's card.
GINA: He really didn't. I watched him. Luca did the flour.
AUNT CONNIE: The little one helped? Then it's perfect. Mangia, everybody. Mangia.
EDDIE: Tommy, tell your aunt about the building. The co-op.
AUNT CONNIE: What co-op? What are you buying? You can't even buy a good shirt.
TOMMY: Maybe the building, Zia. All of us together. If the landlord sells.
AUNT CONNIE: Your grandfather rented on Columbia Street for forty years. He never owned a brick. If you buy this building, I'll light a candle at Saint Rosalia's. A big one.
FRANKIE: She means it. Ma's candles are famous. The church almost caught fire in nineteen ninety.

[Scene: 11:45 p.m. The stoop outside the building. Cold. Christmas lights blink in Ahmed's store window across the street. Luca sleeps inside on Tommy's couch. Tommy and Gina sit on the top step with two small glasses of anisette.]

GINA: Zia Connie took five containers. Five. And Eddie took four.
TOMMY: Just like your mother used to do. Everybody leaves with food. Nobody leaves hungry. That's the rule.
GINA: Dad. Can I tell you something? Since we're doing no secrets.
TOMMY: Uh-oh. Another secret?
GINA: Not a secret. A feeling. An old one. When I was a kid, I used to be angry at the bar.
TOMMY: At the bar? Why?
GINA: Because you were always there. My school plays. My recitals. My soccer games. Mom came. You were pouring beer.
TOMMY: Gina...
GINA: I was Mary in the Christmas pageant. Third grade. I had one line. "There's no room at the inn." I looked for you. You weren't there.
TOMMY: I remember. I remember that night. It was a Friday. Ramon called in sick. I couldn't close.
GINA: I know. I know that now. I didn't know it then. I just knew the seat next to Mom was empty.
TOMMY: I should have been there. I should have closed the bar. One Friday. I'm sorry, sweetheart. I owe you that apology. Twenty-five years late.
GINA: You did your best. I know you did. You were keeping us alive. The rent, the school, Mom's medicine later.
TOMMY: But you were eight. An eight-year-old doesn't care about rent. She cares about the empty seat.
GINA: Okay. Thank you for saying that. I didn't know I needed to hear it. But I did.
TOMMY: Let's put it behind us. Okay? Not forget it. Just... put it behind us. Like the eel.
GINA: Like the eel. Okay. Now I have a present for you. Open it.
TOMMY: What is this? A frame? It's... it's her card. The calamari card. "Tommy, DON'T touch."
GINA: I framed it. For the bar. Hang it behind the register. So she's always telling you what to do.
TOMMY: She'll be bossing me around forever. Perfect. That's perfect. Thank you.
GINA: You're crying again.
TOMMY: It's the anisette. It's strong. Okay. My turn. Here. It's small.
GINA: An envelope? Dad, what's this?
TOMMY: Open it. It's your mother's apron. The one with the lemons. No, wait. The apron is in the bag. The envelope is something else.
GINA: Twenty-five hundred dollars? In cash? Dad, what—
TOMMY: For your share. Not for my share. For yours. In the co-op. If we win.
GINA: Dad, I don't live in the building. I can't buy a share.
TOMMY: Not now. But someday, maybe. 4C. Or 3B. For you and Luca. Your mother always said, "Gina should live close." I'm saving it for that.
GINA: Dad. You have four hundred dollars in your checking account.
TOMMY: I had four hundred. Then I sold the jukebox. To a guy in Bushwick. Twenty-six hundred dollars. He wanted it for his record store.
GINA: You sold the jukebox? The jukebox you regretted buying?
TOMMY: Small regrets. Big decisions. You taught me that.
GINA: What will you play at the bar now? No jukebox.
TOMMY: Eddie has a radio. Irish music on Sundays. Dolores likes it.
GINA: Since when do you know what Dolores likes?
TOMMY: Since she started knocking on Eddie's floor. Everybody knows everything in this building.
GINA: Merry Christmas, Dad.
TOMMY: Merry Christmas, Gina. Your mother would love tonight. Every fish. Every container. Even the flour on the kid.
GINA: She'd say the baccalà needed more lemon.
TOMMY: She'd be right. She was always right. Come on. It's midnight. Let's go get the kid.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **화해 표현**
  - *I owe you an apology.* — "너한테 사과할 게 있어." 진지한 사과의 시작.
  - *I should have been there.* — "내가 거기 있었어야 했는데." 과거에 대한 후회.
  - *You did your best.* — "넌 최선을 다했어." 상대의 사과를 받아 주는 말.
  - *Let's put it behind us.* — "이제 지난 일로 하자." 잊자는 게 아니라 넘어가자는 것.
  - *I didn't know I needed to hear it.* — "그 말이 필요했는지 몰랐어."
- **과거 습관 회상**
  - *She used to sing. / She used to make me take home three containers.* — used to + 동사원형.
  - *Every year, we would fight about the eel.* — would는 과거에 반복된 행동을 이야기처럼 들려준다.
- **이탈리아계 음식 이름**
  - *baccalà* 바칼라(소금에 절인 대구) [바-카-**라**]
  - *calamari* 칼라마리(오징어 튀김) [칼-러-**마**-리]
  - *scampi* 스캄피(마늘 버터 새우) [**스캠**-피]
  - *smelts* 빙어류 작은 생선 / *octopus salad* 문어 샐러드 / *stuffed clams* 속을 채운 조개
  - *anisette* 아니스 향 리큐어 [애-니-**셋**]
- **Zia** — 이탈리아어로 고모·이모. *Mangia!* — "먹어!"
- **Feast of the Seven Fishes** — 이탈리아계 미국인의 크리스마스이브 해산물 만찬.
- **Skinny men can't be trusted.** — 코니 고모식 과장. 칭찬인지 욕인지 모르게 하는 노인의 말투.
- **I'm furious. And I'm proud. Both.** — 상반된 감정을 함께 말하는 표현.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 음식 이름은 강세 음절을 분명하게. 특히 *baccalà*는 마지막 음절에 강세.
- 코니 고모는 느리고 단정적으로, 문장 끝을 확 내린다: *You got fat.↘ / It's a compliment.↘*
- 프랭키의 고백은 머뭇거리며: *Can I... can I say something?* — 말이 끊긴다.
- 마지막 장면은 추운 밤의 속삭임. *There's no room at the inn.* — 지나가 여덟 살의 자신을 떠올리며 조금 아이처럼.
- 토미의 *I should have been there.*는 *should have*를 줄이지 말고 천천히, 또박또박.
- *Small regrets. | Big decisions.* — 52화의 문장을 다시 꺼낸다. 미소 짓듯이.

## 한글 해석

지나: 좋아요. 카드 일곱 장. 생선 일곱 가지. 전부 엄마 글씨예요. 봐요, 아빠. 칼라마리 카드엔 "토미, 손대지 마"라고까지 써 놨어요.
토미: 그거 2003년에 쓴 거야. 내가 칼라마리를 한 번 태웠거든. 딱 한 번. 근데 평생 그 얘기를 하더라.
지나: 그럼 칼라마리는 손대지 마요. 아빠는 바칼라 담당이에요. 월요일부터 담가 뒀잖아요.
토미: 사흘이야. 하루에 두 번씩 물 갈아 주고. 아기처럼. 아주 짠 아기.
루카: 논노, 생선이 왜 목욕해요?
토미: 짜서 그래, 꼬마야. 아주, 아주 짜거든. 물이 소금을 빼 줘. 천천히.
루카: 생선은 목욕 좋아해요?
토미: 생선은 이제 아무것도 안 좋아해. 생선은 은퇴했어.
지나: 아빠. 애한테 트라우마 주지 마요. 루카, 생선은 자고 있는 거야. 알았지?
루카: 알았어요. 잘 자, 생선.
토미: 지나, 하나 말해도 돼? 시작하기 전에?
지나: 물론이죠.
토미: 사실 하기 싫었어. 지난주에 네가 전화했을 때, 또 싫다고 할 뻔했어.
지나: 알아요. 2020년에도 싫다고 했잖아요. 2021년에도요. 그 뒤로 매년요.
토미: 그건 엄마의 밤이었으니까. 네 엄마는 21일부터 시작하곤 했어. 사흘 동안 요리했지. 노래도 불렀어. 끔찍한 노래. 딘 마틴. 가사는 매번 틀리고.
지나: "That's Amore." 조개한테 불러 줬어요. 매년 한 번도 빠짐없이요.
토미: 그리고 매년 우린 장어 때문에 싸웠지. 네 할머니는 장어를 원했고. 네 엄마는 장어를 싫어했고. 난 그냥 경기를 보고 싶었고.
지나: 그리고 매년 아빠가 졌죠.
토미: 매년. 기꺼이. 엄마가 죽고 나서는 크리스마스이브에 이 부엌에 못 들어오겠더라. 너무 조용해서.
지나: 그래서 내가 온 거예요. 다시 시끄럽게 하려고요. 네 살짜리랑요. 얘 엄청 시끄러워요.
루카: 나 엄청 시끄러워요!
토미: 그렇지. 네 증조할아버지 이후로 제일 시끄러운 루소야. 그분은 컬럼비아 스트리트에서 생선을 팔았어. 1920년에. 값을 외쳤지. 뉴저지에서도 들렸대.
지나: 좋아요. 생선 하나: 바칼라. 둘: 칼라마리 튀김. 셋: 새우 스캄피. 넷: 조개 링귀니. 다섯: 작은 생선 튀김. 여섯: 문어 샐러드. 일곱: 속 채운 조개.
토미: 장어는 없어?
지나: 장어 없어요. 그건 엄마가 영원히 이겼어요.
토미: 잘됐다. 장어가 요즘 파운드당 40달러야. 다 미쳤어. 새우는 22달러였고. 조개는 열두 개에 18달러.
지나: 알아요. 내가 냈잖아요. 영수증 보지 마요. 아빠한테 주는 크리스마스 선물이에요. 엄마한테도요.
토미: 지나… 네가 낼 필요 없어. 너 이제 돈 덜 벌잖아.
지나: 돈은 덜 벌죠. 대신 시간이 더 많아요. 그게 맞바꾼 거예요. 그리고 오늘 밤, 그 시간은 이걸 위한 거예요.
루카: 논노, 오징어에 밀가루 뿌려도 돼요?
토미: 그래. 살살. 눈처럼. 눈덩이처럼 말고.
루카: 눈처럼!
지나: 아빠, 얘 밀가루 범벅이에요. 완전히요.
루카: 논노, 할머니도 생선 좋아했어요?
토미: 할머니는 생선을 정말 좋아했지. 조개를 제일 좋아했어. '작은 보물 상자'라고 불렀어.
루카: 보물 상자! 하나 열어 봐도 돼요?
토미: 나중에. 어른이랑 같이. 조개는 반격하거든.
코니 고모: 토미. 이리 와. 얼굴 좀 보자. 살쪘네.
