# THE CORNER HOUSE — 4부 58화

## The Pantry Line (푸드 팬트리의 줄)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 4부 4화(통산 58화) · 시기: 2026년 12월 12일(토) 아침~점심 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 밴 브런트 스트리트 성당 지하 푸드 팬트리 → 줄이 늘어선 인도 → 다시 지하
> - 등장인물: GLORIA, MRS. ESPOSITO, TOMMY, MARISOL, DIEGO, MR. HAYES, LUIS, JESSE, EDDIE, TASHA
> - 사건: 추수감사절에 본 줄을 계기로 건물 사람들이 성당 푸드 팬트리 봉사에 나선다. 18년째 팬트리를 운영하는 글로리아가 규칙을 알려 준다. 존엄, 고르는 쪽은 손님, 묻지 않기. 줄에는 은퇴한 버스 기사 헤이스 씨와 배달 기사 루이스가 있다. 헤이스 씨는 새 근로 요건 때문에 SNAP이 끊겼고, 루이스는 겨울 배달 수입이 줄었다. 정오쯤 달걀이 떨어진다. 디에고가 아이디어를 내고, 제시는 휴대폰 문자 순번 시스템을 만들겠다고 나선다.
> - 학습 포인트: 봉사·나눔 어휘(volunteer, shift, client, intake, sign-in, distribution), 역할 정하기와 반복 지시(You're on… / Make sure you… / Remember: …), 공감 표현(That sounds really hard. / Take your time. / No need to explain. / I'm glad you came.)
> - 미국 현안: SNAP(푸드스탬프) 축소와 확대된 근로 요건, 2025년 정부 셧다운 때 지급이 끊겼던 기억, 겨울철 배달 노동자의 수입 감소, 노인 빈곤, 푸드 팬트리 수요 급증
> - 연결: 55화 '추수감사절 줄 200명'. 16화 배달 기사 루이스(무보험, 앱 독립 계약자). 52화 지나의 새 직장 오픈 블록(푸드 팬트리용 기술). 46화 제시 실직·프리랜서. 51화 부인의 선거 사무원 경력.

## 대본

[Scene: Saturday, 7:00 a.m. The basement of the church on Van Brunt. Folding tables. Boxes of canned food, bread, onions, and eggs. A woman in a purple fleece holds a clipboard bigger than Mrs. Esposito's.]

GLORIA: Good morning, new people! I'm Gloria, and I've run this pantry for eighteen years. The coffee's in the corner, and it's terrible. Drink it anyway.
TOMMY: Terrible coffee? Then I have a feeling I'm going to like it here.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Gloria, your clipboard is bigger than mine, and I honestly didn't know that was possible.
GLORIA: Dolores, I've seen your clipboard, and mine is strictly for emergencies. Okay, listen up. We open at nine, but the line starts at six thirty, and it's already around the corner.
MARISOL: At seven in the morning? In this cold?
GLORIA: In this cold. Last winter, we served about two hundred families a week. This month, it's four hundred, and on some Saturdays it's even more.
DIEGO: Four hundred families! Why so many more?
GLORIA: Good question, young man. Prices went up, but wages didn't, and SNAP got smaller. That's food stamps. The government changed the rules, and a lot of people lost their benefits.
MARISOL: You mean the new work requirements. I see it at my job. Older adults, up to sixty-four, now have to prove their work hours, or they lose it.
GLORIA: And the paperwork is a nightmare. If people miss one form, their benefits are gone for three months, just like that.
TOMMY: And last year, during the shutdown, didn't SNAP stop completely for a few weeks?
GLORIA: It did, in November of twenty twenty-five. People were crying in this basement, and I'll never forget it. Some families still haven't caught up.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Okay, that's enough bad news. What do you need from us?
GLORIA: Assignments. Dolores, you're on sign-in. You take names, household size, and zip code, and that's it. No other questions.
MRS. ESPOSITO: No other questions? Not even why? I always ask why.
GLORIA: Never why. Rule number one: dignity. Nobody has to explain why they're hungry. Nobody.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Understood. Dignity. I'll write it at the top of my page, in capital letters.
GLORIA: Tommy, you're on bread and eggs. It's one loaf per family and one dozen eggs per family. Make sure you count. Eggs are like gold right now.
TOMMY: One loaf, one dozen, and I count. Got it.
GLORIA: Marisol, you're on the produce table. Rule number two: client choice. People choose what they want, because we're not a cafeteria, so please don't fill the bag for them.
MARISOL: Client choice, so I let them pick. Got it.
DIEGO: What about me? I'm only ten, but I'm really strong for my age.
GLORIA: You're on the most important job, which is the runner. When a table runs out of something, you run to the storage room and bring more, as fast as you can.
DIEGO: I'm fast! I'm the fastest in my whole class!
GLORIA: Perfect. And rule number three: smile, but not a sad smile. Just a normal smile, like you're happy to see your neighbors, because you are.
TOMMY: A normal smile? I can do that. I do it every day behind the bar.
GLORIA: Good. Remember: dignity, choice, normal smile. Now let's open the doors before those people freeze.

[Scene: 9:40 a.m. The tables near the door. A long line goes up the stairs and out to the sidewalk. Tommy stands behind the bread and eggs. Jesse has arrived to help carry boxes.]

TOMMY: Good morning. Here's one loaf, and here are your eggs. Have a good weekend.
JESSE: Tommy, I brought ten more boxes in from the truck. Where do you want them?
TOMMY: Stack them behind me, and do it carefully. Those are eggs, Jesse, not footballs.
MR. HAYES: Morning. Could I get the whole wheat, if you have it?
TOMMY: Whole wheat, here you go, sir. And here's a dozen eggs. Wait a second. Mr. Hayes? From the B61?
MR. HAYES: Tommy Russo, from The Hook! Well, look at that. You actually remember me?
TOMMY: You drove the B61 for... how long was it, twenty years? You used to stop right in front of my bar every morning.
MR. HAYES: It was twenty-six years, and I retired in twenty nineteen. You gave me free coffee every morning, and it was terrible.
TOMMY: Everybody says that. Every single person.
MR. HAYES: I didn't think I'd see you here, of all places. I'm a little embarrassed, to be honest.
TOMMY: Mr. Hayes, no need to explain. I'm glad you came. Really, I mean that.
MR. HAYES: I'll explain anyway. I'm sixty-three, and my pension is small. I did part-time work at a parking garage, but they closed in August. Now the new rules say I have to work eighty hours a month, or no SNAP.
JESSE: Eighty hours a month? At sixty-three?
MR. HAYES: I'm looking, believe me, but nobody hires a sixty-three-year-old man with a bad back. Then I missed one form in October, and they cut me off. That's two hundred dollars a month, gone.
TOMMY: That sounds really hard. I'm sorry, Mr. Hayes. You deserve better than that.
MR. HAYES: It's okay. Well, it's not okay, but you know what I mean. This place helps, and Gloria helps. She already helped me fill out the appeal.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Mr. Hayes? I'm Dolores, from sign-in. I also know the appeals office, because I worked for the MTA for thirty years. Paperwork is my love language.
MR. HAYES: The MTA! Which department were you in?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Payroll, from nineteen seventy-eight to two thousand eight. So I probably printed your paychecks.
MR. HAYES: Then you owe me a raise.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Come back to my table when you're done. Take your time. We'll look at your appeal together.
MR. HAYES: Thank you, both of you. Tommy, is the bar still open? I heard some rumors on the bus.
TOMMY: It's still open. Come by sometime, because the coffee is free for bus drivers, for life.
MR. HAYES: Don't threaten me, Tommy.
LUIS: Hey. Tommy? Hey, man.
TOMMY: Luis! The brakes guy! How's the knee doing these days?
LUIS: The knee is good, and the new battery is good, because Marcus fixed everything. But winter is slow, man. There are almost no orders.
JESSE: Slow? I thought winter was busy, because everybody orders food when it's cold.
LUIS: That's what people think. But the app has more drivers now, way too many. And the pay per delivery went down, so some days I make sixty dollars in ten hours.
TOMMY: Sixty dollars. In ten hours. That's six dollars an hour, Luis, and I couldn't legally pay a dishwasher that.
LUIS: And that's before the tips, which are small now, because everybody's broke. So I'm here for my kids. I have two of them, six and nine.
TOMMY: Then take two dozen eggs, and don't argue with me.
LUIS: Tommy, Gloria said one dozen. I don't want to get you in trouble.
TOMMY: Gloria said one dozen per family, and you have a big family. Two kids is a big family. I'll talk to Gloria myself.
LUIS: Thank you, man, really. Can I ask you something, though? Don't tell Marcus I was here. He's proud of me, and I don't want him to worry.
TOMMY: Luis, Marcus would be the first person in line to help you. But okay, if that's what you want. It stays with me.
JESSE: Hey, Luis, can I ask a question? How long did you have to wait outside today?
LUIS: Two hours, since seven thirty. My hands are frozen, and I couldn't even feel my phone.
JESSE: Two hours. And everybody waits like that, every single week?
LUIS: Every week. Old people, moms with babies, in the rain and in the snow. You just stand there and hope they don't run out.

[Scene: 12:10 p.m. The basement. The egg boxes are empty. Gloria looks at the line. Diego runs in from the storage room, breathing hard.]

DIEGO: Gloria! Gloria! The storage room has no more eggs! Zero! I checked behind everything, even behind the onions!
GLORIA: I was afraid of that. We got three hundred dozen this week, but last year we got five hundred. The donations are down.
DIEGO: But there are still people outside, a lot of them. I counted, and there are forty-two.
GLORIA: You counted? Good runner. Okay, then we give them extra canned beans and peanut butter instead, because that's protein.
DIEGO: Can I say an idea? It might be dumb, though.
GLORIA: In this basement, no idea is dumb. Go ahead.
DIEGO: Mrs. Esposito's cousin in New Jersey has chickens. She has four chickens, and Tasha told me.
TASHA: Wait, that's my cousin in Jersey, not Mrs. Esposito's. And she has six now, and they lay a lot, so she gives eggs away every week.
GLORIA: Six chickens won't feed four hundred families, honey. But it's a start, so please ask her.
TASHA: I'll ask her, and I'll ask the bodegas too. Ahmed's store gets eggs from a farm upstate, so maybe he can donate the ones near the date.
EDDIE: Hello? Is this the soup kitchen? I brought coffee, and it's good coffee, because Tasha made it, not Tommy.
GLORIA: And who's this?
EDDIE: Eddie O'Neill, third floor. I'm too old to stand at a table all morning, but I can still carry a thermos.
GLORIA: Eddie, take that coffee outside to the line. There are forty-two people out there with frozen hands. Go slowly, hand it out, and smile normally.
EDDIE: I only smile normally. It's my best feature.
JESSE: Gloria, can I show you something? It's about the line outside.
GLORIA: Show me, but quickly, because I have a fight with a delivery truck in five minutes.
JESSE: People wait outside for two hours in the cold. What if they didn't have to? What if they got a text? "Your number is next. Come in thirty minutes."
GLORIA: Like at a restaurant, with the buzzer.
JESSE: Exactly. It's a simple text system. They sign up once and get a number, and then they wait at home or in a café, instead of on the sidewalk.
GLORIA: That sounds nice, but some of my clients don't have smartphones.
JESSE: It works on any phone, even the old flip phones, even Eddie's old phone, because it uses text messages only.
GLORIA: And how much does something like that cost? We don't have a budget for tech. We barely have a budget for tape.
JESSE: My friend Gina works at a nonprofit called Open Block, and they build exactly this kind of thing for pantries. It's free, or almost free. And I have a lot of free time, so I could set it up.
GLORIA: You'd really do that? For free?
JESSE: I lost my job to a chatbot, so for once, I'd like to use technology to actually help somebody.
GLORIA: Jesse, keep my people out of the snow. Do that, and I'll name a can of soup after you.
JESSE: I'll take the soup. Give me two weeks, and I'll call Gina tonight.
DIEGO: Can I help too? I'm good at numbers, and I'm already the runner.
JESSE: You can be the tester. You stand outside, you get a text, and you run in. It's the perfect job for a runner.
TASHA: Look at this. A ten-year-old and an unemployed guy are fixing the food system in a church basement.
GLORIA: That's how it always gets fixed, honey. Not by the people in charge. By the people in the basement.
DIEGO: Gloria, can I come back next Saturday? Is that allowed?
GLORIA: Every Saturday, if you want, at seven a.m. sharp. Bring your mother, and bring those fast legs.

[Scene: 1:30 p.m. The basement is empty. Folding chairs in a circle. Leftover sandwiches on a tray. Gloria takes off her shoes.]

GLORIA: Sit down and eat. Volunteers eat last, but they do eat. That's rule number four.
TOMMY: So how many families did we serve today?
GLORIA: Four hundred and thirty-one, which is a record. It's not the kind of record you want, though.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Mr. Hayes's appeal is ready. We found the missing form, which was a doctor's note about his back. That means he can be exempt from the work hours.
GLORIA: Dolores, you just got a man two hundred dollars a month, and you did it in forty minutes.
MRS. ESPOSITO: It was thirty-five minutes, actually. I had to sharpen my pencil first.
MARISOL: Gloria, can I ask you something? Why do you do this, for eighteen years, every single Saturday?
GLORIA: Honestly? Because I stood in that line myself, back in two thousand nine, during the recession. I lost my job at a bank, and I had two kids to feed.
DIEGO: You stood outside? In the cold, like the people today?
GLORIA: In the cold, for a whole year. And the woman behind the bread table never once asked me why. She just smiled, a normal smile, and I never forgot that.
TOMMY: So that's where the rules come from.
GLORIA: That's where everything comes from. Her name was Rose, and she died in twenty fifteen. After that, I took over her clipboard.
MARISOL: Mijo, you ran forty-one times today. You're going to sleep like a rock tonight.
DIEGO: Mom, can we come every Saturday? Even after we buy the building?
GLORIA: Buy the building? What building are we talking about?
MARISOL: It's a long story, and to be honest, it's not real yet.
GLORIA: In this neighborhood, everything starts as "not real yet." This pantry did too. Now eat your sandwich.
TOMMY: Gloria, I have a bar. Next spring, if we're still open, I want to do a fundraiser for eggs, and every dollar goes straight to you.
GLORIA: Tommy Russo, if you raise money for eggs, I'll drink your terrible coffee. Every single Saturday.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **역할 정하기와 반복 지시**
  - *You're on sign-in. / You're on bread and eggs.* — "넌 ~ 담당이야."
  - *Make sure you count.* — "꼭 세어."
  - *Remember: dignity, choice, normal smile.* — 지시를 짧은 키워드로 반복해 기억시킨다.
  - *Listen up.* — "다들 잘 들어."
- **공감 표현**
  - *No need to explain.* — "설명 안 해도 돼요."
  - *I'm glad you came.* — "와 줘서 다행이에요."
  - *That sounds really hard.* — "정말 힘드셨겠어요." 해결책보다 먼저 건네는 말.
  - *Take your time.* — "천천히 하세요."
  - *It stays with me.* — "나만 알고 있을게."
- **봉사 어휘** — *volunteer* 자원봉사자 / *client* 이용자(손님) / *sign-in* 접수 / *runner* 물품 운반 담당 / *produce* 농산물 / *appeal* 이의 신청 / *donations are down* 기부가 줄었다
- **SNAP** — Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program. 흔히 *food stamps*라고 부른다.
- **Paperwork is my love language.** — "서류 작업이 내 사랑의 언어예요." *love language*를 비튼 농담.
- **Don't threaten me.** — 평생 공짜 커피라는 '협박'을 받아치는 농담.
- **near the date** — 유통 기한이 임박한.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 글로리아의 지시는 빠르고 리듬감 있게. 18년 차의 자신감. *Rule number one: | dignity.*에서 끊는다.
- 공감 표현은 천천히, 낮게. *That sounds really hard.* — *really*에 강세, 끝을 내린다.
- 헤이스 씨는 처음엔 머뭇거리다가(*I'm a little embarrassed, to be honest.*) 설명할 때 단단해진다.
- 루이스의 *Sixty dollars. In ten hours.*는 토미가 되받으며 숫자를 천천히 곱씹는다.
- 디에고의 보고는 숨이 차게: *Gloria! Gloria! The storage room has no more eggs! Zero!*
- 마지막 글로리아의 *By the people in the basement.* — 조용하지만 분명하게.

## 한글 해석

글로리아: 좋은 아침이에요, 새로 오신 분들! 난 글로리아고, 이 팬트리를 18년째 운영하고 있어요. 커피는 구석에 있는데, 맛없어요. 그래도 마셔요.
토미: 맛없는 커피요? 그럼 여기가 마음에 들 것 같은 예감이 드네요.
에스포지토 부인: 글로리아, 클립보드가 내 것보다 크네요. 솔직히 그게 가능한 줄 몰랐어요.
글로리아: 돌로레스, 당신 클립보드 봤어요. 내 건 철저히 비상용이에요. 자, 다들 잘 들어요. 9시에 문 여는데 줄은 6시 반부터 서요. 벌써 모퉁이를 돌았어요.
마리솔: 아침 7시에요? 이 추위에요?
글로리아: 이 추위에요. 작년 겨울엔 일주일에 200가구 정도였어요. 이번 달엔 400가구고, 어떤 토요일엔 그보다 더 많아요.
디에고: 400가구요! 왜 그렇게 많아졌어요?
글로리아: 좋은 질문이야, 꼬마 신사. 물가는 올랐는데 월급은 안 올랐고, SNAP은 줄었어. 푸드스탬프 말이야. 정부가 규칙을 바꿔서 많은 사람이 혜택을 잃었어.
마리솔: 새 근로 요건 말씀이시죠. 제 직장에서도 봐요. 예순넷까지 노인들이 이제 근로 시간을 증명해야 해요. 안 그러면 잃고요.
글로리아: 그리고 서류 작업이 악몽이에요. 서류 하나만 빠뜨려도 혜택이 3개월 동안 끊겨요. 그냥 그렇게요.
토미: 그리고 작년 셧다운 때 SNAP이 몇 주 동안 완전히 멈추지 않았어요?
글로리아: 멈췄어요. 2025년 11월에요. 사람들이 이 지하에서 울었고, 난 그걸 절대 못 잊어요. 아직 회복 못 한 가족들도 있어요.
에스포지토 부인: 좋아요, 나쁜 소식은 그만하면 됐어요. 우리한테 필요한 게 뭐예요?
글로리아: 담당 배정이요. 돌로레스, 당신은 접수 담당이에요. 이름, 가구원 수, 우편번호만 받아요. 그게 다예요. 다른 질문은 없어요.
에스포지토 부인: 다른 질문은 없다고요? 이유도요? 난 늘 이유를 묻는데요.
글로리아: 이유는 절대 안 돼요. 규칙 1번, 존엄. 아무도 왜 배고픈지 설명할 필요가 없어요. 아무도요.
에스포지토 부인: 알겠어요. 존엄. 내 종이 맨 위에 대문자로 써 둘게요.
글로리아: 토미, 당신은 빵과 달걀 담당이에요. 가구당 빵 한 덩이, 달걀 한 판이에요. 꼭 세요. 요즘 달걀은 금 같아요.
토미: 빵 한 덩이, 달걀 한 판, 그리고 센다. 알겠어요.
글로리아: 마리솔, 당신은 농산물 테이블이에요. 규칙 2번, 고르는 건 손님. 원하는 걸 직접 골라요. 우린 급식소가 아니니까 봉지를 대신 채워 주지 마요.
마리솔: 고르는 건 손님, 그러니까 직접 고르게 둔다. 알겠어요.
디에고: 저는요? 열 살밖에 안 됐지만 나이에 비해 진짜 힘세요.
글로리아: 넌 제일 중요한 일이야. 운반 담당. 테이블에 뭐가 떨어지면 창고로 달려가서 최대한 빨리 더 가져오는 거야.
디에고: 저 빨라요! 우리 반 전체에서 제일 빨라요!
글로리아: 완벽해. 그리고 규칙 3번, 웃기. 근데 슬픈 미소 말고. 그냥 평범한 미소. 이웃을 봐서 반가운 것처럼. 실제로 반가우니까.
