# THE CORNER HOUSE — 4부 58화

## The Pantry Line (푸드 팬트리의 줄)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 4부 4화(통산 58화) · 시기: 2026년 12월 12일(토) 아침~점심 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 밴 브런트 스트리트 성당 지하 푸드 팬트리 → 줄이 늘어선 인도 → 다시 지하
> - 등장인물: GLORIA, MRS. ESPOSITO, TOMMY, MARISOL, DIEGO, MR. HAYES, LUIS, JESSE, EDDIE, TASHA
> - 사건: 추수감사절에 본 줄을 계기로 건물 사람들이 성당 푸드 팬트리 봉사에 나선다. 18년째 팬트리를 운영하는 글로리아가 규칙을 알려 준다. 존엄, 고르는 쪽은 손님, 묻지 않기. 줄에는 은퇴한 버스 기사 헤이스 씨와 배달 기사 루이스가 있다. 헤이스 씨는 새 근로 요건 때문에 SNAP이 끊겼고, 루이스는 겨울 배달 수입이 줄었다. 정오쯤 달걀이 떨어진다. 디에고가 아이디어를 내고, 제시는 휴대폰 문자 순번 시스템을 만들겠다고 나선다.
> - 학습 포인트: 봉사·나눔 어휘(volunteer, shift, client, intake, sign-in, distribution), 역할 정하기와 반복 지시(You're on… / Make sure you… / Remember: …), 공감 표현(That sounds really hard. / Take your time. / No need to explain. / I'm glad you came.)
> - 미국 현안: SNAP(푸드스탬프) 축소와 확대된 근로 요건, 2025년 정부 셧다운 때 지급이 끊겼던 기억, 겨울철 배달 노동자의 수입 감소, 노인 빈곤, 푸드 팬트리 수요 급증
> - 연결: 55화 '추수감사절 줄 200명'. 16화 배달 기사 루이스(무보험, 앱 독립 계약자). 52화 지나의 새 직장 오픈 블록(푸드 팬트리용 기술). 46화 제시 실직·프리랜서. 51화 부인의 선거 사무원 경력.

## 대본

[Scene: Saturday, 7:00 a.m. The basement of the church on Van Brunt. Folding tables. Boxes of canned food, bread, onions, and eggs. A woman in a purple fleece holds a clipboard bigger than Mrs. Esposito's.]

GLORIA: Good morning, new people! I'm Gloria. I've run this pantry for eighteen years. Coffee's in the corner. It's terrible. Drink it anyway.
TOMMY: Terrible coffee? I'm going to like it here.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Gloria, your clipboard is bigger than mine. I didn't know that was possible.
GLORIA: Dolores, I've seen your clipboard. Mine is for emergencies. Okay, listen up. We open at nine. The line starts at six thirty. It's already around the corner.
MARISOL: At seven? In this cold?
GLORIA: In this cold. Last winter, we served about two hundred families a week. This month, it's four hundred. Some Saturdays, more.
DIEGO: Four hundred! Why?
GLORIA: Good question, young man. Prices went up. Wages didn't. And SNAP got smaller. That's food stamps. The government changed the rules. A lot of people lost their benefits.
MARISOL: The new work requirements. I see it at my job. Older adults, up to sixty-four, now have to prove work hours. Or they lose it.
GLORIA: And the paperwork is a nightmare. People miss one form, and it's gone. For three months.
TOMMY: And last year, during the shutdown, didn't SNAP stop completely? For a few weeks?
GLORIA: It did. November. Twenty twenty-five. People were crying in this basement. I'll never forget it. Some families still haven't caught up.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Okay. What do you need from us?
GLORIA: Assignments. Dolores, you're on sign-in. Names, household size, zip code. That's it. No other questions.
MRS. ESPOSITO: No other questions? Not even why?
GLORIA: Never why. Rule number one: dignity. Nobody has to explain why they're hungry. Nobody.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Understood. Dignity. I'll write it at the top of my page.
GLORIA: Tommy, you're on bread and eggs. One loaf per family. One dozen eggs per family. Make sure you count. Eggs are gold right now.
TOMMY: One loaf. One dozen. Got it.
GLORIA: Marisol, you're on the produce table. Rule number two: client choice. People choose what they want. We're not a cafeteria. Don't fill the bag for them.
MARISOL: Client choice. Let them pick. Got it.
DIEGO: What about me? I'm ten. I'm strong.
GLORIA: You're on the most important job. The runner. When a table runs out of something, you run to the storage room. And you bring more. Fast.
DIEGO: I'm fast! I'm the fastest in my class!
GLORIA: Perfect. And rule number three: smile. But not a sad smile. A normal smile. Like you're happy to see your neighbors. Because you are.
TOMMY: A normal smile. I can do that. I do it every day. At the bar.
GLORIA: Good. Remember: dignity, choice, normal smile. Let's open the doors.

[Scene: 9:40 a.m. The tables near the door. A long line goes up the stairs and out to the sidewalk. Tommy stands behind the bread and eggs. Jesse has arrived to help carry boxes.]

TOMMY: Good morning. One loaf, and here are your eggs. Have a good weekend.
JESSE: Tommy, I brought ten more boxes from the truck. Where do they go?
TOMMY: Behind me. Stack them. Carefully. Those are eggs, Jesse. Not footballs.
MR. HAYES: Morning. Can I get the whole wheat? If you have it?
TOMMY: Whole wheat. Here you go, sir. And a dozen eggs. Wait. Mr. Hayes? From the B61?
MR. HAYES: Tommy Russo! From The Hook! Well, look at that. You remember me?
TOMMY: You drove the B61 for... how long? Twenty years? You used to stop right in front of my bar.
MR. HAYES: Twenty-six years. I retired in twenty nineteen. You gave me free coffee every morning. It was terrible.
TOMMY: Everybody says that. Every single person.
MR. HAYES: I didn't think I'd see you here. I'm a little embarrassed, to be honest.
TOMMY: No need to explain, Mr. Hayes. I'm glad you came. Really.
MR. HAYES: I'll explain anyway. I'm sixty-three. My pension is small. I did part-time work at a parking garage. They closed in August. The new rules say I have to work eighty hours a month. Or no SNAP.
JESSE: Eighty hours a month? At sixty-three?
MR. HAYES: I'm looking. Nobody hires a sixty-three-year-old man with a bad back. I missed one form in October. They cut me off. Two hundred dollars a month. Gone.
TOMMY: That sounds really hard. I'm sorry.
MR. HAYES: It's okay. It's not okay. But you know what I mean. This place helps. Gloria helps. She helped me fill out the appeal.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Mr. Hayes? I'm Dolores. From sign-in. I also know the appeals office. I worked for the MTA for thirty years. Paperwork is my love language.
MR. HAYES: The MTA! What department?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Payroll. Nineteen seventy-eight to two thousand eight. I probably printed your paychecks.
MR. HAYES: Then you owe me a raise.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Come back to my table after. Take your time. We'll look at your appeal together.
MR. HAYES: Thank you. Both of you. Tommy, is the bar still open? I heard rumors.
TOMMY: Still open. Come by. The coffee is free. For bus drivers. For life.
MR. HAYES: Don't threaten me, Tommy.
LUIS: Hey. Tommy? Hey, man.
TOMMY: Luis! The brakes guy! How's the knee?
LUIS: The knee is good. The new battery is good. Marcus fixed everything. But winter is slow. No orders.
JESSE: Slow? I thought winter was busy. Everybody orders food when it's cold.
LUIS: That's what people think. But the app has more drivers now. Too many. And the pay per delivery went down. Some days, I make sixty dollars. In ten hours.
TOMMY: Sixty dollars. In ten hours. That's six dollars an hour.
LUIS: Before the tips. And the tips are small now. Everybody's broke. So I'm here. For my kids. I have two. Six and nine.
TOMMY: Then take two dozen eggs. Don't argue.
LUIS: Tommy, Gloria said one dozen. I don't want trouble.
TOMMY: Gloria said one dozen per family. You have a big family. Two kids is a big family. I'll talk to Gloria.
LUIS: Thank you, man. Really. Can I ask you something? Don't tell Marcus I was here. He's proud of me. I don't want him to worry.
TOMMY: Luis. Marcus would be the first person in line to help you. But okay. It stays with me.
JESSE: Hey, Luis, can I ask a question? How long did you wait outside today?
LUIS: Two hours. Since seven thirty. My hands are frozen. I couldn't feel my phone.
JESSE: Two hours. And everybody waits like that? Every week?
LUIS: Every week. Old people. Moms with babies. In the rain, in the snow. You just stand there.

[Scene: 12:10 p.m. The basement. The egg boxes are empty. Gloria looks at the line. Diego runs in from the storage room, breathing hard.]

DIEGO: Gloria! Gloria! The storage room has no more eggs! Zero! I checked behind everything!
GLORIA: I was afraid of that. We got three hundred dozen this week. Last year, we got five hundred. The donations are down.
DIEGO: But there are still people outside. A lot. I counted. Forty-two.
GLORIA: You counted? Good runner. Okay. We give them extra canned beans. Peanut butter. It's protein.
DIEGO: Can I say an idea? It might be dumb.
GLORIA: In this basement, no idea is dumb. Go.
DIEGO: Mrs. Esposito's cousin in New Jersey has chickens. Four chickens. Tasha told me.
TASHA: Wait, that's my cousin. In Jersey. And she has six now. And they lay a lot. She gives eggs away every week.
GLORIA: Six chickens won't feed four hundred families, honey. But it's a start. Ask her.
TASHA: I'll ask her. And the bodegas. Ahmed's store gets eggs from a farm upstate. Maybe he can donate the ones near the date.
EDDIE: Hello? Is this the soup kitchen? I brought coffee. Good coffee. Tasha made it. Not Tommy.
GLORIA: Who's this?
EDDIE: Eddie O'Neill. Third floor. Too old to stand at a table. But I can carry a thermos.
GLORIA: Eddie, take that coffee outside. To the line. Forty-two people with frozen hands. Go slowly. Hand it out. Smile normally.
EDDIE: I only smile normally. It's my best feature.
JESSE: Gloria, can I show you something? It's about the line.
GLORIA: Show me. Quickly. I have a fight with a delivery truck in five minutes.
JESSE: People wait outside for two hours. In the cold. What if they didn't have to? What if they got a text? "Your number is next. Come in thirty minutes."
GLORIA: Like at a restaurant. With the buzzer.
JESSE: Exactly. A simple text system. They sign up once. They get a number. They wait at home. Or in a café. Not on the sidewalk.
GLORIA: Some of my clients don't have smartphones.
JESSE: It works on any phone. Even the old flip phones. Even Eddie's old phone. Text messages only.
GLORIA: How much does something like that cost? We don't have a budget for tech. We barely have a budget for tape.
JESSE: My friend Gina works at a nonprofit. Open Block. They build exactly this. For pantries. For free, or almost free. And I have a lot of free time. I could set it up.
GLORIA: You'd do that? For free?
JESSE: I lost my job to a chatbot. I'd like to use technology to help somebody. For once.
GLORIA: Jesse, keep my people out of the snow. Do that, and I'll name a can of soup after you.
JESSE: I'll take the soup. Give me two weeks. I'll call Gina tonight.
DIEGO: Can I help? I'm good at numbers. And I'm the runner.
JESSE: You can be the tester. You stand outside, you get a text, you run in. Perfect job for a runner.
TASHA: Look at this. A ten-year-old and an unemployed guy are fixing the food system.
GLORIA: That's how it always gets fixed. Not by the people in charge. By the people in the basement.
DIEGO: Gloria, can I come back next Saturday? Is that allowed?
GLORIA: Every Saturday, if you want. Seven a.m. Bring your mother. And your fast legs.

[Scene: 1:30 p.m. The basement is empty. Folding chairs in a circle. Leftover sandwiches on a tray. Gloria takes off her shoes.]

GLORIA: Sit. Eat. Volunteers eat last, but they eat. Rule number four.
TOMMY: How many families today?
GLORIA: Four hundred and thirty-one. A record. Not the kind of record you want.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Mr. Hayes's appeal is ready. We found the missing form. It was a doctor's note. About his back. He can be exempt from the work hours.
GLORIA: Dolores, you just got a man two hundred dollars a month. In forty minutes.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Thirty-five minutes. I had to sharpen my pencil.
MARISOL: Gloria, can I ask you something? Why do you do this? Eighteen years. Every Saturday.
GLORIA: Honestly? Because I stood in that line. In two thousand nine. The recession. I lost my job at a bank. I had two kids.
DIEGO: You stood outside? In the cold?
GLORIA: In the cold. For a year. And the woman behind the bread table never asked me why. She just smiled. A normal smile. I never forgot that.
TOMMY: So that's where the rules come from.
GLORIA: That's where everything comes from. Her name was Rose. She died in twenty fifteen. I took her clipboard.
MARISOL: Mijo, you ran forty-one times. You're going to sleep like a rock tonight.
DIEGO: Mom, can we come every Saturday? Even after we buy the building?
GLORIA: Buy the building? What building?
MARISOL: It's a long story. And it's not real yet.
GLORIA: In this neighborhood, everything starts as "not real yet." This pantry did too. Eat your sandwich.
TOMMY: Gloria, I have a bar. Next spring, if we're still open, I want to do a fundraiser. For eggs. Every dollar goes to you.
GLORIA: Tommy Russo, if you raise money for eggs, I'll drink your terrible coffee. Every Saturday.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **역할 정하기와 반복 지시**
  - *You're on sign-in. / You're on bread and eggs.* — "넌 ~ 담당이야."
  - *Make sure you count.* — "꼭 세어."
  - *Remember: dignity, choice, normal smile.* — 지시를 짧은 키워드로 반복해 기억시킨다.
  - *Listen up.* — "다들 잘 들어."
- **공감 표현**
  - *No need to explain.* — "설명 안 해도 돼요."
  - *I'm glad you came.* — "와 줘서 다행이에요."
  - *That sounds really hard.* — "정말 힘드셨겠어요." 해결책보다 먼저 건네는 말.
  - *Take your time.* — "천천히 하세요."
  - *It stays with me.* — "나만 알고 있을게."
- **봉사 어휘** — *volunteer* 자원봉사자 / *client* 이용자(손님) / *sign-in* 접수 / *runner* 물품 운반 담당 / *produce* 농산물 / *appeal* 이의 신청 / *donations are down* 기부가 줄었다
- **SNAP** — Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program. 흔히 *food stamps*라고 부른다.
- **Paperwork is my love language.** — "서류 작업이 내 사랑의 언어예요." *love language*를 비튼 농담.
- **Don't threaten me.** — 평생 공짜 커피라는 '협박'을 받아치는 농담.
- **near the date** — 유통 기한이 임박한.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 글로리아의 지시는 빠르고 리듬감 있게. 18년 차의 자신감. *Rule number one: | dignity.*에서 끊는다.
- 공감 표현은 천천히, 낮게. *That sounds really hard.* — *really*에 강세, 끝을 내린다.
- 헤이스 씨는 처음엔 머뭇거리다가(*I'm a little embarrassed, to be honest.*) 설명할 때 단단해진다.
- 루이스의 *Sixty dollars. In ten hours.*는 토미가 되받으며 숫자를 천천히 곱씹는다.
- 디에고의 보고는 숨이 차게: *Gloria! Gloria! The storage room has no more eggs! Zero!*
- 마지막 글로리아의 *By the people in the basement.* — 조용하지만 분명하게.
