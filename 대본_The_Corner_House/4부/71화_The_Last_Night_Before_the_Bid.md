# THE CORNER HOUSE — 4부 71화

## The Last Night Before the Bid (제안 전날 밤)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 4부 17화(통산 71화) · 시기: 2027년 2월 25일(목) 밤 ~ 26일(금) 새벽 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 문 닫은 더 훅 → 같은 곳(회원 표결) → 4A 마리솔의 부엌(제안서 마무리) → 새벽의 바
> - 등장인물: MARISOL, SAM, GINA, NADIA, TOMMY, MRS. ESPOSITO, EDDIE, PRIYA, DIEGO
> - 사건: 최종 최선 제안 마감 전날 밤. 모금 이후 소식이 둘 더 들어왔다. 지나가 신청한 재단이 영상을 보고 3월이 아니라 이번 주에 10만 달러 보조금을 확정했다. 랜드 트러스트 이사회도 땅값 한도를 140만으로 올렸다. 샘은 최종가 335만, 보일러 공제 4만 5천을 빼면 실질 330만 5천을 제시한다. 상대 매수자의 360만보다 30만 가까이 낮다. 대신 빠른 마감, 확정된 자금, 그리고 '사람'을 무기로 삼기로 한다. 다섯 가구가 정족수를 채워 공식 표결을 하고, 만장일치로 가결한다. 밤에는 각 가구의 편지, 살의 편지, 디에고의 그림을 제안서에 첨부한다. 새벽 1시, 토미는 텅 빈 바에서 앤젤라의 카드 앞에 선다.
> - 학습 포인트: 합의·표결 진행(Do we have a quorum? / The motion is to… / All in favor? / Let the record show… / It's unanimous.), 긴장 속의 짧은 발화, 확신과 불안의 표현(I'm confident that… / I'm not sure, but… / It's now or never.), 큰 숫자와 표결 숫자 읽기
> - 미국 현안: 경쟁 입찰에서 가격 외의 강점(자금 확정, 마감 속도, 공동체), 재단 보조금, 주민 자치의 의사 결정
> - 연결: 69화 2/26 정오 봉인 제안, 경쟁자 360만, 3/5 결정, 3/1 시 결정. 70화 모금 113,262+마이클 2천. 67화 재단 두 곳('3월에 연락'). 57화 정족수·가구당 1표. 60화 칼라마리 카드. 37화 살의 편지. 41화 디에고의 그림 실력.

## 대본

[Scene: Thursday, 8:00 p.m. The Hook, closed early. A sign on the door: "CLOSED FOR DEMOCRACY." Sam's laptop is connected to the TV. Marisol, Gina, Nadia, and Tommy sit close to the screen.]

SAM: Okay, this is the last time, I promise. I'll show you the final numbers, and then we vote.
TOMMY: You said "last time" on Monday, Sam. And you said it again on Tuesday.
SAM: This time it really is the last time, because the bid is due at noon tomorrow. There's no Saturday to fix anything.
GINA: First, the good news, and there's a lot of it. Can I go first?
MARISOL: Please do. After this week, we really need some good news.
GINA: You remember the foundation, the one that said, "Call us in March"? They called me on Tuesday. They saw Eddie's video and the article, so they moved us up. It's a hundred thousand dollars, approved and in writing.
TOMMY: A hundred thousand? Gina! Why didn't you tell me on Tuesday?
GINA: Because I wanted the letter in my hand first. It came at four o'clock today, and here it is, signed.
NADIA: And I have news too. My board had an emergency meeting last night, and they raised our limit for the land to one point four million.
MARISOL: One point four! Nadia, that's fifty thousand more than before.
NADIA: They watched the Community Board video, and Eddie's video, and they read Dolores's list, all forty-seven items. One board member actually cried at item thirty-one.
TOMMY: What was thirty-one?
NADIA: "Child changed buckets under leaking roof until four a.m." That was Diego.
SAM: Okay, so here's where we are, on slide one. The fundraiser brought in a hundred and fifteen thousand, two hundred sixty-two, with Michael's two thousand. Then add the foundation's hundred thousand, plus the land trust's extra fifty.
GINA: That's two hundred sixty-five thousand, which is more than we needed for three point three.
SAM: Exactly. So the question tonight is actually simple. How high do we go?
MARISOL: We know the other buyer offered three point six. That's the number we're up against.
SAM: We can't reach three point six. If we tried, our monthly fees would go up two hundred dollars a household, and Eddie can't pay that. Dolores can't pay that, and honestly, nobody should have to.
NADIA: So we don't try to beat their price. We beat their offer.
TOMMY: What's the difference? Isn't the offer just the price?
NADIA: Not at all, because their offer has conditions. They want their own inspection and their own financing, and they want to close in July. Ours closes in May, with financing that's already approved, except for the city.
GINA: And the city decides on Monday, March first, which is before Harborline decides on March fifth.
SAM: So here's my recommendation: three point three five. That's one point four for the land and one point nine five for the building. Minus forty-five for the boiler, it comes to three point three oh five, net.
TOMMY: Three point three oh five, versus three point six. That's three hundred thousand less, isn't it?
SAM: It's two hundred ninety-five thousand less. But our money is already in the bank, while theirs is still sitting in a meeting room somewhere.
MARISOL: And what about the fees, if we go to three point three five?
SAM: Compared to what I showed you in December, fees go up forty dollars a month per household. Except for Eddie, because I found a way, so his stays at nine fifty.
TOMMY: How did you manage that?
SAM: The foundation grant has a line for "senior tenant protection." I asked if we could use it for Eddie, and they said yes.
MARISOL: Sam, you're a hero. You're a quiet, spreadsheet kind of hero.
SAM: Please don't make it weird. Let's just vote.

[Scene: 9:00 p.m. The same room. Mrs. Esposito sits at a small table with her clipboard and a pen. Eddie, Sam, and Tommy sit in a row of chairs. Marisol stands. Gina and Nadia watch from the bar.]

MARISOL: Okay. This is an official members' meeting of the Corner House Tenants Association. Dolores, are you ready?
MRS. ESPOSITO: I'm ready, and the minutes are open. The date is February twenty-fifth, and the time is nine oh two p.m.
MARISOL: First question. Do we have a quorum?
MRS. ESPOSITO: We have five households, and all five are present. There's 2A, Mr. Russo, and 2B, myself. Then 3A, Mr. O'Neill, and 4A, Ms. Reyes. And 4B, Mr. Novak, who's voting for his household. So we have a quorum, and in fact, we have everybody.
EDDIE: Everybody. That's never happened before, not even at the block party.
MARISOL: Okay. The motion is to submit a best and final offer of three point three five million dollars, with a forty-five-thousand-dollar credit for the boiler, due tomorrow at noon. Do I hear a motion?
EDDIE: I so move. That's my first motion ever, at eighty-three years old.
TOMMY: I second it. And I'll be honest, my hands are sweating.
MARISOL: Is there any discussion? Anybody at all? Now is the time, so if you have a doubt, please say it.
SAM: I'll say one thing, as the treasurer. This is the edge, because three point three five is all the money we have. If something breaks in the first year, things will be tight. Really tight.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Something always breaks in the first year, Sam. It happens in every building, and in every marriage.
SAM: Exactly, so I want everybody to know where I stand. I'm voting yes. But I'm voting yes with my eyes open, and with one red cell on my spreadsheet.
EDDIE: Then we'll all keep our eyes open, together. That's the deal.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And for the minutes, Sam, what's in the red cell?
SAM: It's roof repairs in year two. That's forty thousand dollars, and it's not funded yet.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Then year two has a fundraiser. I'm writing it down: "Year two: another Super Bowl."
TOMMY: I have something too. It's a doubt, but only a small one.
MARISOL: Go ahead, Tommy.
TOMMY: If we lose... if they pick the other buyer... I don't want anybody to feel like it's their fault. Not Marisol, not Sam, nobody. We did everything we could.
MARISOL: Thank you, Tommy. That goes in the minutes too. Dolores?
MRS. ESPOSITO: It's written: "If we lose, it's nobody's fault." I've underlined it twice.
MARISOL: Is there any more discussion? ...No? Okay, then let's vote. All in favor?
TOMMY: Aye.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Aye.
EDDIE: Aye. That's for Peggy, and for Jimmy.
SAM: Aye, for 4B. Priya says aye too, by text, with three hearts.
MARISOL: And 4A votes aye. Are there any opposed? Any abstentions?
MRS. ESPOSITO: None. Let the record show: the motion carries, with five votes in favor, none opposed, and none abstaining. It's unanimous, at nine oh nine p.m.
EDDIE: Unanimous, in Red Hook. Somebody call the newspaper again, because that's a real story.
TOMMY: Five for five! It's like the Mets in nineteen eighty-six, in game six.
SAM: Tommy, I'm pretty sure the Mets lost game six that year.
TOMMY: They won game six! Game six was the Buckner game, and we won! Sam, you're from Ohio, so don't talk to me about game six.

[Scene: 11:15 p.m. Apartment 4A. The kitchen table is covered with paper. Gina types on a laptop. Priya sorts envelopes. Diego, in pajamas, finishes a drawing with colored pencils. Eddie sits by the window with tea.]

GINA: Okay, the price is in, the financing letters are in, and the city application number is in. Now comes the part that isn't numbers.
PRIYA: That's the letters, one from each household, and I have all five. Dolores typed hers on the typewriter, and of course it has a postscript.
EDDIE: Of course it does. So what does the postscript say?
PRIYA: It says, "P.S. I have a list. Please ask me for it." And she put her phone number under it.
GINA: Perfect. And then there's Sal's letter. Teresa sent a clean copy with his signature, from twenty twenty-three.
EDDIE: Read it out loud once more, would you? For the whole room.
GINA: "To my children. If you ever sell this building, offer it first to the people who live in it. They made it a home. I only made it a building. Sal."
EDDIE: He wrote that three years ago, and tomorrow, we're actually doing it. That's... that's really something.
PRIYA: Diego, is the drawing ready? We need to put it in the envelope tonight.
DIEGO: Almost. I'm doing the windows, because every window has a person in it. Look, that's Eddie with his radio, and that's Mrs. Esposito with Biscuit.
EDDIE: Is that me? I look very handsome in this one.
DIEGO: That's because I drew you younger, like at the lake in nineteen seventy.
PRIYA: And who's up on the roof?
DIEGO: Everybody else is. There's Mom, Tommy, Gina, Luca, Sam, you, Marcus, Tasha, Jesse, and Tess. And the land trust lady, Nadia, is there too, holding the land.
GINA: She's holding the land? What, like, in her hands?
DIEGO: The land is a green square, and she's holding it under the building, so it doesn't float away.
PRIYA: So where's Biscuit in the drawing?
DIEGO: She's in every window, because she moves around, just like in real life.
EDDIE: That's true. That cat has been in every apartment in this building, so if you ask me, she's the real owner.
EDDIE: And the green square is the best explanation of a land trust I've ever heard. Put it in the envelope, Gina, before a lawyer sees it.
MARISOL: Wait, what are you all doing? It's eleven thirty at night. Diego, bed, now.
DIEGO: Mom! I'm finishing the drawing! It's for the bid, so it's important!
MARISOL: It's beautiful, mijo, it really is. Okay, five more minutes, and then bed.
GINA: Marisol, you have to sign the cover letter as president. But read it first, so you know what you're signing.
MARISOL: Okay. "Dear Mr. Whitlock. Enclosed is our best and final offer. It is not the highest number you will receive. But it is the most certain. And it comes with the people who made your building worth buying."
EDDIE: Good. That's good, Marisol. Now sign it.
MARISOL: My hand is shaking. Look at it, it won't stop.
PRIYA: Use Gina's trick. Put your hand under the table for a minute, and then sign.
MARISOL: You can't sign anything under a table, Priya.
GINA: Then sign it shaking. That's honest too, and honestly, that's the whole letter.
MARISOL: Okay, it's signed. "Marisol Reyes, President. February twenty-fifth, eleven thirty-four p.m."
DIEGO: Can I sign too? Under you, really small?
MARISOL: ...Yes, but small. "Diego Reyes. Treasurer. In the future."

[Scene: Friday, 1:10 a.m. The Hook. Only one light is on, over the register. Tommy stands in front of the framed recipe card. Gina comes in from the street with the sealed envelope in a plastic folder.]

GINA: Dad, why are you still here? It's one in the morning.
TOMMY: I couldn't sleep, so I came down here. I've been talking to your mother.
GINA: Is she answering you?
TOMMY: She's saying, "Tommy, don't touch," the same as always.
GINA: Don't touch what, exactly?
TOMMY: Anything. The envelope, the number, the plan. She's saying, "You did your part, so now let it go."
GINA: I'm taking it to Harborline's office at nine, and Marisol and Nadia are coming with me. We'll hand it to the receptionist in person.
TOMMY: In person. Good, that's good. Good news comes by text, but important news comes in person.
GINA: Dad, can I ask you something? If we lose, what are you going to do?
TOMMY: If we lose? I'll pour beer here until May thirty-first, and then I'll pour beer somewhere else. Maybe I'll get a food truck and call it "The Hook on Wheels."
GINA: You'd hate a food truck, Dad. You know you would.
TOMMY: Sure, I'd hate it. But I'd hate it with you, and that's something.
GINA: Okay. I'll take that.
GINA: Dad, whatever happens tomorrow, you fought. I've never seen you fight like this before, not once.
TOMMY: That's because I've never had anything to fight for like this. Except you, and your mother.
GINA: And Luca.
TOMMY: And Luca. And Eddie, and Dolores's clipboard, and Sam's red cell. And a ten-year-old's drawing of Nadia holding the land.
GINA: Please go to bed, Dad. You need to sleep.
TOMMY: In a minute. There's just one more thing. Angela, if you're listening, it's tomorrow at noon. Give us a little luck. Just a little, because we did the rest.
GINA: She's listening, Dad. She always listened, even when you didn't think so.
TOMMY: Okay, lights off. It's now or never.
GINA: It's now. Never is not an option, because that's Mom's rule.
TOMMY: That's not her rule, and you know it.
GINA: Well, it is now, because I just made it up. Goodnight, Dad.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **표결 진행**
  - *Do we have a quorum?* — "정족수가 찼습니까?"
  - *The motion is to submit…* — "안건은 ~을 제출하는 것입니다."
  - *I so move.* — "그렇게 동의합니다." 동의를 내는 격식 표현.
  - *Is there any discussion?* — "토론하실 분 계십니까?"
  - *Let the record show: the motion carries* — "회의록에 기록해 주십시오. 안건은 가결되었습니다."
  - *It's unanimous* — "만장일치입니다."
- **확신과 불안**
  - *I'm voting yes. But I'm voting yes with my eyes open* — 찬성하지만 위험을 알고 찬성한다.
  - *It's now or never.* — "지금 아니면 영영 못 해."
  - *If we lose, it's nobody's fault.* — 미리 탓을 덜어 주는 공동체의 말.
- **경쟁 입찰**
  - *We don't try to beat their price. We beat their offer.* — 가격이 아니라 조건 전체로 이긴다.
  - *It is not the highest number you will receive. But it is the most certain.* — 가장 높지는 않지만 가장 확실한 제안.
  - *enclosed* 동봉된 / *financing letters* 자금 확약서 / *cover letter* 첨부 편지
- **the Buckner game** — 1986년 월드시리즈 6차전. 메츠가 극적으로 이긴 경기. 토미의 인생 경기(7화).
- **CLOSED FOR DEMOCRACY** — "민주주의를 위해 휴업." 바 문에 붙인 농담.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 표결 장면은 의식처럼, 천천히. *All in favor?* 다음에 각 사람의 *Aye*가 하나씩 떨어진다. 에디의 *Aye*가 가장 느리고 무겁다.
- 부인의 회의록 낭독은 시각까지 정확하게: *nine oh two p.m.* / *nine oh nine p.m.*
- 숫자: *three point three five* / *three point three oh five* — *oh*는 0. 
- 살의 편지는 지나가 천천히, 거의 속삭이듯 읽는다.
- 마지막 장면은 새벽 1시. 아주 작은 소리. 토미의 *Give us a little luck. | Just a little.*
- *It's now or never.* — *now*에 강세. 지나의 *Never is not an option*은 미소를 머금고.

## 한글 해석

샘: 좋아요, 이번이 마지막이에요, 약속해요. 최종 숫자를 보여 드리고, 그다음에 표결해요.
토미: 월요일에도 '마지막'이라고 했잖아, 샘. 화요일에도 또 그랬고.
샘: 이번엔 진짜 마지막이에요. 제안 마감이 내일 정오거든요. 뭘 고칠 토요일 같은 건 없어요.
지나: 먼저 좋은 소식부터요, 그것도 많아요. 제가 먼저 해도 돼요?
마리솔: 그래요, 해요. 이번 주를 보내고 나니 정말 좋은 소식이 필요해요.
지나: "3월에 전화 주세요"라고 했던 그 재단 기억나요? 화요일에 저한테 전화했어요. 에디 영상이랑 기사를 보고 우리 순서를 앞당겼대요. 10만 달러요, 승인됐고 서면으로 받았어요.
토미: 10만? 지나! 왜 화요일에 말 안 했어?
지나: 편지를 먼저 손에 쥐고 싶었거든요. 오늘 4시에 왔어요. 여기, 서명까지 있어요.
나디아: 저도 소식이 있어요. 어젯밤에 우리 이사회가 긴급 회의를 열어서, 땅값 한도를 140만으로 올렸어요.
마리솔: 140만! 나디아, 전보다 5만이나 늘었네요.
나디아: 커뮤니티 보드 영상이랑 에디 영상을 보고, 돌로레스의 목록도 47개 전부 읽었대요. 이사 한 분은 31번에서 진짜로 울었어요.
토미: 31번이 뭐였는데?
나디아: "아이가 새는 지붕 밑에서 새벽 4시까지 양동이를 갈았음." 디에고 얘기예요.
샘: 좋아요, 그럼 지금 상황이에요, 슬라이드 1이요. 모금 행사로 11만 5천 262달러가 들어왔어요, 마이클의 2천 포함이요. 거기에 재단 10만, 그리고 랜드 트러스트 추가분 5만을 더해요.
지나: 그럼 26만 5천이네요. 330에 필요했던 것보다 많아요.
샘: 바로 그거예요. 그러니까 오늘 밤 질문은 사실 간단해요. 얼마까지 갈 것인가.
마리솔: 다른 매수자가 360만을 제안한 건 알잖아요. 우리가 상대하는 숫자가 그거예요.
