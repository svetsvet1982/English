# THE CORNER HOUSE — 4부 66화

## Community Board Night (커뮤니티 보드의 밤)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 4부 12화(통산 66화) · 시기: 2027년 1월 28일(목) 저녁 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 레드훅 학교 강당(커뮤니티 보드 6 공청회) → 강당 밖 인도
> - 등장인물: MRS. ESPOSITO, MARISOL, EDDIE, JESSE, FRANKIE, CHAIR, WHITLOCK, MARCUS, PRIYA
> - 사건: 커뮤니티 보드가 '파이어 7 타워스' 재조닝 안건으로 공청회를 연다. 부두 옆 창고 부지에 30층 타워 두 동, 820세대를 짓는 계획이다. 25%는 '적정 임대료'로 공급하고 홍수 방벽을 쌓는다고 한다. 발표자로 나온 사람은 놀랍게도 그랜트 휘틀록이다. 하버라인이 이 사업의 투자 파트너다. 주민들은 2분씩 발언한다. 프랭키는 일자리 때문에 찬성하고, 에디는 홍수와 기억을 이유로 반대한다. 제시는 "집은 더 필요하다, 그런데 누구를 위한 집인가"를 묻는다. 부인은 타이머와 싸우고, 마리솔은 보일러 이야기를 꺼내 장내를 흔든다. 공청회가 끝난 뒤 휘틀록이 마리솔에게 다음 주 비공개 면담을 제안한다.
> - 학습 포인트: 공적 발언 공식(Good evening. My name is… I've lived… I'm here to… In closing… I urge the board to… Thank you for your time.), 2분 안에 요약하기, 찬반 논거(on the one hand / on the other hand / I'm not against X, I'm against Y), 회의 진행 표현(You have two minutes. / Your time is up. / Please wrap up. / Point of order.)
> - 미국 현안: 지역 개발과 재조닝, 주택 부족 대 젠트리피케이션, '적정 임대료' 비율 논쟁, 해안 침수 위험 지역의 고층 개발, 커뮤니티 보드의 자문 권한
> - 연결: 50화 홍수 방벽과 해수면 상승. 51화 프랭키(페인트 사업, 개발 찬성 쪽). 56화 '숫자가 안 맞으면 판다'. 65화 보일러 위반과 긴급 수리. 36·37화 휘틀록(웨스트체스터 거주, 부시윅에도 건물). 68화 역제안 복선.

## 대본

[Scene: Thursday, 6:45 p.m. The auditorium of a school in Red Hook. Folding chairs, about two hundred people. A long table on the stage for the board. A sign-up table by the door. Mrs. Esposito fills out a yellow card.]

MRS. ESPOSITO: You write your name, your address, and "for" or "against." Then you wait for them to call you, and you get two minutes each. They're very strict about it.
EDDIE: Two minutes? I can't even say good morning in two minutes, not properly.
MARISOL: Then don't say good morning, Eddie, say good evening, because it's seven p.m.
JESSE: Okay, I wrote my speech, and it's three minutes long. I timed it in the shower this morning.
MARISOL: Then cut one minute, and cut the jokes. Board members don't laugh, they take notes.
JESSE: Cut the jokes? If I cut the jokes, what's left?
MARISOL: The point. There's always a point hiding under the jokes, so find it and keep it.
FRANKIE: Hey, everybody. Eddie, Dolores, Marisol. Yes, I'm here, so please don't shoot me.
MARISOL: Frankie! Are you speaking tonight?
FRANKIE: I am, and I'm speaking for. In favor of the towers, I mean. I'm sorry, but I have to be honest with you.
EDDIE: For? Frankie, you're a Red Hook guy! Those towers will block the sun, and the harbor too.
FRANKIE: Eddie, I'm a painter. Two towers, eight hundred twenty apartments, so do you know how many walls that is? That's three years of work for my guys, and for a lot of other guys.
MARISOL: That's a fair point, and jobs matter. I'm not going to fight you on jobs.
FRANKIE: Thank you. And my nephew needs an apartment. He's twenty-six, and he lives in my mother's basement, right next to the boiler.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Then speak, Frankie, because that's what this is for. Everybody gets two minutes, even the people who are wrong.
FRANKIE: Wait, you think I'm wrong?
FRANKIE: Half right? Okay, I can see it on your face, and I'll take it. Coming from you, Dolores, that's a compliment.
EDDIE: Does your mother know you're for the towers?
FRANKIE: She knows. She says that if they build them, she wants a view from the thirtieth floor, rent-free.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Connie has always been a realist.
JESSE: Wait, who's presenting for the developer? On the screen it says "Coastline Partners," but I've never heard of them.
MARISOL: Look at the stage, third chair from the left. The man in the gray suit.
EDDIE: Is that... the suit man? The one who drank Tommy's coffee at the bar?

[Scene: 7:15 p.m. The stage. The board chair sits at the center of the long table with a timer. Whitlock stands at the microphone next to a screen showing two glass towers.]

CHAIR: Good evening, everyone, and welcome to this public hearing of Community Board Six. Tonight's item is an application to rezone the waterfront site at Pier Seven. First the applicant will present, and then we'll move to public comment, two minutes each. Please respect the timer.
WHITLOCK: Thank you, Madam Chair. Good evening. I'm Grant Whitlock, and I'm here representing Coastline Partners and our investors.
WHITLOCK: New York has a housing crisis, and all of you know it. Rents are at record highs, and families are leaving the city every day. Pier Seven Towers is part of the solution.
WHITLOCK: We're proposing two buildings, thirty stories each, with eight hundred and twenty homes. Twenty-five percent will be permanently affordable, which means two hundred and five affordable apartments.
WHITLOCK: We'll build a flood wall along the pier and raise the ground floor eight feet. We'll also fund a new ferry dock and create about six hundred construction jobs.
WHITLOCK: We're not here to change Red Hook. We're here to help it grow, and we hope you'll grow with us. Thank you.
CHAIR: Thank you, Mr. Whitlock. We'll now begin public comment. Our first speaker is Francesco Russo.
FRANKIE: Good evening. My name is Frankie Russo, and I've lived in Brooklyn my whole life. I run a small painting business. I'm here to speak in favor.
FRANKIE: On the one hand, I love this neighborhood, and I don't want it to become Manhattan. On the other hand, my guys need work, and my nephew needs an apartment, not in five years but now.
FRANKIE: Six hundred jobs means six hundred families. I urge the board to support this, but please make them hire local, Red Hook first. Thank you.
CHAIR: Thank you, Mr. Russo. Next, we have Edward O'Neill.
EDDIE: Good evening. My name is Edward O'Neill. I've lived on Van Brunt Street since nineteen seventy-four, and I worked on these piers, on Pier Nine, for forty years.
EDDIE: I'm not against housing. I'm against building thirty floors on land that floods. I watched that pier go underwater in twenty twelve, and the water came up Van Brunt like a river.
EDDIE: Mr. Whitlock says he'll raise the ground floor eight feet, and that's good. But what about the streets around it, and the old buildings next door? The water doesn't stop at the property line.
CHAIR: Thirty seconds, Mr. O'Neill.
EDDIE: Thirty seconds, okay. In closing. These towers will stand for a hundred years, and I won't, but the water will. So please, think about the water. Thank you.
CHAIR: Thank you, sir. Our next speaker is Marcus Bell.
MARCUS: Good evening, I'm Marcus Bell, and I own Bell Bikes on Van Brunt. I'll be quick. I'm not for or against, because I'm for conditions.
MARCUS: Eight hundred new homes could mean a thousand new delivery orders a day. Those delivery workers will need safe places to charge their batteries, places to rest, and bathrooms.
MARCUS: If you approve this, put a charging station in the plan, in writing. And build bike lanes that connect to the rest of Brooklyn, not lanes that end at a fence. Thank you.

[Scene: 8:10 p.m. Later in the hearing. More yellow cards. The audience is restless. Mrs. Esposito stands at the microphone with an index card in each hand.]

CHAIR: Next speaker, Dolores Esposito.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Good evening, Madam Chair. My name is Dolores Esposito, and I've lived on Van Brunt Street for thirty-one years. I'm the president of the block association, and I have a list.
CHAIR: You have two minutes, Mrs. Esposito.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I know, because I practiced with an egg timer. My point is simple. Mr. Whitlock promises a flood wall, a ferry dock, and two hundred affordable apartments, but those are only promises.
MRS. ESPOSITO: What I'm interested in is how his company keeps promises. His company bought our building in August, and they promised repairs. Since then, I have recorded forty-six problems, with dates.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Trash left on the sidewalk by their contractor. A roof that leaked into a child's kitchen. No snow removal. And yesterday, no heat, with an eighty-three-year-old man at fifty-one degrees.
WHITLOCK: Madam Chair, point of order. This hearing is about Pier Seven, not about a separate property.
CHAIR: Mrs. Esposito, please keep your comments to the application.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I am, Madam Chair, because I'm talking about the applicant's track record. If you can't keep one boiler running, how will you keep a flood wall standing?
CHAIR: Thirty seconds.
MRS. ESPOSITO: In closing: I urge the board to require written, enforceable commitments. Not promises, but contracts, with penalties. Thank you for your time.
CHAIR: Thank you, Mrs. Esposito. Please, everyone, no clapping, or we'll be here all night. Next, Jesse Moore.
JESSE: Hi, good evening. My name is Jesse Moore. I'm thirty-one, and I've lived in Brooklyn for four years, in six different apartments.
JESSE: I'm going to say something unpopular in this room: we need more housing, a lot more. I slept on a couch for months, and I've seen forty people line up for one studio.
JESSE: So I'm not against towers. But I'm asking, affordable for whom? Twenty-five percent "affordable" usually means affordable to someone making ninety thousand dollars a year, and most people in Red Hook don't make that.
JESSE: I urge the board to ask for deeper affordability, with more apartments for people making forty or fifty thousand. Delivery workers, bartenders, nurses' aides, the people who already live here.
JESSE: That's my point, and I cut the jokes, by the way. There were four, and they were good. Thank you.
CHAIR: Thank you, Mr. Moore. Next, Marisol Reyes.
MARISOL: Good evening. My name is Marisol Reyes. I'm a paralegal, and I'm the president of a tenant association on Van Brunt Street. I'll use my two minutes for one question.
MARISOL: Mr. Whitlock, your company is asking us to trust you with eight hundred families. Yesterday, the city issued a violation for no heat in our building, because Harborline refused to replace a boiler while we're trying to buy the building.
WHITLOCK: Madam Chair—
MARISOL: My question is simple. Will Coastline Partners commit, in writing, to replace any failed heating system within forty-eight hours, in every building it owns, including ours?
CHAIR: Mr. Whitlock, would you like to respond? Briefly, please.
WHITLOCK: I'm not going to discuss a private negotiation in a public hearing. But I will say this: every building we own will meet city standards.
MARISOL: Will meet. Future tense. Our building doesn't meet them now. Present tense. Thank you, Madam Chair, and I yield the rest of my time.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **공적 발언 공식(2분)**
  - 시작: *Good evening. My name is… I've lived on Van Brunt Street since… I'm here to speak in favor / against.*
  - 핵심: *My point is simple.* / *I'm not against housing. I'm against…* — 반대 대상을 좁혀 설득력을 높인다.
  - 양면: *On the one hand, … On the other hand, …*
  - 맺음: *In closing: I urge the board to… Thank you for your time.*
  - 남은 시간 양보: *I yield the rest of my time.*
- **회의 진행 표현**
  - *You have two minutes* / *Thirty seconds.* / *Please keep your comments to the application.* (안건과 관련된 말만 해 주세요)
  - *Point of order.* — "의사 진행 발언입니다." 절차상 문제를 제기할 때.
  - *Madam Chair* — 여성 의장을 부르는 호칭.
- **개발 어휘**
  - *rezone* 용도 지역을 바꾸다 / *applicant* 신청자 / *public comment* 주민 의견 진술
  - *permanently affordable* 영구 적정 임대료 / *deeper affordability* 더 낮은 소득층까지 감당 가능하게
  - *track record* 실적, 과거 행적 / *enforceable commitments* 강제할 수 있는 약속
- **Future tense. Present tense.** — 마리솔의 문법 공격. "will meet(맞출 것이다)"는 미래, "doesn't meet(못 맞추고 있다)"는 현재.
- **That was quite a performance.** — "대단한 연기였네요." 비꼬는 칭찬.
- **Everybody gets two minutes. Even the people who are wrong.** — 부인이 말하는 민주주의.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 공적 발언 톤: 첫 문장은 천천히, 또렷하게. *Good evening. | My name is Edward O'Neill.* — 이름 앞뒤로 쉰다.
- 2분 안에 끊어 읽기: 한 문장에 하나의 생각. 문장과 문장 사이를 짧게, 단락과 단락 사이를 길게.
- *In closing.*은 말투를 바꾸는 신호다. 속도를 늦추고 목소리를 낮춘다.
- 휘틀록의 발표는 매끈하고 자신감 있게, 숫자를 또박또박: *eight hundred and twenty homes.*
- 마리솔의 *Will meet. Future tense. | Our building doesn't meet them now. Present tense.* — 짧게 끊어 대비시킨다.
- 마지막 인도 장면은 바람 속에서 조금 더 크게, 그러나 긴장된 낮은 목소리로.

## 한글 해석

에스포지토 부인: 이름, 주소, 그리고 '찬성'이나 '반대'를 써요. 그다음 부를 때까지 기다리는 거고, 한 사람당 2분이에요. 아주 엄격해요.

마리솔: 그럼 좋은 아침 하지 마세요, 에디. 좋은 저녁이라고 하세요. 저녁 7시니까요.
제시: 좋아, 연설문 썼어요. 3분짜리예요. 오늘 아침 샤워하면서 시간 재 봤어요.
마리솔: 그럼 1분 잘라. 농담도 자르고. 보드 위원들은 안 웃어. 메모를 하지.
제시: 농담을 자르라고요? 농담 자르면 뭐가 남아요?
마리솔: 요점. 농담 밑엔 늘 요점이 숨어 있어. 그걸 찾아서 남겨.
프랭키: 안녕하세요, 여러분. 에디, 돌로레스, 마리솔. 네, 저 왔어요. 그러니 쏘지 마세요.
마리솔: 프랭키! 오늘 발언하세요?
프랭키: 해요. 그리고 찬성 쪽이에요. 타워 찬성이요. 미안하지만 솔직해야 해서요.

프리야: 그럼 우린 더 나은 계산을 해야겠네요. 샘한테 전화할게요. 오늘 밤 스프레드시트 업데이트하고 싶어 할 거예요.
마리솔: 빨간색 쓰라고 해. 빨간색 많이. 화요일을 위해서.
프리야: 그리고 토미는요? 화요일 일을 오늘 밤 말해야 할까요?
마리솔: 오늘 밤, 꼭. 바도 치우고 싶어 할 거고, 좋은 커피도 숨기고 싶어 할 거야.
프리야: 잠깐, 좋은 커피가 있어요?
마리솔: 타샤 거야. 시리얼 뒤에 숨겨져 있어. 아무도 토미한테 말 안 해.
프리야: 걱정 마세요. 비밀 지킬게요.
마리솔: 좋아. 화요일엔 휘틀록한테 토미 커피를 낼 거니까. 일부러.
프리야: 잔인하네요. 너무 좋아요.
