# THE CORNER HOUSE — 4부 66화

## Community Board Night (커뮤니티 보드의 밤)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 4부 12화(통산 66화) · 시기: 2027년 1월 28일(목) 저녁 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 레드훅 학교 강당(커뮤니티 보드 6 공청회) → 강당 밖 인도
> - 등장인물: MRS. ESPOSITO, MARISOL, EDDIE, JESSE, FRANKIE, CHAIR, WHITLOCK, MARCUS, PRIYA
> - 사건: 커뮤니티 보드가 '파이어 7 타워스' 재조닝 안건으로 공청회를 연다. 부두 옆 창고 부지에 30층 타워 두 동, 820세대를 짓는 계획이다. 25%는 '적정 임대료'로 공급하고 홍수 방벽을 쌓는다고 한다. 발표자로 나온 사람은 놀랍게도 그랜트 휘틀록이다. 하버라인이 이 사업의 투자 파트너다. 주민들은 2분씩 발언한다. 프랭키는 일자리 때문에 찬성하고, 에디는 홍수와 기억을 이유로 반대한다. 제시는 "집은 더 필요하다, 그런데 누구를 위한 집인가"를 묻는다. 부인은 타이머와 싸우고, 마리솔은 보일러 이야기를 꺼내 장내를 흔든다. 공청회가 끝난 뒤 휘틀록이 마리솔에게 다음 주 비공개 면담을 제안한다.
> - 학습 포인트: 공적 발언 공식(Good evening. My name is… I've lived… I'm here to… In closing… I urge the board to… Thank you for your time.), 2분 안에 요약하기, 찬반 논거(on the one hand / on the other hand / I'm not against X, I'm against Y), 회의 진행 표현(You have two minutes. / Your time is up. / Please wrap up. / Point of order.)
> - 미국 현안: 지역 개발과 재조닝, 주택 부족 대 젠트리피케이션, '적정 임대료' 비율 논쟁, 해안 침수 위험 지역의 고층 개발, 커뮤니티 보드의 자문 권한
> - 연결: 50화 홍수 방벽과 해수면 상승. 51화 프랭키(페인트 사업, 개발 찬성 쪽). 56화 '숫자가 안 맞으면 판다'. 65화 보일러 위반과 긴급 수리. 36·37화 휘틀록(웨스트체스터 거주, 부시윅에도 건물). 68화 역제안 복선.

## 대본

[Scene: Thursday, 6:45 p.m. The auditorium of a school in Red Hook. Folding chairs, about two hundred people. A long table on the stage for the board. A sign-up table by the door. Mrs. Esposito fills out a yellow card.]

MRS. ESPOSITO: Name, address, and "for" or "against." Then you wait for them to call you. Two minutes each. They're strict.
EDDIE: Two minutes? I can't say good morning in two minutes.
MARISOL: Then don't say good morning, Eddie. Say good evening. It's seven p.m.
JESSE: Okay, I wrote my speech. It's three minutes long. I timed it in the shower.
MARISOL: Cut one minute. Cut the jokes. Board members don't laugh. They take notes.
JESSE: Cut the jokes? Then what's left?
MARISOL: The point. There's always a point under the jokes. Find it.
FRANKIE: Hey, everybody. Eddie, Dolores. Marisol. I'm here. Don't shoot me.
MARISOL: Frankie! Are you speaking tonight?
FRANKIE: I am. And I'm speaking for. In favor. Of the towers. I'm sorry. I have to be honest.
EDDIE: For? Frankie, you're a Red Hook guy. Those towers will block the sun. And the harbor.
FRANKIE: Eddie, I'm a painter. Two towers. Eight hundred twenty apartments. Do you know how many walls that is? That's three years of work. For my guys. For a lot of guys.
MARISOL: That's a fair point. Jobs matter. I'm not going to fight you on jobs.
FRANKIE: Thank you. And my nephew needs an apartment. He's twenty-six. He lives in my mother's basement. With the boiler.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Then speak, Frankie. That's what this is for. Everybody gets two minutes. Even the people who are wrong.
FRANKIE: You think I'm wrong?
FRANKIE: Half right. I'll take it. From you, that's a compliment.
EDDIE: Does your mother know you're for the towers?
FRANKIE: She knows. She says if they build them, she wants a view. From the thirtieth floor. Rent-free.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Connie is a realist.
JESSE: Wait. Who's presenting for the developer? It says "Coastline Partners." On the screen.
MARISOL: Look at the stage. Third chair. In the gray suit.
EDDIE: Is that... the suit man? The one who drank Tommy's coffee?
MARISOL: Grant Whitlock. Harborline is an investor in this project. I found it last night. In the city filings. Page thirty-one.
JESSE: Wait. So the same company that let our boiler die is building eight hundred apartments?
MARISOL: The same company. That's why they need cash. That's why they want our building sold. Fast.

[Scene: 7:15 p.m. The stage. The board chair sits at the center of the long table with a timer. Whitlock stands at the microphone next to a screen showing two glass towers.]

CHAIR: Good evening, everyone. This is a public hearing of Community Board Six. Tonight's item: an application to rezone the waterfront site at Pier Seven. First, the applicant will present. Then, public comment. Two minutes each. Please respect the timer.
WHITLOCK: Thank you, Madam Chair. Good evening. I'm Grant Whitlock, representing Coastline Partners and our investors.
WHITLOCK: New York has a housing crisis. You know it. Rents are at record highs. Families are leaving. Pier Seven Towers is part of the solution.
WHITLOCK: Two buildings. Thirty stories each. Eight hundred and twenty homes. Twenty-five percent permanently affordable. That's two hundred and five affordable apartments.
WHITLOCK: We'll build a flood wall along the pier. Raise the ground floor eight feet. Fund a new ferry dock. And create about six hundred construction jobs.
WHITLOCK: We're not here to change Red Hook. We're here to help it grow. Thank you.
CHAIR: Thank you, Mr. Whitlock. We'll now begin public comment. First speaker. Francesco Russo.
FRANKIE: Good evening. My name is Frankie Russo. I've lived in Brooklyn my whole life. I run a small painting business. I'm here to speak in favor.
FRANKIE: On the one hand, I love this neighborhood. I don't want it to become Manhattan. On the other hand, my guys need work. And my nephew needs an apartment. Not in five years. Now.
FRANKIE: Six hundred jobs is six hundred families. I urge the board to support this. But please make them hire local. Red Hook first. Thank you.
CHAIR: Thank you, Mr. Russo. Next. Edward O'Neill.
EDDIE: Good evening. My name is Edward O'Neill. I've lived on Van Brunt Street since nineteen seventy-four. I worked on these piers. Pier Nine. Forty years.
EDDIE: I'm not against housing. I'm against building thirty floors on land that floods. I watched that pier go underwater in twenty twelve. The water came up Van Brunt like a river.
EDDIE: Mr. Whitlock says he'll raise the ground floor eight feet. Good. But what about the streets around it? What about the old buildings next door? The water doesn't stop at the property line.
CHAIR: Thirty seconds, Mr. O'Neill.
EDDIE: Thirty seconds. Okay. In closing. These towers will stand for a hundred years. I won't. But the water will. Please think about the water. Thank you.
CHAIR: Thank you, sir. Next speaker. Marcus Bell.
MARCUS: Good evening. Marcus Bell. I own Bell Bikes on Van Brunt. I'll be quick. I'm not for or against. I'm for conditions.
MARCUS: Eight hundred new homes means maybe a thousand new delivery orders a day. Delivery workers will need safe places to charge batteries. Places to rest. Bathrooms.
MARCUS: If you approve this, put a charging station in the plan. In writing. And bike lanes that connect to the rest of Brooklyn. Not lanes that end at a fence. Thank you.

[Scene: 8:10 p.m. Later in the hearing. More yellow cards. The audience is restless. Mrs. Esposito stands at the microphone with an index card in each hand.]

CHAIR: Next speaker. Dolores Esposito.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Good evening, Madam Chair. My name is Dolores Esposito. I've lived on Van Brunt Street for thirty-one years. I'm the president of the block association. And I have a list.
CHAIR: You have two minutes, Mrs. Esposito.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I know. I practiced with an egg timer. My point is simple. Mr. Whitlock promises a flood wall. A ferry dock. Two hundred affordable apartments. Those are promises.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I'm interested in how his company keeps promises. His company bought our building in August. They promised repairs. Since then, I have recorded forty-six problems. With dates.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Trash left on the sidewalk by their contractor. A roof that leaked into a child's kitchen. No snow removal. And yesterday, no heat. An eighty-three-year-old man at fifty-one degrees.
WHITLOCK: Madam Chair, point of order. This hearing is about Pier Seven. Not about a separate property.
CHAIR: Mrs. Esposito, please keep your comments to the application.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I am, Madam Chair. I'm talking about the applicant's track record. If you can't keep one boiler running, how will you keep a flood wall standing?
CHAIR: Thirty seconds.
MRS. ESPOSITO: In closing: I urge the board to require written, enforceable commitments. Not promises. Contracts. With penalties. Thank you for your time.
CHAIR: Thank you, Mrs. Esposito. Please, everyone, no clapping. Next. Jesse Moore.
JESSE: Hi. Good evening. My name is Jesse Moore. I'm thirty-one. I've lived in Brooklyn for four years. In six different apartments.
JESSE: I'm going to say something unpopular in this room. We need more housing. A lot more. I slept on a couch for months. I've seen forty people line up for one studio.
JESSE: So I'm not against towers. But I'm asking: affordable for whom? Twenty-five percent "affordable" usually means affordable to someone making ninety thousand dollars a year. Most people in Red Hook don't make that.
JESSE: I urge the board to ask for deeper affordability. More apartments for people making forty, fifty thousand. Delivery workers. Bartenders. Nurses' aides. People who already live here.
JESSE: That's my point. I cut the jokes. There were four. They were good. Thank you.
CHAIR: Thank you, Mr. Moore. Next. Marisol Reyes.
MARISOL: Good evening. My name is Marisol Reyes. I'm a paralegal. I'm the president of a tenant association on Van Brunt Street. I'll use my two minutes for one question.
MARISOL: Mr. Whitlock, your company asked us to trust you with eight hundred families. Yesterday, the city issued a violation for no heat in our building. Harborline refused to replace a boiler. Because we're trying to buy the building.
WHITLOCK: Madam Chair—
MARISOL: My question is simple. Will Coastline Partners commit, in writing, to replace any failed heating system within forty-eight hours? In every building it owns? Including ours?
CHAIR: Mr. Whitlock, would you like to respond? Briefly?
WHITLOCK: I'm not going to discuss a private negotiation in a public hearing. But I will say this: every building we own will meet city standards.
MARISOL: Will meet. Future tense. Our building doesn't meet them now. Present tense. Thank you, Madam Chair. I yield the rest of my time.
CHAIR: Thank you. Let's take a five-minute break. Please, no shouting during the break either.

[Scene: 8:45 p.m. The hallway outside the auditorium. A vending machine hums. Priya hands out bottles of water.]

FRANKIE: Okay. I'll admit it. The kid made a good point. "Affordable for whom?"
JESSE: Thank you, Frankie. You made a good point too. Local hiring. I'd vote for that.
FRANKIE: So we agree? A guy who's for and a guy who's against?
JESSE: I wasn't against. I was "for, with conditions." Like Marcus.
MARCUS: "For, with conditions" is the most Brooklyn position there is.
EDDIE: In my day, you were for or against. And then you went to the bar and argued until two a.m.
PRIYA: That still happens, Eddie. It's called The Hook.
FRANKIE: And Marisol? "Future tense"? She destroyed him.
JESSE: With grammar. She destroyed him with grammar.
FRANKIE: Did you see the board? When Dolores talked? The guy on the end stopped texting.
MARCUS: And the woman next to the chair wrote something down. Twice.
PRIYA: There's a reporter here too. From a Brooklyn news site. She asked me for Dolores's phone number.
EDDIE: Don't give it to her. Dolores will talk for three hours. The reporter will miss her deadline.
PRIYA: Too late. Dolores gave it to her herself. With her email. And the list.
JESSE: What happens now? Does the board vote tonight?
MARCUS: Next month. And it's only advisory. The City Council makes the final decision.
FRANKIE: So all this, and they can just ignore us?
EDDIE: They can. But they usually don't. Not when two hundred people show up on a cold night. That's how it works.
PRIYA: Okay. Break's over. Let's go back in. Two more hours of speeches.

[Scene: 9:40 p.m. The sidewalk outside the school. Cold wind off the harbor. People walk away in small groups. Whitlock approaches Marisol and Priya as they leave.]

WHITLOCK: Ms. Reyes. A minute? Without a microphone this time.
MARISOL: Mr. Whitlock. Priya Shah, our vice president. She'll stay.
PRIYA: Hi. I'm the one who wasn't on the agenda.
WHITLOCK: That was quite a performance in there. You made me look bad. In front of the board. In front of a reporter.
MARISOL: I asked a question. You chose the answer.
WHITLOCK: Fair enough. Look. I'll be direct. Your letter of intent arrived in December. My partners laughed at it. Tenants buying a building? With a land trust? They didn't take it seriously.
PRIYA: And now?
WHITLOCK: And now there's a violation. An emergency repair bill. A city inspector. A lady with a list of forty-six problems. And a reporter who wants to write about a "boiler war" in Red Hook.
MARISOL: Forty-seven, actually. She added one tonight. "Applicant interrupted an eighty-three-year-old speaker." That was Eddie.
WHITLOCK: I didn't interrupt Mr. O'Neill.
MARISOL: You checked your phone while he spoke. She considers that an interruption.
WHITLOCK: ...She's not wrong. Okay. Here's what I'm offering. A meeting. Next week. Just your board and our lawyer. To talk about your offer. And ours.
PRIYA: "And ours"? You have an offer?
WHITLOCK: We have a counteroffer. You won't like it. But it's real. Tuesday. Ten a.m. Our office in Midtown.
MARISOL: Not Midtown. Here. In Red Hook. At The Hook. Ten a.m. Tommy will make coffee.
WHITLOCK: Tommy's coffee? Ms. Reyes, that's a negotiating tactic.
MARISOL: Everything is a negotiating tactic, Mr. Whitlock. You taught us that. Tuesday. Ten a.m.
WHITLOCK: Fine. Tuesday. I'll bring my own coffee.
PRIYA: He's scared. Did you see his face?
MARISOL: He's not scared. He's calculating. Twenty-one thousand a month in interest. Plus a boiler. Plus a reporter. He's doing math.
PRIYA: Then we'd better do better math. I'll call Sam. He'll want to update the spreadsheet tonight.
MARISOL: Tell him to use red. A lot of red. For Tuesday.
PRIYA: And Tommy? Should we tell him tonight? About Tuesday?
MARISOL: Tonight. He'll want to clean the bar. And hide the good coffee.
PRIYA: There's good coffee?
MARISOL: Tasha's. Behind the cereal. Nobody tells Tommy.
PRIYA: Your secret is safe with me.
MARISOL: Good. Because on Tuesday, we serve Tommy's coffee. To Whitlock. On purpose.
PRIYA: That's cruel. I love it.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **공적 발언 공식(2분)**
  - 시작: *Good evening. My name is… I've lived on Van Brunt Street since… I'm here to speak in favor / against.*
  - 핵심: *My point is simple.* / *I'm not against housing. I'm against…* — 반대 대상을 좁혀 설득력을 높인다.
  - 양면: *On the one hand, … On the other hand, …*
  - 맺음: *In closing, … I urge the board to… Thank you for your time.*
  - 남은 시간 양보: *I yield the rest of my time.*
- **회의 진행 표현**
  - *You have two minutes.* / *Thirty seconds.* / *Please keep your comments to the application.* (안건과 관련된 말만 해 주세요)
  - *Point of order.* — "의사 진행 발언입니다." 절차상 문제를 제기할 때.
  - *Madam Chair* — 여성 의장을 부르는 호칭.
- **개발 어휘**
  - *rezone* 용도 지역을 바꾸다 / *applicant* 신청자 / *public comment* 주민 의견 진술
  - *permanently affordable* 영구 적정 임대료 / *deeper affordability* 더 낮은 소득층까지 감당 가능하게
  - *track record* 실적, 과거 행적 / *enforceable commitments* 강제할 수 있는 약속
- **Future tense. Present tense.** — 마리솔의 문법 공격. "will meet(맞출 것이다)"는 미래, "doesn't meet(못 맞추고 있다)"는 현재.
- **That was quite a performance.** — "대단한 연기였네요." 비꼬는 칭찬.
- **Everybody gets two minutes. Even the people who are wrong.** — 부인이 말하는 민주주의.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 공적 발언 톤: 첫 문장은 천천히, 또렷하게. *Good evening. | My name is Edward O'Neill.* — 이름 앞뒤로 쉰다.
- 2분 안에 끊어 읽기: 한 문장에 하나의 생각. 문장과 문장 사이를 짧게, 단락과 단락 사이를 길게.
- *In closing.*은 말투를 바꾸는 신호다. 속도를 늦추고 목소리를 낮춘다.
- 휘틀록의 발표는 매끈하고 자신감 있게, 숫자를 또박또박: *eight hundred and twenty homes.*
- 마리솔의 *Will meet. Future tense. | Our building doesn't meet them now. Present tense.* — 짧게 끊어 대비시킨다.
- 마지막 인도 장면은 바람 속에서 조금 더 크게, 그러나 긴장된 낮은 목소리로.

에스포지토 부인: 그럼 발언해요, 프랭키. 이게 그러라고 있는 거예요. 다들 2분씩 받아요. 틀린 사람들도요.
프랭키: 내가 틀렸다고 생각해요?
프랭키: 반쯤 맞았다. 그거면 됐어요. 부인한테 들으면 칭찬이죠.
에디: 네가 타워 찬성인 거 너희 엄마도 아셔?
프랭키: 알아요. 짓게 되면 전망을 원한대요. 30층에서요. 월세 없이요.
