# THE CORNER HOUSE — 4부 57화

## The Co-op (협동조합)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 4부 3화(통산 57화) · 시기: 2026년 12월 8일(화) 저녁 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 2B 에스포지토 부인의 거실
> - 등장인물: NADIA, MARISOL, MRS. ESPOSITO, TOMMY, EDDIE, PRIYA, SAM, MS. BROOKS
> - 사건: 비영리 단체 '하버 커먼스 랜드 트러스트'의 조직가 나디아 오카포가 찾아온다. 그녀는 협동조합으로 가는 단계를 설명한다. 세입자 협회 법인화, 하버라인에 보낼 매입 의향서, 정관 초안, 임시 이사회 구성이다. 대니엘 브룩스 변호사가 영상 통화로 정관 문장을 함께 다듬는다(shall/must/may). 건물의 투표 가구는 다섯이다(2A 토미, 2B 부인, 3A 에디, 4A 마리솔, 4B 프리야·샘). 정족수와 투표권, 상가의 지위, 나중에 들어올 가구의 소득 상한을 두고 토론한다. 임시 이사회를 뽑는다. 회장 마리솔, 부회장 프리야, 서기 에스포지토 부인. 회계는 반대만 하던 샘이 맡는다.
> - 학습 포인트: 조직 어휘(bylaws, board, quorum, steering committee, letter of intent, incorporate, interim, motion, second, abstain), 규칙 문장(shall / must / may / shall not), 회의 진행 말투(I move that… / Do I hear a second? / All in favor? / Any opposed? / The motion carries.)
> - 미국 현안: 뉴욕의 HDFC 협동조합(주민 소유 주택), 신규 입주 가구 소득 상한, 비영리 랜드 트러스트, 빈집(3B·4C) 활용
> - 연결: 56화 거수 표결과 12/14 모임 예고. 45화 대니엘 브룩스. 53화 부인의 클립보드·여분 열쇠. 4C는 2021년부터 비어 있음(79화 복선). 3B는 리모델링 중이고 DHCR 민원이 걸려 있다.

## 대본

[Scene: Tuesday, 7:00 p.m. Apartment 2B. Lace doilies, a radiator that clanks, Biscuit on the windowsill. Folding chairs borrowed from the church. Nadia sets up a laptop on the coffee table.]

MRS. ESPOSITO: Please, sit anywhere you like, except that chair. That's Biscuit's chair, and she bites.
NADIA: Noted, and thank you for the warning. Thank you for having me, Mrs. Esposito. Your apartment is lovely.
MRS. ESPOSITO: That's thirty-one years of doilies you're looking at. Everybody, this is Nadia Okafor, from the Harbor Commons Land Trust.
NADIA: Hi, everyone. Marisol called us last Tuesday, and I came because, honestly, buildings like yours are exactly why we exist.
TOMMY: Buildings like ours? You mean old, leaky, and owned by people who hate us?
NADIA: I mean small and mixed-use, with long-term tenants and an owner who's overleveraged. That's the perfect storm, but in a good way.
EDDIE: Overleveraged. If I'm not mistaken, that's just a fancy word for "broke."
NADIA: It's a fancy word for "borrowed too much," but that's close enough. So tonight, I'll explain the steps first, and then we'll draft some rules together.
MARISOL: Could you start with the big picture, for people who are new to this? Which, to be fair, is all of us.
NADIA: Sure. Step one: you incorporate your tenant association. Right now, you're neighbors with a fridge rule, but legally, you need to be an organization.
MRS. ESPOSITO: We already have a name for it: "The Corner House Tenants Association." I wrote it on the clipboard back in September.
NADIA: Perfect. Step two: you form a steering committee, which becomes your interim board. That's a temporary board that runs things until the co-op is real.
TOMMY: Interim means temporary. Got it, like my last three diets.
NADIA: Step three is a letter of intent, which we send to Harborline. It says, "If you sell, we want to buy. Here's who we are, and here's our partner."
EDDIE: So it's a letter that says "We're interested." It's like a love letter, except it goes to a landlord.
NADIA: It's a very polite love letter, with a lawyer's signature at the bottom. Step four is the bylaws, which are the rules of the co-op: who votes, how, and on what.
MARISOL: And what about the land trust? What exactly would you do in all this?
NADIA: If there's a sale, we buy the land and you buy the building. Then we sign a ninety-nine-year ground lease, which keeps the building affordable forever.
TOMMY: Ninety-nine years? Eddie, you'll be a hundred and eighty-two by the time that's over.
EDDIE: And I'll still be on my stool, ordering coffee. "Just coffee. It's a weekday."
NADIA: There's one more thing you should know. In New York, most co-ops like this are called HDFC co-ops, which is a legal structure for affordable, resident-owned housing.
MRS. ESPOSITO: H-D-F-C. Let me write that down before I forget. What does it stand for?
NADIA: Housing Development Fund Corporation, but nobody remembers that. Everybody just says "H-D-F-C."
MARISOL: Are there conditions? In my experience, there are always conditions.
NADIA: There are, and the big one is income limits. They don't apply to you, because current tenants can stay whatever they earn. But for families who buy in later, there's a maximum income.
TOMMY: So what you're saying is, rich people can't move in.
NADIA: Rich people can move in, just next door, into the glass towers. This building stays for working people.
EDDIE: I like this woman a lot. Dolores, go get her the good tea, not the stuff you give me.
NADIA: Let me tell you one more honest thing. This takes time, usually about a year, and sometimes three.
TOMMY: My lease ends in May, Nadia. I don't have three years to wait around.
NADIA: I know, and that's exactly why we move fast. We'll put the bar in the letter of intent, so Harborline will see that you're not alone.
MARISOL: And if they sell before May, then the new owner is us, not some wine bar.
TOMMY: From your mouth to God's ears, Marisol.
EDDIE: God's ears are old, Tommy, so you'd better speak up.

[Scene: 8:00 p.m. Same room. Priya and Sam have arrived. Danielle Brooks is on video on the laptop, propped against the sugar bowl. Nadia takes notes quietly. Tommy and Eddie have gone down to check on the bar.]

MS. BROOKS: Can everyone hear me? I'm sitting in my car outside a courthouse, so please don't ask.
PRIYA: We can hear you, Danielle! You look really tired, though.
MS. BROOKS: I'm always tired, so don't worry about it. Okay, let's draft the first bylaws in simple sentences. Lawyers love three words: "shall," "must," and "may."
SAM: Honestly, what's the difference? I always thought they all meant the same thing.
MS. BROOKS: "Shall" and "must" are requirements, so there's no choice. "May" is permission, which means you can, but you don't have to.
MARISOL: And "shall not" means it's forbidden, right?
MS. BROOKS: Right. Okay, Article One is membership. Go ahead and read it, Marisol.
MARISOL: "Each household shall have one vote. A household may have more than one member, but it shall cast only one vote."
SAM: So Priya and I get one vote together, not two.
PRIYA: Which means we'll have to agree every single time. That's basically marriage.
MS. BROOKS: Good. Now, how many households are there in the building?
MRS. ESPOSITO: There are five: Tommy in 2A, me in 2B, Eddie in 3A, Marisol and Diego in 4A, and Priya and Sam in 4B.
MARISOL: And 3B and 4C are empty. 3B is under construction, and 4C has been empty since twenty twenty-one.
PRIYA: So what happens to the empty apartments if we buy the building?
MS. BROOKS: The co-op decides. You could sell those shares to new families, as long as they're within the income limits.
MRS. ESPOSITO: 4C needs a lot of work. Nobody has opened that door in five years, except me, to water a plant.
SAM: Wait, there's a plant in 4C?
MRS. ESPOSITO: There was. These days, it's more of an idea than a plant.
MARISOL: Let's talk about 4C later, okay? We should take one problem at a time.
MS. BROOKS: So we have five voting households. Article Two is quorum.
SAM: What's a quorum? It sounds like something you'd catch on an airplane.
MARISOL: It's the minimum number of people you need to make a decision. If only one person shows up, they can't decide everything alone.
MS. BROOKS: "A quorum shall be a majority of households. No vote shall be valid without a quorum." With five households, that's three.
SAM: Three. So if Eddie's sleeping and Dolores is at bingo, the rest of us can still decide.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I don't play bingo, Sam. I run bingo, and there's a big difference.
MS. BROOKS: Article Three is the board. "The board must meet at least once a month. Minutes must be recorded and shared with all members."
MRS. ESPOSITO: Minutes? That's me. I've been taking minutes at the block association since nineteen ninety-four.
SAM: Can I raise a concern about repairs? If the board decides everything, a few people could end up spending a lot of money.
MS. BROOKS: That's a good point, so let's add a limit. "Any repair or contract over five thousand dollars must be approved by a vote of the members."
SAM: Five thousand, okay. But what about emergencies, like a burst pipe at three a.m.?
MS. BROOKS: "In an emergency, the president may approve up to ten thousand dollars without a vote. The president shall report it within seven days."
SAM: Okay, that's reasonable, and I hate that it's reasonable. I really wanted to argue.
PRIYA: He really did. He practiced his arguments in the elevator.
SAM: Priya, we don't have an elevator.
PRIYA: Fine, he practiced on the stairs, so that's four floors of practice.
MARISOL: What about the stores, Marcus and The Hook? Marcus asked me last week if he'd get a vote.
MS. BROOKS: That's your choice. Some co-ops give commercial tenants a seat at meetings, but no vote. "Commercial tenants may attend meetings and may speak, but shall not vote."
MARISOL: And what about Tommy? He's a household and a commercial tenant, so does he get two votes?
MS. BROOKS: No, he gets one vote, as a household. The bar doesn't vote.
MRS. ESPOSITO: The bar doesn't vote? Angela would be furious, because she always said the bar had opinions.
MARISOL: Here's the last one for tonight, which is resale. Nadia says this is the heart of a limited-equity co-op.
MS. BROOKS: Here's the rule: "No member shall sell a share for more than the price set by the resale formula." The formula is usually the purchase price plus a small percentage each year.
PRIYA: So if we paid five thousand for our share and left in ten years, we'd get back maybe seven, or eight?
MS. BROOKS: Roughly, plus whatever you put into improvements. You don't lose, but you don't win the lottery either.
PRIYA: That's fine with me. I don't need the lottery. I just need a kitchen that doesn't leak.
SAM: Can I say something? It's kind of personal.
MARISOL: Of course you can, Sam.
SAM: When Marisol first said all this, I thought it was a fantasy. But tonight, with articles and quorums, it sounds like a plan. I'm... a little moved. And I work in insurance, so we don't get moved.
MS. BROOKS: Sam, that's the nicest thing anyone has ever said in a bylaws meeting, and I've been to about two hundred of them.

[Scene: 9:15 p.m. The video call is over. Nadia has left. Marisol stands by the radiator with Mrs. Esposito's clipboard.]

MARISOL: Okay, here's the last item. We need an interim board with four positions: president, vice president, secretary, and treasurer.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Secretary is me, and there's no discussion, because I have the clipboard.
EDDIE: She's right, you know. Nobody in this building argues with the clipboard.
TOMMY: I move that Marisol is president. She started this, and she's the only one who really understands it.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I second the motion.
MARISOL: Wait, shouldn't someone else do it? I don't want it to look like I'm taking over.
EDDIE: Marisol, you are taking over, and that's the whole point. So take over.
MARISOL: Okay, then. All in favor of me? That feels so weird to say out loud.
TOMMY: Aye!
EDDIE: Aye.
SAM: Aye, and Priya says aye too. I know it's one household, one vote, but we agree.
MARISOL: Any opposed? Any abstentions? None, so the motion carries. I'm... the president of a building we don't own.
TOMMY: Yet.
MARISOL: Yet. Priya said yes to vice president in the hallway, but treasurer is harder. We need someone careful, someone who reads every number.
TOMMY: Well, it can't be me. I can't even read my own receipts.
EDDIE: And it can't be me, because I count on my fingers, and I've lost feeling in two of them.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Then what about Sam?
SAM: Me? But I was the bad guy last week. I asked all the hard questions.
MARISOL: Exactly. The treasurer's job is to ask hard questions every month, about every single dollar.
SAM: Hmm. Okay, I see what you're doing here. You're turning my weakness into a job.
EDDIE: That's what a neighborhood does, son. It finds a job for your worst habit.
SAM: Fine, yes, I'll be treasurer. But I'm making a spreadsheet, a real one, with colors.
TOMMY: God help us all. He wants colors.
SAM: Green is for good news, and red is for Harborline.
MARISOL: Okay, then. All in favor of Sam as treasurer?
TOMMY: Aye.
EDDIE: Aye.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Aye. And let the minutes show: the motion carried at nine twenty-two p.m.
MARISOL: Then that's it, we're officially a board. Here's the homework: I'll send the letter of intent this Friday, Sam builds the spreadsheet, and Dolores types the minutes.
MRS. ESPOSITO: They'll be typed by tomorrow, on my typewriter.
SAM: Wait, you have a typewriter? An actual typewriter?
MRS. ESPOSITO: I typed the note under Tommy's door in May, didn't I tell you? No, wait, that was Teresa. Never mind.
TOMMY: I'll tell you a secret. For a whole week, I thought it was you, Dolores, because of the typewriter.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Me? If I'd written it, I would have signed it, with my full name and a postscript.
EDDIE: So is the meeting adjourned, or do I have to keep sitting here?
MARISOL: Meeting adjourned. Everybody go home and get some sleep, because Saturday morning is the food pantry, at seven a.m.
TOMMY: Seven a.m. on a Saturday? I'm on the board of nothing, and I'm already tired.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **규칙 문장**
  - *shall / must* — 의무. *Each household shall have one vote.* / *The board must meet at least once a month.*
  - *may* — 허용(해도 된다). *Commercial tenants may attend meetings and may speak.*
  - *shall not* — 금지. *No member shall sell a share for more than the price set by the resale formula.*
  - *No vote shall be valid without a quorum.* — 정족수가 없으면 어떤 표결도 유효하지 않다.
- **조직 어휘**
  - *bylaws* 정관 / *board* 이사회 / *interim board* 임시 이사회 / *steering committee* 준비 위원회
  - *quorum* 정족수 / *minutes* 회의록 / *letter of intent* 의향서 / *incorporate* 법인으로 등록하다
  - *ground lease* 토지 장기 임대 / *resale formula* 재판매 가격 공식 / *overleveraged* 빚을 과도하게 진
- **회의 진행 말투**
  - *I move that…* — "~을 제안합니다(동의)."
  - *I second the motion.* — "재청합니다."
  - *All in favor? — Aye!* — "찬성하시는 분? — 찬성!"
  - *Any opposed? Any abstentions?* — "반대? 기권?"
  - *The motion carries.* — "안건이 가결되었습니다."
  - *Let the minutes show…* — "회의록에 기록해 주세요."
  - *Meeting adjourned.* — "회의를 마칩니다."
- **the perfect storm** — 여러 조건이 한꺼번에 맞아떨어진 상황. 여기서는 좋은 뜻으로 비틀었다.
- **It finds a job for your worst habit.** — 에디식 지혜. "동네는 네 최악의 버릇에 일자리를 찾아 준다."

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 정관 문장은 법률 문서처럼 또박또박, 천천히. *shall*은 [섈], 약하게 하지 말고 분명히.
- **H-D-F-C** — 한 글자씩: [에이치 디 에프 씨].
- 회의 진행 말투는 형식적으로, 약간 과장해서: *All in favor?↗ — Aye!↘ / The motion carries.↘*
- *Aye*는 [아이]. 짧고 크게.
- 샘의 고백(*I'm... a little moved.*)은 쑥스럽게, 말을 고르듯 쉬면서.
- 부인의 *Let the minutes show:*는 서기답게 사무적으로, 시간은 정확하게 *nine twenty-two p.m.*
