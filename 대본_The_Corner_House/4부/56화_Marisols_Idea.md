# THE CORNER HOUSE — 4부 56화

## Marisol's Idea (마리솔의 아이디어)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 4부 2화(통산 56화) · 시기: 2026년 11월 30일(월) 밤 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 문 닫은 더 훅 → 4층 계단참
> - 등장인물: MARISOL, TOMMY, EDDIE, MRS. ESPOSITO, PRIYA, SAM, MARCUS, GINA, DIEGO
> - 사건: 약속한 일주일 뒤, 마리솔이 화이트보드를 들고 온다. "우리가 이 건물을 사면 어떨까요?" 에스포지토 부인은 시 등기 기록(ACRIS)에서 하버라인의 매입가 340만 달러와 대출 270만 달러를 찾아왔다. 마리솔은 커뮤니티 랜드 트러스트가 땅을 갖고, 세입자들이 지분 제한 협동조합으로 건물을 갖는 구조를 설명한다. 지나는 금요일에 통화한 브루클린 네이버후드 펀드의 답을 전한다. "건물과 사람들을 데려오면 이야기하자." 샘은 반대 측 입장에서 가장 어려운 질문들을 던진다. 마지막에 '알아보기만 하자'는 거수 표결을 한다. 밤에는 디에고가 엄마에게 왜 이 일을 하는지 묻는다.
> - 학습 포인트: 제안·설득(What if we…? / Hear me out. / Picture this. / The upside is… The downside is… / Here's the catch.), 반론(Let me play devil's advocate. / With all due respect… / That's a fair question.), 장단점 논의
> - 미국 현안: 세입자 우선매수 제도 논의(뉴욕은 아직 법이 아님), 커뮤니티 랜드 트러스트, 지분 제한 협동조합(limited-equity co-op), 고금리 사모 대출, 공공 등기 기록
> - 연결: 54화 '숫자가 안 맞으면 판다'와 살의 편지. 51화 루이스 후보의 세입자 우선매수 공약(결과는 이 화에서 공개). 14화 마리솔 부모의 레예스 타코스 폐업. 38화 바이아웃 거절. 45화 대니얼 브룩스가 언급한 랜드 트러스트.

## 대본

[Scene: Monday, 10:30 p.m. The Hook, closed. A small whiteboard leans on a chair. Marisol holds a marker. Tommy, Eddie, Mrs. Esposito, and Priya sit around one table.]

MARISOL: Okay. Thank you for coming. I know it's late. I'll try to be clear. And quick.
TOMMY: Quick-ish. It's a family word now.
MARISOL: Quick-ish. Okay, so here's the thing. A week ago, Brian said Harborline might sell. Because the numbers don't work.
EDDIE: "They always sell." I remember.
MARISOL: So hear me out. What if, when they sell, we're the buyers?
PRIYA: We? As in, us? The people at this table?
MARISOL: The people in this building. All of us. Together.
TOMMY: Marisol, I have four hundred dollars in my checking account. And a jukebox I can't sell.
MARISOL: I know. Nobody here has the money. Alone. That's why it's not alone.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Before you explain, I did some homework. May I?
MARISOL: Please. Dolores found something important.
MRS. ESPOSITO: The city has a public website. It's called ACRIS. Every property sale in New York is recorded there. Every mortgage.
EDDIE: You can just look? Anybody?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Anybody. It took me two hours and three cups of tea. Harborline paid three point four million dollars. On August twenty-fourth.
PRIYA: Three point four million. For this building.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And they borrowed two point seven million. From a private lender. Not a bank. The interest rate is in the document. Nine point five percent.
TOMMY: Nine and a half percent? My credit card is twenty-four. But for a building, that's a lot, right?
MARISOL: It's a lot. Their monthly payment is huge. About twenty-one thousand dollars. Just the interest.
EDDIE: Twenty-one thousand a month. And they're getting maybe twelve from us.
MARISOL: Exactly. Plus the construction. Plus the lawyers. They're losing money every month. That's why they want Tommy out. And us out. To raise the rents fast.
PRIYA: Okay. So they're desperate. But that doesn't mean they'll sell to us.
MARISOL: That's a fair point. Here's my idea. Picture this. Two pieces.
MARISOL: Piece one: a community land trust buys the land. A nonprofit. They own the ground under the building. Forever. They never sell it to a speculator.
EDDIE: A land trust. Danielle Brooks said that word. In October.
MARISOL: She did. Piece two: we, the tenants, form a co-op. A cooperative. The co-op owns the building. Each family owns a share.
TOMMY: A share. Like a stock?
MARISOL: Kind of. But a limited-equity co-op. That means when you sell your share, you can't sell it for a crazy price. There's a limit. So the apartments stay affordable. For the next family.
MRS. ESPOSITO: So nobody gets rich.
MARISOL: Nobody gets rich. But nobody gets kicked out either. That's the deal.
PRIYA: And the monthly cost? What would I pay?
MARISOL: I don't know yet. That's the honest answer. We'd pay a monthly fee. For the loan, the taxes, the repairs. Gina is working on numbers.
EDDIE: And the bar? Tommy's bar? Marcus's shop?
MARISOL: The co-op would be their landlord. A landlord who wants them to stay.
TOMMY: A landlord who drinks at my bar. That's a conflict of interest.
EDDIE: That's the best kind of interest.
PRIYA: Can I be honest? This scares me a little. Sam and I are getting married. We were thinking about buying a place. In Jersey City.
TOMMY: Jersey City? Priya. Don't say that word in my bar.
PRIYA: It's cheaper, Tommy! And there's the PATH train.
MARISOL: I understand. Nobody has to stay forever. But what if your place to buy is right here? On the fourth floor?
PRIYA: A share of our own building. Sam would need numbers. Lots of numbers.
MARISOL: Then he'll get numbers. Gina is coming at eleven. With Sam and Marcus.
EDDIE: I'll be honest too. I'm eighty-three. Why would I buy anything? I won't see the end of the loan.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Eddie!
EDDIE: It's true, Dolores. A thirty-year loan. I'd be a hundred and thirteen.
MARISOL: Eddie, you wouldn't do it for you. You'd do it so the next person in 3A never gets a letter like yours. Forty thousand dollars to disappear.
EDDIE: So it's like planting a tree. For shade you'll never sit in.
MARISOL: Exactly like that.

[Scene: 11:00 p.m. Same table. Sam, Marcus, and Gina have joined. Priya and Eddie have stepped out for air. Mrs. Esposito went home to feed Biscuit.]

SAM: Okay, I'm sorry I'm late. Priya texted me the whole thing. Can I ask the hard questions?
MARISOL: Please. That's why I asked you to come.
SAM: Let me play devil's advocate. I'm not against this. But someone has to be the bad guy.
TOMMY: You're good at it. You work in insurance.
SAM: Thanks, Tommy. Question one: who owns the problems? If the boiler dies, who pays? If the roof leaks, who fixes it at three a.m.?
MARISOL: We do. That's the downside. When you own it, you own the problems too.
SAM: Question two: meetings. How many meetings? Forever? I already go to work meetings. I don't want building meetings every Tuesday.
GINA: Probably once a month. And a board. Three or five people. They handle most things.
SAM: Question three, and this is the big one. Priya and I are getting married in the spring. We might have a kid. We might need two bedrooms. If we're locked into a co-op share, can we leave?
MARISOL: Yes. You can always leave. You sell your share back. At the limited price. You won't lose your money. But you won't make a fortune.
SAM: Okay. That's fair. And question four. With all due respect, Marisol, where does three million dollars come from?
GINA: That's where I come in. Can I?
MARISOL: Go ahead.
GINA: On Friday, I called the Brooklyn Neighborhood Fund. The one Linda Chen told us about. I talked to a woman named Renée. Forty minutes.
TOMMY: What did she say?
GINA: She said they've financed eleven tenant co-ops since twenty eighteen. In Brooklyn and the Bronx. Smaller than ours. And bigger.
MARCUS: Eleven. So it's real. It's not a fairy tale.
GINA: It's real. But here's the catch. They need three things. A building that's actually for sale. A group of tenants that's organized. And a nonprofit partner. Like a land trust.
SAM: And the money? The actual money?
GINA: Usually a mix. A loan from them. Money from a city program. Some grants. And a small down payment from the tenants.
SAM: How small is "small"?
GINA: She said, "Usually a few thousand per family. Sometimes less. Sometimes we find it for them."
SAM: A few thousand. Okay. That's not crazy.
MARCUS: Can I ask a question? I'm not a resident. I'm commercial. Do I get a vote? Or am I just... the guy with the bikes?
MARISOL: That's a great question. I don't know. Some co-ops include the stores. Some don't. We decide that together.
MARCUS: Because I'm in. If I can be in. My lease is two years. After that, Harborline does whatever it wants. With you guys, I'd sleep better.
TOMMY: Same here. I don't need to own anything. I just need a landlord who isn't a spreadsheet.
SAM: Okay. One more. The really hard one. What if Harborline never sells? What if they hold on? Or sell to another company? We do all this work for nothing.
MARISOL: Then we lose. And we're exactly where we are now. Organized. Angry. And smarter.
GINA: And there's one more thing. The election. Ruiz won.
SAM: Wait, Ruiz won? Last I heard, it was too close to call.
GINA: They finished counting the mail-in ballots on Wednesday. She won by four hundred and twelve votes.
MARCUS: Four hundred and twelve. That's two buildings and a bar.
GINA: And her first bill is the tenant opportunity bill. The right of first offer. If it passes in Albany, tenants get the first chance to buy. Before a company.
SAM: If it passes. That's a big "if." Albany is slow.
MARISOL: Albany is very slow. We can't wait for it. But it helps. It means people are talking about this. Politicians. Lenders.
SAM: Okay. I'm done being the bad guy. For now.
TOMMY: You were a very good bad guy. Four stars.
SAM: Thank you. Honestly? I came here thinking, "This is crazy." Now I think it's... complicated crazy. Not stupid crazy.
MARISOL: I'll take complicated crazy.

[Scene: 11:40 p.m. Everyone is back at the table, standing. Priya, Eddie, and Mrs. Esposito have returned.]

MARISOL: Okay. I'm not asking anyone to sign anything. Rule number four. I'm asking one small question.
EDDIE: Ask it. My legs are tired.
MARISOL: Should we explore this? Just explore. Talk to the land trust. Get real numbers. Meet again in two weeks. Hands up if yes.
EDDIE: Yes. One hand. The other one holds the cane.
PRIYA: Yes. Both hands. I'm in.
SAM: Half a hand. Is half a hand okay?
MARISOL: Half a hand is a yes with questions. I love questions. Tommy already said yes at the bar. So that's unanimous. Kind of.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Yes from me. And yes from Biscuit. She's a resident.
MARISOL: Biscuit can't vote, Dolores.
MRS. ESPOSITO: She's lived here longer than Sam.
SAM: That's... technically true.
MARISOL: Okay. Two weeks. December fourteenth. Same table. Bring questions.
EDDIE: And bring your own coffee.

[Scene: 12:15 a.m. The fourth-floor landing. Diego sits on the top step in pajamas. Marisol comes up the stairs with her whiteboard.]

DIEGO: Mom. You were gone forever.
MARISOL: Mijo! Why are you awake? It's after midnight. You have school.
DIEGO: I couldn't sleep. I heard everybody. Through the floor. Are we buying the building?
MARISOL: Maybe. Someday. It's very complicated. Go to bed.
DIEGO: Why do you want to buy a building? We don't even have a car.
MARISOL: Ha. That's true. Come here. Sit with me for one minute.
DIEGO: Okay. One minute.
MARISOL: You know Abuelo's restaurant? Reyes Tacos? On Fifth Avenue?
DIEGO: The one that's a coffee shop now. With the eight-dollar latte.
MARISOL: That one. Abuelo cooked there for twenty-two years. Every day. Then the landlord tripled the rent. And it was over. In one month.
DIEGO: Abuelo told me. He said the landlord never even came inside. Not once.
MARISOL: Not once. I was pregnant. With you. I couldn't do anything. I watched him cry in the kitchen. I promised myself: never again. Not to someone I love.
DIEGO: So you became a paralegal.
MARISOL: So I became a paralegal. And now it's happening again. To Tommy. To Eddie. To us. And this time, I'm not scared. This time, I know things.
DIEGO: Like ACRIS.
MARISOL: How do you know about ACRIS?
DIEGO: Through the floor, Mom. Mrs. Esposito talks loud.
MARISOL: She does. Okay. Bed. Now. And Diego? Don't tell anyone at school. It's not real yet.
DIEGO: I won't. But if we buy it, can I be on the board? I'm good at math.
MARISOL: If we buy it, mijo, you can be the treasurer.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **제안·설득**
  - *Hear me out.* — "끝까지 들어 봐." 엉뚱하게 들릴 제안 앞에.
  - *What if, when they sell, we're the buyers?* — *What if…?*는 가능성을 여는 질문.
  - *Picture this.* — "이렇게 상상해 봐."
  - *That's the downside.* / *Here's the catch.* — 단점, 함정을 솔직하게 먼저 말하면 신뢰가 생긴다.
  - *That's the deal.* — "그게 조건이야."
- **반론·질문**
  - *Let me play devil's advocate.* — "일부러 반대 입장에서 말해 볼게."
  - *With all due respect, …* — "외람되지만…" 51화 *With respect*보다 더 격식 있다.
  - *That's a fair point / a fair question.* — 반론을 인정하며 받는다.
  - *That's a big "if."* — "그건 큰 '만약'이야."
- **community land trust** — 비영리 단체가 땅을 영구 소유해 투기를 막는 방식.
- **limited-equity co-op** — 지분 되팔기 가격에 상한이 있는 협동조합. 다음 가구도 감당할 수 있게 한다.
- **conflict of interest** — 이해 충돌. 토미의 말장난(*the best kind of interest*).
- **complicated crazy, not stupid crazy** — "복잡하게 미친 거지, 멍청하게 미친 건 아니야."
- **unanimous** — 만장일치.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- **What if** — *if*에 강세를 두고 끝을 올린다. *What IF, | when they sell, | we're the buyers?↗*
- **설득의 억양**: 문제 제시는 올리고(*They're losing money every month.↗*), 해결은 내린다(*That's why it's not alone.↘*).
- 큰 숫자: *three point four million* [쓰리 포인트 포어 밀리언], *two point seven million*, *nine point five percent*.
- 샘의 질문은 번호를 붙여 또박또박: *Question one. … Question two. …*
- 마지막 장면은 속삭이듯. 마리솔의 *never again*에 조용한 힘을 싣는다.
