# THE CORNER HOUSE — 4부 56화

## Marisol's Idea (마리솔의 아이디어)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 4부 2화(통산 56화) · 시기: 2026년 11월 30일(월) 밤 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 문 닫은 더 훅 → 4층 계단참
> - 등장인물: MARISOL, TOMMY, EDDIE, MRS. ESPOSITO, PRIYA, SAM, MARCUS, GINA, DIEGO
> - 사건: 약속한 일주일 뒤, 마리솔이 화이트보드를 들고 온다. "우리가 이 건물을 사면 어떨까요?" 에스포지토 부인은 시 등기 기록(ACRIS)에서 하버라인의 매입가 340만 달러와 대출 270만 달러를 찾아왔다. 마리솔은 커뮤니티 랜드 트러스트가 땅을 갖고, 세입자들이 지분 제한 협동조합으로 건물을 갖는 구조를 설명한다. 지나는 금요일에 통화한 브루클린 네이버후드 펀드의 답을 전한다. "건물과 사람들을 데려오면 이야기하자." 샘은 반대 측 입장에서 가장 어려운 질문들을 던진다. 마지막에 '알아보기만 하자'는 거수 표결을 한다. 밤에는 디에고가 엄마에게 왜 이 일을 하는지 묻는다.
> - 학습 포인트: 제안·설득(What if we…? / Hear me out. / Picture this. / The upside is… The downside is… / Here's the catch.), 반론(Let me play devil's advocate. / With all due respect… / That's a fair question.), 장단점 논의
> - 미국 현안: 세입자 우선매수 제도 논의(뉴욕은 아직 법이 아님), 커뮤니티 랜드 트러스트, 지분 제한 협동조합(limited-equity co-op), 고금리 사모 대출, 공공 등기 기록
> - 연결: 54화 '숫자가 안 맞으면 판다'와 살의 편지. 51화 루이스 후보의 세입자 우선매수 공약(결과는 이 화에서 공개). 14화 마리솔 부모의 레예스 타코스 폐업. 38화 바이아웃 거절. 45화 대니엘 브룩스가 언급한 랜드 트러스트.

## 대본

[Scene: Monday, 10:30 p.m. The Hook, closed. A small whiteboard leans on a chair. Marisol holds a marker. Tommy, Eddie, Mrs. Esposito, and Priya sit around one table.]

MARISOL: Okay, thank you all for coming. I know it's late and you're tired, so I'll try to be clear, and quick.
TOMMY: Quick-ish, you mean. It's a family word now, so we all have to use it.
MARISOL: Quick-ish, then. Okay, so here's the thing. A week ago, Brian told us Harborline might sell, because the numbers don't work.
EDDIE: "They always sell." I remember, because he said it like he was talking about the weather.
MARISOL: So hear me out. I know it might sound crazy at first. What if, when they sell, we're the buyers?
PRIYA: We? As in, us? You mean the people sitting at this table right now?
MARISOL: I mean the people in this building, all of us, buying it together.
TOMMY: Marisol, I have four hundred dollars in my checking account, and a jukebox nobody wants to buy.
MARISOL: I know, and nobody here has that kind of money alone. That's why it's not alone.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Before you explain the whole plan, I did a little homework of my own. May I share it?
MARISOL: Please do. Dolores found something really important, and I want everyone to hear it from her.
MRS. ESPOSITO: The city has a public website called ACRIS, where every property sale in New York is recorded. Every mortgage is in there too.
EDDIE: Wait, you can just look it up? Anybody can do that, without a lawyer?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Anybody at all. It took me two hours and three cups of tea, but I found it. Harborline paid three point four million dollars, on August twenty-fourth.
PRIYA: Three point four million dollars, for this building? For our creaky old building with the leaky roof?
MRS. ESPOSITO: And they borrowed two point seven million from a private lender, not a bank. The interest rate is right there in the document: nine point five percent.
TOMMY: Nine and a half percent? My credit card is twenty-four, so that doesn't sound so bad to me. But for a building, that's a lot, right?
MARISOL: It's a lot, Tommy. It means their monthly payment is huge, about twenty-one thousand dollars, and that's just the interest.
EDDIE: Twenty-one thousand a month, and they're probably getting about twelve from all of us put together.
MARISOL: Exactly. Add the construction and the lawyers, and they're losing money every month. That's why they want Tommy out, and us out, so they can raise the rents fast.
PRIYA: Okay, so they're desperate, I get that. But that doesn't mean they'll want to sell to us.
MARISOL: That's a fair point, and I've thought about it a lot. Here's my idea. Picture this. There are two pieces, and they fit together.
MARISOL: Piece one: a community land trust, which is a nonprofit, buys the land. They own the ground under the building forever, and they never sell it to a speculator.
EDDIE: A land trust. Danielle Brooks used that word back in October, when she came to talk to us.
MARISOL: She did. Piece two: we, the tenants, form a co-op, which is short for cooperative. The co-op owns the building, and each family owns a share of it.
TOMMY: A share, like a stock? Like the stuff people yell about on TV all day?
MARISOL: Kind of, but this is a limited-equity co-op. That means when you sell your share, you can't sell it for a crazy price, because there's a limit. That way the apartments stay affordable for the next family.
MRS. ESPOSITO: So if I understand you correctly, nobody gets rich.
MARISOL: Nobody gets rich, that's true. But nobody gets kicked out either, and that's the deal.
PRIYA: And what about the monthly cost? What would Sam and I actually have to pay?
MARISOL: I don't know yet, and that's the honest answer. We'd pay a monthly fee that covers the loan, the taxes, and the repairs, and Gina is working on the numbers.
EDDIE: And what happens to the bar? What about Tommy, and Marcus's shop?
MARISOL: The co-op would be their landlord, and for once, it would be a landlord who actually wants them to stay.
TOMMY: A landlord who drinks at my bar every night? That sounds like a conflict of interest.
EDDIE: Maybe it is, but it's the best kind of interest there is.
PRIYA: Can I be honest for a second? This scares me a little, because Sam and I are getting married, and we were thinking about buying a place in Jersey City.
TOMMY: Jersey City? Priya, please don't say that word in my bar.
PRIYA: It's cheaper, Tommy! And there's the PATH train, so it's not like it's the moon.
MARISOL: I understand, and nobody has to stay forever. But what if your place to buy is right here, on the fourth floor?
PRIYA: A share of our own building... Sam would need numbers for that, and lots of them.
MARISOL: Then he'll get numbers. Gina is coming at eleven with Sam and Marcus, and she's bringing everything she has.
EDDIE: I'll be honest too, then. I'm eighty-three years old, so why would I buy anything? I won't be around to see the end of the loan.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Eddie! Don't you dare talk like that!
EDDIE: It's true, Dolores, so just do the math. With a thirty-year loan, I'd be a hundred and thirteen.
MARISOL: Eddie, you wouldn't be doing it for you. You'd do it so the next person in 3A never gets a letter like yours, offering forty thousand dollars to disappear.
EDDIE: So it's like planting a tree for shade you'll never get to sit in.
MARISOL: Exactly like that, Eddie. That's exactly what it is.

[Scene: 11:00 p.m. Same table. Sam, Marcus, and Gina have joined. Priya and Eddie have stepped out for air. Mrs. Esposito went home to feed Biscuit.]

SAM: Okay, I'm sorry I'm late, but Priya texted me the whole thing on my way over. Can I ask the hard questions?
MARISOL: Please do. Honestly, that's exactly why I asked you to come.
SAM: Then let me play devil's advocate. I'm not against this, but someone has to be the bad guy.
TOMMY: Well, you're good at it, Sam. You work in insurance, so you get paid to say no.
SAM: Thanks, Tommy. Question one: who owns the problems? If the boiler dies, who pays for it, and if the roof leaks, who fixes it at three a.m.?
MARISOL: We do, and that's the downside. When you own the building, you own all the problems too.
SAM: Question two is meetings. How many meetings are we talking about, and do they go on forever? I already sit through work meetings, so I don't want building meetings every Tuesday.
GINA: Probably once a month, plus a board of three or five people. They'd handle most things, so the rest of us wouldn't have to.
SAM: Question three, and this is the big one. Priya and I are getting married in the spring, and we might have a kid and need two bedrooms. If we're locked into a co-op share, can we leave?
MARISOL: Yes, you can always leave. You sell your share back at the limited price, so you won't lose your money, but you won't make a fortune either.
SAM: Okay, that's fair. And question four, with all due respect, Marisol: where does three million dollars come from?
GINA: That's where I come in, actually. Can I take this one?
MARISOL: Go ahead, Gina. You did the work, so you should be the one to explain it.
GINA: On Friday, I called the Brooklyn Neighborhood Fund, the one Linda Chen told us about. I talked to a woman named Renée for forty minutes.
TOMMY: Forty minutes on the phone? So what did she say?
GINA: She said they've financed eleven tenant co-ops since twenty eighteen, in Brooklyn and the Bronx. Some were smaller than ours, and some were bigger.
MARCUS: Eleven of them? So it's real, then. It's not just a fairy tale.
GINA: It's real, but here's the catch. They need three things: a building that's actually for sale, a group of tenants that's organized, and a nonprofit partner, like a land trust.
SAM: And what about the money? I mean the actual money, not the theory.
GINA: It's usually a mix: a loan from them, money from a city program, some grants, and a small down payment from the tenants.
SAM: And how small is "small," exactly? Because "small" means very different things to different people.
GINA: She said, "Usually a few thousand per family. Sometimes less. Sometimes we find it for them."
SAM: A few thousand dollars per family. Okay, I have to admit that's not crazy.
MARCUS: Can I ask a question? I'm not a resident, I'm commercial, so do I get a vote? Or am I just... the guy with the bikes?
MARISOL: That's a great question, and I don't know the answer yet. Some co-ops include the stores and some don't, so we'll decide that together.
MARCUS: Because I'm in, if I can be in. My lease is two years, and after that, Harborline can do whatever it wants. With you guys as my landlord, I'd sleep a lot better.
TOMMY: Same here. Honestly, I don't need to own anything. I just need a landlord who isn't a spreadsheet.
SAM: Okay, one more, and it's the really hard one. What if Harborline never sells, or holds on, or sells to another company? Then we've done all this work for nothing.
MARISOL: Then we lose, and we're exactly where we are now, except that we're organized, angry, and smarter.
GINA: And there's one more thing you should all know about, which is the election. Ruiz won.
SAM: Wait, Ruiz won? The last I heard, it was still too close to call.
GINA: They finished counting the mail-in ballots on Wednesday, and she won by four hundred and twelve votes.
MARCUS: Four hundred and twelve votes? That's basically two buildings and a bar.
GINA: And her first bill is the tenant opportunity bill, the right of first offer. If it passes in Albany, tenants get the first chance to buy, before a company does.
SAM: If it passes, and that's a big "if." Albany is famously slow.
MARISOL: Albany is very slow, so we can't wait for it. But it still helps, because it means people are talking about this, including politicians and lenders.
SAM: Okay, I'm done being the bad guy, at least for now.
TOMMY: You were a very good bad guy, Sam. I'd give you four stars.
SAM: Thank you. Honestly? I came here thinking, "This is crazy." Now I think it's... complicated crazy, not stupid crazy.
MARISOL: I'll happily take complicated crazy, Sam. That's more than I expected tonight.

[Scene: 11:40 p.m. Everyone is back at the table, standing. Priya, Eddie, and Mrs. Esposito have returned.]

MARISOL: Okay, I'm not asking anyone to sign anything, because that's rule number four. I'm just asking one small question.
EDDIE: Then ask it quickly, please, because my legs are getting tired.
MARISOL: Should we explore this? I mean just explore: talk to the land trust, get real numbers, and meet again in two weeks. Hands up if yes.
EDDIE: Yes, with one hand. I need the other one to hold my cane.
PRIYA: Yes, with both hands. I'm still scared, but I'm in.
SAM: Half a hand for me. Is half a hand okay?
MARISOL: Half a hand is a yes with questions, and I love questions. Tommy already said yes at the bar, so that's unanimous, kind of.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Yes from me, and yes from Biscuit, too. She's a resident, after all.
MARISOL: Dolores, I'm sorry, but Biscuit can't vote.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Why not? She's lived here longer than Sam has.
SAM: That's... technically true, and I hate it.
MARISOL: Okay, two weeks from now, on December fourteenth, at the same table. Bring your questions.
EDDIE: And bring your own coffee, for everybody's sake.

[Scene: 12:15 a.m. The fourth-floor landing. Diego sits on the top step in pajamas. Marisol comes up the stairs with her whiteboard.]

DIEGO: Mom, where were you? You were gone forever.
MARISOL: Mijo! Why are you still awake? It's after midnight, and you have school in the morning.
DIEGO: I couldn't sleep, because I could hear everybody through the floor. Are we really buying the building?
MARISOL: Maybe, someday, but it's very complicated. Now go to bed.
DIEGO: But why do you want to buy a building? We don't even have a car.
MARISOL: Ha. Okay, that's true. Come here and sit with me for one minute.
DIEGO: Okay, one minute, but I'm going to count.
MARISOL: You know Abuelo's old restaurant, Reyes Tacos, on Fifth Avenue?
DIEGO: The one that's a coffee shop now? With the eight-dollar latte?
MARISOL: That's the one. Abuelo cooked there every day for twenty-two years. Then the landlord tripled the rent, and in one month, it was over.
DIEGO: Abuelo told me about that. He said the landlord never even came inside, not even once.
MARISOL: Not once. I was pregnant with you, and I couldn't do anything, so I just watched him cry in the kitchen. That's when I promised myself: never again, not to someone I love.
DIEGO: And that's why you became a paralegal.
MARISOL: That's why I became a paralegal. And now it's happening again, to Tommy, to Eddie, and to us. But this time I'm not scared, because this time, I know things.
DIEGO: You mean like ACRIS?
MARISOL: Wait, how do you know about ACRIS?
DIEGO: Through the floor, Mom. Mrs. Esposito talks really loud.
MARISOL: She does, doesn't she? Okay, bed, right now. And Diego? Don't tell anyone at school, because it's not real yet.
DIEGO: I won't, I promise. But if we buy it, can I be on the board? I'm really good at math.
MARISOL: If we buy it, mijo, you can be the treasurer.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **제안·설득**
  - *Hear me out.* — "끝까지 들어 봐." 엉뚱하게 들릴 제안 앞에.
  - *What if, when they sell, we're the buyers?* — *What if…?*는 가능성을 여는 질문.
  - *Picture this.* — "이렇게 상상해 봐."
  - *That's the downside.* / *Here's the catch.* — 단점, 함정을 솔직하게 먼저 말하면 신뢰가 생긴다.
  - *That's the deal.* — "그게 조건이야."
- **반론·질문**
  - *Let me play devil's advocate.* — "일부러 반대 입장에서 말해 볼게."
  - *With all due respect, …* — "외람되지만…" 51화 *With respect*보다 더 격식 있다.
  - *That's a fair point / a fair question.* — 반론을 인정하며 받는다.
  - *That's a big "if."* — "그건 큰 '만약'이야."
- **community land trust** — 비영리 단체가 땅을 영구 소유해 투기를 막는 방식.
- **limited-equity co-op** — 지분 되팔기 가격에 상한이 있는 협동조합. 다음 가구도 감당할 수 있게 한다.
- **conflict of interest** — 이해 충돌. 토미의 말장난(*the best kind of interest*).
- **complicated crazy, not stupid crazy** — "복잡하게 미친 거지, 멍청하게 미친 건 아니야."
- **unanimous** — 만장일치.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- **What if** — *if*에 강세를 두고 끝을 올린다. *What IF, | when they sell, | we're the buyers?↗*
- **설득의 억양**: 문제 제시는 올리고(*They're losing money every month.↗*), 해결은 내린다(*That's why it's not alone.↘*).
- 큰 숫자: *three point four million* [쓰리 포인트 포어 밀리언], *two point seven million*, *nine point five percent*.
- 샘의 질문은 번호를 붙여 또박또박: *Question one: … Question two is meetings.*
- 마지막 장면은 속삭이듯. 마리솔의 *never again*에 조용한 힘을 싣는다.
