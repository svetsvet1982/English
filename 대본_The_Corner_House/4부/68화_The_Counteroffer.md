# THE CORNER HOUSE — 4부 68화

## The Counteroffer (역제안)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 4부 14화(통산 68화) · 시기: 2027년 2월 2일(화) 오전·저녁·밤 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 개점 전 더 훅 → 같은 테이블(협상) → 저녁의 바(이사회 내부 회의) → 3층 계단참
> - 등장인물: TOMMY, MRS. ESPOSITO, EDDIE, WHITLOCK, MARISOL, MS. HARGROVE, PRIYA, SAM
> - 사건: 휘틀록과 하버라인 변호사 하그로브가 더 훅에 온다. 역제안은 두 갈래다. A안은 4월 30일까지 조합이 350만 달러에 사는 것이다. B안은 A안이 무산되면 세입자들에게 '열쇠 값'을 주고 내보내는 것이다. 에디 7만 5천, 부인 6만 5천, 마리솔 5만, 프리야·샘 3만 5천, 토미의 바 6만이다. 개별 제안은 14일 뒤 만료되고, 받으려면 비밀 유지에 서명해야 한다. 이사회는 계획대로 듣고, 적고, "서면으로 답하겠다"고만 한다. 저녁 내부 회의에서는 유혹이 드러난다. 프리야와 샘은 3만 5천이면 결혼식과 저지시티 집 계약금이 생긴다. 마리솔은 5만이 디에고의 대학 자금이 된다는 사실을 무시하지 못한다. 밤 계단에서 에디는 아들 마이클이 7만 5천을 받으라고 했다고 털어놓는다.
> - 학습 포인트: 협상 화법(Let me make sure I understand. / So what you're saying is… / What's the catch? / That's contingent on… / We'll respond in writing.), 조건 계산(If A, then B. Unless… / On the condition that…), 반박(With respect, that's not an offer. That's a deadline.)
> - 미국 현안: '열쇠 값(cash for keys)' 바이아웃, 시한부 제안(exploding offer)과 비밀 유지 조항, 세입자 연대를 갈라놓는 개별 협상, 빈집으로 만든 뒤 고가 매각
> - 연결: 38화 첫 바이아웃(에디 4만·부인 3.5만·마리솔 1.2만·프리야·샘 1만)과 서면 거절 후 180일 연락 금지(45화). 59화 하버라인 손익분기 330만. 66화 휘틀록의 화요일 면담 제안과 토미 커피 작전. 67화 3월 1일 시한. 57화 냉장고 규칙 4번 '절대 혼자 서명하지 않기'.

## 대본

[Scene: Tuesday, 9:50 a.m. The Hook, before opening. One long table in the middle. A pot of coffee. Tommy stands behind the bar. Eddie and Mrs. Esposito sit at the end of the bar, like an audience.]

TOMMY: Okay, the coffee's ready. It's extra strong and extra terrible, on Dolores's orders.
MRS. ESPOSITO: They're not my orders, they're Marisol's strategy. I just suggested the "extra."
EDDIE: Tommy, it smells like a tire fire in here. You've really outdone yourself this time.
TOMMY: Thank you, Eddie. I've been training for this for twenty years.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Remember, we're not on the board, and we're not at the table. We're observers, which means we don't speak.
EDDIE: Dolores, you've never not spoken in your entire life.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Then today is the first time. I brought a notebook, so I'll speak in pencil.
MARISOL: They're here, there's a black car outside. Everybody, calm faces, please. Tommy, coffee.
WHITLOCK: Good morning, Ms. Reyes, Mr. Russo. And I see the observers are here as well. Mrs. Esposito, Mr. O'Neill.
EDDIE: Good morning, Mr. Whitlock. Welcome back to the last real bar in Brooklyn.
WHITLOCK: This is Ms. Hargrove, our attorney. She'll walk you through the offer.
TOMMY: Coffee? It's fresh, and I made it myself.
WHITLOCK: I brought my own, thank you. I've had your coffee before, back in September.
TOMMY: Your loss, then. How about you, Ms. Hargrove?
[Ms. Hargrove smiles politely and holds out a cup.]
MRS. ESPOSITO: She's brave, I'll give her that. Write that down as number one.

[Scene: 10:10 a.m. The long table. Marisol, Priya, and Sam on one side. Whitlock and Ms. Hargrove on the other. Ms. Hargrove sips the coffee and keeps a perfectly straight face.]

MS. HARGROVE: Thank you for meeting with us, and I'll be brief. Harborline is prepared to offer you two paths, which we're calling Path A and Path B.
MARISOL: Go ahead. We'll listen first, and then we'll ask questions.
MS. HARGROVE: Under Path A, Harborline will sell the property to your cooperative for three point five million dollars, with closing no later than April thirtieth.
SAM: Three point five? Our offer was three point one.
MS. HARGROVE: I'm aware of that. But three point five is our number, because it covers the loan and the construction costs.
PRIYA: And what happens if we can't reach three point five by April thirtieth?
MS. HARGROVE: Then Path B begins on May first. Harborline will offer relocation payments to each household, in exchange for voluntarily vacating the apartment.
MARISOL: In other words, cash for keys.
MS. HARGROVE: We call it a relocation package, and the amounts are generous. Mr. O'Neill would receive seventy-five thousand, and Mrs. Esposito sixty-five thousand. Ms. Reyes would receive fifty thousand, and Ms. Shah and Mr. Novak thirty-five thousand.
SAM: And what about the bar?
MS. HARGROVE: The Hook would receive sixty thousand to surrender the lease early. Bell Bikes would be offered a relocation to another Harborline property in Bushwick.
PRIYA: Let me make sure I understand. If we don't pay three point five, you pay everybody to leave. And then what?
WHITLOCK: And then the building is sold, vacant, to a buyer who will renovate it fully and rent the apartments at market rate.
SAM: Vacant buildings sell for more, though. How much more are we talking about, four million? Four point two?
WHITLOCK: I won't discuss other buyers.
SAM: You don't have to, because I can do the math. A vacant building is worth more than an occupied, rent-stabilized one. That's why you'd pay three hundred thousand dollars to empty it.
PRIYA: Marcus has a two-year lease, so you can't just move him out.
MS. HARGROVE: We wouldn't force anything, we'd simply make an offer. Leases can be bought out, and in my experience, everything has a price.
MARISOL: Not everything has a price. Mr. O'Neill turned down forty thousand in September, and he did it in writing.
MS. HARGROVE: Forty is not seventy-five, Ms. Reyes. People's answers tend to change when the number changes.
SAM: And when the boiler dies in January.
WHITLOCK: That was not intentional.
SAM: Brian Kowalski said, on the record, that Harborline refused to replace it. "Let the tenants buy the boiler too." Those were his exact words.
WHITLOCK: ...Brian talks too much.
MARISOL: Brian told the truth, and that's a different thing.
MS. HARGROVE: There's one more detail. The relocation offers are individual, so each household negotiates separately. Each offer expires fourteen days after it's made, and each requires a confidentiality agreement.
MARISOL: So what you're saying is: the offers are secret, they expire quickly, and every family decides alone.
MS. HARGROVE: That's standard practice in this kind of situation.
MARISOL: With respect, Ms. Hargrove, that's not an offer. That's a deadline. With a gag order.
WHITLOCK: Ms. Reyes, let me be honest with you. My partners prefer Path A, because it's a clean sale. No headlines, no more boilers, and no more reporters.
PRIYA: Then why make it impossible? Three point five is four hundred thousand more than we can do.
WHITLOCK: Because three point five is what we need. I'm not trying to win here, I'm trying not to lose.
SAM: Mr. Whitlock, every month you wait, you lose twenty-one thousand in interest, plus the emergency repair bill and the violation. Three point five by April isn't a real number. It's a wish.
WHITLOCK: Then make me a real one, in writing, by next Tuesday.
MARISOL: Thank you. We'll respond in writing by next Tuesday. Is there anything else?
MS. HARGROVE: Only this. Path B is contingent on Path A failing. If you show us financing for three point five, Path B disappears.
MARISOL: Understood, and thank you for coming. Tommy, can you get the door?
MS. HARGROVE: And Mr. Russo? The coffee was... memorable.
SAM: He'll take that as a compliment. Everybody does, eventually.

[Scene: 7:30 p.m. The Hook, back table. Marisol, Priya, Sam, Eddie, and Tommy sit with copies of a one-page summary Mrs. Esposito typed during the meeting.]

MARISOL: Okay, before we talk strategy, I want to do something. I want everybody to be honest about the money, about Path B, and about how it feels.
TOMMY: You want honest? Okay, they're offering sixty thousand for the bar. Two months ago I said no to thirty, and now it's double.
EDDIE: So is it tempting?
TOMMY: Everything is tempting at fifty-eight, Eddie. My back hurts, and the ice machine is dying. Sixty thousand is a lot of ice machines.
MARISOL: Priya? Sam?
PRIYA: Can I be honest? It's scary how tempting it is. Thirty-five thousand is our whole wedding, the big one with all the cousins, plus a down payment in Jersey City.
SAM: I ran the numbers, and I'm sorry, I can't help it. If we took thirty-five and bought a condo in Jersey City, our monthly cost would be higher. But we'd own it, really own it, not just a limited share.
MARISOL: That's true. A limited-equity share is never going to make you rich.
SAM: And I keep thinking about kids, because Priya wants two. A two-bedroom in Red Hook costs five thousand a month on the open market.
PRIYA: Sam, stop. Please don't do the kid math, not tonight.
SAM: Sorry, it's what I do when I'm scared. I do math.
EDDIE: What about you, Marisol? They offered you fifty thousand.
MARISOL: Honestly, I thought about Diego and college. Fifty thousand is almost four years at a state school, and I've never had fifty thousand dollars in my life, not even close.
TOMMY: So everybody's tempted, even you.
MARISOL: Even me, and that's exactly why I asked. I don't want anybody to feel ashamed. They want us to feel tempted alone, in secret, with a fourteen-day clock running.
EDDIE: And with a confidentiality agreement, so that you can't even tell your neighbor.
MARISOL: Exactly. The strategy isn't the money, it's the silence. If we each sit alone with our number, we'll each start doing math, like Sam.
SAM: Hey! Okay, that's fair, but still, hey.
PRIYA: So what do we do?
MARISOL: We do what we did tonight, which is say the numbers out loud, together. Then we decide together. Rule number four.
TOMMY: Never sign alone.
EDDIE: Never sign alone. I wrote it on my fridge in September, in big letters, right next to Peggy's picture.
PRIYA: Okay, then here's my real answer. Thirty-five thousand would pay for a wedding, but I want to get married on the pier, here, with all of you there. Not in Jersey City with my mother's hundred and forty cousins.
SAM: And I'm the treasurer. If I take the money, I'm the treasurer who quit, and I don't want to be that guy.
TOMMY: And me? If I take sixty, Angela will haunt me. She will, every single night, with a frying pan.
MARISOL: So what do we offer them next Tuesday?
SAM: We can't do three point five, but maybe we can move a little. Three point two, maybe three point two five, if the city gives us more and the grants come through.
MARISOL: And we ask for a sixty-thousand-dollar credit for the boiler. Harborline let it die, so the buyer shouldn't have to pay for that.
SAM: Three point two five, minus sixty for the boiler, is three point one nine net. That's closer to their number, but not too far from ours.
TOMMY: Will they take it?
MARISOL: I don't know. But it's a real number, and Whitlock asked for a real number, so we'll give him one.
PRIYA: And if they don't take it?
MARISOL: Then we sit down again, and again, until March first. They have twenty-one thousand reasons a month to keep talking to us.

[Scene: 10:15 p.m. The landing between the second and third floors. Eddie sits on his step. Mrs. Esposito comes up with two mugs of tea.]

MRS. ESPOSITO: You didn't say much at the meeting tonight. I could hear everybody through the floor, except you.
EDDIE: I said a little, and I asked some questions. Old men ask questions so they don't have to answer them.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Then I'll ask you one. Seventy-five thousand dollars. Is it tempting?
EDDIE: Michael called at six. Somebody sent him the article about the boiler, and about the offers.
MRS. ESPOSITO: How does he know about the offers?
EDDIE: I told him last week. I tell him everything now, no secrets, because that's Gina's rule.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And what did he say?
EDDIE: He said, "Dad, take the seventy-five and come to Florida. Linda's mother has a room with a ramp and no stairs. You'll be warm."
MRS. ESPOSITO: No stairs, and warm. That's not nothing, Eddie.
EDDIE: It's not nothing. In January, at fifty-one degrees, sitting in my chair, I thought about Florida. I'll be honest with you. I thought, "Maybe the boy is right."
MRS. ESPOSITO: And now?
EDDIE: Now I think about Peggy, and about Jimmy, and the hook over my door. About a boy who wants to skate backward, and about two knocks at eight o'clock.
MRS. ESPOSITO: The knocks are mine too, you know. If you go to Florida, who am I supposed to knock for?
EDDIE: You'd find somebody. Sam, maybe. He'd knock back with a spreadsheet.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Eddie, I'm being serious.
EDDIE: I know, and I'm serious too. I'm not taking it, Dolores, and I told Michael tonight. He was angry, and then he was quiet. Then he said, "Okay, Dad. But if it gets cold again, you call me first."
MRS. ESPOSITO: That's a good son you raised, Eddie.
EDDIE: He's a good son, he just lives too far away. And he doesn't understand that this staircase is the whole world.
MRS. ESPOSITO: It's a very small world, though. Forty-two steps.
EDDIE: Forty-two steps. I've counted them for fifty-two years, and some days they're the longest walk in Brooklyn. But they're mine.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Ours.
EDDIE: Ours. Okay, help me up, my knees are frozen. Eight o'clock, two knocks.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Two knocks, like always. And Eddie, thank you for staying.
EDDIE: Don't thank me yet, because we haven't won anything.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Can I tell you something? They offered me sixty-five thousand today, and I thought about it too, for one minute.
EDDIE: You? Dolores Esposito? Tempted?
MRS. ESPOSITO: My sister lives in Staten Island, and she has a garden. I thought, "Tomatoes, a garden, and no Harborline."
EDDIE: And then?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Then I thought, "Who will write the minutes?" And the minute was over.
EDDIE: Sixty seconds of temptation. That's very disciplined of you.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I timed it with the egg timer.
EDDIE: Of course you did.
MRS. ESPOSITO: We did win tonight, Eddie. Nobody signed alone. That's a win.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **협상 화법**
  - *Let me make sure I understand.* — "제가 제대로 이해했는지 확인할게요." 상대의 말을 다시 정리해 함정을 드러낸다.
  - *So what you're saying is…* — "그러니까 말씀하시는 건…" 요약하며 압박한다.
  - *That's contingent on…* — "그건 ~을 조건으로 한다."
  - *We'll respond in writing by next Tuesday.* — "서면으로 답하겠습니다." 즉답을 피하는 정석.
  - *Then make me a real one, in writing, by next Tuesday.* — "그럼 진짜 숫자를 줘 보세요."
- **반박**
  - *With respect, Ms. Hargrove, that's not an offer. That's a deadline. With a gag order.* — "외람되지만, 그건 제안이 아니라 시한이에요. 입막음까지 붙은."
  - *Three point five by April isn't a real number. It's a wish.* — 숫자가 아니라 바람일 뿐이라는 반박.
- **조건 표현**
  - *If you show us financing for three point five, Path B disappears.* — 조건과 결과.
  - *Path B begins on May first.* / *Each offer expires fourteen days after it's made*
- **어휘**
  - *cash for keys* 열쇠 값(나가는 대가로 주는 돈) / *relocation package* 이주 지원금(완곡한 표현)
  - *vacate* 비우다 / *surrender the lease* 임대 계약을 조기 반납하다 / *confidentiality agreement* 비밀 유지 계약
  - *gag order* 함구령 / *standard practice* 업계 관행 / *net* 공제 후 실질 금액 / *credit* 매매가에서 빼 주는 금액
- **The coffee was... memorable.** — "커피가… 기억에 남네요." 정중한 혹평.
- **Old men ask questions so they don't have to answer them.** — 에디의 자기 고백.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 하그로브 변호사는 감정 없이 매끄럽게. 숫자는 또박또박: *seventy-five thousand*, *sixty-five thousand*.
- 마리솔의 *So what you're saying is:* 다음에 세 가지를 나열하며 점점 무게를 싣는다: *the offers are secret, | they expire quickly, | and every family decides alone.*
- *That's not an offer. | That's a deadline. | With a gag order.* — 세 마디를 끊어 단단하게.
- 저녁 회의는 고백의 톤. 각자 "솔직히"로 시작할 때 목소리를 조금 낮춘다.
- 계단 장면은 속삭임. *Forty-two steps.*는 숫자를 세듯 천천히.
- 마지막 부인의 *Nobody signed alone. | That's a win.* — 조용한 승리감.

마리솔: 왔어요, 밖에 검은 차가 있어요. 다들 침착한 얼굴로요. 토미, 커피.
휘틀록: 좋은 아침입니다, 레예스 씨, 루소 씨. 참관인들도 와 계시네요. 에스포지토 부인, 오닐 씨.
에디: 좋은 아침이오, 휘틀록 씨. 브루클린의 마지막 진짜 바에 다시 온 걸 환영하오.
휘틀록: 이쪽은 저희 변호사 하그로브 씨입니다. 제안을 설명해 드릴 겁니다.
토미: 커피 드릴까요? 갓 끓였고, 제가 직접 만들었어요.

토미: 그럼 손해 보시는 거죠. 하그로브 씨는요?
에스포지토 부인: 용감하네, 그건 인정해야겠어요. 1번으로 적어 둬요.
하그로브 변호사: 만나 주셔서 감사합니다, 짧게 말씀드리죠. 하버라인은 두 가지 길을 제안할 준비가 되어 있습니다. A안과 B안이라고 부르겠습니다.
마리솔: 말씀하세요. 먼저 듣고, 그다음에 질문하겠습니다.
하그로브 변호사: A안에 따르면 하버라인은 이 부동산을 귀 조합에 350만 달러에 매도하고, 거래는 늦어도 4월 30일까지 마칩니다.
샘: 350만이요? 우리 제안은 310만이었는데요.
하그로브 변호사: 알고 있습니다. 하지만 350만이 저희 숫자입니다. 대출과 공사비를 메우는 금액이니까요.
프리야: 그리고 4월 30일까지 350만을 못 맞추면 어떻게 되죠?

마리솔: 다시 말해, 열쇠 값이네요.
하그로브 변호사: 저희는 이주 지원 패키지라고 부르고, 금액은 넉넉합니다. 오닐 씨는 7만 5천, 에스포지토 부인은 6만 5천을 받게 됩니다. 레예스 씨는 5만, 샤 씨와 노박 씨는 3만 5천입니다.
샘: 그럼 바는요?
