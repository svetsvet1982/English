# THE CORNER HOUSE — 4부 68화

## The Counteroffer (역제안)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 4부 14화(통산 68화) · 시기: 2027년 2월 2일(화) 오전·저녁·밤 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 개점 전 더 훅 → 같은 테이블(협상) → 저녁의 바(이사회 내부 회의) → 3층 계단참
> - 등장인물: TOMMY, MRS. ESPOSITO, EDDIE, WHITLOCK, MARISOL, MS. HARGROVE, PRIYA, SAM
> - 사건: 휘틀록과 하버라인 변호사 하그로브가 더 훅에 온다. 역제안은 두 갈래다. A안은 4월 30일까지 조합이 350만 달러에 사는 것이다. B안은 A안이 무산되면 세입자들에게 '열쇠 값'을 주고 내보내는 것이다. 에디 7만 5천, 부인 6만 5천, 마리솔 5만, 프리야·샘 3만 5천, 토미의 바 6만이다. 개별 제안은 14일 뒤 만료되고, 받으려면 비밀 유지에 서명해야 한다. 이사회는 계획대로 듣고, 적고, "서면으로 답하겠다"고만 한다. 저녁 내부 회의에서는 유혹이 드러난다. 프리야와 샘은 3만 5천이면 결혼식과 저지시티 집 계약금이 생긴다. 마리솔은 5만이 디에고의 대학 자금이 된다는 사실을 무시하지 못한다. 밤 계단에서 에디는 아들 마이클이 7만 5천을 받으라고 했다고 털어놓는다.
> - 학습 포인트: 협상 화법(Let me make sure I understand. / So what you're saying is… / What's the catch? / That's contingent on… / We'll respond in writing.), 조건 계산(If A, then B. Unless… / On the condition that…), 반박(With respect, that's not an offer. That's a deadline.)
> - 미국 현안: '열쇠 값(cash for keys)' 바이아웃, 시한부 제안(exploding offer)과 비밀 유지 조항, 세입자 연대를 갈라놓는 개별 협상, 빈집으로 만든 뒤 고가 매각
> - 연결: 38화 첫 바이아웃(에디 4만·부인 3.5만·마리솔 1.2만·프리야·샘 1만)과 서면 거절 후 180일 연락 금지(45화). 59화 하버라인 손익분기 330만. 66화 휘틀록의 화요일 면담 제안과 토미 커피 작전. 67화 3월 1일 시한. 57화 냉장고 규칙 4번 '절대 혼자 서명하지 않기'.

## 대본

[Scene: Tuesday, 9:50 a.m. The Hook, before opening. One long table in the middle. A pot of coffee. Tommy stands behind the bar. Eddie and Mrs. Esposito sit at the end of the bar, like an audience.]

TOMMY: Okay. Coffee's ready. Extra strong. Extra terrible. Dolores's orders.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Not my orders. Marisol's strategy. I just suggested the extra.
EDDIE: Tommy, it smells like a tire fire. You've outdone yourself.
TOMMY: Thank you, Eddie. I've been training for twenty years.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Remember, we're not on the board. We're not at the table. We're observers. We don't speak.
EDDIE: Dolores, you've never not spoken in your life.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Today is the first time. I brought a notebook. I'll speak in pencil.
MARISOL: They're here. Black car. Outside. Everybody, calm faces. Tommy, coffee.
WHITLOCK: Good morning. Ms. Reyes. Mr. Russo. And... the observers, I see. Mrs. Esposito. Mr. O'Neill.
EDDIE: Good morning, Mr. Whitlock. Welcome back to the last real bar in Brooklyn.
WHITLOCK: This is Ms. Hargrove. Our attorney. She'll walk you through the offer.
TOMMY: Coffee? It's fresh. I made it myself.
WHITLOCK: I brought my own, thank you. I've had your coffee before. In September.
TOMMY: Your loss. Ms. Hargrove?
[Ms. Hargrove smiles politely and holds out a cup.]
MRS. ESPOSITO: She's brave. Write that down. Number one.

[Scene: 10:10 a.m. The long table. Marisol, Priya, and Sam on one side. Whitlock and Ms. Hargrove on the other. Ms. Hargrove sips the coffee and keeps a perfectly straight face.]

MS. HARGROVE: Thank you for meeting with us. I'll be brief. Harborline is prepared to offer two paths. Path A and Path B.
MARISOL: Go ahead. We'll listen first. Then we'll ask questions.
MS. HARGROVE: Path A: Harborline will sell the property to your cooperative for three point five million dollars. Closing no later than April thirtieth.
SAM: Three point five. Our offer was three point one.
MS. HARGROVE: I'm aware. Three point five is our number. It covers the loan and the construction costs.
PRIYA: And if we can't reach three point five by April thirtieth?
MS. HARGROVE: Then Path B begins. On May first, Harborline will offer relocation payments to each household. In exchange for voluntarily vacating the apartment.
MARISOL: Cash for keys.
MS. HARGROVE: We call it a relocation package. The amounts are generous. Mr. O'Neill, seventy-five thousand. Mrs. Esposito, sixty-five thousand. Ms. Reyes, fifty thousand. Ms. Shah and Mr. Novak, thirty-five thousand.
SAM: And the bar?
MS. HARGROVE: The Hook would receive sixty thousand to surrender the lease early. Bell Bikes would be offered a relocation to another Harborline property. In Bushwick.
PRIYA: Let me make sure I understand. If we don't pay three point five, you pay everybody to leave. And then?
WHITLOCK: And then the building is sold. Vacant. To a buyer who will renovate it fully. Market-rate apartments.
SAM: Vacant buildings sell for more. How much more? Four million? Four point two?
WHITLOCK: I won't discuss other buyers.
SAM: You don't have to. I can do the math. A vacant building is worth more than an occupied, rent-stabilized one. That's why you'd pay three hundred thousand dollars to empty it.
PRIYA: Marcus has a lease. Two years. You can't just move him.
MS. HARGROVE: We wouldn't force anything. We'd offer. Leases can be bought out. Everything has a price.
MARISOL: Not everything. Mr. O'Neill turned down forty thousand in September. In writing.
MS. HARGROVE: Forty is not seventy-five, Ms. Reyes. People's answers change when the number changes.
SAM: And when the boiler dies in January.
WHITLOCK: That was not intentional.
SAM: Brian Kowalski said, on the record, that Harborline refused to replace it. "Let the tenants buy the boiler too." Those were the words.
WHITLOCK: ...Brian talks too much.
MARISOL: Brian told the truth. That's different.
MS. HARGROVE: There's one more detail. The relocation offers are individual. Each household negotiates separately. Each offer expires fourteen days after it's made. And each requires a confidentiality agreement.
MARISOL: So what you're saying is: the offers are secret, they expire quickly, and every family decides alone.
MS. HARGROVE: That's standard practice.
MARISOL: With respect, Ms. Hargrove, that's not an offer. That's a deadline. With a gag order.
WHITLOCK: Ms. Reyes, let me be honest. My partners prefer Path A. A clean sale. No headlines. No more boilers. No more reporters.
PRIYA: Then why make it impossible? Three point five is four hundred thousand more than we can do.
WHITLOCK: Because three point five is what we need. I'm not trying to win. I'm trying not to lose.
SAM: Mr. Whitlock, every month you wait, you lose twenty-one thousand in interest. Plus the emergency repair bill. Plus the violation. Three point five by April is not a real number. It's a wish.
WHITLOCK: Then make me a real one. In writing. By next Tuesday.
MARISOL: Thank you. We'll respond in writing. By next Tuesday. Is there anything else?
MS. HARGROVE: Only this. Path B is contingent on Path A failing. If you show us financing for three point five, Path B disappears.
MARISOL: Understood. Thank you for coming. Tommy, can you get the door?
MS. HARGROVE: And Mr. Russo? The coffee was... memorable.
SAM: He'll take that as a compliment. Everybody does, eventually.

[Scene: 7:30 p.m. The Hook, back table. Marisol, Priya, Sam, Eddie, and Tommy sit with copies of a one-page summary Mrs. Esposito typed during the meeting.]

MARISOL: Okay. Before we talk strategy, I want to do something. I want everybody to be honest. About the money. Path B. How it feels.
TOMMY: Honest? Okay. Sixty thousand. For the bar. Two months ago, I said no to thirty. Now it's double.
EDDIE: Is it tempting?
TOMMY: Everything is tempting at fifty-eight, Eddie. My back hurts. The ice machine is dying. Sixty thousand is a lot of ice machines.
MARISOL: Priya? Sam?
PRIYA: Can I be honest? It's scary how tempting it is. Thirty-five thousand. That's our whole wedding. The big one. With all the cousins. And a down payment in Jersey City.
SAM: I ran the numbers. I'm sorry. I can't help it. If we take thirty-five, and buy a condo in Jersey City, our monthly cost is higher. But we own. Really own. Not a limited share.
MARISOL: That's true. A limited-equity share won't make you rich.
SAM: And I keep thinking about kids. Priya wants two. A two-bedroom in Red Hook costs five thousand a month on the market.
PRIYA: Sam. Stop. Don't do the kid math. Not tonight.
SAM: Sorry. It's what I do when I'm scared. I do math.
EDDIE: Marisol? What about you? Fifty thousand.
MARISOL: Honestly? I thought about Diego. College. Fifty thousand is four years of a state school. Almost. I've never had fifty thousand dollars in my life. Not even close.
TOMMY: So everybody's tempted. Even you.
MARISOL: Even me. That's why I asked. I don't want anybody to feel ashamed. They want us to feel tempted alone. Secretly. With a fourteen-day clock.
EDDIE: And a confidentiality agreement. So you can't even tell your neighbor.
MARISOL: Exactly. That's the strategy. Not the money. The silence. If we each sit alone with our number, we'll each start doing math. Like Sam.
SAM: Hey. That's fair. But hey.
PRIYA: So what do we do?
MARISOL: We do what we did tonight. We say the numbers out loud. Together. Then we decide together. Rule number four.
TOMMY: Never sign alone.
EDDIE: Never sign alone. I wrote it on my fridge in September. In big letters. Next to Peggy's picture.
PRIYA: Okay. Then here's my real answer. Thirty-five thousand would pay for a wedding. But I want to get married on the pier. Here. With all of you there. Not in Jersey City with my mother's hundred and forty cousins.
SAM: And I'm the treasurer. If I take the money, I'm the treasurer who quit. I don't want to be that guy.
TOMMY: And me. If I take sixty, Angela haunts me. She will. Every night. With a frying pan.
MARISOL: So what do we offer next Tuesday?
SAM: We can't do three point five. But maybe we can move. Three point two. Maybe three point two five. If the city gives us more. If the grants come through.
MARISOL: And we ask for a credit. For the boiler. Sixty thousand. Harborline let it die. The buyer shouldn't pay for that.
SAM: Three point two five, minus sixty for the boiler. That's three point one nine net. Closer to their number. Not too far from ours.
TOMMY: Will they take it?
MARISOL: I don't know. But it's a real number. Whitlock asked for a real number. We'll give him one.
PRIYA: And if they don't take it?
MARISOL: Then we sit down again. And again. Until March first. They have twenty-one thousand reasons a month to keep talking to us.

[Scene: 10:15 p.m. The landing between the second and third floors. Eddie sits on his step. Mrs. Esposito comes up with two mugs of tea.]

MRS. ESPOSITO: You didn't say much tonight. At the meeting. I heard everybody through the floor. Except you.
EDDIE: I said a little. I asked questions. Old men ask questions so they don't have to answer them.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Then I'll ask you one. Seventy-five thousand. Is it tempting?
EDDIE: Michael called. At six. Somebody sent him the article. About the boiler. And about the offers.
MRS. ESPOSITO: How does he know about the offers?
EDDIE: I told him. Last week. I tell him everything now. No secrets. Gina's rule.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And what did he say?
EDDIE: He said, "Dad, take the seventy-five. Come to Florida. Linda's mother has a room. It has a ramp. No stairs. You'll be warm."
MRS. ESPOSITO: No stairs. Warm. That's not nothing, Eddie.
EDDIE: It's not nothing. In January, at fifty-one degrees, in my chair, I thought about Florida. I'll be honest. I thought, "Maybe the boy is right."
MRS. ESPOSITO: And now?
EDDIE: Now I think about Peggy. About Jimmy. About the hook over my door. About a boy who wants to skate backward. About two knocks at eight o'clock.
MRS. ESPOSITO: The knocks are mine too, you know. If you go to Florida, who do I knock for?
EDDIE: You'd find somebody. Sam, maybe. He'd knock back with a spreadsheet.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Eddie. I'm serious.
EDDIE: I know. I'm serious too. I'm not taking it, Dolores. I told Michael tonight. He was angry. Then he was quiet. Then he said, "Okay, Dad. But if it gets cold again, you call me first."
MRS. ESPOSITO: That's a good son.
EDDIE: He's a good son. He just lives too far away. And he doesn't understand that this staircase is the whole world.
MRS. ESPOSITO: It's a very small world. Forty-two steps.
EDDIE: Forty-two steps. I've counted them for fifty-two years. Some days they're the longest walk in Brooklyn. But they're mine.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Ours.
EDDIE: Ours. Okay. Help me up. Eight o'clock. Two knocks.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Two knocks. And Eddie? Thank you. For staying.
EDDIE: Don't thank me yet. We haven't won anything.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Can I tell you something? They offered me sixty-five thousand today. I thought about it too. For one minute.
EDDIE: You? Dolores Esposito? Tempted?
MRS. ESPOSITO: My sister lives in Staten Island. She has a garden. I thought, "Tomatoes. A garden. No Harborline."
EDDIE: And then?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Then I thought, "Who will write the minutes?" And the minute was over.
EDDIE: Sixty seconds of temptation. That's very disciplined.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I timed it. With the egg timer.
EDDIE: Of course you did.
MRS. ESPOSITO: We've won tonight. Nobody signed alone. That's a win.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **협상 화법**
  - *Let me make sure I understand.* — "제가 제대로 이해했는지 확인할게요." 상대의 말을 다시 정리해 함정을 드러낸다.
  - *So what you're saying is…* — "그러니까 말씀하시는 건…" 요약하며 압박한다.
  - *That's contingent on…* — "그건 ~을 조건으로 한다."
  - *We'll respond in writing.* — "서면으로 답하겠습니다." 즉답을 피하는 정석.
  - *Then make me a real one.* — "그럼 진짜 숫자를 줘 보세요."
- **반박**
  - *With respect, that's not an offer. That's a deadline. With a gag order.* — "외람되지만, 그건 제안이 아니라 시한이에요. 입막음까지 붙은."
  - *It's not a real number. It's a wish.* — 숫자가 아니라 바람일 뿐이라는 반박.
- **조건 표현**
  - *If you show us financing for three point five, Path B disappears.* — 조건과 결과.
  - *Path B begins on May first.* / *Each offer expires fourteen days after it's made.*
- **어휘**
  - *cash for keys* 열쇠 값(나가는 대가로 주는 돈) / *relocation package* 이주 지원금(완곡한 표현)
  - *vacate* 비우다 / *surrender the lease* 임대 계약을 조기 반납하다 / *confidentiality agreement* 비밀 유지 계약
  - *gag order* 함구령 / *standard practice* 업계 관행 / *net* 공제 후 실질 금액 / *credit* 매매가에서 빼 주는 금액
- **The coffee was... memorable.** — "커피가… 기억에 남네요." 정중한 혹평.
- **Old men ask questions so they don't have to answer them.** — 에디의 자기 고백.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 하그로브 변호사는 감정 없이 매끄럽게. 숫자는 또박또박: *seventy-five thousand*, *sixty-five thousand*.
- 마리솔의 *So what you're saying is:* 다음에 세 가지를 나열하며 점점 무게를 싣는다: *the offers are secret, | they expire quickly, | and every family decides alone.*
- *That's not an offer. | That's a deadline. | With a gag order.* — 세 마디를 끊어 단단하게.
- 저녁 회의는 고백의 톤. 각자 "솔직히"로 시작할 때 목소리를 조금 낮춘다.
- 계단 장면은 속삭임. *Forty-two steps.*는 숫자를 세듯 천천히.
- 마지막 부인의 *Nobody signed alone. | That's a win.* — 조용한 승리감.
