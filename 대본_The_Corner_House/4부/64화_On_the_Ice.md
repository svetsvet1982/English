# THE CORNER HOUSE — 4부 64화

## On the Ice (얼음 위에서)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 4부 10화(통산 64화) · 시기: 2027년 1월 23일(토) 오전~오후 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: F선 열차 안 → 프로스펙트 파크 레이크사이드 스케이트장 → 호숫가 벤치
> - 등장인물: EDDIE, DIEGO, AISHA
> - 사건: 에디가 디에고를 데리고 프로스펙트 파크 스케이트장에 간다. 에디는 예전처럼 직접 타지 못한다. 대신 난간 옆에서 무릎 굽히는 법, 몸을 앞으로 기울이는 법, 넘어지는 법을 가르친다. 41화에서 학교를 안내했던 7학년 아이샤가 우연히 나타나 디에고를 도와준다. 에디는 1952년 아홉 살 때 아버지와 얼어붙은 호수 위를 타던 기억, 1970년 2월 같은 호수에서 뒤로 타던 페기를 처음 만난 기억을 들려준다. 마지막에 에디는 스케이트 보조기를 잡고 디에고와 한 바퀴를 아주 천천히 돈다.
> - 학습 포인트: 서정적 서술, 신체 어휘(ankles, knees, balance, lean, glide, wobble, shin, hip), 과거 습관(used to / would), 감각 묘사(the ice sang / my breath hung in the air)
> - 미국 현안: 겨울 레저와 도시 공원, 노년의 몸과 이동, 세대를 잇는 기억, 예전엔 얼던 호수가 이제는 거의 얼지 않는 겨울
> - 연결: 6화 에디 1943년생. 26화·50화 페기. 41화 아이샤와 고와너스 로봇 학교(디에고 1지망, 결과는 봄). 43화 에디 왼무릎 관절염. 53화 '다리가 일하게 해'. 62화 '여든다섯 전에'.

## 대본

[Scene: Saturday, 10:15 a.m. The F train, rattling toward Fifteenth Street–Prospect Park. Diego holds a pair of rented-skate vouchers. Eddie holds his cane between his knees.]

DIEGO: Eddie, why did you want to go skating? You can't even skate. Your knee.
EDDIE: I can't skate anymore. That's different from "can't skate." I used to skate. Very well.
DIEGO: When?
EDDIE: When I was your age. Younger, even. My father used to take me. Every January. If the lake froze.
DIEGO: Which lake?
EDDIE: The lake in Prospect Park. Where we're going. The real lake. Not the rink. In those days, it froze solid. Thick enough for a thousand people.
DIEGO: People skated on the lake? On the actual water?
EDDIE: On the actual water. Frozen. The city would test the ice. If it was four inches, they'd raise a red ball on a pole. Red ball meant "Go."
DIEGO: A red ball! Like a traffic light for ice!
EDDIE: Exactly like that. And when the ball went up, every kid in Brooklyn ran. We'd run so fast, our skates would bang on our backs.
DIEGO: Does the lake still freeze?
EDDIE: Almost never. Not like that. The winters are warmer now. Kids today skate on a rink. With a machine that makes the ice.
DIEGO: That's kind of sad.
EDDIE: It is. Hey. What did your mother say this morning? At the door?
DIEGO: She said, "Eddie is not allowed to do anything stupid."
EDDIE: Your mother is very wise. I promise. Nothing stupid. Only a little stupid.
DIEGO: Eddie, the middle school letters come in the spring. Do you think I'll get the robot school?
EDDIE: A boy who measures snow for fun? I think you have a good chance.
DIEGO: Malik says it's like a lottery. There's an algorithm.
EDDIE: Everything's an algorithm now. In my day, it was a nun with a list.
DIEGO: Was the nun nice?
EDDIE: Sister Agnes? She was terrifying. But fair. Like Dolores.
DIEGO: Ha! Don't tell her that.
EDDIE: Anyway. The rink is good too. You'll see. Okay. Next stop. Help me up. Slowly. My knees are older than this train.

[Scene: 11:00 a.m. The rink at Lakeside. Music plays from speakers. Families, couples, a teenager doing spins. Diego, in rented skates, grips the wall. Eddie stands on the other side of the wall, leaning on his cane.]

DIEGO: Eddie! I can't! My ankles are folding! Like spaghetti!
EDDIE: That's normal. Everybody's ankles fold the first time. Now listen to me. Bend your knees.
DIEGO: They're bent!
EDDIE: More. Like you're sitting on a small chair. An invisible chair. A chair for a mouse.
DIEGO: A mouse chair. Okay. Like this?
EDDIE: Better. Now lean forward. A little. Your nose should be over your toes. Not behind your heels.
DIEGO: If I lean forward, I'll fall forward!
EDDIE: If you lean back, you'll fall back. And falling back hurts more. Trust me. I have the X-rays.
DIEGO: Okay. Nose over toes. Nose over toes. I'm wobbling!
EDDIE: Wobbling is fine. Wobbling means you're learning. Now push. Not with your toe. With the side of the blade. Like a duck. Push and glide.
DIEGO: Push... and glide! I moved! Eddie! I moved two feet!
EDDIE: Two feet! That's how everybody starts. Two feet. Then ten. Then the whole rink.
DIEGO: Whoa, whoa, whoa! I'm falling!
EDDIE: Fall to the side! Not backward! Put your hands down! Good!
DIEGO: Ow. Ow. My hip. And my pride.
EDDIE: Your pride will heal faster than your hip. Get up. Turn onto your knees first. Then one foot. Then the other.
AISHA: Hey, do you need help? Wait. I know you. You came to our school! In October! The robot kid!
DIEGO: Aisha! The tour guide! What are you doing here?
AISHA: I skate every Saturday. My mom works here. At the snack bar. Here, take my hand. I'll pull you up.
DIEGO: Thanks. This is Eddie. He's my... he's my neighbor. And my coach. He can't skate. His knee.
EDDIE: Pleased to meet you, Aisha. You skate beautifully. I saw your spin.
AISHA: Thanks! It took me two years. And a lot of falling. Did you skate when you were young?
EDDIE: I did. On the lake. Right over there. Behind those trees.
AISHA: On the lake? It never freezes. Not for real.
EDDIE: It did in nineteen fifty-two. And in nineteen seventy. Let me tell you both something. If you don't mind an old man talking.
AISHA: I don't mind. My grandpa talks all the time. I like it.
EDDIE: Nineteen fifty-two. January. I was nine. My father woke me up at six in the morning. He said, "Edward. The red ball is up."
DIEGO: The red ball!
EDDIE: We walked from Red Hook. Two hours. In the dark. My father carried my skates on his shoulder. Tied together by the laces.
EDDIE: When we got to the lake, the sun was coming up. Pink and orange. And the whole lake was white. Flat. Silent. Like a page nobody had written on.
AISHA: That sounds beautiful.
EDDIE: Then the first skater went out. And the ice sang. You've never heard it. When the ice is very thick and very cold, it makes a sound. Like a whale. Far away. Under your feet.
DIEGO: The ice sang? Like music?
EDDIE: Like music from somewhere deep. My father held my hand. We skated to the middle. My breath hung in the air like a little cloud. I thought, "This is the best moment of my life."
AISHA: Was it?
EDDIE: It was. Until nineteen seventy.
DIEGO: What happened in nineteen seventy?
EDDIE: February. The lake froze again. I was twenty-six. Home from the war. Not sleeping. Not happy. Jimmy Doyle said, "Eddie, go skate. Clear your head."
EDDIE: So I went. Alone. And in the middle of the lake, there was a girl. Skating backward. In a red coat. Fast. Laughing. Backward.
DIEGO: Peggy?
EDDIE: Peggy. She skated right into me. Backward. Boom. We both fell down. On the ice. She looked at me and said, "You were in my way."
AISHA: Ha! She blamed you?
EDDIE: She blamed me. For fifty years, she blamed me. Every anniversary. "Eddie, you were in my way."
DIEGO: That's the best story ever.
EDDIE: It's the only story I have. Everything else came after it. Michael. This neighborhood. Fifty-two years in 3A. All of it started with a girl skating backward.
AISHA: My mom says the best things happen when you fall down.
EDDIE: Your mom is a wise woman. Tell her an old man agrees.

[Scene: 12:30 p.m. The rink. The music changes to something slow. Eddie stands at the rink gate with a skate aid, a metal frame on blades. A rink worker has fitted him with skates. Diego and Aisha wait on the ice.]

DIEGO: Eddie, are you sure? Dr. Levine said no shoveling. She didn't say anything about skating.
EDDIE: Exactly. She didn't say anything. So it's legal. One lap. Very slow. With this walker on blades. And with you two.
AISHA: I'll stay on your left. Diego, take his right side. If he wobbles, we hold.
EDDIE: I won't wobble. I'll glide. Like nineteen fifty-two. Only slower. Like nineteen fifty-two in slow motion.
DIEGO: Okay. Ready? One, two, three. Push.
EDDIE: Push... and glide. Oh. Oh, there it is.
DIEGO: There what is?
EDDIE: The feeling. In my legs. The ice under the blade. My body remembers. My knee is complaining. But my legs remember.
AISHA: You're doing great, Mr. Eddie! Bend your knees! Nose over toes!
EDDIE: She's using my own words against me. Smart girl.
DIEGO: Eddie, we're halfway! Look! We passed the snack bar!
EDDIE: Halfway. Slow down, Diego. I want to remember this part.
DIEGO: Why this part?
EDDIE: Because the sun is on the ice. And a boy is holding my arm. And I'm eighty-three. And I'm skating. That's a good part to remember.
AISHA: Hey, look! My mom is waving! From the snack bar! Mom! This is Eddie! He met his wife on the lake!
EDDIE: She doesn't need to know everything, Aisha.
AISHA: Too late. She's crying. She cries at everything.
DIEGO: Almost there. Ten more feet. Five. Three. We made it! The whole lap!
EDDIE: The whole lap. Fifty years after my last one. Not bad. Not bad at all.
AISHA: That was so cool! Can I post a video? With your permission?
EDDIE: A video? Of me? On the internet? Michael will see it in Florida.
AISHA: Is that bad?
EDDIE: He'll call me. He'll yell. "Dad! You'll break a hip!" And then he'll show it to everybody at work. Post it.
DIEGO: Eddie, you're smiling. Like, really smiling. Not the normal smile. A big one.
EDDIE: Am I? I didn't notice. It happens on the ice.

[Scene: 1:30 p.m. A bench by the lake. The water is gray and open, not frozen. Eddie and Diego drink hot chocolate from paper cups. Aisha has gone back to her mother.]

DIEGO: The lake isn't frozen at all.
EDDIE: No. Not even a little. In my day, by late January, you'd see a thousand people out there.
DIEGO: Will it ever freeze again? Like in nineteen fifty-two?
EDDIE: Maybe. Some winter. A very cold one. But I won't be here for it.
DIEGO: Don't say that.
EDDIE: It's true, kid. I'm not sad about it. I'm telling you because I want you to do something for me.
DIEGO: What?
EDDIE: If the red ball ever goes up again. If the lake ever freezes. You come here. Early. Six in the morning. And you skate to the middle.
DIEGO: And listen for the ice singing?
EDDIE: And listen for the ice singing. And if a girl skates into you backward, you say, "Sorry. I was in your way."
DIEGO: Eddie! Gross!
EDDIE: Ha! Okay, okay. Or a boy. Or nobody. Just you and the ice. That's enough too.
DIEGO: Okay. I promise. Six in the morning. The middle of the lake.
EDDIE: Good. And one more thing. Bring your kids someday. Tell them about the old man with the cane.
DIEGO: I'll tell them you skated a whole lap. At eighty-three.
EDDIE: Tell them I skated it fast. Lie a little. It's a story.
DIEGO: Ms. Park says stories have to be true.
EDDIE: Ms. Park is right about school. Stories at a bar are different.
DIEGO: Eddie, did you ever skate with Peggy again? After the first time?
EDDIE: Every winter the lake froze. Which wasn't often. The last time was nineteen seventy-seven. Michael was five. He rode on my shoulders. Peggy skated backward around us.
DIEGO: Backward! Still!
EDDIE: Still backward. She said forward was boring.
DIEGO: Then when I come back, I'll learn to skate backward. For Peggy.
EDDIE: That's a deal. Now help me up. My knee says we're done. My legs say we're not. My knee wins. It always wins.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **과거 습관: used to / would**
  - *I used to skate. Very well.* — 예전엔 탔다(지금은 아니다).
  - *My father used to take me. Every January.* — 반복된 과거의 습관.
  - *The city would test the ice. If it was four inches, they'd raise a red ball.* — would는 이야기하듯 과거의 반복을 묘사한다.
  - *We'd run so fast, our skates would bang on our backs.*
  - ※ *used to*는 상태에도 쓰지만(*I used to be thin.*), *would*는 동작에만 쓴다.
- **신체·스케이트 어휘**
  - *ankles* 발목 / *knees* 무릎 / *hip* 엉덩이·고관절 / *heels* 발뒤꿈치 / *toes* 발가락
  - *bend your knees* 무릎을 굽혀 / *lean forward* 앞으로 기울여 / *push and glide* 밀고 미끄러지기
  - *wobble* 휘청거리다 / *fall to the side* 옆으로 넘어지다 / *blade* 스케이트 날
- **감각 묘사**
  - *The ice sang.* — 얼음이 노래했다(두꺼운 얼음이 내는 낮은 울림).
  - *My breath hung in the air like a little cloud.* — 입김이 작은 구름처럼 공중에 걸렸다.
  - *Like a page nobody had written on.* — 아무도 쓰지 않은 종이처럼.
- **Nose over toes.** — "코는 발끝 위로." 스케이트 기본자세를 외우는 말.
- **Your pride will heal faster than your hip.** — "자존심은 엉덩이보다 빨리 낫는다."
- **You were in my way.** — "네가 내 길을 막았잖아." 페기의 첫마디이자 50년 농담.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 이 화는 서정적 낭독이 중심이다. 에디의 회상은 아주 느리게, 문장마다 숨을 쉬듯 쉰다.
- *Pink and orange. | And the whole lake was white. | Flat. | Silent.* — 단어 하나하나를 그림처럼 놓는다.
- *And the ice sang.* — 앞에서 길게 멈추고, 속삭이듯.
- 스케이트 지도 장면은 짧고 리듬 있게: *Push... and glide.* — *push*에 힘, *glide*는 길게 늘인다.
- 디에고의 비명(*Whoa, whoa, whoa!*)은 숨 가쁘게, 점점 높게.
- 마지막 장면은 다시 느리게. *My knee wins. | It always wins.* — 미소 섞인 체념.

## 한글 해석

디에고: 에디, 왜 스케이트 타러 가고 싶었어요? 타지도 못하시잖아요. 무릎 때문에.
에디: 이제는 못 타는 거지. 그건 '탈 줄 모른다'랑 달라. 예전엔 탔어. 아주 잘.
디에고: 언제요?
에디: 네 나이 때. 더 어릴 때도. 아버지가 데려가곤 하셨어. 1월마다. 호수가 얼면.
디에고: 무슨 호수요?
에디: 프로스펙트 파크에 있는 호수. 우리가 가는 데. 진짜 호수. 링크 말고. 그 시절엔 꽁꽁 얼었어. 천 명이 올라가도 될 만큼 두껍게.
디에고: 사람들이 호수에서 탔어요? 진짜 물 위에서요?
에디: 진짜 물 위에서. 얼어붙은. 시에서 얼음을 검사하곤 했어. 4인치가 되면 기둥에 빨간 공을 올렸지. 빨간 공은 "가도 좋다"는 뜻이었어.
디에고: 빨간 공! 얼음용 신호등 같네요!
에디: 딱 그거야. 그리고 공이 올라가면 브루클린의 모든 아이가 뛰었어. 얼마나 빨리 뛰었는지 스케이트가 등에 쾅쾅 부딪혔지.
디에고: 호수가 아직도 얼어요?
에디: 거의 안 얼어. 그때처럼은. 요즘 겨울은 더 따뜻해. 요즘 애들은 링크에서 타. 얼음을 만드는 기계가 있는 데서.
디에고: 좀 슬프네요.
에디: 그렇지. 참. 오늘 아침에 엄마가 뭐라고 하셨어? 문 앞에서?
디에고: "에디는 바보 같은 짓 하면 안 돼"라고 했어요.
에디: 너희 엄마 참 현명하시다. 약속할게. 바보 같은 짓은 안 해. 아주 조금만 해.
디에고: 에디, 중학교 결과 편지가 봄에 와요. 제가 로봇 학교 붙을 것 같아요?
에디: 재미로 눈 깊이를 재는 녀석이? 가능성 꽤 있다고 봐.
디에고: 말릭이 그러는데 복권 같대요. 알고리즘이 있대요.
에디: 요즘은 다 알고리즘이지. 내 때는 명단 든 수녀님이었어.
디에고: 그 수녀님 착했어요?
에디: 아그네스 수녀님? 무시무시했지. 그래도 공정했어. 돌로레스처럼.
디에고: 하하! 할머니한텐 말하지 마요.
에디: 아무튼. 링크도 좋아. 보면 알 거야. 자. 다음 역이다. 일으켜 줘. 천천히. 내 무릎은 이 열차보다 늙었어.
디에고: 에디! 못 하겠어요! 발목이 접혀요! 스파게티처럼요!
에디: 그건 정상이야. 처음엔 다들 발목이 접혀. 자, 잘 들어. 무릎을 굽혀.
디에고: 굽혔어요!
에디: 더. 작은 의자에 앉는 것처럼. 보이지 않는 의자. 쥐가 앉는 의자.
디에고: 쥐 의자. 좋아요. 이렇게요?
에디: 낫네. 이제 앞으로 기울여. 조금. 코가 발끝 위에 있어야 해. 발뒤꿈치 뒤가 아니라.
디에고: 앞으로 기울이면 앞으로 넘어질 거예요!
에디: 뒤로 기울이면 뒤로 넘어져. 그리고 뒤로 넘어지는 게 더 아파. 날 믿어. 엑스레이가 있거든.
디에고: 좋아요. 코는 발끝 위로. 코는 발끝 위로. 휘청거려요!
에디: 휘청거리는 건 괜찮아. 휘청거린다는 건 배우고 있다는 뜻이야. 이제 밀어. 발끝으로 말고. 날 옆면으로. 오리처럼. 밀고, 미끄러지고.
디에고: 밀고… 미끄러지고! 움직였어요! 에디! 2피트 움직였어요!
에디: 2피트! 다들 그렇게 시작해. 2피트. 그다음엔 10피트. 그다음엔 링크 한 바퀴.
디에고: 어어어! 넘어져요!
에디: 옆으로 넘어져! 뒤로 말고! 손 짚어! 잘했어!
디에고: 아야. 아야. 엉덩이. 그리고 자존심.
에디: 자존심은 엉덩이보다 빨리 나아. 일어나. 먼저 무릎으로 돌아. 그다음 한 발. 그다음 다른 발.
아이샤: 저기, 도와줄까? 잠깐. 너 알아. 우리 학교 왔었잖아! 10월에! 로봇 좋아하는 애!
디에고: 아이샤! 안내해 준 누나! 여기서 뭐 해?
아이샤: 토요일마다 타. 우리 엄마가 여기서 일해. 매점에서. 자, 내 손 잡아. 일으켜 줄게.
디에고: 고마워. 이분은 에디야. 내… 우리 이웃이야. 그리고 내 코치야. 스케이트는 못 타셔. 무릎 때문에.
에디: 만나서 반갑다, 아이샤. 정말 잘 타는구나. 네 스핀 봤어.
아이샤: 고맙습니다! 2년 걸렸어요. 엄청 넘어지면서요. 젊으셨을 때 스케이트 타셨어요?

에디: 그랬지. 1970년까지는.
