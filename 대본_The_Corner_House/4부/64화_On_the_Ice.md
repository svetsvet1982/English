# THE CORNER HOUSE — 4부 64화

## On the Ice (얼음 위에서)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 4부 10화(통산 64화) · 시기: 2027년 1월 23일(토) 오전~오후 · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: F선 열차 안 → 프로스펙트 파크 레이크사이드 스케이트장 → 호숫가 벤치
> - 등장인물: EDDIE, DIEGO, AISHA
> - 사건: 에디가 디에고를 데리고 프로스펙트 파크 스케이트장에 간다. 에디는 예전처럼 직접 타지 못한다. 대신 난간 옆에서 무릎 굽히는 법, 몸을 앞으로 기울이는 법, 넘어지는 법을 가르친다. 41화에서 학교를 안내했던 7학년 아이샤가 우연히 나타나 디에고를 도와준다. 에디는 1952년 아홉 살 때 아버지와 얼어붙은 호수 위를 타던 기억, 1970년 2월 같은 호수에서 뒤로 타던 페기를 처음 만난 기억을 들려준다. 마지막에 에디는 스케이트 보조기를 잡고 디에고와 한 바퀴를 아주 천천히 돈다.
> - 학습 포인트: 서정적 서술, 신체 어휘(ankles, knees, balance, lean, glide, wobble, shin, hip), 과거 습관(used to / would), 감각 묘사(the ice sang / my breath hung in the air)
> - 미국 현안: 겨울 레저와 도시 공원, 노년의 몸과 이동, 세대를 잇는 기억, 예전엔 얼던 호수가 이제는 거의 얼지 않는 겨울
> - 연결: 6화 에디 1943년생. 26화·50화 페기. 41화 아이샤와 고와너스 로봇 학교(디에고 1지망, 결과는 봄). 43화 에디 왼무릎 관절염. 53화 '다리가 일하게 해'. 62화 '여든다섯 전에'.

## 대본

[Scene: Saturday, 10:15 a.m. The F train, rattling toward Fifteenth Street–Prospect Park. Diego holds a pair of rented-skate vouchers. Eddie holds his cane between his knees.]

DIEGO: Eddie, why did you want to go skating today? You can't even skate anymore, because of your knee.
EDDIE: I can't skate anymore, and that's different from "can't skate." I used to skate, and I was very good at it, believe it or not.
DIEGO: Really? When was that?
EDDIE: When I was your age, or even younger. My father used to take me every January, but only if the lake froze.
DIEGO: Which lake are you talking about?
EDDIE: The lake in Prospect Park, right where we're going. The real lake, not the rink. In those days it froze solid, thick enough to hold a thousand people.
DIEGO: People skated on the lake? On the actual water?
EDDIE: On the actual water, frozen hard. The city would test the ice, and if it was four inches thick, they'd raise a red ball on a pole. A red ball meant "Go."
DIEGO: A red ball! That's like a traffic light, but for ice!
EDDIE: Exactly like that. And when the ball went up, every kid in Brooklyn ran. We'd run so fast that our skates would bang on our backs.
DIEGO: Does the lake still freeze like that, or not anymore?
EDDIE: Almost never, and not like that, because the winters are warmer now. Kids today skate on a rink, with a machine that makes the ice for them.
DIEGO: Honestly, that's kind of sad when you think about it.
EDDIE: It is. Hey, what did your mother say to me this morning, at the door, when she handed you over?
DIEGO: She said, "Eddie is not allowed to do anything stupid." She said it twice, actually.
EDDIE: Your mother is a very wise woman. I promise, nothing stupid, only a little stupid, and only if it's absolutely necessary.
DIEGO: Eddie, the middle school letters come in the spring. Do you think I'll actually get into the robot school?
EDDIE: A boy who measures snow for fun? If anybody has a good chance, I'd say it's you.
DIEGO: Malik says it's basically a lottery, because there's an algorithm that decides everything.
EDDIE: Everything's an algorithm these days. In my day, it was a nun with a list, and if she didn't like your face, good luck.
DIEGO: Was the nun nice, at least?
EDDIE: Sister Agnes? She was terrifying, but she was fair, which is more than you can say for most people. A lot like Dolores, actually.
DIEGO: Ha! Please don't ever tell her that, or she'll be mad for a week.
EDDIE: Anyway, the rink is good too, and you'll see. Okay, this is our stop next, so help me up, slowly. My knees are older than this train.

[Scene: 11:00 a.m. The rink at Lakeside. Music plays from speakers. Families, couples, a teenager doing spins. Diego, in rented skates, grips the wall. Eddie stands on the other side of the wall, leaning on his cane.]

DIEGO: Eddie! I can't do this! My ankles are folding, like wet spaghetti!
EDDIE: That's normal, so don't panic. Everybody's ankles fold the first time. Now listen to me carefully, and bend your knees.
DIEGO: They're already bent, I promise!
EDDIE: Bend them more, like you're sitting on a small chair. An invisible chair, a chair so small that only a mouse could use it.
DIEGO: A mouse chair. Okay, I can do that. Like this?
EDDIE: Better. Now lean forward, just a little, so that your nose is over your toes and not behind your heels.
DIEGO: But if I lean forward, I'm going to fall forward!
EDDIE: If you lean back, you'll fall back, and falling back hurts a lot more. Trust me on this one, because I have the X-rays to prove it.
DIEGO: Okay, nose over toes, nose over toes. Eddie, I'm wobbling!
EDDIE: Wobbling is fine, because wobbling means you're learning. Now push, not with your toe but with the side of the blade, like a duck. Push and glide.
DIEGO: Push... and glide! I moved! Eddie, look, I actually moved two feet!
EDDIE: Two feet! That's how everybody starts. First it's two feet, then it's ten, and before you know it, it's the whole rink.
DIEGO: Whoa, whoa, whoa! I'm falling, I'm falling!
EDDIE: Fall to the side, not backward! Put your hands down! Good, that's it!
DIEGO: Ow. Ow. That hurt my hip, and it hurt my pride even more.
EDDIE: Your pride will heal faster than your hip. Now get up the right way: turn onto your knees first, then put one foot down, then the other.
AISHA: Hey, do you need some help? Wait, I know you! You came to our school back in October. You're the robot kid!
DIEGO: Aisha! The tour guide! What are you doing all the way out here?
AISHA: I skate here every Saturday, because my mom works at the snack bar. Here, take my hand, and I'll pull you up.
DIEGO: Thanks. This is Eddie. He's my... he's my neighbor, and also my coach. He can't skate, though, because of his knee.
EDDIE: Pleased to meet you, Aisha. You skate beautifully, and I saw that spin of yours from over here.
AISHA: Thanks! It took me two years and a lot of falling to learn it. Did you skate when you were young?
EDDIE: I did, on the lake, right over there behind those trees.
AISHA: On the lake? But it never freezes, at least not for real.
EDDIE: It did in nineteen fifty-two, and again in nineteen seventy. Let me tell you both something, if you don't mind an old man talking for a while.
AISHA: I don't mind at all. My grandpa talks all the time, and honestly, I like it.
EDDIE: Nineteen fifty-two, in January, when I was nine. My father woke me up at six in the morning and said, "Edward, the red ball is up."
DIEGO: The red ball!
EDDIE: We walked all the way from Red Hook, two hours in the dark. My father carried my skates on his shoulder, tied together by the laces.
EDDIE: When we got to the lake, the sun was coming up, pink and orange. And the whole lake was white, flat and silent, like a page nobody had written on yet.
AISHA: That sounds really beautiful.
EDDIE: Then the first skater went out, and the ice sang. You've never heard it, but when the ice is very thick and very cold, it makes a sound, like a whale far away under your feet.
DIEGO: The ice sang? You mean like actual music?
EDDIE: Like music from somewhere deep. My father held my hand, and we skated all the way to the middle. My breath hung in the air like a little cloud, and I thought, "This is the best moment of my life."
AISHA: And was it?
EDDIE: It was, until nineteen seventy.
DIEGO: Why? What happened in nineteen seventy?
EDDIE: In February, the lake froze again. I was twenty-six, home from the war, not sleeping and not happy. Jimmy Doyle said, "Eddie, go skate and clear your head."
EDDIE: So I went, by myself. And in the middle of the lake, there was a girl in a red coat, skating backward, fast, and laughing the whole time.
DIEGO: Was that Peggy?
EDDIE: That was Peggy. She skated right into me, backward, boom, and we both fell down on the ice. She looked at me and said, "You were in my way."
AISHA: Ha! Wait, she blamed you for it?
EDDIE: She blamed me, and she kept blaming me for fifty years. Every anniversary, she'd say, "Eddie, you were in my way."
DIEGO: That's honestly the best story I've ever heard.
EDDIE: It's the only story I have, really, because everything else came after it: Michael, this neighborhood, fifty-two years in 3A. All of it started with a girl skating backward.
AISHA: My mom always says that the best things happen when you fall down.
EDDIE: Your mom is a wise woman. Tell her an old man agrees with her completely.

[Scene: 12:30 p.m. The rink. The music changes to something slow. Eddie stands at the rink gate with a skate aid, a metal frame on blades. A rink worker has fitted him with skates. Diego and Aisha wait on the ice.]

DIEGO: Eddie, are you sure about this? Dr. Levine said no shoveling, although she didn't say anything about skating.
EDDIE: Exactly, she didn't say anything, so technically it's legal. One lap, very slow, with this walker on blades, and with you two right next to me.
AISHA: I'll stay on your left, and Diego, you take his right side. If he wobbles, we hold on to him.
EDDIE: I won't wobble, I'll glide, just like nineteen fifty-two. Only slower, like nineteen fifty-two in slow motion.
DIEGO: Okay, are you ready? We go on three. One, two, three, push.
EDDIE: Push... and glide. Oh. Oh, there it is.
DIEGO: There what is? What are you talking about?
EDDIE: The feeling, in my legs, the ice under the blade. My body remembers it. My knee is complaining, sure, but my legs remember.
AISHA: You're doing great, Mr. Eddie! Bend your knees! Nose over toes!
EDDIE: She's using my own words against me. That's a smart girl you've got there, Diego.
DIEGO: Eddie, we're already halfway! Look, we just passed the snack bar!
EDDIE: Halfway. Slow down a little, Diego, because I want to remember this part.
DIEGO: Why this part, though?
EDDIE: Because the sun is on the ice, and a boy is holding my arm. Because I'm eighty-three years old, and I'm skating. That's a good part to remember.
AISHA: Hey, look, my mom is waving from the snack bar! Mom! This is Eddie, and he met his wife on the lake!
EDDIE: She doesn't need to know everything about me, Aisha.
AISHA: Too late, because she's already crying. She cries at everything, even commercials.
DIEGO: Almost there, ten more feet. Five. Three. We made it! We did the whole lap!
EDDIE: The whole lap, fifty years after my last one. Not bad. Not bad at all.
AISHA: That was so cool! Can I post a video of it? Only with your permission, of course.
EDDIE: A video? Of me? On the internet? Michael will see it all the way down in Florida.
AISHA: Is that a bad thing?
EDDIE: He'll call me, and he'll yell, "Dad! You're going to break a hip!" And then he'll show it to everybody at work. So go ahead and post it.
DIEGO: Eddie, you're smiling, like really smiling. Not your normal smile, but a big one.
EDDIE: Am I? I didn't even notice. It happens to me on the ice, I guess.

[Scene: 1:30 p.m. A bench by the lake. The water is gray and open, not frozen. Eddie and Diego drink hot chocolate from paper cups. Aisha has gone back to her mother.]

DIEGO: The lake isn't frozen at all, not even near the edges.
EDDIE: No, not even a little. In my day, by late January, you'd see a thousand people out there on the ice.
DIEGO: Do you think it'll ever freeze again, like in nineteen fifty-two?
EDDIE: Maybe, some winter, if it's a very cold one. But if it does, I won't be here for it.
DIEGO: Eddie, please don't say that.
EDDIE: It's true, kid, and I'm not sad about it. I'm telling you because I want you to do something for me.
DIEGO: Okay. What is it?
EDDIE: If the red ball ever goes up again, if the lake ever freezes, you come here early, at six in the morning. And you skate all the way to the middle.
DIEGO: And listen for the ice singing?
EDDIE: And listen for the ice singing. And if a girl skates into you backward, you say, "Sorry, I was in your way."
DIEGO: Eddie! Gross!
EDDIE: Ha! Okay, okay. Or a boy, or nobody at all. Just you and the ice, and that's enough too.
DIEGO: Okay, I promise. Six in the morning, in the middle of the lake.
EDDIE: Good. And one more thing: bring your kids here someday, and tell them about the old man with the cane.
DIEGO: I'll tell them you skated a whole lap when you were eighty-three.
EDDIE: Tell them I skated it fast. Lie a little, because it's a story, and stories are allowed to grow.
DIEGO: But Ms. Park says that stories have to be true.
EDDIE: Ms. Park is right about school. Stories at a bar are a different thing entirely.
DIEGO: Eddie, did you ever skate with Peggy again, after that first time?
EDDIE: Every winter the lake froze, which wasn't often. The last time was nineteen seventy-seven, when Michael was five. He rode on my shoulders while Peggy skated backward around us.
DIEGO: Backward! Even then!
EDDIE: Still backward, always. She said skating forward was boring.
DIEGO: Then when I come back, I'll learn to skate backward, for Peggy.
EDDIE: That's a deal. Now help me up, kid. My knee says we're done, and my legs say we're not. My knee wins, because it always wins.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **과거 습관: used to / would**
  - *I used to skate, and I was very good at it* — 예전엔 탔다(지금은 아니다).
  - *My father used to take me every January* — 반복된 과거의 습관.
  - *The city would test the ice, and if it was four inches thick, they'd raise a red ball on a pole.* — would는 이야기하듯 과거의 반복을 묘사한다.
  - *We'd run so fast that our skates would bang on our backs.*
  - ※ *used to*는 상태에도 쓰지만(예: I used to be thin.), *would*는 동작에만 쓴다.
- **신체·스케이트 어휘**
  - *ankles* 발목 / *knees* 무릎 / *hip* 엉덩이·고관절 / *heels* 발뒤꿈치 / *toes* 발가락
  - *bend your knees* 무릎을 굽혀 / *lean forward* 앞으로 기울여 / *push and glide* 밀고 미끄러지기
  - *wobble* 휘청거리다 / *fall to the side* 옆으로 넘어지다 / *blade* 스케이트 날
- **감각 묘사**
  - *The ice sang.* — 얼음이 노래했다(두꺼운 얼음이 내는 낮은 울림).
  - *My breath hung in the air like a little cloud* — 입김이 작은 구름처럼 공중에 걸렸다.
  - *like a page nobody had written on yet* — 아직 아무도 쓰지 않은 종이처럼.
- **Nose over toes.** — "코는 발끝 위로." 스케이트 기본자세를 외우는 말.
- **Your pride will heal faster than your hip.** — "자존심은 엉덩이보다 빨리 낫는다."
- **You were in my way.** — "네가 내 길을 막았잖아." 페기의 첫마디이자 50년 농담.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 이 화는 서정적 낭독이 중심이다. 에디의 회상은 아주 느리게, 문장마다 숨을 쉬듯 쉰다.
- *the sun was coming up, | pink and orange. | And the whole lake was white, | flat and silent* — 단어 하나하나를 그림처럼 놓는다.
- *And the ice sang.* — 앞에서 길게 멈추고, 속삭이듯.
- 스케이트 지도 장면은 짧고 리듬 있게: *Push... and glide.* — *push*에 힘, *glide*는 길게 늘인다.
- 디에고의 비명(*Whoa, whoa, whoa!*)은 숨 가쁘게, 점점 높게.
- 마지막 장면은 다시 느리게. *My knee wins, | because it always wins.* — 미소 섞인 체념.

## 한글 해석

디에고: 에디, 오늘 왜 스케이트 타러 가고 싶었어요? 이제 타지도 못하시잖아요, 무릎 때문에.
에디: 이제는 못 타는 거지. 그건 '탈 줄 모른다'랑 달라. 예전엔 탔어. 믿기 어렵겠지만 아주 잘 탔다고.
디에고: 정말요? 그게 언제였는데요?
에디: 네 나이 때, 아니 더 어릴 때도. 아버지가 1월마다 데려가곤 하셨어. 호수가 얼었을 때만.
디에고: 무슨 호수 말씀하시는 거예요?
에디: 프로스펙트 파크에 있는 호수, 바로 우리가 가는 데. 링크 말고 진짜 호수. 그 시절엔 꽁꽁 얼었어. 천 명이 올라가도 될 만큼 두껍게.
디에고: 사람들이 호수에서 탔어요? 진짜 물 위에서요?
에디: 진짜 물 위에서, 단단히 얼어붙은. 시에서 얼음을 검사하곤 했는데, 두께가 4인치가 되면 기둥에 빨간 공을 올렸지. 빨간 공은 "가도 좋다"는 뜻이었어.
디에고: 빨간 공! 신호등 같은 거네요, 얼음용으로!
에디: 딱 그거야. 그리고 공이 올라가면 브루클린의 모든 아이가 뛰었어. 얼마나 빨리 뛰었는지 스케이트가 등에 쾅쾅 부딪혔지.
디에고: 호수가 아직도 그렇게 얼어요, 아니면 이젠 안 얼어요?
에디: 거의 안 얼어. 그때처럼은 절대. 요즘 겨울은 더 따뜻하거든. 요즘 애들은 링크에서 타. 얼음을 대신 만들어 주는 기계가 있는 데서.
디에고: 솔직히 생각해 보면 좀 슬프네요.
에디: 그렇지. 참, 오늘 아침에 엄마가 문 앞에서 너 맡기면서 나한테 뭐라고 하셨지?
디에고: "에디는 바보 같은 짓 하면 안 돼"라고 했어요. 사실 두 번 말했어요.
에디: 너희 엄마 참 현명하시다. 약속할게. 바보 같은 짓은 안 해. 아주 조금만, 그것도 꼭 필요할 때만.
디에고: 에디, 중학교 결과 편지가 봄에 와요. 제가 정말 로봇 학교 붙을 것 같아요?
에디: 재미로 눈 깊이를 재는 녀석이? 누가 가능성이 있다면 그건 너라고 봐.
디에고: 말릭이 그러는데 그냥 복권 같대요. 알고리즘이 다 정한대요.
에디: 요즘은 다 알고리즘이지. 내 때는 명단 든 수녀님이었어. 그분이 네 얼굴을 맘에 안 들어 하면, 행운을 빌어야지.
디에고: 그 수녀님 착하긴 했어요?
에디: 아그네스 수녀님? 무시무시했지만 공정했어. 대부분의 사람들보다 낫지. 사실 돌로레스랑 많이 닮았어.
디에고: 하하! 할머니한텐 절대 말하지 마요. 일주일 내내 화내실 거예요.
에디: 아무튼 링크도 좋아. 보면 알 거야. 자, 다음 역이 우리 역이니까 일으켜 줘, 천천히. 내 무릎은 이 열차보다 늙었어.
디에고: 에디! 못 하겠어요! 발목이 접혀요, 삶은 스파게티처럼요!
에디: 그건 정상이니까 겁먹지 마. 처음엔 다들 발목이 접혀. 자, 잘 들어. 무릎을 굽혀.
디에고: 벌써 굽혔어요, 진짜로요!
에디: 더 굽혀. 작은 의자에 앉는 것처럼. 보이지 않는 의자, 쥐나 앉을 수 있을 만큼 작은 의자.
디에고: 쥐 의자. 좋아요, 할 수 있어요. 이렇게요?
에디: 낫네. 이제 앞으로 기울여, 조금만. 코가 발뒤꿈치 뒤가 아니라 발끝 위에 오도록.
디에고: 근데 앞으로 기울이면 앞으로 넘어질 거예요!
