# THE CORNER HOUSE — 4부 63화

## Snow Day? (눈 오는 날?)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 4부 9화(통산 63화) · 시기: 2027년 1월 18일(월, 마틴 루서 킹 데이) 밤 ~ 1월 19일(화) · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 4A 마리솔의 거실 → 다음 날 아침 4A 식탁(원격 수업) → 오후 건물 앞 인도
> - 등장인물: MARISOL, DIEGO, KEISHA, MALIK, MS. PARK, EDDIE, MRS. ESPOSITO, TOMMY, MARCUS
> - 사건: 폭설 예보가 나온다(10~14인치). 디에고와 말릭은 '진짜 눈 오는 날'을 기대한다. 그런데 밤 9시 시의 발표는 휴교가 아니라 원격 수업이다. 키샤는 야간 근무 뒤 아이를 누가 보느냐로 한숨을 쉰다. 다음 날 아침 수업 로그인 시스템이 다운되고, 화면 속 박 선생님은 '눈 측정 수학 숙제'를 낸다. 오후에는 하버라인이 보내기로 한 제설 인력이 오지 않는다. 결국 에디, 부인, 토미, 마커스, 아이들이 직접 삽을 든다. 눈이 그친 뒤 4시간 안에 치워야 벌금을 피한다.
> - 학습 포인트: 추측·확률 표현(likely / unlikely / chances are / there's a good chance / I bet / no way / it depends), 날씨 서술(flurries, accumulation, whiteout, slush, wind chill), 기대와 실망의 억양
> - 미국 현안: 뉴욕시 공립학교의 '눈 오는 날 휴교' 폐지와 원격 수업 전환, 원격 수업 첫날 시스템 장애, 맞벌이·교대 근무 부모의 돌봄 공백, 건물주 제설 의무(눈이 그친 뒤 4시간)
> - 연결: 41화 박 선생님(5학년 담임), 중학교 지원서는 12월 초 제출(결과는 봄). 49화 말릭. 키샤는 로어 맨해튼 병원 간호사(야간). 53화 에디의 무릎·지팡이. 57화 이사회. 하버라인 관리 부실 목록(부인).

## 대본

[Scene: Monday, 8:30 p.m. Martin Luther King Jr. Day. Apartment 4A. Snow is just starting outside the window. Diego and Malik press their faces to the glass. Marisol and Keisha drink tea on the couch.]

DIEGO: It's starting, it's starting! Look, Malik, those are flurries, and they're getting bigger!
MALIK: Those aren't flurries, Diego. Those are real flakes, big ones, like cornflakes falling out of the sky.
KEISHA: Boys, get your noses off the glass, please. You'll leave marks, and Marisol just cleaned that window.
DIEGO: Mom, what does the weather app say now? Read it out loud, please, read it!
MARISOL: Okay, here it is. "Winter storm warning. Accumulation of ten to fourteen inches, heaviest between midnight and noon. Wind gusts up to forty miles per hour, with a wind chill near zero."
MALIK: Fourteen inches is taller than my cat when she stands up!
DIEGO: That's a snow day! A real snow day! There's no way we have school tomorrow, and I bet you a hundred dollars!
MARISOL: You don't have a hundred dollars, mijo. You don't even have ten.
DIEGO: Fine, then I bet you my bike fund. That's forty-three dollars, and I'm serious.
KEISHA: Don't get your hopes up, baby. Chances are, it's a remote day, not a snow day.
MALIK: No way! Not remote! Mom, remote is the worst, because it's still school, but in pajamas, and you can't throw snowballs.
KEISHA: I know, honey. But the city changed the rule a few years ago, so there are no more traditional snow days. When it snows, school just goes online.
DIEGO: That's so unfair, because when you guys were kids, you had snow days, real ones!
MARISOL: We did. In nineteen ninety-six, I had three days off in a row, and I built a snowman with your Tío Héctor. He named it "Mr. Tortilla."
DIEGO: See? Mr. Tortilla! I want a Mr. Tortilla too, and I never get one!
KEISHA: Honestly, I'm more worried about tomorrow than you are. I work tonight, from seven p.m. to seven a.m. If school is remote, who watches Malik in the morning?
MARISOL: Isn't your sister around? I thought she helped out on your night shifts.
KEISHA: She's in Atlanta now, because her job moved her. So it's just me. I come home at eight a.m., and I'm supposed to be his teacher's assistant after twelve hours in the ER?
MARISOL: Then send him here. Diego and Malik can do remote school together at my table, since I'm working from home tomorrow anyway.
KEISHA: Are you sure about this? Marisol, you have your own work, and you've got a whole co-op to buy.
MARISOL: I'm totally sure. It's two boys at one table, so how bad could it be?
KEISHA: Honestly, it could be bad. It could be very, very bad. But thank you, I mean it.
DIEGO: Wait, wait! The mayor is about to talk, it's on the news! Somebody turn it up!
MALIK: Please be a snow day. Please be a snow day. Please, please be a snow day.
DIEGO: I'm crossing my fingers. And my toes, and even my eyes, just to be safe.
MARISOL: Shh, she's saying it now. Everybody, listen to her.
KEISHA: "All public schools will move to remote learning tomorrow. Students should log in at the normal time."
MALIK: NOOOOO!
DIEGO: Remote! It's remote! I knew it, I knew it would happen! My life is over!
MARISOL: Your life is not over, mijo. You'll be on a laptop with snow outside, just like every grown-up in America.
KEISHA: Okay, Malik, pajamas, right now. Is your laptop charged? And is your login written down somewhere?
MALIK: It's on a sticky note on the laptop, somewhere, I think.
KEISHA: "Somewhere." Wonderful. It's likely that we'll be looking for that sticky note at seven forty-five tomorrow morning.
MARISOL: Honestly, I'd say it's a near certainty, based on my own experience.

[Scene: Tuesday, 8:15 a.m. Apartment 4A. Snow covers everything outside. The kitchen table is a classroom: two laptops, two cereal bowls, one cat-shaped eraser. Malik is in pajamas. Diego is in a hoodie.]

DIEGO: Mom! It's not working! It just says "Error. Please try again later."
MALIK: Mine isn't working either! It's completely frozen, just like everything outside!
MARISOL: Okay, okay, calm down, both of you. Try again, and refresh the page.
DIEGO: I already refreshed it nine times, and it still says "Error"!
MARISOL: Let me check the news. Oh. Okay. "The city's school login system is experiencing problems due to heavy traffic. Families are asked to be patient."
MALIK: So does that mean we don't have school today?
MARISOL: No, you have school. It's just... not working right now.
DIEGO: But that's the same as a snow day, technically! So can I go outside?
MARISOL: No, not yet, mijo. Wait five more minutes, because they'll probably fix it soon.
MALIK: What's the chance they actually fix it? Like, as a percentage?
MARISOL: I'd say fifty-fifty, maybe sixty-forty. It depends. It depends on how many million kids are trying to log in at the same time.
MALIK: Can I eat my cereal while we wait for it?
MARISOL: You're already eating it, Malik. You're on your second bowl.
MALIK: I mean officially, so I don't get in trouble.
MARISOL: Officially, yes, you may. And Diego, please stop drawing on your hand.
DIEGO: It's not a drawing, it's a tally for the login. I'm counting the failures, and that's ten so far.
MARISOL: Then write "patience" on your other hand, so you can look at it when you get mad.
DIEGO: Wait! It's working! It's loading! I can see Ms. Park, and she's wearing a sweater with snowmen on it!
MS. PARK: Good morning, class! Can everyone hear me okay? Give me a thumbs-up if you can hear me.
DIEGO: Thumbs-up from me! Hi, Ms. Park! Malik is here too, at my table, because his mom worked all night!
MS. PARK: Hi, Diego. Hi, Malik. Malik, I can't help noticing that you're in pajamas. With dinosaurs on them.
MALIK: They're pajamas from when I was eight, but they still fit, so they're still good.
MS. PARK: I'm sure they are. Okay, class, about half of you are here, and the other half are probably still fighting the login page. So let's all be patient.
DIEGO: Ms. Park, is it likely that we'll have a real snow day this year? Or ever?
MS. PARK: Honestly, Diego, it's unlikely. But I have something better for you today, which is a snow day assignment.
MALIK: Something better than a snow day? That's impossible, Ms. Park.
MS. PARK: Listen first, and then decide. Today's math is outside. You'll measure the snow with a ruler in three places: a sidewalk, a car roof, and a windowsill.
DIEGO: Wait, we actually get to go outside for math?
MS. PARK: Yes, it's all for math. Then write down your numbers, calculate the average, and tell me why the numbers are different.
MALIK: Is it because the wind blows the snow around?
MS. PARK: There's a good chance that's part of it. That's called a hypothesis, so go test it, as long as you have your mom's permission.
DIEGO: Mom, can we go? It's for school, and it's literally the assignment!
MARISOL: Ms. Park, you just made my whole morning a lot easier. Thank you so much.
MS. PARK: Ms. Reyes, I have twenty-eight kids at home today, so I'm making my morning easier too. Boots and gloves, everybody, and report back at ten thirty.

[Scene: 1:30 p.m. The sidewalk in front of the corner building. The snow has stopped. Two feet of drifts against the door of The Hook. Eddie stands with a shovel and his cane. Mrs. Esposito holds a broom.]

EDDIE: Dolores, it stopped at eleven forty, because I checked. The law says the owner has four hours to clear the sidewalk, and after that, there's a fine.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Four hours, so that means by three forty. And Bridgewater promised us a snow crew. "Before noon," Brian said, and now it's one thirty.
EDDIE: No crew, no Brian, no nothing. That's typical for them.
TOMMY: Eddie, put that shovel down right now. You'll throw out your back, or your knee, or your everything.
EDDIE: I shoveled this sidewalk for fifty years, Tommy, every single winter. That was before Sal even had money for a crew.
TOMMY: And Dr. Levine said no shoveling, because I heard her say it, back in September. So give it to me.
EDDIE: Fine, then I'll supervise with my cane. I'll point at the snow, and you'll move it.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I'm writing it down as number forty-four on my list. "January nineteenth. No snow removal by owner. Tenants shoveled."
MARCUS: I'm here, everybody! I brought two shovels and a bag of salt from the shop, the pet-safe kind, for Biscuit.
MRS. ESPOSITO: That's very sweet, Marcus, but Biscuit doesn't go outside.
TOMMY: Is there any news from Marisol about Harborline's lawyer?
MRS. ESPOSITO: She called him on the fifth, and he said, "My client is reviewing all options." Then he hung up on her.
MARCUS: "Reviewing all options." That's lawyer for "We're scared."
EDDIE: Or it's lawyer for "We're hungry." It depends on the lawyer.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And the city application went in on Friday. Gina and Nadia stayed up until three a.m. to finish it, because it's forty-two pages.
TOMMY: Wait, forty-two pages? Gina always says some things need four pages, but I guess this one needed forty-two.
MARCUS: So when do we hear back from the city?
MRS. ESPOSITO: The end of February, or maybe March, because the city is slow. It's like the B61.
EDDIE: The B61 came on time once, back in nineteen eighty-eight, and people still talk about it.
TOMMY: Okay, let's split it up. Marcus, you take the side by your shop, and I'll do the front of the bar. And Eddie, you get to point.

[Scene: 2:15 p.m. Same sidewalk. Marcus has gone back into his shop to warm up. Diego and Malik run out in boots, with a ruler and a notebook.]

DIEGO: We're here, and we're doing math for school!
TOMMY: You're doing math out here, in the snow?
DIEGO: It's a school assignment! We have to measure in three places, the sidewalk, a car roof, and a windowsill, and Malik has the ruler.
MALIK: The car roof is eleven inches! No, wait, eleven and a half! I'm writing it down!
DIEGO: The sidewalk here is... nineteen inches! That's because of the drift by the door!
EDDIE: That's the wind, kid. It blows off the harbor, and it always piles up by The Hook. It's done that every storm since nineteen seventy-four.
DIEGO: Eddie, can you be our expert? Ms. Park said we need a source for our answer.
EDDIE: I'm a source, so write this down. "Edward O'Neill, eighty-three. Has seen every storm on this corner for fifty-two years."
MALIK: Windowsill! Mrs. Esposito's windowsill! It's seven inches!
MRS. ESPOSITO: Just don't touch the plant behind it, please. It's in hibernation until April.
TOMMY: Okay, the front of the bar is clear, and Marcus finished his side and salted it. It's three o'clock, so we beat the four-hour rule, but only barely.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And now we send Bridgewater the bill for our labor. Four adults, two children, and one supervisor.
TOMMY: Do you really think they'll pay it?
MRS. ESPOSITO: It's very unlikely. But the list gets longer, and the list goes to the lawyer, and the lawyer takes it into the negotiation.
EDDIE: Dolores, you're a dangerous woman when you have a list.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I'm dangerous with a pencil, Eddie. The list is just the weapon.
DIEGO: Okay, we have all the numbers! Eleven and a half, nineteen, and seven. Malik, what's the average?
MALIK: First you add them up. Thirty-seven and a half. Divide by three. Twelve and a half!
DIEGO: Twelve and a half inches! The forecast said ten to fourteen, so that means the forecast was actually right!
TOMMY: That's the first time in history.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And chances are, it'll never happen again. By tomorrow, it'll all be gray slush anyway.
DIEGO: Now can we have a snowball fight? The assignment is done. Ms. Park said report back at ten thirty, and it's three, so we're late anyway.
EDDIE: Diego, wait a second. You were supposed to report back at ten thirty?
DIEGO: We did report back, with numbers! Then we came out again, for more numbers!
MRS. ESPOSITO: Your mother texted me. You get one snowball fight, fifteen minutes long. Then hot chocolate at my place, and then homework.
MALIK: Fifteen whole minutes! Diego, you're going down!
DIEGO: No way! You're going down! I have the drift, and the drift is mine!
EDDIE: Boys, remember one thing! Aim for Tommy, because he's the biggest target.
TOMMY: Hey, Eddie! Whose side are you on, anyway?
EDDIE: The children's side, Tommy. Always the children's side, and especially on a snow day.
MRS. ESPOSITO: It's not a snow day, Eddie, it's a remote day. The city says so.
EDDIE: The city can say whatever it wants, Dolores. Look at that boy's face. That's a snow day.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **추측·확률 표현** (확률이 높은 순서)
  - *a near certainty* — 거의 확실함.
  - *I bet…* — "장담하는데…" 자신 있는 추측.
  - *likely / it's likely (that)…* — 가능성이 높다.
  - *Chances are, …* — "아마 ~일 거야." 구어에서 아주 흔하다.
  - *There's a good chance…* — ~할 가능성이 꽤 있다.
  - *fifty-fifty / sixty-forty* — 반반 / 6 대 4.
  - *It depends.* — 상황에 따라 다르다.
  - *unlikely* — 가능성이 낮다.
  - *No way!* — "말도 안 돼!" 강한 부정·불신.
- **날씨 어휘**
  - *flurries* 눈발 / *flakes* 눈송이 / *winter storm warning* 겨울 폭풍 경보
  - *gusts* 돌풍 / *drift* 바람에 쌓인 눈더미 / *accumulation* 적설량
  - *wind chill* 체감 온도 / *slush* 녹아 질척한 눈
- **Don't get your hopes up.** — "너무 기대하지 마."
- **Crossing my fingers.** — "행운을 빌어(손가락 꼬기)."
- **You're going down!** — "넌 끝났어!" 놀이·경기에서 쓰는 도발.
- **throw out your back** — 허리를 삐끗하다.
- **hypothesis** — 가설. 박 선생님이 아이들에게 과학적 사고를 가르치는 말.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 기대의 억양: 디에고의 *That's a snow day! A real snow day!*는 높게 끌어올린다.
- 실망의 억양: *Remote! It's remote!*는 첫 단어에서 확 떨어뜨리고, *My life is over!*에서 과장되게 늘어진다.
- *Chances are,* 다음에서 살짝 쉬고 결론을 말한다: *Chances are, | it's a remote day.*
- 박 선생님은 화면 너머로 또박또박, 밝은 교사 톤. *Give me a thumbs-up if you can hear me.*
- 숫자를 계산하는 말릭: *Thirty-seven and a half. | Divide by three. | Twelve and a half!* — 끊어서 점점 빠르게.
- 마지막 에디의 *That's a snow day.* — 조용하고 단호하게. 웃음을 머금고.
