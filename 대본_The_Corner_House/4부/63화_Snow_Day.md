# THE CORNER HOUSE — 4부 63화

## Snow Day? (눈 오는 날?)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 4부 9화(통산 63화) · 시기: 2027년 1월 18일(월, 마틴 루서 킹 데이) 밤 ~ 1월 19일(화) · 난이도: B2~C1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 4A 마리솔의 거실 → 다음 날 아침 4A 식탁(원격 수업) → 오후 건물 앞 인도
> - 등장인물: MARISOL, DIEGO, KEISHA, MALIK, MS. PARK, EDDIE, MRS. ESPOSITO, TOMMY, MARCUS
> - 사건: 폭설 예보가 나온다(10~14인치). 디에고와 말릭은 '진짜 눈 오는 날'을 기대한다. 그런데 밤 9시 시의 발표는 휴교가 아니라 원격 수업이다. 키샤는 야간 근무 뒤 아이를 누가 보느냐로 한숨을 쉰다. 다음 날 아침 수업 로그인 시스템이 다운되고, 화면 속 박 선생님은 '눈 측정 수학 숙제'를 낸다. 오후에는 하버라인이 보내기로 한 제설 인력이 오지 않는다. 결국 에디, 부인, 토미, 마커스, 아이들이 직접 삽을 든다. 눈이 그친 뒤 4시간 안에 치워야 벌금을 피한다.
> - 학습 포인트: 추측·확률 표현(likely / unlikely / chances are / there's a good chance / I bet / no way / it depends), 날씨 서술(flurries, accumulation, whiteout, slush, wind chill), 기대와 실망의 억양
> - 미국 현안: 뉴욕시 공립학교의 '눈 오는 날 휴교' 폐지와 원격 수업 전환, 원격 수업 첫날 시스템 장애, 맞벌이·교대 근무 부모의 돌봄 공백, 건물주 제설 의무(눈이 그친 뒤 4시간)
> - 연결: 41화 박 선생님(5학년 담임), 중학교 지원서는 12월 초 제출(결과는 봄). 49화 말릭. 키샤는 로어 맨해튼 병원 간호사(야간). 53화 에디의 무릎·지팡이. 57화 이사회. 하버라인 관리 부실 목록(부인).

## 대본

[Scene: Monday, 8:30 p.m. Martin Luther King Jr. Day. Apartment 4A. Snow is just starting outside the window. Diego and Malik press their faces to the glass. Marisol and Keisha drink tea on the couch.]

DIEGO: It's starting! It's starting! Look! Flurries!
MALIK: Those aren't flurries. Those are real flakes. Big ones. Like cornflakes.
KEISHA: Boys, get your noses off the glass. You'll leave marks.
DIEGO: Mom, what does the weather app say now? Read it! Read it!
MARISOL: Okay. "Winter storm warning. Ten to fourteen inches. Heaviest between midnight and noon. Wind gusts up to forty miles per hour."
MALIK: Fourteen inches! That's taller than my cat!
DIEGO: That's a snow day! A real snow day! No school tomorrow! I bet you a hundred dollars!
MARISOL: You don't have a hundred dollars, mijo.
DIEGO: I bet you my bike fund. Forty-three dollars.
KEISHA: Don't get your hopes up, baby. Chances are, it's a remote day. Not a snow day.
MALIK: No way! Not remote! Mom, remote is the worst. It's school, but in pajamas, and you can't throw snowballs.
KEISHA: I know. But the city changed the rule a few years ago. No more traditional snow days. When it snows, school goes online.
DIEGO: That's so unfair! When you were kids, you had snow days! Real ones!
MARISOL: We did. In nineteen ninety-six, I had three days off in a row. I built a snowman with your Tío Héctor. He named it "Mr. Tortilla."
DIEGO: See? Mr. Tortilla! I want a Mr. Tortilla!
KEISHA: Honestly, I'm more worried about tomorrow than you are. I work tonight. Seven p.m. to seven a.m. If school is remote, who watches Malik in the morning?
MARISOL: Isn't your sister around?
KEISHA: She's in Atlanta. Her job moved her. So it's just me. I come home at eight a.m., and I'm supposed to be his teacher's assistant? After twelve hours in the ER?
MARISOL: Send him here. Diego and Malik can do remote school together. At my table. I work from home tomorrow anyway.
KEISHA: Are you sure? Marisol, you have work. And a co-op to buy.
MARISOL: I'm sure. Two boys at one table. How bad could it be?
KEISHA: Very bad. It could be very bad. But thank you.
DIEGO: Wait, wait! The mayor is about to talk! It's on the news! Turn it up!
MALIK: Please be a snow day. Please be a snow day. Please be a snow day.
DIEGO: Crossing my fingers. And my toes. And my eyes.
MARISOL: She's saying it. Listen.
KEISHA: "All public schools will move to remote learning tomorrow. Students should log in at the normal time."
MALIK: NOOOOO!
DIEGO: Remote! It's remote! I knew it! My life is over!
MARISOL: Your life is not over. You'll be on a laptop. With snow outside. Like every grown-up in America.
KEISHA: Okay, Malik. Pajamas. Your laptop is charged? Your login is written down?
MALIK: It's on a sticky note. On the laptop. Somewhere.
KEISHA: "Somewhere." Wonderful. It's likely we'll be looking for that sticky note at seven forty-five tomorrow.
MARISOL: I'd say it's a near certainty.

[Scene: Tuesday, 8:15 a.m. Apartment 4A. Snow covers everything outside. The kitchen table is a classroom: two laptops, two cereal bowls, one cat-shaped eraser. Malik is in pajamas. Diego is in a hoodie.]

DIEGO: Mom! It's not working! It says "Error. Please try again later."
MALIK: Mine too! It's frozen! Like outside!
MARISOL: Okay, okay. Calm down. Try again. Refresh the page.
DIEGO: I refreshed it nine times! It still says "Error"!
MARISOL: Let me check the news. Oh. Okay. "The city's school login system is experiencing problems due to heavy traffic. Families are asked to be patient."
MALIK: So we don't have school?
MARISOL: You have school. It's just... not working.
DIEGO: That's the same as a snow day! Technically! Can I go outside?
MARISOL: No. Wait. Five more minutes. They'll probably fix it.
MALIK: What's the chance they fix it? Like, percentage?
MARISOL: I'd say fifty-fifty. Maybe sixty-forty. It depends how many million kids are trying to log in.
MALIK: Can I eat my cereal while we wait?
MARISOL: You're already eating it, Malik.
MALIK: I mean officially.
MARISOL: Officially, yes. Diego, stop drawing on your hand.
DIEGO: It's a tally. For the login. I'm counting failures. Ten so far.
MARISOL: Then write "patience" on your other hand.
DIEGO: Wait! It's working! It's loading! I see Ms. Park! She's in a sweater with snowmen on it!
MS. PARK: Good morning, class! Can everyone hear me? Give me a thumbs-up if you can hear me.
DIEGO: Thumbs-up! Hi, Ms. Park! Malik is here too! At my table!
MS. PARK: Hi, Diego. Hi, Malik. Malik, you're in pajamas. With dinosaurs.
MALIK: They're pajamas from when I was eight. They're still good.
MS. PARK: I'm sure they are. Okay, class. About half of you are here. The other half are probably still fighting the login page. Let's be patient.
DIEGO: Ms. Park, is it likely we'll have a real snow day this year? Ever?
MS. PARK: Honestly? It's unlikely, Diego. But I have something better. A snow day assignment.
MALIK: Better than a snow day? That's impossible.
MS. PARK: Listen first. Today's math is outside. You'll measure the snow. In three places. With a ruler. A sidewalk, a car roof, and a windowsill.
DIEGO: We get to go outside? For math?
MS. PARK: For math. Then, write down your numbers. Calculate the average. And tell me why the numbers are different.
MALIK: Because the wind blows the snow around?
MS. PARK: There's a good chance that's part of it. That's a hypothesis. Go test it. With your mom's permission.
DIEGO: Mom! Can we? For school? It's literally the assignment!
MARISOL: Ms. Park, you just made my morning easier. Thank you.
MS. PARK: Ms. Reyes, I have twenty-eight kids at home today. I'm making my morning easier too. Boots and gloves, everybody. Report back at ten thirty.

[Scene: 1:30 p.m. The sidewalk in front of the corner building. The snow has stopped. Two feet of drifts against the door of The Hook. Eddie stands with a shovel and his cane. Mrs. Esposito holds a broom.]

EDDIE: Dolores, it stopped at eleven forty. I checked. The law says the owner has four hours. After that, a fine.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Four hours. So by three forty. And Bridgewater promised a snow crew. "Before noon," Brian said. It's one thirty.
EDDIE: No crew. No Brian. No nothing. Typical.
TOMMY: Eddie! Put that shovel down. You'll throw out your back. Your knee. Your everything.
EDDIE: I shoveled this sidewalk for fifty years, Tommy. Every winter. Before Sal had money for a crew.
TOMMY: And Dr. Levine said no shoveling. I heard her. In September. Give it to me.
EDDIE: Fine. I'll supervise. With my cane. I'll point at the snow.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I'm writing it down. Number forty-four on my list. "January nineteenth. No snow removal by owner. Tenants shoveled."
MARCUS: I'm here! I brought two shovels and a bag of salt. From the shop. The pet-safe kind. For Biscuit.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Biscuit doesn't go outside, Marcus.
TOMMY: Any news from Marisol? About Harborline's lawyer?
MRS. ESPOSITO: She called him on the fifth. He said, "My client is reviewing all options." Then he hung up.
MARCUS: "Reviewing all options." That's lawyer for "We're scared."
EDDIE: Or lawyer for "We're hungry." It depends on the lawyer.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And the city application went in on Friday. Gina and Nadia stayed up until three a.m. Forty-two pages.
TOMMY: Forty-two pages. Gina always says some things need four pages. This one needed forty-two.
MARCUS: When do we hear back?
MRS. ESPOSITO: End of February. Maybe March. The city is slow. Like the B61.
EDDIE: The B61 came on time once. In nineteen eighty-eight. People still talk about it.
TOMMY: Okay. Marcus, the side by your shop. I'll do the front of the bar. Eddie, you point.
[Scene: 2:15 p.m. Same sidewalk. Marcus has gone back into his shop to warm up. Diego and Malik run out in boots, with a ruler and a notebook.]

DIEGO: We're here! We're here! We're doing math!
TOMMY: Math? In the snow?
DIEGO: It's a school assignment! We have to measure in three places. Sidewalk, car roof, and windowsill. Malik has the ruler.
MALIK: The car roof is eleven inches! Eleven and a half! I'm writing it down!
DIEGO: The sidewalk here is... nineteen inches! Because of the drift! By the door!
EDDIE: That's the wind. It blows off the harbor. It always piles up by The Hook. Every storm since nineteen seventy-four.
DIEGO: Eddie, can you be our expert? Ms. Park said we need a source.
EDDIE: I'm a source. Write it down. "Edward O'Neill, eighty-three. Has seen every storm on this corner for fifty-two years."
MALIK: Windowsill! Mrs. Esposito's windowsill! It's seven inches!
MRS. ESPOSITO: Don't touch the plant behind it. It's in hibernation.
TOMMY: Okay. Front of the bar is clear. Marcus finished his side. Salted. It's three o'clock. We beat the four-hour rule. Barely.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And now we send Bridgewater the bill. For labor. Four adults. Two children. One supervisor.
TOMMY: Will they pay it?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Unlikely. But the list gets longer. And the list goes to the lawyer. And the lawyer goes to the negotiation.
EDDIE: Dolores, you're dangerous. With a list.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I'm dangerous with a pencil, Eddie. The list is just the weapon.
DIEGO: Okay! We have the numbers! Eleven and a half, nineteen, and seven. Malik, what's the average?
MALIK: Add them up. Thirty-seven and a half. Divide by three. Twelve and a half!
DIEGO: Twelve and a half inches! The forecast said ten to fourteen. So the forecast was right!
TOMMY: First time in history.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Chances are, it'll never happen again.
DIEGO: Now can we have a snowball fight? The assignment is done. Ms. Park said report back at ten thirty. It's three. So we're late anyway.
EDDIE: Diego! You were supposed to report back at ten thirty?
DIEGO: We reported back. With numbers! Then we came out again! For more numbers!
MRS. ESPOSITO: Your mother texted me. One snowball fight. Fifteen minutes. Then hot chocolate. At my place. Then homework.
MALIK: Fifteen minutes! Diego, you're going down!
DIEGO: No way! You're going down! I have the drift! The drift is mine!
EDDIE: Boys! Remember! Aim for Tommy. He's the biggest target.
TOMMY: Eddie! Whose side are you on?
EDDIE: The children's side, Tommy. Always the children's side. Especially on a snow day.
MRS. ESPOSITO: It's not a snow day, Eddie. It's a remote day. The city says so.
EDDIE: The city can say what it wants, Dolores. Look at that boy's face. That's a snow day.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **추측·확률 표현** (확률이 높은 순서)
  - *a near certainty* — 거의 확실함.
  - *I bet…* — "장담하는데…" 자신 있는 추측.
  - *likely / it's likely (that)…* — 가능성이 높다.
  - *Chances are, …* — "아마 ~일 거야." 구어에서 아주 흔하다.
  - *There's a good chance…* — ~할 가능성이 꽤 있다.
  - *fifty-fifty / sixty-forty* — 반반 / 6 대 4.
  - *It depends.* — 상황에 따라 다르다.
  - *unlikely* — 가능성이 낮다.
  - *No way!* — "말도 안 돼!" 강한 부정·불신.
- **날씨 어휘**
  - *flurries* 눈발 / *flakes* 눈송이 / *winter storm warning* 겨울 폭풍 경보
  - *gusts* 돌풍 / *drift* 바람에 쌓인 눈더미 / *accumulation* 적설량
  - *wind chill* 체감 온도 / *slush* 녹아 질척한 눈
- **Don't get your hopes up.** — "너무 기대하지 마."
- **Crossing my fingers.** — "행운을 빌어(손가락 꼬기)."
- **You're going down!** — "넌 끝났어!" 놀이·경기에서 쓰는 도발.
- **throw out your back** — 허리를 삐끗하다.
- **hypothesis** — 가설. 박 선생님이 아이들에게 과학적 사고를 가르치는 말.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 기대의 억양: 디에고의 *That's a snow day! A real snow day!*는 높게 끌어올린다.
- 실망의 억양: *Remote! It's remote!*는 첫 단어에서 확 떨어뜨리고, *My life is over!*에서 과장되게 늘어진다.
- *Chances are,* 다음에서 살짝 쉬고 결론을 말한다: *Chances are, | it's a remote day.*
- 박 선생님은 화면 너머로 또박또박, 밝은 교사 톤. *Give me a thumbs-up if you can hear me.*
- 숫자를 계산하는 말릭: *Thirty-seven and a half. | Divide by three. | Twelve and a half!* — 끊어서 점점 빠르게.
- 마지막 에디의 *That's a snow day.* — 조용하고 단호하게. 웃음을 머금고.
