# THE CORNER HOUSE — 1부 12화

## The Night Before the Stoop Sale (스툽 세일 전날 밤)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 1부 12화 · 시기: 2026년 5월 초, 금요일 밤 · 난이도: A2~B1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 4A 마리솔의 집 → 2A 토미의 집 벽장 → 건물 앞 계단(스툽)
> - 등장인물: MARISOL, DIEGO, TOMMY, GINA, MRS. ESPOSITO, JESSE
> - 사건: 블록 전체 스툽 세일 전날 밤. 다들 내놓을 물건을 정리하며 추억을 꺼낸다. 에스포지토 부인이 클립보드를 들고 규칙을 발표한다.
> - 학습 포인트: 계획 말하기(be going to / be -ing), 시간 표현
> - 미국 현안: 물가와 중고 거래, 블록 협회의 거리 행사 허가
> - 연결: 디에고의 '자전거 저금'(Bike Fund) 시작. 앤젤라의 고장 난 램프와 레코드. 13화로 바로 이어짐. 82화에서 같은 상황을 1년 뒤에 반복.

## 대본

[Scene: Friday, 7:45 p.m. Apartment 4A. Boxes everywhere.]

MARISOL: Okay, Diego. Tomorrow is the stoop sale. What are you going to sell?
DIEGO: Nothing. I like all my stuff, Mom.
MARISOL: Everything? What about this, the baby puzzle with the farm animals?
DIEGO: That's for babies, and I'm not a baby. Okay, sell it.
MARISOL: And what about these sneakers? They're too small for you now.
DIEGO: Those are my lucky sneakers! I scored three goals in them.
MARISOL: Can you still put them on your feet?
DIEGO: ...No. They make my toes hurt.
MARISOL: Then they're going to be somebody else's lucky sneakers.
DIEGO: Fine. But I'm going to keep the Lego.
MARISOL: Which Lego? You have three whole boxes of it.
DIEGO: All of them. I'm going to build a whole city this summer.
MARISOL: You said the same thing last summer.
DIEGO: This summer is different, because I'm older now.
MARISOL: Okay, here's the deal. Keep two boxes and sell one.
DIEGO: Can I keep the money if I sell my stuff?
MARISOL: You can keep half of it. The other half goes in your bike fund.
DIEGO: My bike fund? Wait, I have a bike fund?
MARISOL: Since today, for the summer. Remember? I said, "We'll see."
DIEGO: So "we'll see" really means yes!
MARISOL: It means "we're going to see." That's if you sell enough.
DIEGO: Okay! Then I'm going to sell everything! The puzzles, the sneakers, the books!
MARISOL: Not the books. The books are going to Abuela's church.
DIEGO: So what time does it start tomorrow?
MARISOL: At nine. But Mrs. Esposito says we have to be ready at eight thirty.
DIEGO: Eight thirty? On a Saturday morning?
MARISOL: The early bird gets the customers. Bring the boxes downstairs tonight.
DIEGO: Tonight? But it's already eight o'clock.
MARISOL: Then you'd better start now.

[Scene: The same time. Apartment 2A. Tommy and Gina in front of an open closet.]

GINA: Dad, you have way too much stuff. Look at this closet.
TOMMY: That's not stuff, sweetheart. That's history.
GINA: It's a broken lamp, three umbrellas, and a box of old Mets programs.
TOMMY: The programs stay. Those are from eighty-six, the best year ever.
GINA: Fine, they stay. What about the lamp?
TOMMY: The lamp can go. It never worked anyway.
GINA: Then why did you keep it all this time?
TOMMY: Your mother liked it. She said she was going to fix it, for fifteen years.
GINA: Oh. So it's Mom's lamp.
TOMMY: Yeah. Mom's lamp.
GINA: We don't have to sell it, Dad. Not if you don't want to.
TOMMY: No, it's time. Somebody's going to fix it, and somebody's going to love it.
GINA: What's in this box? It's really heavy.
TOMMY: Go ahead and open it.
GINA: Records! These are Mom's records. And her record player!
TOMMY: Bruce Springsteen, Billy Joel, Whitney Houston. She played these every Sunday.
GINA: I remember that. She danced in the kitchen with a wooden spoon.
TOMMY: She was a terrible dancer, you know.
GINA: She was the worst. But she didn't care at all.
TOMMY: And that's why I married her.
GINA: Dad, I'm not going to let you sell these.
TOMMY: Who said anything about selling? I'm giving them to you, for Luca.
GINA: Really? Are you serious?
TOMMY: Luca's going to dance in your kitchen with a wooden spoon.
GINA: Okay, I'm going to cry now. Give me a second.
TOMMY: Take your time. Then take the lamp downstairs for me.
GINA: How much are you going to ask for it?
TOMMY: Five dollars, as is. It doesn't work, so that's fair.
GINA: Write "As is" in big letters, or people will complain.
TOMMY: People always complain. Then they buy it anyway.

[Scene: 8:45 p.m. The front stoop. Neighbors sitting on the steps. Mrs. Esposito stands with a clipboard.]

MRS. ESPOSITO: Okay, everybody, gather around. I have the rules for tomorrow.
TOMMY: Of course you do. And they're on a clipboard.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Yes, on a clipboard. Somebody has to be organized. Number one. We start at nine, not before.
JESSE: Why not before? What if people come early?
MRS. ESPOSITO: The city permit says nine to five, so we follow the permit.
MARISOL: We have a permit? For a stoop sale?
MRS. ESPOSITO: The block association got a street permit. They're going to close the street to cars, from Pioneer to Visitation Place.
DIEGO: No cars at all? On the whole street?
MRS. ESPOSITO: On the whole block, so the kids can play. Number two. Tables stay on the sidewalk, not in the bike lane.
TOMMY: But there's no bike lane on this block.
MRS. ESPOSITO: There's going to be one next year. That's what the city says.
TOMMY: The city says a lot of things, Dolores.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Number three. Every item has a price, so use the stickers. Here, take the yellow ones.
JESSE: What if I don't have anything to sell?
MARISOL: You have nothing? Not even a little thing?
JESSE: I have a couch. But it's Kyle's couch, and I'm sleeping on it.
DIEGO: You can sell lemonade! You can have a lemonade stand!
JESSE: Lemonade. You know, that's actually a good idea.
TOMMY: Sell some coffee too. I'll give you a pot.
JESSE: Your coffee? Tommy, I want people to come back.
TOMMY: Everybody's a critic around here. Even the couch fella.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Number four. At five o'clock, everything that's left goes to the church donation truck. Nothing stays on the street.
MARISOL: That's good. Last year, there was junk on the corner for a whole week.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Last year, nobody listened to me. This year, I have a clipboard.
DIEGO: Mrs. Esposito, what are you going to sell?
MRS. ESPOSITO: My good dishes. They were my mother's, and I'm never going to use them.
MARISOL: Your mother's dishes? Are you sure about that?
MRS. ESPOSITO: I'm sure. There are twelve plates, and I live alone with one cat. We don't need twelve plates.
TOMMY: I'm going to sell Angela's lamp.
MRS. ESPOSITO: The lamp in your window? The one that doesn't work?
TOMMY: That's the one. You really know everything around here.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I live across the hall, so I see it every night. It's always dark.
DIEGO: I'm going to sell my lucky sneakers for my bike fund.
JESSE: Lucky sneakers? How lucky are we talking?
DIEGO: Three goals lucky.
JESSE: That's very lucky. You should charge ten dollars.
MARISOL: Ten dollars for old sneakers? Nobody's going to pay that much.
DIEGO: Somebody's going to pay that. You'll see, Mom.
TOMMY: So, what time are we setting up tomorrow?
MRS. ESPOSITO: At eight thirty. But I'm going to be here at eight.
TOMMY: Of course you are.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And what about the weather? Is it going to rain?
MARISOL: My app says it's going to be sunny and seventy degrees. Perfect.
TOMMY: Eddie's knee says sunny too. I asked him this afternoon.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Then it's going to be sunny. Eddie's knee is never wrong.
JESSE: Is Eddie selling anything tomorrow?
TOMMY: Eddie? He's never going to sell anything. He's kept everything since nineteen seventy-four.
MARISOL: He's going to sit on the stoop and talk to everybody. That's his job.
DIEGO: He's going to tell his stories about the docks.
JESSE: For free?
TOMMY: Nothing's free tomorrow. He's going to charge a dollar a story.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Okay, the meeting is over. Everybody, go to bed early tonight.
JESSE: But it's only nine o'clock.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Early. I said early, Jesse.
DIEGO: I can't sleep tonight. I'm way too excited.
MARISOL: Oh, you're going to sleep. But first you're going to carry boxes.
DIEGO: More boxes? Seriously?
MARISOL: Three more boxes, and the lucky sneakers.
TOMMY: Good night, everybody. I'll see you all at eight thirty.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Eight thirty sharp. Not eight thirty-five.
TOMMY: Yes, ma'am, eight thirty sharp. The block association has spoken.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **stoop / stoop sale** — 뉴욕 브라운스톤 건물 현관 앞 돌계단 / 그 계단 앞에서 여는 동네 벼룩시장.
- **be going to + 동사원형** — 이미 마음먹은 계획이나 근거 있는 예측. *What are you going to sell? / I'm going to keep the Lego. / Is it going to rain?*
- **be -ing (현재진행형으로 미래)** — 정해진 일정. *Is Eddie selling anything tomorrow? / What time are we setting up tomorrow?*
- **will과의 차이** — 말하는 순간 정한 일·즉석 약속은 will. *I'll give you a pot.*(그 자리에서 제안)
- **The early bird gets the worm.** — "일찍 일어나는 새가 벌레를 잡는다." 마리솔은 worm을 customers로 바꿔 말한다.
- **you'd better** — "~하는 게 좋을 거야." 충고·경고. (Then you'd better start now.)
- **as is** — (중고품을) 있는 그대로, 하자 포함. 환불·불평 불가라는 뜻.
- **Take your time.** — "천천히 해."
- **Gather around.** — "다들 모여요."
- **Of course you do. / Of course you are.** — "그럼 그렇지." 예상대로라는 놀림.
- **sharp** — 정각에. (Eight thirty sharp.)
- **The block association has spoken.** — "블록 협회께서 말씀하셨다." 반쯤 장난스러운 복종.
- **시간 표현** — *tomorrow, tonight, at nine, from nine to five, this summer, last summer, next year, since today*.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- **gonna**: 대본은 going to로 썼지만 구어에서는 [거너]로 줄여 읽어도 된다. 단, 장소로 '가는' going to(The books are going to Abuela's church)는 줄이지 않는다.
- **나열 억양**: *Bruce Springsteen ↗, Billy Joel ↗, Whitney Houston ↘.* / *The puzzles ↗, the sneakers ↗, the books! ↘* 마지막 항목만 내린다.
- **Number one. / Number two.** 에스포지토 부인은 번호 뒤에서 잠깐 쉬고 규칙을 또박또박.
- **eight thirty** [에잇 써리] — 플랩 T. *eight thirty-five*에서 five에 강세(정각과 대비).
- **records** [레커r즈](명사, 앞 강세) — 동사 re-**CORD**와 다르다.
- 앤젤라 이야기 장면은 속도를 늦추고 목소리를 낮춘다. "Yeah. Mom's lamp."는 지나의 말을 그대로 받아 조용히.

## 한글 해석

마리솔: 자, 디에고. 내일이 스툽 세일이야. 뭐 팔 거야?
디에고: 아무것도. 내 물건 다 좋단 말이야, 엄마.

디에고: …아니. 신으면 발가락 아파.

디에고: 알았어. 근데 레고는 가질 거야.

디에고: 전부 다. 이번 여름에 도시 하나를 통째로 만들 거야.
마리솔: 작년 여름에도 똑같은 말 했잖아.
디에고: 이번 여름은 달라, 이제 내가 더 컸으니까.
마리솔: 좋아, 이렇게 하자. 두 상자는 갖고 하나는 팔아.
디에고: 내 물건 팔면 돈은 내가 가져도 돼?

디에고: 자전거 저금통? 잠깐, 나 자전거 저금통 있어?
마리솔: 오늘부터, 여름을 위해서. 기억나? 내가 "두고 보자"고 했잖아.
디에고: 그럼 "두고 보자"가 진짜 된다는 뜻이었네!

디에고: 좋아! 그럼 다 팔 거야! 퍼즐, 운동화, 책까지!
마리솔: 책은 안 돼. 책은 할머니 교회로 갈 거야.
디에고: 그럼 내일 몇 시에 시작해?
마리솔: 9시. 근데 에스포지토 부인이 8시 반까지 준비 끝내래.
디에고: 8시 반? 토요일 아침에?
마리솔: 일찍 나와야 손님을 잡지. 오늘 밤에 상자들 아래층에 갖다 놔.
디에고: 오늘 밤에? 근데 벌써 8시인데.
마리솔: 그럼 지금 시작하는 게 좋을걸.
지나: 아빠, 물건이 너무너무 많아요. 이 벽장 좀 봐요.
토미: 그건 물건이 아니야, 얘야. 역사지.
지나: 고장 난 램프, 우산 세 개, 그리고 옛날 메츠 경기 프로그램 한 상자예요.
토미: 프로그램은 남겨. 86년 거야, 최고의 해였지.
지나: 알았어요, 남겨요. 램프는요?
토미: 램프는 내놔도 돼. 어차피 한 번도 안 켜졌어.
지나: 그럼 왜 여태 갖고 있었어요?
토미: 네 엄마가 좋아했거든. 고칠 거라고 했지, 15년 동안.
지나: 아. 그럼 엄마 램프구나.
토미: 응. 엄마 램프.
지나: 안 팔아도 돼요, 아빠. 아빠가 싫으면요.
토미: 아니, 때가 됐어. 누군가 고치겠지, 그리고 누군가 아껴 주겠지.
지나: 이 상자엔 뭐가 들었어요? 진짜 무겁네.
토미: 어서 열어 봐.
지나: 레코드! 엄마 레코드예요. 그리고 엄마 턴테이블!
토미: 브루스 스프링스틴, 빌리 조엘, 휘트니 휴스턴. 일요일마다 틀었지.

토미: 춤 진짜 못 췄어, 알지.
지나: 최악이었죠. 근데 엄만 전혀 신경 안 썼어요.
토미: 그래서 내가 결혼한 거야.
지나: 아빠, 이건 절대 못 팔게 할 거예요.
토미: 누가 판다고 했어? 너 주는 거야, 루카 주라고.

토미: 루카가 네 부엌에서 나무 주걱 들고 춤추겠지.
지나: 아, 나 지금 울 것 같아요. 잠깐만요.
토미: 천천히 해. 그다음에 램프 좀 아래층에 갖다 줘.

토미: 사람들은 늘 불평해. 그러고 나서 결국 사 가지.
에스포지토 부인: 자, 여러분, 모이세요. 내일 규칙 알려 드릴게요.
토미: 그럼 그렇지. 게다가 클립보드에 있고.
에스포지토 부인: 네, 클립보드에요. 누군가는 정리를 해야죠. 첫째. 9시에 시작합니다, 그 전엔 안 돼요.

토미: 여긴 다들 평론가야. 소파 청년까지.
