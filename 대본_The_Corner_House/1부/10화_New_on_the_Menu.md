## New on the Menu (새 메뉴)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 1부 10화 · 시기: 2026년 4월, 수요일 오후 · 난이도: A2~B1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 바 The Hook 주방 → 바 테이블(시식회)
> - 등장인물: TOMMY, TASHA, GINA, MARCUS, EDDIE
> - 사건: 수입 치즈와 올리브유값이 올라 메뉴를 바꾸기로 한다. 타샤 할머니의 엠파나다, 피클 튀김, 허니핫 윙, 미국산 치즈 모차렐라 스틱을 놓고 시식회를 연다.
> - 학습 포인트: 음식 어휘, 맛 표현(crispy, juicy, sour, spicy, creamy), 취향 비교(I'd rather / I prefer / I like X better than Y)
> - 미국 현안: 관세로 오른 수입 식재료, 배달 앱 수수료
> - 연결: 타샤는 레드훅 공공주택(Red Hook Houses)에서 자란 푸에르토리코계. 윙 12→14달러. 'Abuela's Empanadas'가 칠판에 오른다.

## 대본

[Scene: Wednesday, 2:00 p.m. The Hook's small kitchen. Tommy is reading a bill.]

TASHA: Tommy, what's wrong? You look like the Mets lost again.
TOMMY: Worse. Look at this invoice. From my food supplier.
TASHA: Let me see. Parmesan cheese... Wait. That's twenty percent more than last month.
TOMMY: Twenty-two percent. It's from Italy. Tariffs again.
TASHA: And the olive oil?
TOMMY: Also from Italy. Also more expensive. And the avocados from Mexico.
TASHA: So no more guacamole?
TOMMY: Guacamole is now a luxury. Like caviar.
TASHA: What about the mozzarella sticks? People love those.
TOMMY: The cheese is from Wisconsin. That's fine. But the breadcrumbs went up too.
TASHA: Breadcrumbs? Why breadcrumbs?
TOMMY: I don't know. Everything goes up. Nobody knows why.
TASHA: Okay. So let's change the menu. Cook things that are cheaper.
TOMMY: Like what? People come here for wings and burgers.
TASHA: Keep the wings. Add something new. Something cheap to make, but good.
TOMMY: You have an idea. I can see it on your face.
TASHA: Empanadas. My grandmother's recipe. Beef, onions, olives, a little sofrito.
TOMMY: Your abuela's empanadas? From the Red Hook Houses?
TASHA: The famous ones. Everybody in the projects wanted them. She sold them from her kitchen window.
TOMMY: Are they expensive to make?
TASHA: Not at all. Flour, ground beef, onions, peppers. Maybe a dollar each.
TOMMY: And we sell them for... four dollars?
TASHA: Three for ten. People like a deal.
TOMMY: Okay. What else?
TASHA: Fried pickles. They're cheap, they're salty, and people drink more beer.
TOMMY: Salty food, more beer. Now you're talking business.
TASHA: And maybe a new wing sauce. Honey and hot sauce. Sweet and spicy.
TOMMY: Honey-hot wings. I like the sound of that.
TASHA: So let's do a tasting. Tonight. Before we put it on the board.
TOMMY: A tasting? With who?
TASHA: With the experts. Gina, Marcus, Eddie. Free food. They'll come.
TOMMY: Free food. Of course they'll come.

[Scene: 5:00 p.m. A table near the window. Four plates.]

GINA: Okay, Dad. I left work early for this. It better be good.
MARCUS: I closed the shop for this. It better be very good.
EDDIE: I didn't leave anything. I was already here.
TASHA: Okay, everybody. Here are the rules. Taste everything. Give it a number, one to ten.
TOMMY: And be honest. But not too honest.
GINA: Dad, you said "be honest."
TOMMY: I said "not too honest." There's a difference.
TASHA: First, the empanadas. Careful. They're hot.
MARCUS: Oh, wow. That's really good. The outside is so crispy.
GINA: And the inside is juicy. What's that flavor? It's a little sweet.
TASHA: Raisins. Just a few. And olives. Sweet and salty together.
EDDIE: Raisins? In meat? That's... interesting.
TASHA: Interesting good, or interesting bad?
EDDIE: Let me take another bite... Interesting good. Very good.
GINA: I give it a nine. Maybe a nine and a half.
MARCUS: Ten. My grandmother would be jealous. Don't tell her.
EDDIE: Eight. I prefer meat without fruit. But I'd eat ten of these.
TOMMY: Okay. Empanadas, a winner. Next, the fried pickles.
GINA: Fried pickles? I've never tried them.
MARCUS: You've never had fried pickles? Where did you grow up?
GINA: Right here! Upstairs! My dad never made them.
TOMMY: Because they're a Southern thing. I'm from Brooklyn.
GINA: Hm. They're... sour. And salty. And crunchy.
MARCUS: That's the point. Dip it in the ranch.
GINA: Oh. With the sauce, it's much better. A seven.
EDDIE: These are too sour for me. Pickles should be cold. Not hot.
TASHA: What number, Eddie?
EDDIE: A five. I'd rather have a plain pickle. From the jar.
MARCUS: I love them. They're perfect with beer. Eight.
TOMMY: Good for business. Salty food means thirsty people.
GINA: That's very smart, Dad. And a little evil.
TOMMY: That's the bar business, sweetheart.
TASHA: Number three. Honey-hot wings.
MARCUS: Now we're talking. Let me try... Oh. Oh, that's spicy.
GINA: Spicy? Really? I don't feel anything... Wait. Oh my God. Water!
TASHA: It comes later. That's the trick. Sweet first, then fire.
EDDIE: I'll try one. A small one.
TOMMY: Careful, Eddie. Your heart.
EDDIE: My heart survived the Mets for sixty years. It can survive a wing.
MARCUS: Look at his face. Eddie, you're turning red.
GINA: Here, Eddie. Milk. Milk helps.
EDDIE: Don't tell Dolores. She says milk is bad for cats.
MARCUS: You're not a cat, Eddie.
EDDIE: Tonight, I'm not sure what I am.
TASHA: Okay. Numbers. Wings?
MARCUS: Nine. Hot, but I can't stop.
GINA: Six. Too spicy for me. Maybe less hot sauce, more honey?
EDDIE: Three. I can't feel my tongue.
TOMMY: So it's too hot. Okay. We make two versions. Mild and hot.
TASHA: Good idea. The "Eddie" and the "Marcus."
EDDIE: I want my name on the mild one. That's fine.
TASHA: And the last one. Mozzarella sticks. But with Wisconsin cheese, not Italian.
GINA: Do people notice the difference?
TOMMY: That's the question. Taste. Tell me.
MARCUS: They taste... like mozzarella sticks. Good. Normal.
GINA: They're a little less creamy than before. But it's fine.
EDDIE: I like these better than the pickles. Seven.
TOMMY: Seven is good. Nobody misses the Italian cheese?
GINA: Honestly, Dad, nobody can tell. Inside fried bread, cheese is cheese.
TOMMY: Don't tell your nonna in heaven you said that.

[Scene: After the tasting. Tommy and Tasha at the chalkboard.]

MARCUS: So what are the prices, Tommy?
TOMMY: Empanadas, three for ten. Fried pickles, eight. Wings, fourteen.
GINA: Fourteen for wings? They were twelve last year.
TOMMY: Chicken went up. Everything went up. I have to.
MARCUS: You should sell these on the delivery apps. People would order them every night.
TOMMY: The apps take thirty percent. Thirty! For what? For a guy on a bike.
MARCUS: Hey, my customers are those guys on bikes.
TOMMY: I love the guys. I hate the apps. The guys don't get the thirty percent.
MARCUS: That's true. They get almost nothing.
GINA: Dad, let me do the math. If an empanada costs a dollar to make...
TOMMY: A dollar ten. With the oil and the gas.
GINA: A dollar ten. And you sell three for ten dollars. That's about three thirty each.
TASHA: So we make two dollars on each one. That's good, right?
GINA: That's very good. Better than the wings. Chicken is expensive.
TOMMY: So empanadas are the best business. Better than my wings. My famous wings.
GINA: Sorry, Dad. Numbers don't lie.
TOMMY: Okay. Final menu. Tasha, the board.
TASHA: Empanadas, yes. Pickles, yes. Wings, mild and hot. Mozzarella sticks, yes.
EDDIE: And the guacamole?
TOMMY: Guacamole is gone. Rest in peace.
MARCUS: Wow. That's sad. I liked the guacamole more than the pickles.
TOMMY: Then pay twenty dollars for it.
MARCUS: I'd rather eat pickles.
GINA: I'd rather eat empanadas. All day.
TASHA: So my abuela wins. Against tariffs.
EDDIE: Abuela beats tariffs. Put that on the board.
TOMMY: "Abuela's Empanadas. Three for ten." How's that?
TASHA: Perfect. She'd be so proud. She'd also ask for her cut.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **invoice** — 거래 명세서, 청구서.
- **Now you're talking (business).** — "이제야 말이 통하네." 마음에 드는 제안을 들었을 때.
- **I like the sound of that.** — "그거 듣기 좋네, 마음에 들어."
- **It better be good.** — "맛있어야 할 거야." (It had better be…)
- **맛 표현** — *crispy*(바삭한), *crunchy*(아삭한), *juicy*(즙이 많은), *creamy*(크림처럼 부드러운), *sour*(신), *salty*(짠), *sweet*, *spicy / hot*(매운), *mild*(순한).
- **Interesting good, or interesting bad?** — interesting은 맛 평가에서 애매한 칭찬일 때가 많다. 그래서 타샤가 되묻는다.
- **취향 비교** — *I prefer meat without fruit.* / *I'd rather have a plain pickle.* / *I like these better than the pickles.* / *I liked the guacamole more than the pickles.*
- **That's the point.** — "바로 그게 핵심이야."
- **a Southern thing** — 미국 남부 문화.
- **Three for ten.** — "세 개에 10달러." 묶음 가격.
- **Numbers don't lie.** — "숫자는 거짓말 안 해."
- **Rest in peace.** — "고이 잠들라." 메뉴가 없어진 것을 장례처럼 말하는 농담.
- **her cut** — 그녀의 몫(수익 배분).
- **projects** — 공공임대주택 단지를 가리키는 구어.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- **형용사 강세로 감정 싣기**: *That's **really** good. / **So** crispy. / That's **very** hot.* 강조어(really, so, very)를 크게, 길게.
- **let me**는 [레미]로 붙는다: *Let me try* [레미 츄라이], *Let me see* [레미 씨], *let me do the math* [레미 두 더 매쓰].
- **empanadas** [엠파**나**다스], **sofrito** [소**프리**토], **abuela** [아**부**엘라] — 스페인어 단어는 원어 강세대로.
- **mozzarella** [마처**렐**라] — 미국식은 [마쩌렐라]에 가깝게.
- **I'd rather** [아이드 래더r] — I'd를 분명하게. *I rather*로 잘못 말하지 않도록.
- 매운 윙 장면: "Oh. Oh, that's spicy." → 처음은 놀람, 두 번째는 숨 막히듯. 지나의 "Water!"는 짧고 높게.

에디: 난 이게 피클보다 좋아. 7점.
토미: 7점이면 괜찮지. 이탈리아 치즈 아쉬워하는 사람 없어?
지나: 솔직히, 아빠, 아무도 몰라요. 튀긴 빵 속에 들어가면 치즈는 그냥 치즈예요.
토미: 하늘에 계신 할머니한텐 그 말 하지 마라.
마커스: 그래서 가격은요, 토미?
토미: 엠파나다 세 개에 10달러. 피클 튀김 8달러. 윙 14달러.
지나: 윙이 14달러요? 작년엔 12달러였잖아요.
토미: 닭값이 올랐어. 다 올랐어. 어쩔 수 없어.
마커스: 이거 배달 앱에서 팔아요. 사람들이 매일 밤 시킬 거예요.
토미: 앱은 30%를 떼 가. 30%! 뭐 때문에? 자전거 타는 사람 한 명 때문에.
마커스: 저기요, 그 자전거 타는 사람들이 제 손님이에요.
토미: 그 사람들은 좋아. 앱이 싫은 거지. 그 30%는 그 사람들한테 안 가잖아.
마커스: 그건 맞아요. 그 사람들은 거의 아무것도 못 받아요.
지나: 아빠, 계산해 볼게요. 엠파나다 하나 만드는 데 1달러면…
토미: 1달러 10센트. 기름값이랑 가스비까지 하면.
지나: 1달러 10센트. 그리고 세 개에 10달러에 팔면. 하나에 3달러 30센트쯤이네요.
타샤: 그럼 하나에 2달러씩 남는 거네. 그거 좋은 거지?
지나: 아주 좋아요. 윙보다 나아요. 닭은 비싸니까요.
토미: 그러니까 엠파나다가 제일 남는 장사라고. 내 윙보다. 내 유명한 윙보다.
지나: 미안해요, 아빠. 숫자는 거짓말 안 해요.
토미: 좋아. 최종 메뉴. 타샤, 칠판.
타샤: 엠파나다, 확정. 피클, 확정. 윙, 순한 맛이랑 매운맛. 모차렐라 스틱, 확정.

토미: 과카몰리는 끝났어요. 고이 잠들라.
마커스: 와. 슬프다. 전 피클보다 과카몰리가 더 좋았는데.
토미: 그럼 20달러 내고 먹든가.
마커스: 차라리 피클 먹을게요.
지나: 전 차라리 엠파나다 먹을래요. 하루 종일.
타샤: 그러니까 우리 할머니가 이겼네. 관세를 상대로.

토미: "아부엘라의 엠파나다. 세 개에 10달러." 어때?
타샤: 완벽해. 할머니가 엄청 자랑스러워하실 거야. 그리고 몫도 달라고 하시겠지.
