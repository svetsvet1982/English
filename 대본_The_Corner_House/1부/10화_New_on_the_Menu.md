# THE CORNER HOUSE — 1부 10화

## New on the Menu (새 메뉴)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 1부 10화 · 시기: 2026년 4월, 수요일 오후 · 난이도: A2~B1 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 바 The Hook 주방 → 바 테이블(시식회)
> - 등장인물: TOMMY, TASHA, GINA, MARCUS, EDDIE
> - 사건: 수입 치즈와 올리브유값이 올라 메뉴를 바꾸기로 한다. 타샤 할머니의 엠파나다, 피클 튀김, 허니핫 윙, 미국산 치즈 모차렐라 스틱을 놓고 시식회를 연다.
> - 학습 포인트: 음식 어휘, 맛 표현(crispy, juicy, sour, spicy, creamy), 취향 비교(I'd rather / I prefer / I like X better than Y)
> - 미국 현안: 관세로 오른 수입 식재료, 배달 앱 수수료
> - 연결: 타샤는 레드훅 공공주택(Red Hook Houses)에서 자란 푸에르토리코계. 윙 12→14달러. 'Abuela's Empanadas'가 칠판에 오른다.

## 대본

[Scene: Wednesday, 2:00 p.m. The Hook's small kitchen. Tommy is reading a bill.]

TASHA: Tommy, what's wrong? You look like the Mets lost again.
TOMMY: It's worse than that. Look at this invoice from my food supplier.
TASHA: Let me see. Parmesan cheese... Wait. That's twenty percent more than last month.
TOMMY: It's twenty-two percent. The cheese comes from Italy, so it's tariffs again.
TASHA: And what about the olive oil?
TOMMY: That's from Italy too, so it's also more expensive. And so are the avocados from Mexico.
TASHA: So does that mean no more guacamole?
TOMMY: Guacamole is a luxury now, just like caviar.
TASHA: What about the mozzarella sticks? People really love those.
TOMMY: The cheese is from Wisconsin, so that's fine. But the breadcrumbs went up too.
TASHA: Breadcrumbs? Why would breadcrumbs go up?
TOMMY: I don't know. Everything goes up, and nobody knows why.
TASHA: Okay, so let's change the menu. We can cook things that are cheaper.
TOMMY: Like what? People come here for the wings and the burgers.
TASHA: Keep the wings, and add something new. Something cheap to make, but really good.
TOMMY: You have an idea, don't you? I can see it on your face.
TASHA: Empanadas, from my grandmother's recipe. Beef, onions, olives, and a little sofrito.
TOMMY: Your abuela's empanadas? The ones from the Red Hook Houses?
TASHA: The famous ones. Everybody in the projects wanted them. She sold them from her kitchen window.
TOMMY: Are they expensive to make?
TASHA: Not at all. You need flour, ground beef, onions, and peppers. Maybe a dollar each.
TOMMY: And we sell them for... four dollars each?
TASHA: Three for ten. People like a good deal.
TOMMY: Okay, I like it. What else have you got?
TASHA: Fried pickles. They're cheap, they're salty, and people drink more beer with them.
TOMMY: Salty food means more beer. Now you're talking business.
TASHA: And maybe a new wing sauce, too. Honey and hot sauce, so it's sweet and spicy.
TOMMY: Honey-hot wings. I like the sound of that.
TASHA: So let's do a tasting tonight, before we put anything on the board.
TOMMY: A tasting? And who's going to taste it?
TASHA: The experts, of course. Gina, Marcus, and Eddie. It's free food, so they'll come.
TOMMY: Free food? Of course they'll come.

[Scene: 5:00 p.m. A table near the window. Four plates.]

GINA: Okay, Dad. I left work early for this. It better be good.
MARCUS: And I closed the shop for this. It better be very good.
EDDIE: I didn't leave anything. I was already sitting here.
TASHA: Okay, everybody, here are the rules. Taste everything and give it a number from one to ten.
TOMMY: And be honest. But don't be too honest.
GINA: Dad, you just said "be honest."
TOMMY: I said "not too honest." There's a big difference.
TASHA: First, we have the empanadas. Be careful, because they're hot.
MARCUS: Oh, wow. That's really good. The outside is so crispy.
GINA: And the inside is juicy. What's that flavor? It's a little bit sweet.
TASHA: That's raisins, just a few. And olives, so it's sweet and salty together.
EDDIE: Raisins? In meat? Well, that's... interesting.
TASHA: Is that interesting good, or interesting bad?
EDDIE: Let me take another bite... Interesting good. Actually, it's very good.
GINA: I give it a nine. Maybe even a nine and a half.
MARCUS: Ten. My grandmother would be jealous, so don't tell her.
EDDIE: Eight. I prefer meat without fruit. But I'd still eat ten of these.
TOMMY: Okay, so the empanadas are a winner. Next, we have the fried pickles.
GINA: Fried pickles? I've never tried them before.
MARCUS: You've never had fried pickles? Where did you grow up?
GINA: Right here, upstairs! My dad never made them for me.
TOMMY: That's because they're a Southern thing. I'm from Brooklyn.
GINA: Hm. They're... sour. And salty, and crunchy too.
MARCUS: That's the point. Here, dip it in the ranch.
GINA: Oh. With the sauce, it's much better. I'll give it a seven.
EDDIE: These are too sour for me. Pickles should be cold, not hot.
TASHA: So what number do you give it, Eddie?
EDDIE: A five. I'd rather have a plain pickle, right from the jar.
MARCUS: Well, I love them. They're perfect with beer. Eight from me.
TOMMY: They're good for business. Salty food means thirsty people.
GINA: That's very smart, Dad. And it's also a little evil.
TOMMY: That's the bar business, sweetheart.
TASHA: Okay, number three is the honey-hot wings.
MARCUS: Now we're talking. Let me try one... Oh. Oh, that's spicy.
GINA: Spicy? Really? I don't feel anything at all... Wait. Oh my God. Water!
TASHA: The heat comes later. That's the trick. It's sweet first, and then it's fire.
EDDIE: I'll try one too. Just a small one, please.
TOMMY: Careful, Eddie. Think about your heart.
EDDIE: My heart survived the Mets for sixty years. It can survive one wing.
MARCUS: Look at his face. Eddie, you're turning red, man.
GINA: Here, Eddie, drink some milk. Milk helps with the heat.
EDDIE: Just don't tell Dolores. She says milk is bad for cats.
MARCUS: But you're not a cat, Eddie.
EDDIE: Tonight, I'm not sure what I am anymore.
TASHA: Okay, numbers, please. What about the wings?
MARCUS: Nine. They're hot, but I can't stop eating them.
GINA: Six. They're too spicy for me. Maybe use less hot sauce and more honey?
EDDIE: Three. I can't feel my tongue anymore.
TOMMY: So they're too hot for some people. Okay, we'll make two versions, mild and hot.
TASHA: Good idea. We'll call them the "Eddie" and the "Marcus."
EDDIE: I want my name on the mild one. That's fine with me.
TASHA: And here's the last one, mozzarella sticks. But with Wisconsin cheese, not Italian.
GINA: Do people really notice the difference?
TOMMY: That's the big question. So taste them and tell me.
MARCUS: They taste... like mozzarella sticks. They're good, just normal.
GINA: They're a little less creamy than before. But it's fine.
EDDIE: I like these better than the pickles. I'll give them a seven.
TOMMY: Seven is good. So nobody misses the Italian cheese?
GINA: Honestly, Dad, nobody can tell. Inside fried bread, cheese is just cheese.
TOMMY: Well, don't tell your nonna in heaven you said that.

[Scene: After the tasting. Tommy and Tasha at the chalkboard.]

MARCUS: So what are the prices going to be, Tommy?
TOMMY: Empanadas are three for ten. Fried pickles are eight, and wings are fourteen.
GINA: Fourteen dollars for wings? They were only twelve last year.
TOMMY: Chicken went up. Everything went up, so I have to.
MARCUS: You should sell these on the delivery apps. People would order them every night.
TOMMY: The apps take thirty percent. Thirty! And for what? For a guy on a bike.
MARCUS: Hey, those guys on bikes are my customers.
TOMMY: I love the guys. It's the apps I hate. The guys don't get the thirty percent.
MARCUS: That's true. They get almost nothing.
GINA: Dad, let me do the math. If an empanada costs a dollar to make...
TOMMY: It's a dollar ten, when you add the oil and the gas.
GINA: Okay, a dollar ten. And you sell three for ten dollars. That's about three thirty each.
TASHA: So we make two dollars on each one. That's good, right?
GINA: That's very good. It's better than the wings, because chicken is expensive.
TOMMY: So the empanadas are the best business, better than my wings. My famous wings!
GINA: Sorry, Dad. Numbers don't lie.
TOMMY: Okay, let's make the final menu. Tasha, the board, please.
TASHA: Empanadas, yes. Pickles, yes. Wings, both mild and hot. And mozzarella sticks, yes.
EDDIE: And what about the guacamole?
TOMMY: The guacamole is gone, Eddie. Rest in peace.
MARCUS: Wow, that's sad. I liked the guacamole more than the pickles.
TOMMY: Then you can pay twenty dollars for it.
MARCUS: No thanks. I'd rather eat pickles.
GINA: And I'd rather eat empanadas, all day long.
TASHA: So my abuela wins. She beat the tariffs.
EDDIE: Abuela beats tariffs! Put that on the board too.
TOMMY: "Abuela's Empanadas. Three for ten." How's that?
TASHA: It's perfect. She'd be so proud. She'd also ask for her cut.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **invoice** — 거래 명세서, 청구서.
- **Now you're talking (business).** — "이제야 말이 통하네." 마음에 드는 제안을 들었을 때.
- **I like the sound of that.** — "그거 듣기 좋네, 마음에 들어."
- **It better be good.** — "맛있어야 할 거야." (It had better be…)
- **맛 표현** — *crispy*(바삭한), *crunchy*(아삭한), *juicy*(즙이 많은), *creamy*(크림처럼 부드러운), *sour*(신), *salty*(짠), *sweet*, *spicy / hot*(매운), *mild*(순한).
- **Interesting good, or interesting bad?** — interesting은 맛 평가에서 애매한 칭찬일 때가 많다. 그래서 타샤가 되묻는다.
- **취향 비교** — *I prefer meat without fruit.* / *I'd rather have a plain pickle.* / *I like these better than the pickles.* / *I liked the guacamole more than the pickles.*
- **That's the point.** — "바로 그게 핵심이야."
- **a Southern thing** — 미국 남부 문화.
- **Three for ten.** — "세 개에 10달러." 묶음 가격.
- **Numbers don't lie.** — "숫자는 거짓말 안 해."
- **Rest in peace.** — "고이 잠들라." 메뉴가 없어진 것을 장례처럼 말하는 농담.
- **her cut** — 그녀의 몫(수익 배분).
- **projects** — 공공임대주택 단지를 가리키는 구어.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- **형용사 강세로 감정 싣기**: *That's **really** good. / **So** crispy. / That's **very** hot.* 강조어(really, so, very)를 크게, 길게.
- **let me**는 [레미]로 붙는다: *Let me try* [레미 츄라이], *Let me see* [레미 씨], *let me do the math* [레미 두 더 매쓰].
- **empanadas** [엠파**나**다스], **sofrito** [소**프리**토], **abuela** [아**부**엘라] — 스페인어 단어는 원어 강세대로.
- **mozzarella** [마처**렐**라] — 미국식은 [마쩌렐라]에 가깝게.
- **I'd rather** [아이드 래더r] — I'd를 분명하게. *I rather*로 잘못 말하지 않도록.
- 매운 윙 장면: "Oh. Oh, that's spicy." → 처음은 놀람, 두 번째는 숨 막히듯. 지나의 "Water!"는 짧고 높게.
