## Replaced by a Chatbot (챗봇에게 밀려나다)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 3부 10화(통산 46화) · 시기: 2026년 10월 중순, 화요일 · 난이도: B1~B2 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 영상통화(고와너스의 제시 방) → 오후, 바 The Hook
> - 등장인물: SARAH(인사 담당), JESSE, TASHA, TOMMY, MARCUS, TESS
> - 사건: 제시의 2년짜리 마케팅 계약이 2주 통보로 끝난다. 이유는 'AI 콘텐츠 전략'. 제시는 바에서 친구들의 도움을 받아 실업수당을 신청한다. 마커스는 1099 자영업자에게는 실업수당도 병가도 없고 세금은 두 배라고 털어놓는다. 토미와 동네 가게들이 제시에게 작은 일거리를 준다.
> - 학습 포인트: 고용 형태 어휘(contract worker, staffing agency, payroll, self-employed, independent contractor), 서류 질문과 답하기, 불안 표현
> - 미국 현안: 생성형 AI로 인한 사무직 대체, 뉴욕주 실업수당(평균 주급의 약 절반, 최대 26주, 부분 수급, 구직 기록), 자영업자의 자영업세(약 15%)와 분기별 예상세, 오른 개인 보험료
> - 연결: 마지막 근무일 10월 31일. 바텐더 일은 계속(주 12시간). 동네 일거리: 더 훅 인스타그램, Bell Bikes 웹사이트, 아메드 가게 간판. 제시의 '숨은 농담' 습관.

## 대본

[Scene: Tuesday, 11:00 a.m. Jesse's room in Gowanus. A video call on his laptop.]

SARAH: Hi, Jesse, and thanks for joining on such short notice. Can you hear me okay?
JESSE: Hi, Sarah. Yes, I can hear you fine. Is something wrong, or is this a normal check-in? This meeting wasn't on my calendar until twenty minutes ago.
SARAH: I'll get right to it. I'm sorry to tell you this, but your contract will not be renewed. Your last day is October thirty-first.
JESSE: Not renewed? But... my manager told me my work was great. That was just last month.
SARAH: Your work has been great, and everyone on the team knows it. This isn't about performance.
JESSE: Then what is it about, if it's not about my work?
SARAH: The company is moving to a new content strategy. Most of the social media posts and emails will be generated with AI tools from now on.
JESSE: Generated with AI. So, basically, my job.
SARAH: The marketing team will keep one person to review and edit the AI content. That role is already filled.
JESSE: By who? Can I ask, or is that a secret?
SARAH: I'm afraid I can't share that information with you.
JESSE: Okay. Wow. Okay. How long have you known about this?
SARAH: The decision was made last week. I'm sorry for the short notice. I know it's not fair to you.
JESSE: Two weeks' notice for a two-year contract. That's one week for every year.
SARAH: I understand this is difficult. You'll receive your final paycheck on November fifteenth, and it will include your unused vacation days.
JESSE: Do I get any severance, or is that just for full-time people?
SARAH: As a contract worker, you don't get severance. I'm really sorry about that.
JESSE: Am I... am I allowed to apply for unemployment, at least?
SARAH: You were employed through our staffing agency, so you were on their payroll. That means you should be eligible. The agency will send you a form this week.
JESSE: A form. Okay. I'm sure it's a very nice form.
SARAH: Is there anything else I can answer for you right now?
JESSE: Yeah, I have one question. Did the AI write the script that you're reading for this call?
SARAH: ...No. I wrote it. I'm sorry, Jesse. Honestly, I don't like this either, and I've had to make six of these calls this week.
JESSE: Thanks for being honest with me. At least a human told me, and not an email.
SARAH: Good luck, Jesse. I mean it. If you need a reference for your next job, I'll be happy to give you one.
JESSE: I'll take it. Thank you, Sarah. You've always been kind to me.
SARAH: Take care of yourself.
JESSE: You too. And Sarah? Be careful, because if they can replace me, you might be next.
SARAH: I know. Believe me, I've already thought about it. I know.
JESSE: Goodbye.

JESSE: I got replaced. By a chatbot.
TOMMY: What? Who replaced you? Some kid from the internet?

MARCUS: Man. That's cold. How much notice did they give you?
JESSE: Two weeks. My last day is October thirty-first, which is Halloween. Very scary.
TASHA: Oh, honey. Sit down before you fall down. Tommy, give him something to drink.
TOMMY: Coffee or beer?
JESSE: Beer. Your coffee would finish me.
TOMMY: Even in tragedy, they insult my coffee. I can't believe it.
MARCUS: So what are you going to do now? Do you have a plan?
JESSE: I'm going to apply for unemployment today, before I start to panic. Marisol told me to do it in the first week, because the money takes time.
TASHA: Then do it here, with us. We'll help you, and you won't feel so alone.
JESSE: Okay. The state website. Let me log in... "Create an account." Of course I need a new account.
MARCUS: Here we go. Now come the forms, and the forms never end.
JESSE: "Question one. Were you employed by a company in New York State in the last eighteen months?" Yes, sadly.
TASHA: That one was easy, so that's one down.
JESSE: "Name of your employer." Wait, is it the company I worked for, or the staffing agency?
TOMMY: Who paid you? Whose name was printed on the paycheck every two weeks?
JESSE: The agency's name was on it. Bright Talent Partners.
TOMMY: Then that's your employer, at least on paper. That's the one the state cares about.
JESSE: "Reason for separation." The options are "Quit," "Fired," "Lack of work," or "Other."
MARCUS: Lack of work. Definitely, because they took the work away.
TASHA: Not "fired," because you didn't do anything wrong, and don't forget that.
JESSE: "Lack of work." Because the work went to a robot.
TOMMY: Write that down exactly. "Lack of work, because of a robot."
JESSE: There's no box for robots, Tommy, because I already checked.
TASHA: There should be. Somebody should write to the governor.
JESSE: "Are you currently working anywhere?" Hm. I work here on weekends, so do I say yes?
TOMMY: Say yes. Be honest. Always be honest with the government, because they always find out.
MARCUS: If you lie and they catch it, they make you pay it all back, plus a penalty.
JESSE: Okay. "Yes. Part-time. About twelve hours a week."
TASHA: Will that reduce the money they give you?
JESSE: It says I'd get partial benefits. If I work a few hours, they reduce it a little, but I still get some.
MARCUS: That's fair, actually, and I didn't expect the government to be fair.
JESSE: "Your weekly benefit amount is about half of your average weekly wage." Half. So that's about four hundred a week for me.
TOMMY: Four hundred a week. That's not a lot in this city. For how long?
JESSE: Up to twenty-six weeks, which is six months, as long as I'm looking for work.
TASHA: Six months to find something new. That's not bad, honey.

MARCUS: Records and notebooks. Dolores would love this.
JESSE: Okay. Submit. Done. "Your claim has been received." That's it, I think.
TASHA: See? You did it, and you're still alive.
JESSE: Barely. But Marcus, you're lucky, because you're your own boss. Nobody can replace you with AI.
MARCUS: Lucky? Ha. Let me tell you a little about lucky.
JESSE: What do you mean? You own the shop.
MARCUS: I'm a ten-ninety-nine, which means I'm self-employed. If my shop closes, guess what I get from unemployment?
JESSE: What?
MARCUS: Nothing. Zero. Independent contractors can't get it, because I don't pay into the system that way.
TASHA: Nothing at all? Not even a little?
MARCUS: Nothing. No unemployment, no sick days, and no paid vacation. If I'm sick, the shop is closed, and if the shop is closed, there's no money.
TOMMY: It's the same for me with the bar. I'm the owner, so there's no unemployment for me either.
JESSE: I didn't know that. I always thought that being your own boss was... freedom.
MARCUS: It is freedom. Freedom to pay all your own taxes.
TASHA: What do you mean, all your own taxes? Everybody has to pay taxes.
MARCUS: When you're an employee, the company pays half of your Social Security and Medicare taxes. When you're self-employed, you pay both halves. That's about fifteen percent, before income tax.
JESSE: Fifteen percent! And then you pay income tax on top of that?
MARCUS: Plus income tax. And I have to pay every three months, which they call estimated taxes. If I forget a payment, I pay a penalty.
TOMMY: The next one is January fifteenth. I only know that because I always forget it.
MARCUS: Last year I forgot, and I got a two-hundred-dollar penalty. Two hundred dollars, just for forgetting a date.
TASHA: That's a lot of math for a guy who fixes bikes.
MARCUS: That's why I save thirty percent of everything in a separate account. I don't touch it, ever. That's the tax account.
JESSE: Thirty percent. Of everything you make?
MARCUS: Of everything. Every repair and every tire. Thirty cents of every dollar goes straight to the tax account.
TOMMY: Smart. That's smarter than me, because I use the shoebox.
MARCUS: And health insurance? I pay that myself too, just like you, Jesse. Yours is four-fifty, right? Mine just went up to five-twenty.
JESSE: Five-twenty a month! Marcus...
MARCUS: So don't call me lucky. Call me tired.
JESSE: Sorry, man. I really didn't know any of that.
MARCUS: It's okay. Nobody knows. Everybody thinks small business owners are rich, but most of us are just tired.

JESSE: Hey. I'm okay. Not great, but okay. I already applied for unemployment, so that's done.
TESS: Good, because that's step one. I'm so sorry, Jesse. Losing your job to a chatbot is horrible.
JESSE: You know the worst part? The AI is going to write about the water bottles with no love and no jokes.
TESS: Wait, your posts had jokes?
JESSE: Every post had one joke, and it was hidden. Nobody ever noticed it, but I knew it was there.
TESS: That's exactly what a robot can't do. It can't hide a joke just for nobody.
TOMMY: She's right, and you know what I think? You're a good writer, so use it.
JESSE: How, though, when every company is using AI now? Nobody needs writers anymore.
TOMMY: Not every company, and not the small ones. The Hook needs an Instagram, a real one. Gina started it, but nobody ever posts anything.
MARCUS: Bell Bikes needs a website too. Mine is from twenty twenty, and it looks like a broken phone.
TASHA: And Ahmed needs a sign, a good one, because his spelling is terrible.

TOMMY: Small jobs, and small money. But they're for real people with real stories, not water bottles.

JESSE: Thirty percent. Got it. I'll start a tax account tomorrow.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **해고 통보 표현(격식)** — *I'll get right to it. / Your contract will not be renewed. / This isn't about performance. / The decision was made last week. / I'm sorry for the short notice. / That role is already filled.*
- **고용 형태** — *a contract worker*(계약직), *a staffing agency*(인력 파견 회사), *on their payroll*(그 회사가 급여를 주는), *an employee*(직원), *self-employed / an independent contractor / a ten-ninety-nine*(자영업자 / 독립 계약자 / 1099 세금 양식을 받는 사람).
- **서류 질문 읽기** — *Name of your employer. / Reason for separation.*(퇴직 사유) *Quit / Fired / Lack of work.*(자진 퇴사 / 해고 / 일감 부족) / *Are you currently working anywhere?*
- **실업수당 어휘** — *apply for unemployment, be eligible*(자격이 있다), *a claim*(청구), *partial benefits*(부분 수급), *weekly benefit amount*, *search for work and keep a record*.
- **세금 어휘** — *Social Security and Medicare taxes*(사회보장세·메디케어세), *income tax*, *estimated taxes*(분기별 예상세), *a penalty*(가산세).
- **That's cold.** — "냉정하네, 너무하네."
- **Your coffee would finish me.** — "토미 커피 마시면 끝장날 거예요." 농담.
- **I'll take it.** — "(추천서) 받을게요."

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 세라의 통보는 미리 써 둔 대본을 읽듯 일정한 톤. *...No. I wrote it.*에서 처음으로 사람 목소리가 나온다.
- 제시는 충격으로 짧게 반복한다: *Okay. Wow. Okay.* — 단어 사이 쉼을 길게.
- **ten-ninety-nine** [텐 나인티 나인], **eligible** [**엘**리저블], **separation** [세퍼**레**이션], **estimated** [**에**스티메이티드].
- 서류 질문은 문서 읽는 톤으로 따옴표처럼, 대답은 구어로 툭.
- 마커스의 *Freedom to pay all your own taxes.*는 씁쓸한 웃음으로.
- 마지막 대사는 다시 기운을 차린 제시의 목소리. *With hidden jokes.*에서 미소.
