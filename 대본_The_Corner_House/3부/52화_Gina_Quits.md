## 대본

[Scene: Thursday, 10:00 a.m. A small glass meeting room at Brightline, Midtown. Craig has a laptop open.]

CRAIG: Thanks for coming in, Gina. This will be quick. Quick-ish, anyway, because there's a lot to cover.
GINA: Quick-ish. Okay. In my experience, that's never a good sign, Craig.
CRAIG: No, it's a good thing, I promise. It's a big project, and Brad picked you for it personally.
GINA: All right, I'm listening. What is it?
CRAIG: We're redesigning the whole team. Brad calls it "agent-first." The AI agents do the first draft of everything, and the humans just review.
GINA: But we already do that, Craig. We've been doing it since September.
CRAIG: This is the next step. Brad wants a plan by December, and he wants it on one page. He says you write the best one-pagers in the company now.
GINA: That's funny, because in July he complained that I wrote four pages.
CRAIG: Well, you learned, and that's growth. Anyway, about the plan. The agents will handle the design mockups, the images, and the layouts.
GINA: Wait. Design? That's Lauren's job.
CRAIG: Lauren's role will... change a little.
GINA: Change into what, Craig? Be specific, please.
CRAIG: Into nothing, probably. In the plan, design is fully automated by March, so there's no design role after that.
GINA: So you want me to write the plan that removes Lauren from the company.
CRAIG: I want you to write a plan for the future. Lauren is part of the present, and the present doesn't last forever.
GINA: She has a one-hour commute, Craig, and that's each way. She's done it for five years, and she's the best designer I've ever worked with.
CRAIG: I know that. I like Lauren too, honestly. But this isn't personal, Gina.
GINA: It is personal. Maybe not for you, but for Lauren it's very personal.
CRAIG: Look, if you don't write it, someone else will. At least if you write it, you'll do it fairly.
GINA: That's exactly what you said about Omar. "At least we'll do it fairly." Then he got fifteen minutes with HR, and his laptop was locked before lunch.
CRAIG: Come on, that's not fair, Gina.
GINA: Maybe not, but it's true. I'm sorry, Craig. I won't write it.
CRAIG: You won't write it. Do you understand what that means, especially with Brad?
GINA: I understand. Actually, Craig, I have something to say, and I've been thinking about it for weeks.
CRAIG: Okay. Go ahead. I'm listening.
GINA: I'm giving my two weeks' notice. My last day will be November twenty-fifth. That's the day before Thanksgiving.
CRAIG: Wait, what? You're quitting? You're quitting because of one plan?
GINA: Not because of one plan. It's because of the last six months, and the plan is just the last straw.
CRAIG: Gina, slow down. Let's talk about this like adults. Is it the money?
GINA: No, it's not the money.
CRAIG: Is it the five days in the office? Because I think I can get you Fridays at home. I can at least ask.
GINA: It's not the Fridays either. I should have said this in September. Honestly, I should have said it when Omar left.
CRAIG: Do you have another job lined up already?
GINA: Yes. I accepted an offer yesterday from a small nonprofit in Downtown Brooklyn. It's called Open Block.
CRAIG: Open Block? I've never heard of it, and I've heard of everybody.
GINA: Nobody has. They build simple tech for libraries, tenant groups, and food pantries, and there are only twelve people.
CRAIG: Twelve people. Gina, you lead product at a company with four hundred employees.
GINA: I lead product at a company that's trying very hard to have forty.
CRAIG: Wow, that was honest. You were always honest, though. Brad says you're too honest.
BRAD: Knock knock. Craig, Gina. Is this a bad time? I could see your faces through the glass, and they didn't look happy.
CRAIG: Brad, Gina just gave her notice.
BRAD: You're kidding me. Gina, no. You're one of our best people, and everybody knows it.
GINA: Thank you, Brad. I really appreciate that.
BRAD: Is there anything we can do to change your mind? Seriously, anything at all. Name it.
GINA: I don't think so, Brad. I've made my decision.
BRAD: Fifteen percent. A raise. Starting January. And a new title, too, which is Senior Director of Product.
CRAIG: That's a really good offer, Gina. You should think about it before you say anything.
GINA: It is a good offer, and I appreciate it. But my answer is still no.
BRAD: Can I ask why? Honestly, between us?
GINA: Honestly? You asked me to write a plan to replace a person I respect with software, and I couldn't do it.
BRAD: That's business, Gina. Every company in this city is doing it, and if we don't do it, we lose.
GINA: Maybe you're right, and maybe that's just business. But it's not my business, not anymore.
BRAD: Okay. I respect that. I don't agree. But I respect it.
GINA: That's fair, Brad. Thank you for saying it.
BRAD: Will you finish the launch before you go? We really need the December release to go well.
GINA: I'll write a full handoff document with every project and every contact, so you'll be in good hands.
BRAD: And it'll be one page, right?
GINA: Four pages, Brad. Some things need four pages, whether you like it or not.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **사직 화법**
  - *I'm giving my two weeks' notice.* — "2주 뒤 퇴사를 통보합니다." 미국 직장의 관행.
  - *My last day will be November twenty-fifth.* — "마지막 근무일은 11월 25일입니다."
  - *I accepted an offer yesterday…* — 다른 회사 제안을 수락했다.
  - *I've made my decision.* — "결정했어요." 단호하지만 공손하다.
  - *I'll write a full handoff document…* — 인수인계 문서를 쓰겠다.
- **붙잡기와 거절**
  - *Is there anything we can do to change your mind? / Name it.* — "마음을 바꿀 방법이 있을까요? 말만 해요."
  - *I appreciate it. But my answer is still no.* — 감사를 먼저, 거절은 그다음에.
  - *I respect that. I don't agree. But I respect it.* — 동의하지 않아도 존중한다.
- **후회 표현**
  - *I should have said this in September.* — 그때 말했어야 했는데 (안 했다).
  - *I could have warned Omar too.* — 경고할 수도 있었는데 (안 했다).
  - *I shouldn't have bought that jukebox…* — 사지 말았어야 했는데 (샀다).
  - *You couldn't have saved Omar, Gina.* — 네가 구할 수는 없었을 거야.
- **the last straw** — 결정타, 마지막 한 방울.
- **Quick-ish.** — "빠르게…쯤?" -ish는 '대략'.
- **off the record** — 비공식적으로. *It has to be off the record, okay?* "비공식적으로만, 알았지?"
- **put your name on the table** — 네 이름을 추천하겠다.
- **Money comes back. Years don't.** — "돈은 돌아와. 세월은 안 돌아와."

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- **should have → 'shoulda'** [슈더브/슈다]: *I should have said this* [아이 슈더 세드 디스]. *could have* → [쿠다], *shouldn't have* → [슈든어].
- 결심문은 하강 억양으로 끝을 단단히: *I've made my decision.↘ / My answer is still no.↘*
- 브래드의 제안은 빠르고 자신만만하게: *Fifteen percent. A raise. Starting January.*
- 지나가 로런에게 말할 때는 목소리를 낮춘다(*It has to be off the record, okay?*).
- 오마르의 *Money comes back. | Years don't.* — 짧은 두 문장. 사이에서 쉰다.
- 토미의 *Small regrets. | Big decisions.* — 대칭 구조, 강세는 *small*과 *big*.
