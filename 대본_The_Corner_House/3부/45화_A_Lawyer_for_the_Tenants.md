# THE CORNER HOUSE — 3부 45화

## A Lawyer for the Tenants (세입자를 위한 변호사)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 3부 9화(통산 45화) · 시기: 2026년 10월, 토요일 오전 · 난이도: B1~B2 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 2B 에스포지토 부인의 집(협회 법률 상담회)
> - 등장인물: MARISOL, MS. BROOKS(세입자 변호사), MRS. ESPOSITO, EDDIE, SAM
> - 사건: 세입자 변호사 대니얼 브룩스가 무료로 협회를 찾아온다. 임대료 안정화 세입자의 세 가지 권리, 정당 사유 퇴거 보호법, 바이아웃 제안을 서면으로 거절하면 180일간 다시 제안할 수 없다는 규칙, '시끄러운 것'과 '살 수 없는 것'의 차이를 쉬운 말로 설명한다. 살의 편지는 법적 무기는 아니지만 "이야기는 중요하다"고 말한다.
> - 학습 포인트: 법률 상담 어휘를 쉬운 말로 풀기, 2형 조건문(If they asked for twenty percent, we could… / If I were you, I would…), unless
> - 미국 현안: 임대료 안정화 권리, 정당 사유 퇴거 보호법(인상 기준 = 물가 + 5%, 최대 10%), 뉴욕시 바이아웃 규칙(거절 고지 의무, 서면 거절 후 180일 제안 금지), 공사를 이용한 괴롭힘, 2019년 이후 리모델링에 따른 인상 제한, 세입자 우선매수 제도(워싱턴 D.C.에는 있고 뉴욕은 논의 중)
> - 연결: 에스포지토 부인의 2019년 3월 14일 메모("살에게 3B 월세 얘기함. 리치에게 물어보라고 함") → 카일 사건의 증거. 거절 편지 양식, 공사 문제 기록 공유 문서. 냉장고 규칙에 "혼자 서명하지 않기" 추가. 커뮤니티 랜드 트러스트·협동조합 언급 → 56화.

## 대본

[Scene: Saturday, 10:00 a.m. Apartment 2B. Coffee on the twelve blue plates. A woman in a blazer with a thick folder.]

MARISOL: Everyone, this is Danielle Brooks. She's a tenant attorney. She's here for free. So be nice.
MS. BROOKS: Thank you, Marisol. And thank you all for having me. Please call me Danielle.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Ms. Brooks. For now.
MS. BROOKS: Ms. Brooks is fine. I've heard about you, Mrs. Esposito. The notebooks.
MRS. ESPOSITO: My reputation is growing.
EDDIE: Ms. Brooks, I'm Eddie O'Neill. Third floor. Fifty-two years. Can they kick me out?
MS. BROOKS: Straight to the point. I like that. Short answer? Not easily. Let me explain why. In plain English.
EDDIE: Plain English. Please. No Latin.
MS. BROOKS: No Latin. Promise. Mr. O'Neill, your apartment is rent-stabilized. That means three things.
EDDIE: Three things. Okay.
MS. BROOKS: One. Your rent can only go up by what the city board allows. Two. You have the right to renew your lease. Every time. Three. They can only evict you for specific reasons.
SAM: What reasons?
MS. BROOKS: Not paying rent. Serious damage. Illegal activity. Things like that. "We want to renovate" is not a reason.
EDDIE: So unless I stop paying, or I burn the building down...
MS. BROOKS: Unless something like that happens, you can stay. For the rest of your life, if you want.
EDDIE: The rest of my life. That's what I want.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And me? I'm stabilized too.
MS. BROOKS: Same for you. Same rights.
SAM: And us? Priya and me? We're market rate.
MS. BROOKS: Different, but not hopeless. Because of Good Cause Eviction. Your landlord owns more than ten units in New York. So the law applies to you.
SAM: What does it actually do? In plain English?
MS. BROOKS: When your lease ends, they can't just say "goodbye." They need a good reason. Like not paying. Or a very big rent increase they can justify.
SAM: And the rent increase? Marisol said there's a limit.
MS. BROOKS: There's a standard. Each year, it's about inflation plus five percent. Never more than ten. If they raise it more than that, you can challenge it in court. They have to prove the increase is reasonable.
SAM: So if they asked for twenty percent, we could say no?
MS. BROOKS: You could refuse. And if they tried to evict you for refusing, a judge would ask them to explain. They'd probably lose.
SAM: Probably.
MS. BROOKS: Lawyers always say "probably." Nothing is certain in court. But your position is strong.
MARISOL: Danielle, can you talk about the buyout offers? Everybody got one.
MS. BROOKS: Yes. Let me see them... Ten thousand. Twelve. Thirty-five. Forty. Okay. These are low.
EDDIE: Forty thousand is low?
MS. BROOKS: For a stabilized apartment in Red Hook? With a long-term tenant? Very low. I've seen buyouts in Brooklyn for two hundred thousand. More.
EDDIE: Two hundred thousand! For leaving?
MS. BROOKS: Because your apartment is worth a lot to them empty. At market rent, it makes them a lot of money. Your low rent costs them a lot.
MRS. ESPOSITO: So they're offering us a little, to make a lot.
MS. BROOKS: Exactly. Now, important. The city has rules about buyout offers. Did their letters say that you can refuse?
MARISOL: Let me check. Yes. In small print. At the bottom. "You may decline this offer and remain in your apartment."
MS. BROOKS: Good. They have to say that. And here's the useful part. If you refuse in writing, they can't contact you about a buyout again for one hundred eighty days.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Six months! No letters for six months?
MS. BROOKS: About buyouts, yes. Not about repairs or rent. But no buyout pressure. If they contact you anyway, that can be harassment.
EDDIE: Then I'll refuse in writing. Today. In big letters.
MS. BROOKS: If I were you, Mr. O'Neill, I would do exactly that. Unless you want to move.
EDDIE: I don't want to move. I want to die on the third floor. Sorry, Dolores.
MRS. ESPOSITO: You can die on the third floor. Just not on a Sunday. I need you to take your pills.
SAM: What about us? Should we refuse too?
MS. BROOKS: That's your choice. If you want to stay, refuse. If you're thinking about leaving, don't refuse yet. Negotiate. Never sign without a lawyer reading it.
SAM: Why? Isn't it just money for leaving?
MS. BROOKS: Buyout agreements have traps. Some say you lose your security deposit. Some say you can't talk about the agreement. Some say you leave in two weeks. Read everything.
SAM: If we negotiated, how much could we ask for?
MS. BROOKS: For market rate with Good Cause protection? Maybe three or four times what they offered. Maybe more. It depends on how much they want the apartment.
SAM: Three or four times. Forty thousand?
MS. BROOKS: It's possible. I'm not promising. But ten is a starting number. Not a final number.
MARISOL: Now, the harder question. The construction. They say twelve to eighteen months. Can they make life miserable?
MS. BROOKS: They can make it noisy. They can't make it unlivable. There's a difference.
MRS. ESPOSITO: What's the difference?
MS. BROOKS: Noisy is legal. Within permitted hours. Unlivable is not. No heat. No water. Dust everywhere. Workers in your apartment without notice. Blocking your door. Those things can be harassment.
EDDIE: And if that happens?
MS. BROOKS: You document everything. Photos. Dates. Times. You call 311. And you call me. We can go to housing court. A judge can order them to stop. And fine them.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Documents. I'm very good at documents.
MS. BROOKS: Marisol told me. She said you have notebooks from nineteen ninety-five.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And a note from twenty nineteen. When I told Sal that Three B was overcharged.
MS. BROOKS: You have that? With a date?
MRS. ESPOSITO: March fourteenth, twenty nineteen. "Told Sal about Three B rent. He said, ask Richie."
MS. BROOKS: That could be very useful. For Kyle's case. It shows the owner was told.
MARISOL: Speaking of Three B. They're renovating it right now. If the state says it's stabilized, does the renovation change that?
MS. BROOKS: Good question. Before twenty nineteen, a big renovation could raise the rent a lot. Sometimes enough to leave stabilization.
MARISOL: And now?
MS. BROOKS: Now, the increase from renovations is very limited. They can spend a hundred thousand on a new kitchen. The rent goes up only a little. It stays stabilized.
EDDIE: So their fancy kitchen... doesn't help them?
MS. BROOKS: Not much. Unless Three B was never really stabilized. That's what the complaint will decide.
SAM: So they might spend a lot of money on an apartment they can't rent at market rate.
MS. BROOKS: That's their risk. They bought the building with that risk.
MARISOL: Danielle, one more thing. Teresa Cappello gave us a letter. From her father. Sal wrote that the tenants should have the first chance to buy.
MS. BROOKS: May I see it?
MARISOL: Here. It's a copy.
MS. BROOKS: "They made it a home. I only made it a building." That's beautiful.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Is it legal? Can we use it?
MS. BROOKS: Legally? Unfortunately, no. It's not a contract. It doesn't stop a sale.
EDDIE: Then it's just paper.
MS. BROOKS: It's not just paper. In court, it's not a weapon. But in public, it's a story. And stories matter.
MARISOL: What do you mean?
MS. BROOKS: The City Council is talking about a law. Giving tenants the first chance to buy their buildings. Like in Washington, D.C. It's not a law here yet.
SAM: So if that law passed...
MS. BROOKS: If it passed, you'd have rights next time. Not this time. This building is already sold.
EDDIE: Next time. There won't be a next time for me.
MS. BROOKS: Maybe not. But Harborline might sell someday. Investors sell. And when they sell, you want to be ready.
MARISOL: Ready how?
MS. BROOKS: Organized. With money. With partners. Some nonprofits help tenants buy buildings. Land trusts. Co-ops. It's hard. But it happens.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Tenants buying a building. Richie said that's impossible.
MS. BROOKS: Richie also stopped registering apartments. Richie isn't an expert on what's possible.
EDDIE: Ha! I like this lawyer.
MS. BROOKS: Thank you, Mr. O'Neill. So, homework. I'll send a refusal letter template. For anyone who wants to stay.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Homework! Finally, someone who gives homework.
MS. BROOKS: Second, start a log. Every problem with the construction. Every day.
MARISOL: We'll make a shared document. Dolores, you're in charge.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I'm always in charge.
MS. BROOKS: And third, call me before you sign anything. Anything. Even a delivery receipt from Harborline.
SAM: Even a delivery receipt?
MS. BROOKS: Okay, maybe not a delivery receipt. But you understand.

[Scene: Noon. The meeting is ending. Eddie stays in his chair.]

EDDIE: Ms. Brooks. Can I ask you something personal?
MS. BROOKS: Of course.
EDDIE: Why do you do this? For free? You could work for Harborline. Make a lot of money.
MS. BROOKS: I grew up in Crown Heights. In a stabilized apartment. When I was twelve, the landlord tried to push my grandmother out.
MRS. ESPOSITO: What happened?
MS. BROOKS: A legal aid lawyer helped her. For free. She stayed. Until she died. Twenty more years.
EDDIE: Twenty more years.
MS. BROOKS: I watched that lawyer in court. I was twelve. I decided right there.
MARISOL: That's why my office loves you.
MS. BROOKS: And I'm sure your office is very tired of me. I call every day.
SAM: Thank you, Danielle. I mean, Ms. Brooks. Seriously. I feel less scared.
MS. BROOKS: Good. Scared people sign bad papers. Informed people don't.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I'll write that down. "Scared people sign bad papers."
EDDIE: Put it on the fridge. Next to Diego's rules.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Which rules?
EDDIE: Never click links. Never pay with gift cards. Hang up and call back.
MS. BROOKS: Add one. Never sign alone.
EDDIE: Never sign alone. Okay. Four rules.
MARISOL: Four rules for the fridge. Four rules for the association.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Everything in fours. Very organized.
EDDIE: Bossy.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **법률 용어를 쉬운 말로(브룩스의 방식)** — *evict*(내보내다) / *specific reasons*(정해진 이유) / *challenge it in court*(법원에서 다투다) / *justify*(정당화하다) / *harassment*(괴롭힘) / *document everything*(모든 걸 기록하다) / *a template*(양식). 어려운 단어 뒤에 바로 예를 든다: *Not paying rent. Serious damage. Illegal activity.*
- **2형 조건문** — *If they asked for twenty percent, we could say no? / If they tried to evict you, a judge would ask them to explain. / If I were you, I would do exactly that. / If it passed, you'd have rights next time.*
- **unless** — "~하지 않는 한." *Unless something like that happens, you can stay. / Unless you want to move.*
- **Straight to the point.** — "바로 본론이네요."
- **not hopeless** — 희망이 없는 건 아닌.
- **a starting number, not a final number** — 첫 제안일 뿐 최종 금액이 아니다.
- **noisy vs unlivable** — 시끄러운 것(합법) vs 살 수 없는 것(불법이 될 수 있음).
- **In court, it's not a weapon. But in public, it's a story.** — 법적 효력과 여론의 힘을 구분하는 문장.
- **Scared people sign bad papers. Informed people don't.** — "겁먹은 사람은 나쁜 서류에 서명하고, 아는 사람은 안 한다."
- **land trusts / co-ops** — 커뮤니티 랜드 트러스트(비영리가 땅을 소유해 주거비를 낮게 유지) / 협동조합 주택.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- **One. … Two. … Three. …** — 브룩스의 설명은 번호마다 쉬고, 핵심어(*go up / renew / evict*)에 강세.
- **harassment** [허**래**스먼트] 또는 [**해**러스먼트] — 둘 다 쓴다. **attorney** [어**터**r니], **template** [**템**플릿], **stabilization** [스테이벌러**제**이션].
- **one hundred eighty days** — 날짜 수는 또박또박. 부인의 *Six months!*는 놀라서 크게.
- 에디의 *I want to die on the third floor.*는 웃으며, 부인의 대답 *Just not on a Sunday.*는 진지한 척.
- 브룩스의 할머니 이야기는 목소리를 낮추고 천천히. *I decided right there.*에서 끝.
- 마지막 *Bossy.*는 에디가 부인의 말버릇을 거꾸로 받아치는 장난. 짧게.

## 한글 해석

마리솔: 여러분, 이분은 대니얼 브룩스 변호사님이에요. 세입자 전문 변호사세요. 무료로 와 주셨어요. 그러니까 잘해 드려요.
브룩스: 고마워요, 마리솔. 그리고 불러 주셔서 다들 감사합니다. 대니얼이라고 불러 주세요.
에스포지토 부인: 브룩스 변호사님. 당분간은요.
브룩스: 브룩스도 좋아요. 에스포지토 부인 얘기 들었어요. 수첩이요.
에스포지토 부인: 내 명성이 높아지고 있네요.
에디: 브룩스 변호사님, 전 에디 오닐이에요. 3층. 52년. 저 쫓겨날 수 있어요?
브룩스: 바로 본론이네요. 좋아요. 짧게 답하면요? 쉽지 않아요. 왜 그런지 설명할게요. 쉬운 영어로요.
에디: 쉬운 영어. 제발요. 라틴어 말고요.
브룩스: 라틴어 없이. 약속해요. 오닐 씨, 오닐 씨 집은 임대료 안정화 아파트예요. 그건 세 가지를 뜻해요.
에디: 세 가지. 네.
브룩스: 하나. 월세는 시 위원회가 허용하는 만큼만 오를 수 있어요. 둘. 계약을 갱신할 권리가 있어요. 매번요. 셋. 정해진 이유가 있을 때만 내보낼 수 있어요.
샘: 어떤 이유요?
브룩스: 월세를 안 내는 거. 심각한 파손. 불법 행위. 그런 거요. "리모델링하고 싶다"는 이유가 안 돼요.
에디: 그러니까 내가 월세를 안 내거나, 건물을 태우지 않는 한…
브룩스: 그런 일이 없는 한 계속 사실 수 있어요. 원하시면 평생요.
에디: 평생. 그게 내가 원하는 거예요.
에스포지토 부인: 그럼 나는요? 나도 안정화 세입자예요.
브룩스: 부인도 마찬가지예요. 같은 권리요.
샘: 그럼 저희는요? 프리야랑 저요? 저흰 시세 세입자예요.
브룩스: 다르지만 희망이 없진 않아요. 정당 사유 퇴거 보호법 때문이에요. 집주인이 뉴욕에 열 세대 넘게 갖고 있어요. 그래서 그 법이 적용돼요.
샘: 실제로 뭘 해 주는데요? 쉬운 영어로요?
브룩스: 계약이 끝나도 그냥 "안녕히 가세요"라고 할 수 없어요. 정당한 이유가 필요해요. 월세를 안 냈다든가. 아니면 정당화할 수 있는 큰 인상이라든가요.
샘: 인상은요? 마리솔이 상한이 있다고 했어요.
브룩스: 기준이 있어요. 매년 대략 물가 상승률에 5%를 더한 거예요. 10%는 절대 안 넘고요. 그보다 많이 올리면 법원에서 다툴 수 있어요. 그 인상이 합리적이라는 걸 저쪽이 증명해야 해요.
샘: 그럼 20%를 요구하면 거절할 수 있는 거예요?
브룩스: 거절할 수 있어요. 그리고 거절했다고 내보내려 하면 판사가 이유를 설명하라고 할 거예요. 아마 저쪽이 질 거예요.
샘: 아마.
브룩스: 변호사들은 늘 "아마"라고 해요. 법정에서 확실한 건 없거든요. 그래도 입장이 유리해요.
마리솔: 대니얼, 바이아웃 제안 얘기 좀 해 줄 수 있어요? 다들 하나씩 받았어요.
브룩스: 네. 좀 볼게요… 1만. 1만 2천. 3만 5천. 4만. 그렇군요. 낮네요.
에디: 4만이 낮다고요?
브룩스: 레드훅의 안정화 아파트에요? 오래 산 세입자한테요? 아주 낮아요. 브루클린에서 20만 달러짜리 바이아웃도 봤어요. 그 이상도요.
에디: 20만 달러! 나가는 대가로?
브룩스: 저쪽한테는 오닐 씨 집이 비어 있을 때 가치가 크니까요. 시세 월세면 큰돈을 벌죠. 오닐 씨의 낮은 월세가 저쪽한텐 큰 비용이에요.
에스포지토 부인: 그러니까 많이 벌려고 우리한테 조금 주는 거네요.
브룩스: 바로 그거예요. 자, 중요한 거요. 시에는 바이아웃 제안에 관한 규칙이 있어요. 편지에 거절할 수 있다고 써 있었어요?
마리솔: 확인해 볼게요. 네. 작은 글씨로요. 맨 아래에요. "귀하는 이 제안을 거절하고 현재 집에 계속 거주하실 수 있습니다."
브룩스: 좋아요. 그건 반드시 써야 해요. 그리고 쓸모 있는 부분은 이거예요. 서면으로 거절하면 180일 동안 다시 바이아웃 얘기로 연락할 수 없어요.
에스포지토 부인: 6개월! 6개월 동안 편지가 안 온다고요?
