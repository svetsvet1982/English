# THE CORNER HOUSE — 3부 37화

## The Investor (투자자)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 3부 1화(통산 37화) · 시기: 2026년 8월 말 토요일 아침 → 9월 9일(수) 오전 · 난이도: B1~B2 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 동네 다이너(테레사와의 만남) → 개업 전 바 The Hook(휘틀록 방문)
> - 등장인물: TERESA, TOMMY, MARISOL, WHITLOCK, MRS. ESPOSITO
> - 사건: 테레사가 세 가지 진실을 전한다. 리치의 빚, 2019년부터 끊긴 안정화 등록, 그리고 "팔게 되면 먼저 사는 사람들에게 제안하라"는 살의 2023년 손편지. 열흘 뒤 하버라인의 그랜트 휘틀록이 바를 찾아와 12~18개월의 공사, 빈집의 시세 임대, '자발적 이주 패키지'를 이야기한다.
> - 학습 포인트: 격식체(Mr./Ms., Would you mind…?, I'd prefer not to…, I'm afraid I can't…), 정중한 질문과 회피, 호칭 전환(Please, call me Grant. / We'll call you Mr. Whitlock for now.)
> - 미국 현안: 사모펀드 집주인의 리모델링 계획, 빈집의 시세 전환, 바이아웃('자발적 이주 패키지'), 점검 통지 요구
> - 연결: 살의 편지 사본 → 56화 협동조합 아이디어의 씨앗. 휘틀록은 웨스트체스터 거주, 부시윅에도 건물. 점검 일정은 금요일까지 서면으로. 공사는 10월 시작 예정.

## 대본

[Scene: Saturday, 8:00 a.m. A diner on Court Street. A woman in her fifties waits in a booth.]

TERESA: Mr. Russo? Thank you for coming. I know it's early, and I know it's a Saturday.
TOMMY: Call me Tommy, because everybody does. This is Marisol, and she's the president of our tenants' association.
MARISOL: Nice to meet you, Teresa. Thank you for calling Tommy last night.
TERESA: Thank you for meeting me. I wasn't sure you would come, especially after the note.
TOMMY: The note scared everybody, but it helped. We started organizing because of it.
TERESA: Good, because that's why I wrote it. I couldn't stop the sale, but I could still warn you.
MARISOL: Why couldn't you stop it? You're Sal's daughter too, so you should have a say.
TERESA: My brother has power of attorney. My father signed it in twenty twenty-two, before his memory got bad, so Richie can make decisions alone.
TOMMY: And your father? Does he know what happened to the building?
TERESA: He doesn't understand anymore. Some days, he thinks it's nineteen eighty, and he asks me if the hardware store is still open.
TOMMY: Mr. Rizzo's store, right where my bar is now.
TERESA: Yes. He loved that corner. He bought the building in nineteen seventy, and he was so proud of it.
MARISOL: Teresa, you said on the phone that Richie didn't tell the truth. What did he lie about?
TERESA: A few things. First, money. Richie has big debts, because a restaurant he opened in New Jersey failed, and then another one failed too.
TOMMY: So he needed cash, and he needed it fast.
TERESA: Fast. He told the family it was the best price, but it wasn't. It was the fastest price.
MARISOL: And the second thing?
TERESA: The registrations for the stabilized apartments. My father did them himself for forty years, every single year, by hand.
MARISOL: And then Richie took over the paperwork in twenty nineteen.
TERESA: How do you know that?
MARISOL: We got the rent history for Three B, and the registrations stopped in twenty nineteen.
TERESA: I knew it. I asked him about it once, and he said, "It's just paperwork. Nobody checks."
TOMMY: Well, somebody checked.
TERESA: Good, I'm glad. And now the third thing, which is the most important one.
MARISOL: Go ahead. We're listening.
TERESA: In twenty twenty-three, my father wrote a letter by hand. It was to the family, and it was about the building.
TOMMY: What did it say?
TERESA: Here, I made a copy for you. Please, read it.
MARISOL: "To my children. If you ever sell this building, offer it first to the people who live in it. They made it a home. I only made it a building. Sal."
TOMMY: He wrote that? Sal really wrote that?
TERESA: He showed it to me at Christmas that year. He said, "Make sure Richie reads this."
MARISOL: And did Richie read it?
TERESA: He read it, and then he laughed. He said, "Tenants can't buy a building, Pop. They can't even pay rent on time."
TOMMY: That son of a...
TERESA: I know. Believe me, I know.
MARISOL: Teresa, is this letter legal? Is it part of the will, or some kind of contract?
TERESA: No, it's not legal, and a lawyer never saw it. It's just... his wish.
MARISOL: Then it can't stop the sale, because the sale is already closed.
TERESA: I know, and I'm not trying to stop it, because it's too late. But I wanted you to know what he wanted.
TOMMY: Why are you telling us all this? You're going to have problems with your brother.
TERESA: I already have problems with my brother. I've had them since we were kids.
MARISOL: Can I keep this copy for our records? We're keeping a file on everything now.
TERESA: Keep it. And if you ever need me, as a witness or for anything else, just call me.
TOMMY: Thank you, Teresa. Your father was a good man, and he'd be proud of you for this.
TERESA: He'd be proud of all of you. That's the reason I'm here this morning.

[Scene: Wednesday, September 9, 10:00 a.m. The Hook, before opening. A man in a gray suit knocks on the glass.]

WHITLOCK: Good morning. Mr. Russo, I presume? I'm Grant Whitlock, from Harborline Capital.
TOMMY: Mr. Whitlock. Come in. We're not open yet, but come in anyway.
WHITLOCK: Thank you, I appreciate it. I hope I'm not interrupting your morning.
TOMMY: You are, but that's okay. This is Marisol Reyes, our tenants' association president, and this is Mrs. Esposito, our secretary.
WHITLOCK: A pleasure, Ms. Reyes. Mrs. Esposito. I received your association's letter last week, and I want to thank you for writing.
MARISOL: Thank you for coming, Mr. Whitlock. We have some questions that we'd like to ask you.
WHITLOCK: Of course. And please, call me Grant.
MRS. ESPOSITO: We'll call you Mr. Whitlock for now. If you don't mind.
WHITLOCK: Not at all. Whatever you prefer is fine with me.
TOMMY: Can I get you anything before we start? Coffee, maybe?
WHITLOCK: Coffee would be lovely, thank you.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Are you sure about that? Tommy made it.
WHITLOCK: I'm sure. I've had a lot of bad coffee in my career, so I'm sure I'll survive one more cup.
MARISOL: Mr. Whitlock, here's our first question. What exactly are your plans for the building?
WHITLOCK: That's a fair question. Our plan is to invest in the property, because it's a beautiful building, but it hasn't been maintained very well.
MRS. ESPOSITO: That's true, I'll give you that. The boiler is from nineteen ninety-two.
WHITLOCK: Exactly. The boiler, the roof, the windows, and the electrical system are all old, and we intend to modernize all of it.
MARISOL: Would you mind telling us the timeline? And how it's going to affect the tenants who live here?
WHITLOCK: We'd like to begin in October, and the work may take twelve to eighteen months. We'll do our best to minimize the disruption.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Eighteen months? Eighteen months of construction, with people living in the building the whole time?
WHITLOCK: I understand your concern, and construction is never pleasant. That's why we'll provide schedules well in advance.
MARISOL: And what about the vacant apartments, Three B and Four C?
WHITLOCK: Those will be fully renovated, with new kitchens and new bathrooms, and then we'll rent them.
MARISOL: At what rent?
WHITLOCK: At market rate, as you'd probably expect.
MARISOL: Mr. Whitlock, I'm sure you know there's an overcharge complaint for Three B with the state. The apartment may still be rent-stabilized.
WHITLOCK: I'm aware of the complaint, and our lawyers are reviewing it. I'd prefer not to comment on that while it's pending.
MARISOL: I understand. But did you know about the complaint before you bought the building?
WHITLOCK: I'm afraid I can't discuss the details of the acquisition.
TOMMY: That means yes.
WHITLOCK: It means I can't discuss it, Mr. Russo.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Your letter said you would inspect every apartment in the building. Why do you need to do that?
WHITLOCK: We need to assess the condition of each unit for the renovation plan. It's standard practice in our business.
MARISOL: We'd ask that all inspections be scheduled in writing, with at least twenty-four hours' notice. And a tenant representative should be present if the tenant wants one.
WHITLOCK: That's very reasonable. My property manager will send you a schedule.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Mr. O'Neill, on the third floor, is eighty-three years old, and he doesn't like strangers in his home.
WHITLOCK: I completely understand, and we'll be very respectful. Perhaps you could be present for that one, Mrs. Esposito?
MRS. ESPOSITO: I will be present, and I'll bring my notebook.
WHITLOCK: I wouldn't expect anything less.
TOMMY: And what about my bar? My lease ends in May, and I'd like to know where I stand.
WHITLOCK: Yes, I've seen your lease. The rent is... quite low for the location.
TOMMY: It's low because I've been here twenty years, and because Sal was fair to me.
WHITLOCK: I'm sure Mr. Cappello was very fair. We'll discuss the renewal terms closer to the date.
TOMMY: Can you at least give me an idea? Some kind of number?
WHITLOCK: I'd rather not speculate today, because market conditions change.
TOMMY: Mr. Whitlock, I have twenty people who depend on this bar, between the staff, the customers, and the block. I need to plan.
WHITLOCK: I appreciate that, so let me be honest with you. The current rent is well below market, and any renewal will reflect that.
TOMMY: Well below market. Okay. So, a lot.
WHITLOCK: I'd say "significantly." But we're always open to conversation.
MARISOL: One more question. Some owners offer tenants money to leave, which people call buyouts. Is that part of your plan?
WHITLOCK: We may offer voluntary relocation packages to tenants who might prefer to move. It would be entirely voluntary, of course.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Voluntary. So you're telling us that you're not forcing anybody out.
WHITLOCK: Absolutely not. That would be illegal, and it's not how we do business.
MARISOL: Then let me say this clearly. Mr. O'Neill has lived here since nineteen seventy-four, and Mrs. Esposito since ninety-five. I've been here ten years, and we are not interested in leaving.
WHITLOCK: I hear you, Ms. Reyes, and I respect that.
MARISOL: And we expect the building to stay safe and livable during construction, which means heat, hot water, and quiet hours.
WHITLOCK: You have my word.
MRS. ESPOSITO: In writing, please.
WHITLOCK: In writing. Of course.
TOMMY: Can I ask you something personal before you go, Mr. Whitlock?
WHITLOCK: You may ask. I may not answer.
TOMMY: Where do you live?
WHITLOCK: In Westchester. Why do you ask?
TOMMY: Do you know your neighbors? Do you know their names?
WHITLOCK: ...Some of them, but not all of them.
TOMMY: We know all of ours, every single one, even the cat. That's what you bought here, not just a pile of bricks.
WHITLOCK: That's... a nice way to put it, Mr. Russo.
MRS. ESPOSITO: It's not nice. It's true.
WHITLOCK: Well, I should go, because I have another building in Bushwick at eleven. Thank you for the coffee.
TOMMY: So how was it?
WHITLOCK: Honestly? It was terrible. But thank you.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Finally, an honest answer from the man.
MARISOL: We'll expect the inspection schedule in writing, Mr. Whitlock, so please don't forget.
WHITLOCK: You'll have it by Friday. Good day, everyone.
TOMMY: Westchester. He doesn't even know his own neighbors, and now he owns ours.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **격식체 요청** — *Would you mind telling us…? / We'd ask that all inspections be scheduled in writing, with at least twenty-four hours' notice.* (ask that + 주어 + 동사원형: 격식 있는 요구) / *If you don't mind.*
- **정중한 회피** — *I'd prefer not to comment on that. / I'm afraid I can't discuss… / I'd rather not speculate today, because market conditions change. / You may ask. I may not answer.* 직접 "No"라고 하지 않고 거절하는 표현들.
- **완곡한 숫자 표현** — *quite low for the location / well below market / significantly* — "엄청 올리겠다"를 돌려 말한 것.
- **호칭 전환** — *Please, call me Grant.*(이름으로 부르라는 친근함의 제안) ↔ *We'll call you Mr. Whitlock for now.*(아직은 거리를 두겠다는 뜻). 영어에서 존대는 호칭으로 표시된다.
- **I presume?** — "~씨 맞으시죠?" 격식체 확인.
- **A pleasure.** — "만나서 반갑습니다." 격식체.
- **I wouldn't expect anything less.** — "당연히 그러시겠죠." 칭찬 반, 비꼼 반.
- **You have my word. / In writing, please.** — "약속드리죠." / "서면으로 주세요."
- **power of attorney / pending / acquisition / voluntary relocation package** — 위임장 / 처리 중인 / 인수 / 자발적 이주 패키지(바이아웃의 완곡어).
- **That son of a...** — [속어] 말을 끝까지 하지 않은 욕설(son of a bitch). 화가 났지만 참는 장면이라 끊는다. 격식 자리나 아이 앞에서는 쓰지 않는다.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- **휘틀록의 말투**: 느리고 또박또박, 높낮이가 적다. 문장 끝을 부드럽게 내리며 미소를 띤 듯. *I'm afraid I can't discuss…*에서 afraid를 가볍게.
- **토미의 짧은 반격**: *That means yes. / So, a lot.* — 휘틀록의 긴 문장 뒤에 툭 던진다. 대비가 장면의 리듬.
- **acquisition** [애퀴**지**션], **speculate** [**스페**큘레잇], **significantly** [시그**니**피컨틀리], **modernize** [**마**더r나이즈].
- 살의 편지는 천천히, 한 문장마다 쉬며. *They made it a home. I only made it a building.*의 대구를 살린다.
- 마리솔의 *Then let me say this clearly.*는 낮고 분명하게. 3부의 시작을 알리는 문장.

## 한글 해석

테레사: 루소 씨? 와 주셔서 감사해요. 이른 시간인 거 알아요. 게다가 토요일이고요.
토미: 토미라고 부르세요. 다들 그렇게 불러요. 이쪽은 마리솔이에요. 우리 세입자 협회 회장이에요.
마리솔: 만나서 반가워요, 테레사. 어젯밤 토미한테 전화해 주셔서 감사해요.
테레사: 만나 주셔서 감사해요. 와 주실지 몰랐어요. 특히 그 쪽지 일도 있고요.
토미: 그 쪽지 때문에 다들 겁먹었죠. 근데 도움이 됐어요. 그것 덕분에 조직을 만들기 시작했거든요.
테레사: 다행이에요. 그래서 쓴 거니까요. 매각을 막을 순 없었지만, 경고는 할 수 있었죠.
마리솔: 왜 막을 수 없었어요? 당신도 살의 딸이잖아요. 그러니 발언권이 있어야죠.
테레사: 오빠가 위임장을 갖고 있어요. 아버지가 기억이 나빠지기 전인 2022년에 서명하셨어요. 그래서 리치는 혼자 결정할 수 있어요.
토미: 그럼 아버님은요? 건물이 어떻게 됐는지 알고 계세요?
테레사: 이제 이해를 못 하세요. 어떤 날은 1980년인 줄 아시고, 철물점이 아직 하냐고 물어보세요.
토미: 리조 씨 가게요. 딱 지금 내 바 자리죠.
테레사: 네. 아버지는 그 모퉁이를 사랑하셨어요. 1970년에 그 건물을 사셨고, 정말 자랑스러워하셨죠.
마리솔: 테레사, 전화로 리치가 진실을 말하지 않았다고 하셨죠. 뭘 거짓말했나요?
테레사: 몇 가지요. 첫째, 돈이요. 리치는 큰 빚이 있어요. 뉴저지에 차린 식당이 망했고, 그다음 것도 망했거든요.
토미: 그러니까 현금이 필요했군요. 그것도 급하게.
테레사: 급하게요. 가족들한텐 최고 가격이라고 했지만, 아니었어요. 제일 빨리 받을 수 있는 가격이었죠.
마리솔: 그럼 두 번째는요?
테레사: 안정화 아파트 등록이요. 아버지는 40년 동안 직접 하셨어요. 한 해도 빠짐없이, 손으로요.
마리솔: 그러다 2019년에 리치가 서류 일을 넘겨받았죠.
테레사: 그걸 어떻게 아세요?
마리솔: 3B 임대료 이력을 받았는데, 등록이 2019년에 끊겼더라고요.
테레사: 그럴 줄 알았어요. 한 번 물어본 적이 있는데, "그냥 서류야. 아무도 확인 안 해"라고 하더라고요.
토미: 글쎄요, 확인한 사람이 있네요.
테레사: 다행이에요, 정말 다행이에요. 그리고 이제 세 번째요. 이게 제일 중요해요.
마리솔: 말씀하세요. 듣고 있어요.
테레사: 2023년에 아버지가 손으로 편지를 쓰셨어요. 가족들한테 쓴 거고, 건물에 관한 거였어요.
토미: 뭐라고 쓰셨는데요?
테레사: 여기요, 사본을 만들어 왔어요. 읽어 보세요.
마리솔: "내 아이들에게. 혹시 이 건물을 팔게 되면, 먼저 그 안에 사는 사람들에게 제안해라. 그 사람들이 이곳을 집으로 만들었다. 나는 그저 건물로 만들었을 뿐이다. 살."
토미: 그런 걸 쓰셨다고요? 살이 정말 그걸 썼어요?
테레사: 그해 크리스마스 때 저한테 보여 주셨어요. "리치가 꼭 읽게 해라"라고 하셨어요.
마리솔: 그래서 리치가 읽었어요?
테레사: 읽었어요. 그러고는 웃었어요. "세입자들은 건물 못 사요, 아버지. 월세도 제때 못 내는데요"라고 했어요.
토미: 그 개자…
테레사: 알아요. 정말, 알아요.
마리솔: 테레사, 이 편지가 법적 효력이 있어요? 유언장의 일부예요, 아니면 일종의 계약서예요?
테레사: 아니요, 법적 효력은 없어요. 변호사는 본 적도 없고요. 그냥… 아버지의 바람이에요.
마리솔: 그럼 매각을 막을 순 없겠네요. 매매가 이미 끝났으니까요.
테레사: 알아요. 너무 늦었으니 막으려는 게 아니에요. 그래도 아버지가 뭘 원하셨는지 알려 드리고 싶었어요.
토미: 왜 우리한테 이걸 다 말해 주는 거예요? 오빠랑 문제가 생길 텐데.
테레사: 오빠랑은 이미 문제가 있어요. 어릴 때부터 그랬어요.
마리솔: 이 사본 기록용으로 제가 가져도 될까요? 요즘은 모든 걸 파일로 모으고 있거든요.
테레사: 가지세요. 그리고 혹시 증인이든 뭐든 제가 필요하시면, 그냥 전화 주세요.
토미: 고마워요, 테레사. 아버님은 좋은 분이셨어요. 이번 일로 당신을 자랑스러워하실 거예요.
테레사: 여러분 모두를 자랑스러워하실 거예요. 그래서 제가 오늘 아침 여기 온 거예요.
