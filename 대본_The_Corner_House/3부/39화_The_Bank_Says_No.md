# THE CORNER HOUSE — 3부 39화

## The Bank Says No (은행이 거절하다)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 3부 3화(통산 39화) · 시기: 2026년 9월, 목요일 오전 · 난이도: B1~B2 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 은행 지점 대기석 → 대출 담당자 사무실 → 은행 밖 거리
> - 등장인물: GINA, TOMMY, MS. CHEN
> - 사건: 토미가 15만 달러 소상공인(SBA) 대출을 신청한다. 담당자 첸은 정중하게 세 가지 걱정을 설명한다. 8개월 뒤 끝나는 상가 계약, 담보 없음, 빠듯한 이익. 여기에 바닥 공사비 1만 1천 달러를 카드로 낸 탓에 신용점수도 떨어졌다. 대출은 거절되지만, 첸은 '비공식적으로' 비영리 지역 대출 기관을 알려 준다.
> - 학습 포인트: 금융 어휘(collateral, credit score, revenue, profit, interest rate, guarantee), 격식 화법(May I be direct? / At this time, we're unable to…), 실망 표현
> - 미국 현안: 고금리 시대의 소상공인 대출, 상가 임대 불안이 대출을 막는 악순환, 지역개발 금융기관(CDFI)
> - 연결: 'Brooklyn Neighborhood Fund'(가상) 연락처 → 54화. 첸의 아버지도 2019년 선셋파크 식당 임대를 잃음. "혼자 싸우지 마세요."

## 대본

[Scene: Thursday, 9:50 a.m. A bank branch on Court Street. Tommy and Gina wait on a bench.]

GINA: Dad, stop moving your leg. You're shaking the whole bench.
TOMMY: I'm nervous. The last time I was in a bank office was two thousand six. To open the bar.
GINA: And they said yes.
TOMMY: Sal co-signed. That's why they said yes. Sal isn't here now.
GINA: You have twenty years of history now. That's worth something.
TOMMY: Twenty years of history and a shoebox.
GINA: Not a shoebox anymore. I put everything in a folder. Look. Tax returns. Bank statements. A business plan.
TOMMY: A business plan. I never had a business plan. I had a corner.
GINA: Now you have both. Remember the numbers. A hundred and fifty thousand. For the outdoor seating, the new kitchen equipment, and a cash reserve.
TOMMY: A cash reserve. For the new rent.
GINA: For the new rent. If it goes up, you need six months of savings. That's what the plan says.
TOMMY: What if she asks about the lease?
GINA: Tell the truth. It ends in May. The new owner wants more. We're negotiating.
TOMMY: We're not negotiating. Whitlock won't give me a number.
GINA: Then say "the terms are under discussion." That sounds better.
TOMMY: "The terms are under discussion." You sound like your boss.
GINA: Don't insult me, Dad. I'm here on a sick day.
TOMMY: A sick day? For this?
GINA: I'm sick of my job. It counts.
TOMMY: Ha. Okay. Here she comes.

[Scene: 10:00 a.m. A small glass office. A woman in her forties with a file.]

MS. CHEN: Mr. Russo? Good morning. I'm Linda Chen. Please come in.
TOMMY: Good morning, Ms. Chen. This is my daughter, Gina. She helped with the application.
MS. CHEN: Nice to meet you both. Please, have a seat. Can I offer you some water?
GINA: No, thank you. We're fine.
MS. CHEN: Mr. Russo, I've reviewed your application. Thank you for the documents. They're very complete.
TOMMY: That's Gina. Not me.
MS. CHEN: Well, it shows. Let me start by saying your business has a long history. Twenty years is impressive. Most restaurants close in five.
TOMMY: Thank you. We've survived a lot. Sandy. The pandemic. The Mets.
MS. CHEN: I'm a Yankees fan, so I can't comment on that last one.
TOMMY: I'll try not to hold it against you.
MS. CHEN: I appreciate that. Now, the request. You're asking for a hundred and fifty thousand dollars. Through the SBA seven-a program.
TOMMY: That's right.
MS. CHEN: Could you tell me, in your own words, what the loan is for?
TOMMY: Three things. Outdoor seating, so we can grow in the summer. New kitchen equipment, because my fryer is from two thousand nine. And a reserve. In case my rent goes up.
MS. CHEN: Thank you. That's clear. I do have some concerns. May I be direct?
TOMMY: Please. Everybody's direct with me lately.
MS. CHEN: The first concern is the lease. Your lease expires in May. That's eight months from now.
GINA: The terms are under discussion with the new owner.
MS. CHEN: I understand. But for the bank, it's a serious risk. The loan would be for ten years. Your right to the space is less than one year.
TOMMY: So if they don't renew, I have a loan and no bar.
MS. CHEN: Exactly. And the outdoor seating and the equipment depend on the location.
TOMMY: What's the second concern?
MS. CHEN: Collateral. We usually need something to secure the loan. Real estate, for example. Do you own any property?
TOMMY: No. I rent my apartment. Upstairs. In the same building.
MS. CHEN: I see. So the equipment would be the only collateral. And used kitchen equipment doesn't have much value.
GINA: The SBA guarantees part of the loan, right? Doesn't that help?
MS. CHEN: It helps. For a loan this size, the SBA guarantees up to eighty-five percent. But we still have to show the business can repay. Which brings me to the third concern.
TOMMY: The numbers.
MS. CHEN: The numbers. Your revenue is steady. About four hundred and fifty thousand a year. But your profit is thin. Around forty-five thousand.
TOMMY: Forty-five thousand. That's my salary. That's me.
MS. CHEN: I understand. Now, at current interest rates, a loan of this size would mean payments of about two thousand a month.
GINA: Two thousand a month. Out of a profit of about four thousand a month.
MS. CHEN: That's half of your income. And if your rent increases significantly, the business may not cover both.
TOMMY: So the loan is for the higher rent. But the higher rent makes the loan impossible.
MS. CHEN: I know it sounds circular. I'm sorry.
TOMMY: What about my credit?
MS. CHEN: Your personal credit score is fair. Six eighty. It went down this summer.
TOMMY: The floor. I put the floor on my credit card. Eleven thousand dollars.
MS. CHEN: That explains it. The card balance is high compared to the limit.
GINA: He had no choice. The landlord refused to pay. The floor was rotten.
MS. CHEN: I believe you. Unfortunately, the credit report doesn't know the reasons. It only knows the balance.
TOMMY: So what's the answer, Ms. Chen?
MS. CHEN: I'm sorry, Mr. Russo. At this time, we're unable to approve the loan.
TOMMY: At this time.
MS. CHEN: At this time. If the lease is renewed, for five or ten years, please come back. That would change the picture completely.
TOMMY: But I need the money to renew the lease.
MS. CHEN: I understand the problem. I really do.
GINA: Is there anything else? Any other option?
MS. CHEN: Can I make a suggestion? Off the record?
TOMMY: Please.
MS. CHEN: There are nonprofit lenders. Community development lenders. They work with small businesses that banks can't help.
GINA: What's the difference?
MS. CHEN: They're more flexible. They look at the history. The community. Not only the collateral. Some have programs for businesses facing displacement.
TOMMY: Displacement. That's a fancy word for "getting kicked out."
MS. CHEN: Yes. It is. There's one in Brooklyn I know well. I'll write down the name.
GINA: Thank you, Ms. Chen. That's very kind.
MS. CHEN: And Mr. Russo, one more thing. Off the record again.
TOMMY: Sure.
MS. CHEN: My father had a restaurant. In Sunset Park. Twenty-five years. He lost the lease in twenty nineteen.
TOMMY: I'm sorry.
MS. CHEN: He said the hardest part wasn't the money. It was that he had to fight alone. Please don't fight alone.
TOMMY: I'm not alone. I have a whole building. And a daughter with a folder.
MS. CHEN: Then you're already ahead of my father. Good luck. I mean that.
TOMMY: Thank you. Go Mets.
MS. CHEN: Go Yankees. Have a good day.

[Scene: 10:40 a.m. Court Street. Tommy and Gina stand outside the bank.]

TOMMY: Well. That's it. The bank says no.
GINA: The bank says "not at this time."
TOMMY: That's a no with a tie. A no in a suit.
GINA: Dad. She gave us a name. A nonprofit lender. That's not nothing.
TOMMY: I feel stupid. Twenty years. And I can't get a loan for a fryer.
GINA: You're not stupid. The system is backwards. She said it herself. "Circular."
TOMMY: I put eleven thousand on a credit card for a floor that isn't even mine. Now my credit is bad because of it.
GINA: Because of Richie. And Harborline. Not because of you.
TOMMY: Your mother would know what to do.
GINA: Mom would say, "Tommy, eat something. Then we'll talk."
TOMMY: Ha. That's true. That's exactly what she'd say.
GINA: So let's eat something. Then we call the nonprofit.
TOMMY: And if they say no?
GINA: Then we try something else. And something else. Like Marisol says. Records, rules, and one more letter.
TOMMY: You're your mother's daughter. Stubborn.
GINA: I'm your daughter too. Also stubborn.
TOMMY: Gina, can I tell you something? I'm tired. I'm really tired.
GINA: I know, Dad.
TOMMY: For twenty years, I thought, "If I work hard, it will be okay." I worked hard. It's not okay.
GINA: Working hard isn't enough anymore. Not alone. Ms. Chen's father learned that.
TOMMY: She's a good person. For a Yankees fan.
GINA: Ha! There he is. My dad.
TOMMY: What's the name of the nonprofit?
GINA: She wrote it here. "Brooklyn Neighborhood Fund." With a phone number. And her name. "Tell them Linda sent you."
TOMMY: "Linda sent you." Like a club.
GINA: Everything in this city is a club. You just need to know somebody.
TOMMY: I know a lot of people. Twenty years behind a bar.
GINA: Then maybe that's your collateral. People.
TOMMY: Try telling that to a bank.
GINA: I'll tell it to the nonprofit. In big letters.
TOMMY: Okay. Food first. Then the call.
GINA: Food first. Where?
TOMMY: Not my bar. I don't want to see my fryer right now.
GINA: The diner on Court Street?
TOMMY: The diner. Eggs. And their coffee. Their coffee is worse than mine. That always makes me feel better.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **금융 어휘** — *a loan / apply for a loan / approve*(대출 / 대출을 신청하다 / 승인하다), *collateral*(담보), *secure the loan*(대출을 담보로 보장하다), *revenue*(매출), *profit*(이익), *a cash reserve*(예비 자금), *interest rate*(금리), *payments of about two thousand a month*(월 상환액), *a credit score / credit report / balance / limit*(신용점수 / 신용 보고서 / 잔액 / 한도), *co-sign*(공동 서명·보증하다), *guarantee*(보증하다).
- **SBA 7(a)** — 미국 중소기업청이 은행 대출의 일부를 보증해 주는 대표 프로그램.
- **격식 화법** — *May I be direct? / I do have some concerns. / Could you tell me, in your own words, …? / At this time, we're unable to approve the loan.*(거절의 공식 표현) / *That would change the picture completely.*
- **off the record** — 비공식적으로(기록에 남기지 않고).
- **circular** — 순환적인, 닭이 먼저냐 달걀이 먼저냐.
- **displacement** — (주민·상인의) 내몰림. 토미는 "쫓겨난다는 말의 고급 버전"이라고 한다.
- **I'll try not to hold it against you.** — "그걸로 당신을 미워하진 않도록 할게요." 양키스 팬이라는 말에 대한 농담.
- **That's a no with a tie. A no in a suit.** — "넥타이 맨 거절이네. 양복 입은 '노'." 완곡어를 비꼬는 토미식 표현.
- **That's not nothing.** — "그것도 아무것도 아닌 건 아니야(작지만 의미 있어)."
- **stubborn** — 고집 센.
- **실망 표현** — *Well. That's it. / I feel stupid. / I'm tired. I'm really tired. / It's not okay.*

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- **collateral** [컬**래**터럴], **revenue** [**레**버뉴], **displacement** [디스**플레**이스먼트], **circular** [**써**r큘러r].
- **seven-a** [세븐 에이], **six eighty** [식스 에이리] — 신용점수 680.
- 첸의 말투: 따뜻하지만 정확하다. 걱정을 *The first concern… / What's the second… / the third concern*으로 번호 매기듯 정리한다.
- *At this time, we're unable to approve the loan.* — 첸은 미안한 마음으로 천천히, 토미는 *At this time.*을 비꼬듯 되받는다.
- 첸의 아버지 이야기(*Please don't fight alone.*)는 목소리를 낮추고 진심으로.
- 마지막 다이너 커피 농담은 기운을 되찾은 톤으로.
