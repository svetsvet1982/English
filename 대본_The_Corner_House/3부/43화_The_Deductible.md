# THE CORNER HOUSE — 3부 43화

## The Deductible (본인 부담금)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 3부 7화(통산 43화) · 시기: 2026년 10월 중순, 수요일 · 난이도: B1~B2 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 주치의 레빈 박사의 진료실 → 저녁, 바 The Hook
> - 등장인물: DR. LEVINE, EDDIE, MRS. ESPOSITO, JESSE, TOMMY, TESS
> - 사건: 에디의 정기 검진. 무릎 관절염, 약간 높은 혈압, 그리고 충격적인 소식. 에디의 메디케어 어드밴티지 플랜이 내년부터 레빈 박사를 보장하지 않는다. 12월 7일까지 새 플랜을 골라야 한다. 같은 날 제시는 보험료가 월 120달러에서 450달러로 오른 통지를 받는다.
> - 학습 포인트: 의료·보험 어휘(symptoms, blood pressure, network, deductible, premium, copay, out-of-pocket maximum), 증상 설명, 의사와의 격식 대화
> - 미국 현안: 메디케어 연례 가입 기간(10월 15일~12월 7일), 플랜의 의료기관 네트워크 이탈, 처방약 본인 부담 연간 상한(약 2천 달러), ACA 추가 보조금 만료 후 개인 보험료 급등, 비용 걱정에 구급차를 부르지 않는 환자들
> - 연결: 에디와 제시가 도서관에서 무료 상담을 함께 받기로(테스 동행). 토미는 20년째 타샤와 라몬 보험료의 절반을 내 왔다.

## 대본

[Scene: Wednesday, 10:00 a.m. Dr. Levine's office. Eddie on the exam table. Mrs. Esposito in a chair with her notebook.]

DR. LEVINE: Mr. O'Neill. Good to see you. How have you been since July?
EDDIE: Still alive, Doctor. That's the main thing.
DR. LEVINE: That is the main thing. And who's this? Your daughter?
MRS. ESPOSITO: His neighbor. Dolores Esposito. I'm here to take notes.
EDDIE: She takes notes on everything. She'll take notes on your notes.
DR. LEVINE: Wonderful. I wish all my patients had a Dolores. So, Mr. O'Neill. Any new symptoms?
EDDIE: My knee. The left one. It hurts on the stairs. Especially in the morning.
DR. LEVINE: How bad? On a scale of one to ten?
EDDIE: In the morning, a six. By lunch, a three. After a beer, a one.
DR. LEVINE: I'll write down "six." Not the beer part. Any swelling?
EDDIE: A little. When it rains.
MRS. ESPOSITO: His knee predicts the weather. Better than the app.
DR. LEVINE: Many knees do. It's probably arthritis. We'll do an X-ray. And your heart? Any chest pain? Shortness of breath?
EDDIE: Short of breath on the stairs. Third floor. But I've lived on the third floor for fifty-two years.
DR. LEVINE: Has it gotten worse? Compared to last year?
EDDIE: A little. I stop on the second floor now. To rest. And to say hello to Dolores.
MRS. ESPOSITO: He says hello every day. It's very nice. And I can hear him breathing.
DR. LEVINE: Okay. Let me listen. Deep breath in... and out. Again.
EDDIE: How does it sound?
DR. LEVINE: Your heart sounds steady. A little murmur. The same as last year. Your blood pressure is one forty-two over eighty-eight. A bit high.
EDDIE: Is that bad?
DR. LEVINE: It's not terrible. But I'd like it lower. Are you taking your pills every day?
EDDIE: Every day. Most days.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Most days. He forgets on Sundays.
EDDIE: Sunday is my day of rest.
DR. LEVINE: Your heart doesn't rest on Sunday, Mr. O'Neill. Every day, please. Maybe Dolores can remind you.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I'll put it on my clipboard.
DR. LEVINE: Now, something important. It's October. Open enrollment for Medicare. Have you looked at your plan for next year?
EDDIE: My plan? I've had the same plan for ten years. Why would I change?
DR. LEVINE: Because plans change every year. The costs, the doctors, the drugs they cover. You should check.
EDDIE: What's there to check?
DR. LEVINE: Well, you have a Medicare Advantage plan, right? Through a private company?
EDDIE: That's what Michael chose. My son. He said it was cheaper.
DR. LEVINE: It can be. But I got a letter. Your plan is leaving our network next year. In January.
EDDIE: Leaving your network? What does that mean?
DR. LEVINE: It means your plan won't pay for visits with me. Or with this hospital. Unless you change plans.
EDDIE: You've been my doctor for twenty years! Since Peggy.
DR. LEVINE: I know. That's why I'm telling you. You have until December seventh to choose a new plan.
MRS. ESPOSITO: December seventh. Writing that down. In red.
EDDIE: How do I choose? There are so many plans. The mail is full of them. Every day, a new envelope.
DR. LEVINE: Don't choose from the mail. Those are ads. There's a free counseling program for seniors. They help you compare. They don't sell anything.
MRS. ESPOSITO: A free program. We like free.
DR. LEVINE: Ask them three questions. Is Dr. Levine in the network? Are my medicines covered? And what's the deductible?
EDDIE: The deductible. What's the deductible, exactly? I always forget.
DR. LEVINE: The amount you pay first. Before the insurance starts paying. Some plans have a high deductible for drugs.
EDDIE: And my heart pills? Are they expensive?
DR. LEVINE: One of them is brand-name. Without good coverage, it's about four hundred a month.
EDDIE: Four hundred! For little pills!
DR. LEVINE: The good news is there's a cap now. For Medicare drug plans. You won't pay more than about two thousand a year for drugs. Out of your own pocket.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Two thousand a year. That's still a lot. On a pension.
DR. LEVINE: It's a lot. But there used to be no limit. Some of my patients paid eight, ten thousand.
EDDIE: Ten thousand. I'd rather have the heart attack.
DR. LEVINE: Please don't say that, Mr. O'Neill. Also, ask about extra help. If your income is low, there are programs that help with the costs.
EDDIE: Extra help. Like the air conditioner?
DR. LEVINE: Exactly like the air conditioner. Did you get it?
EDDIE: I did. It's beautiful. Quiet. Thank you for the note.
DR. LEVINE: You're welcome. Okay. An X-ray for the knee. Pills every day. Even Sunday. And a new plan by December seventh.
EDDIE: Yes, Doctor.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I wrote everything. Three pages.
DR. LEVINE: Dolores, would you like a job? I need an assistant.

[Scene: 7:00 p.m. The Hook. Jesse drops an envelope on the bar.]

JESSE: Tommy, can I have a beer? A big one?
TOMMY: It's Wednesday. You're not working. What happened?
JESSE: This happened. Look. My health insurance. For next year.
TOMMY: Let me see. "Your monthly premium will be..." Four hundred and fifty dollars?
JESSE: Four fifty. This year, I paid a hundred and twenty.
EDDIE: What? Why? That's almost four times more.
JESSE: The extra subsidies ended. The government help. It expired at the end of last year. So now I pay almost everything.
TOMMY: Four hundred and fifty a month. That's more than a third of your rent.
JESSE: Exactly. With my rent, my phone, my student loans... I can't do it.
TESS: Hey! Sorry I'm late. My shift ran over. What's wrong? Everybody looks sad.
JESSE: My insurance. It went from one twenty to four fifty.
TESS: Oh no. I see this every day at the ER. People coming in sick. Because they dropped their insurance.
JESSE: I'm thinking about dropping it too. I'm young. I'm healthy.
TESS: Jesse, no. Please. Young and healthy people get hit by cars. Fall off bikes. Break their arms.
EDDIE: And get appendicitis. My cousin did. At twenty-five.
TESS: An appendix surgery without insurance can cost thirty or forty thousand dollars.
JESSE: Forty thousand. Okay. That's scarier than four fifty.
TESS: Did you look at other plans? On the marketplace? There are cheaper ones.
JESSE: The cheaper ones have a huge deductible. Like six thousand dollars. I'd pay six thousand before they pay anything.
TOMMY: So you pay every month, and they still don't pay?
JESSE: Not until six thousand. Unless it's a checkup. Checkups are free.
TESS: That kind of plan is for emergencies. Bad accidents. Big surgery. It protects you from the forty thousand.
EDDIE: So it's like an umbrella. You hope you never need it.
TESS: Exactly. An umbrella for a hurricane.
JESSE: What about New York's Essential Plan? I heard about it.
TESS: That's for lower incomes. Very cheap or free. But you need to be under the limit. With your two jobs, you might be just over.
JESSE: Just over. Of course. I'm always just over or just under.
TOMMY: Like the forty-times rule.
JESSE: Like the forty-times rule. I'm the "just" guy.
TESS: Check it anyway. And there are free navigators. People who help you choose. In libraries.
EDDIE: That's what my doctor said! A free counselor! For Medicare!
JESSE: Eddie, you had insurance problems today too?
EDDIE: My plan is leaving my doctor's network. After twenty years. I have to choose a new one by December seventh.
JESSE: So we both need a new plan. You at eighty-three. Me at twenty-seven.
TOMMY: And me at fifty-eight. My insurance went up too. Twenty-two percent. For the bar's plan.
TESS: Twenty-two!
TOMMY: I pay for Tasha and Ramón too. Half of their insurance. It's the right thing. But it's expensive.
JESSE: Tommy, you pay half of Tasha's insurance?
TOMMY: Twenty years. Since she started. Don't tell her I told you. She'll get emotional.
EDDIE: Everybody gets emotional in this bar.
TESS: You know what's crazy? In my hospital, we see the same thing every day. Good people. Working people. Scared of a bill.
JESSE: Do you ever get used to it?
TESS: Never. Last week, a man with chest pain didn't want an ambulance. Because of the cost. He walked to the ER.
EDDIE: He walked? With chest pain?
TESS: Six blocks. He was having a heart attack. He's okay now. But he walked.
EDDIE: That would be me. I'd walk too. To save money.
TESS: Eddie, promise me. If you have chest pain, you call 911. You don't walk.
EDDIE: I promise. I'll call. And then I'll call Dolores. She'll take notes.
TOMMY: Okay. Here's the plan. Jesse, you go to the library navigator. Eddie, you go to the senior counselor.
JESSE: Can we go together? Same day?
EDDIE: A field trip. Me and the couch fella. At the library.
JESSE: I'm not the couch fella anymore. I'm the "just over" guy.
TESS: And I'll come too. On my day off. I know the insurance words. Deductible, copay, premium, out-of-pocket maximum.
EDDIE: You speak insurance? That's like speaking Chinese.
TESS: Harder. Chinese has rules.
TOMMY: Ha! Okay, a round. For the uninsured. And the underinsured. And the overcharged.
JESSE: That's everybody, Tommy.
TOMMY: That's why it's a round for everybody.
EDDIE: Not for me. Just coffee. It's a weekday. And my blood pressure is one forty-two.
TESS: Good choice, Eddie. Doctor's orders. Nurse's orders too.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **증상 설명** — *It hurts on the stairs. / On a scale of one to ten, a six. / Any swelling? / short of breath / chest pain / Has it gotten worse?*
- **진료 어휘** — *symptoms, arthritis*(관절염), *an X-ray, a murmur*(심잡음), *blood pressure one forty-two over eighty-eight*(142/88), *brand-name*(오리지널 약, 반대는 generic).
- **보험 어휘** — *a premium*(월 보험료), *a deductible*(보험이 지급하기 전에 먼저 내는 본인 부담금), *a copay*(진료 1회당 정액 본인 부담), *out-of-pocket maximum*(연간 본인 부담 상한), *in the network / leaving the network*(보장 의료기관 목록에 있는 / 빠지는), *coverage*(보장), *subsidies*(보조금), *the marketplace*(개인 보험 거래소), *a navigator*(무료 가입 상담원).
- **Medicare / Medicare Advantage** — 65세 이상 연방 건강보험 / 민간 보험사가 운영하는 메디케어 대체 플랜. 가입 변경은 매년 10월 15일~12월 7일.
- **the Essential Plan** — 뉴욕주의 저소득층용 저가 건강보험.
- **My shift ran over.** — "근무가 늦게 끝났어."
- **drop (insurance)** — (보험을) 끊다.
- **I'm the "just" guy.** — "난 늘 '아슬아슬하게' 기준을 넘거나 못 미치는 사람이야." 제시의 자조.
- **Doctor's orders.** — "의사 지시야."
- **I'd rather have the heart attack.** — [농담] "차라리 심장마비가 낫겠다." 레빈 박사는 웃지 않는다.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- **혈압 읽기**: *one forty-two over eighty-eight* — over 앞에서 살짝 끊는다.
- **deductible** [디**덕**터블], **premium** [**프리**미엄], **arthritis** [아r**쓰라**이리스], **appendicitis** [어펜더**사**이리스], **enrollment** [인**로**울먼트].
- **On a scale of one to ten** — 레빈 박사가 일상적으로 묻는 톤. 에디의 대답은 *a six… a three… a one*으로 점점 밝게.
- 레빈 박사는 격식 있지만 따뜻하다. *Please don't say that, Mr. O'Neill.*은 단호하게.
- 테스의 응급실 이야기(*He walked.*)는 짧고 무겁게. 그 뒤 에디의 *That would be me.*는 조용히.
- 마지막 *Chinese has rules.*는 테스의 농담. 짧게 툭.
