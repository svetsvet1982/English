# THE CORNER HOUSE — 3부 43화

## The Deductible (본인 부담금)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 3부 7화(통산 43화) · 시기: 2026년 10월 중순, 수요일 · 난이도: B1~B2 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 주치의 레빈 박사의 진료실 → 저녁, 바 The Hook
> - 등장인물: DR. LEVINE, EDDIE, MRS. ESPOSITO, JESSE, TOMMY, TESS
> - 사건: 에디의 정기 검진. 무릎 관절염, 약간 높은 혈압, 그리고 충격적인 소식. 에디의 메디케어 어드밴티지 플랜이 내년부터 레빈 박사를 보장하지 않는다. 12월 7일까지 새 플랜을 골라야 한다. 같은 날 제시는 보험료가 월 120달러에서 450달러로 오른 통지를 받는다.
> - 학습 포인트: 의료·보험 어휘(symptoms, blood pressure, network, deductible, premium, copay, out-of-pocket maximum), 증상 설명, 의사와의 격식 대화
> - 미국 현안: 메디케어 연례 가입 기간(10월 15일~12월 7일), 플랜의 의료기관 네트워크 이탈, 처방약 본인 부담 연간 상한(약 2천 달러), ACA 추가 보조금 만료 후 개인 보험료 급등, 비용 걱정에 구급차를 부르지 않는 환자들
> - 연결: 에디와 제시가 도서관에서 무료 상담을 함께 받기로(테스 동행). 토미는 20년째 타샤와 라몬 보험료의 절반을 내 왔다.

## 대본

[Scene: Wednesday, 10:00 a.m. Dr. Levine's office. Eddie on the exam table. Mrs. Esposito in a chair with her notebook.]

DR. LEVINE: Mr. O'Neill, it's good to see you again. How have you been since July?
EDDIE: Still alive, Doctor. At my age, that's the main thing.
DR. LEVINE: That is the main thing. And who's this with you today? Is she your daughter?
MRS. ESPOSITO: I'm his neighbor, Dolores Esposito. I'm only here to take notes, so please ignore me.
EDDIE: She takes notes on everything. If you're not careful, she'll take notes on your notes.
DR. LEVINE: Wonderful. I wish all my patients had a Dolores, because half of them forget what I said by the parking lot. So, Mr. O'Neill, any new symptoms?
EDDIE: It's my knee, the left one. It hurts on the stairs, especially in the morning when I first get up.
DR. LEVINE: How bad is the pain? On a scale of one to ten?
EDDIE: In the morning, it's a six. By lunch, a three. After a beer, a one.
DR. LEVINE: I'll write down "six," and I'll leave out the beer part. Any swelling?
EDDIE: A little bit, but only when it rains.
MRS. ESPOSITO: His knee predicts the weather. Honestly, it's more accurate than the app on my phone.
DR. LEVINE: Many knees do. It's probably arthritis, but we'll do an X-ray to be sure. And how's your heart? Any chest pain or shortness of breath?
EDDIE: I get short of breath on the stairs, because I'm on the third floor. But I've lived on the third floor for fifty-two years.
DR. LEVINE: Has it gotten worse compared to last year?
EDDIE: A little. I stop on the second floor now to rest, and also to say hello to Dolores.
MRS. ESPOSITO: He says hello every single day, which is very nice. But I can also hear him breathing through the door.
DR. LEVINE: Okay, let me listen. Take a deep breath in... and out. One more time.
EDDIE: So how does it sound in there?
DR. LEVINE: Your heart sounds steady. There's a little murmur, but it's the same as last year. Your blood pressure is one forty-two over eighty-eight, which is a bit high.
EDDIE: Is that bad?
DR. LEVINE: It's not terrible, but I'd like it lower. Are you taking your pills every day?
EDDIE: Every day. Well, most days.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Most days, yes. He forgets on Sundays, every week.
EDDIE: Sunday is my day of rest.
DR. LEVINE: Your heart doesn't rest on Sunday, Mr. O'Neill, so every day, please. Maybe Dolores can remind you.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I'll put it on my clipboard, right under the recycling schedule.
DR. LEVINE: Now, something important. It's October, which means it's open enrollment for Medicare. Have you looked at your plan for next year?
EDDIE: My plan? I've had the same plan for ten years, and it works fine. Why would I change?
DR. LEVINE: Because plans change every year, even when you don't. The costs change, the doctors change, and so do the drugs they cover. You should always check.
EDDIE: But what is there to check, exactly?
DR. LEVINE: Well, you have a Medicare Advantage plan, right? The kind that goes through a private company?
EDDIE: That's the one my son Michael chose for me. He said it was cheaper than the regular kind.
DR. LEVINE: It can be. But I got a letter last week, and your plan is leaving our network next year, starting in January.
EDDIE: Leaving your network? What does that mean in plain English?
DR. LEVINE: It means your plan won't pay for your visits with me, or for this hospital, unless you change plans.
EDDIE: But you've been my doctor for twenty years! You were my doctor back when Peggy was still with us.
DR. LEVINE: I know, and that's exactly why I'm telling you now. You have until December seventh to choose a new plan.
MRS. ESPOSITO: December seventh. I'm writing that down in red, so nobody can miss it.
EDDIE: But how do I choose? There are so many plans, and my mailbox is full of them. Every day there's a new envelope with a smiling old couple on it.
DR. LEVINE: Don't choose from the mail, because those are just ads. There's a free counseling program for seniors that helps you compare plans, and they don't sell anything.
MRS. ESPOSITO: A free program with nothing to sell. We like free.
DR. LEVINE: Ask them three questions. Is Dr. Levine in the network? Are my medicines covered? And what's the deductible?
EDDIE: The deductible. What's the deductible, exactly? Somebody explains it to me every year, and I always forget.
DR. LEVINE: It's the amount you pay first, before the insurance starts paying anything. Some plans have a high deductible for drugs.
EDDIE: And what about my heart pills? Are they expensive?
DR. LEVINE: One of them is brand-name, so without good coverage, it's about four hundred dollars a month.
EDDIE: Four hundred! For those little pills!
DR. LEVINE: The good news is that there's a cap now for Medicare drug plans. You won't pay more than about two thousand dollars a year for drugs out of your own pocket.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Two thousand a year is still a lot of money when you live on a pension.
DR. LEVINE: It's a lot, I agree. But there used to be no limit at all, and some of my patients paid eight or ten thousand.
EDDIE: Ten thousand dollars? I'd rather have the heart attack.
DR. LEVINE: Please don't say that, Mr. O'Neill. Also, ask them about extra help. If your income is low, there are programs that help with the costs.
EDDIE: Extra help? You mean like the air conditioner?
DR. LEVINE: Exactly like the air conditioner. Did you get it, by the way?
EDDIE: I did, and it's beautiful. It's so quiet I can hear the Mets lose. Thank you for writing the note.
DR. LEVINE: You're welcome. Okay, so here's the list. An X-ray for the knee, and pills every day, even on Sunday. And a new plan by December seventh.
EDDIE: Yes, Doctor.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I wrote down everything you said. It's three pages, front and back.
DR. LEVINE: Dolores, would you like a job? I need an assistant.

[Scene: 7:00 p.m. The Hook. Jesse drops an envelope on the bar.]

JESSE: Tommy, can I have a beer? A big one, please.
TOMMY: It's Wednesday, and you're not working tonight. So what happened?
JESSE: This happened. Look at this letter about my health insurance for next year.
TOMMY: Let me see. "Your monthly premium will be..." Four hundred and fifty dollars?
JESSE: Four fifty a month. This year, I paid a hundred and twenty.
EDDIE: What? Why? That's almost four times more than before.
JESSE: Because the extra subsidies ended. That was the government help, and it expired at the end of last year. So now I pay almost everything myself.
TOMMY: Four hundred and fifty a month. That's more than a third of your rent.
JESSE: Exactly. When you add my rent, my phone, and my student loans, I just can't do it.
TESS: Hey! Sorry I'm late, my shift ran over. What's wrong? Everybody looks so sad.
JESSE: It's my insurance. It went from one twenty to four fifty.
TESS: Oh no. I see this every day at the ER. People come in really sick, because they dropped their insurance to save money.
JESSE: Honestly, I'm thinking about dropping it too. I'm young, and I'm healthy.
TESS: Jesse, no. Please don't. Young and healthy people get hit by cars, and they fall off bikes and break their arms.
EDDIE: And they get appendicitis. My cousin did, when he was only twenty-five.
TESS: An appendix surgery without insurance can cost thirty or forty thousand dollars.
JESSE: Forty thousand. Okay, that's a lot scarier than four fifty.
TESS: Did you look at other plans on the marketplace? There are always cheaper ones.
JESSE: The cheaper ones have a huge deductible, like six thousand dollars. I'd pay six thousand before they pay anything.
TOMMY: So you pay them every month, and they still don't pay you?
JESSE: Not until I hit six thousand, unless it's a checkup. Checkups are free.
TESS: That kind of plan is for emergencies, like a bad accident or a big surgery. It protects you from the forty thousand.
EDDIE: So it's like an umbrella. You carry it around, and you hope you never need it.
TESS: Exactly. It's an umbrella for a hurricane.
JESSE: What about New York's Essential Plan? I heard somebody talk about it.
TESS: That one is for lower incomes, and it's very cheap or even free. But you need to be under the limit, and with your two jobs, you might be just over.
JESSE: Just over. Of course I am. I'm always just over or just under.
TOMMY: Like the forty-times rule.
JESSE: Like the forty-times rule. I'm the "just" guy.
TESS: Check it anyway. There are also free navigators, which are people who help you choose a plan. You can find them at the library.
EDDIE: That's what my doctor said this morning! He told me about a free counselor for Medicare!
JESSE: Wait, Eddie, you had insurance problems today too?
EDDIE: My plan is leaving my doctor's network after twenty years. I have to choose a new one by December seventh.
JESSE: So we both need a new plan, you at eighty-three and me at twenty-seven.
TOMMY: And me at fifty-eight, because my insurance went up too. The bar's plan went up twenty-two percent.
TESS: Twenty-two percent!
TOMMY: I pay for Tasha and Ramón too, half of their insurance. It's the right thing to do, but it's expensive.
JESSE: Tommy, you pay half of Tasha's insurance? Seriously?
TOMMY: For twenty years, ever since she started here. Don't tell her I told you, because she'll get emotional.
EDDIE: Everybody gets emotional in this bar. It's the onions in the kitchen.
TESS: You know what's crazy? In my hospital, we see the same thing every day. Good, hardworking people who are more scared of a bill than of being sick.
JESSE: Do you ever get used to it?
TESS: Never. Last week, a man with chest pain didn't want an ambulance, because he was worried about the cost. So he walked to the ER.
EDDIE: He walked? With chest pain?
TESS: Six blocks. He was having a heart attack the whole time. He's okay now, but he walked.
EDDIE: That would be me. I'd walk too, just to save money.
TESS: Eddie, promise me something. If you ever have chest pain, you call 911. You don't walk anywhere.
EDDIE: I promise I'll call. And then I'll call Dolores, so she can take notes.
TOMMY: Okay, here's the plan. Jesse, you go to the library navigator, and Eddie, you go to the senior counselor.
JESSE: Can we go together on the same day? It's less scary that way.
EDDIE: A field trip to the library. Me and the couch fella.
JESSE: I'm not the couch fella anymore, Eddie. Now I'm the "just over" guy.
TESS: And I'll come too, on my day off, because I know all the insurance words. Deductible, copay, premium, out-of-pocket maximum.
EDDIE: You speak insurance? That's like speaking Chinese.
TESS: It's harder. Chinese has rules.
TOMMY: Ha! Okay, a round on me. For the uninsured, and the underinsured, and the overcharged.
JESSE: That's everybody, Tommy.
TOMMY: That's why it's a round for everybody.
EDDIE: Not for me. Just coffee, because it's a weekday. And my blood pressure is one forty-two.
TESS: Good choice, Eddie. That's doctor's orders, and it's nurse's orders too.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **증상 설명** — *It hurts on the stairs / On a scale of one to ten? / In the morning, it's a six. / Any swelling? / short of breath / chest pain / Has it gotten worse compared to last year?*
- **진료 어휘** — *symptoms, arthritis*(관절염), *an X-ray, a murmur*(심잡음), *blood pressure one forty-two over eighty-eight*(142/88), *brand-name*(오리지널 약, 반대는 generic).
- **보험 어휘** — *a premium*(월 보험료), *a deductible*(보험이 지급하기 전에 먼저 내는 본인 부담금), *a copay*(진료 1회당 정액 본인 부담), *out-of-pocket maximum*(연간 본인 부담 상한), *in the network / leaving our network*(보장 의료기관 목록에 있는 / 빠지는), *coverage*(보장), *subsidies*(보조금), *the marketplace*(개인 보험 거래소), *a navigator*(무료 가입 상담원).
- **Medicare / Medicare Advantage** — 65세 이상 연방 건강보험 / 민간 보험사가 운영하는 메디케어 대체 플랜. 가입 변경은 매년 10월 15일~12월 7일.
- **the Essential Plan** — 뉴욕주의 저소득층용 저가 건강보험.
- **My shift ran over.** — "근무가 늦게 끝났어."
- **drop (insurance)** — (보험을) 끊다.
- **I'm the "just" guy.** — "난 늘 '아슬아슬하게' 기준을 넘거나 못 미치는 사람이야." 제시의 자조.
- **Doctor's orders.** — "의사 지시야."
- **I'd rather have the heart attack.** — [농담] "차라리 심장마비가 낫겠다." 레빈 박사는 웃지 않는다.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- **혈압 읽기**: *one forty-two over eighty-eight* — over 앞에서 살짝 끊는다.
- **deductible** [디**덕**터블], **premium** [**프리**미엄], **arthritis** [아r**쓰라**이리스], **appendicitis** [어펜더**사**이리스], **enrollment** [인**로**울먼트].
- **On a scale of one to ten** — 레빈 박사가 일상적으로 묻는 톤. 에디의 대답은 *a six… a three… a one*으로 점점 밝게.
- 레빈 박사는 격식 있지만 따뜻하다. *Please don't say that, Mr. O'Neill.*은 단호하게.
- 테스의 응급실 이야기(*He walked.*)는 짧고 무겁게. 그 뒤 에디의 *That would be me.*는 조용히.
- 마지막 *Chinese has rules.*는 테스의 농담. 짧게 툭.

레빈 박사: "6"이라고 적을게요. 맥주 부분은 빼고요. 붓기는요?
에디: 조금요. 근데 비 올 때만요.

에디: 조금요. 이제 2층에서 멈춰서 쉬어요. 돌로레스한테 인사도 하고요.
에스포지토 부인: 하루도 빠짐없이 인사해요. 아주 다정하죠. 근데 문 너머로 숨소리도 들려요.

레빈 박사: 끔찍하진 않지만 더 낮았으면 좋겠어요. 약은 매일 드세요?
에디: 매일요. 음, 거의 매일요.
에스포지토 부인: 네, 거의 매일이죠. 매주 일요일엔 까먹어요.
에디: 일요일은 쉬는 날이잖아요.
레빈 박사: 심장은 일요일에 쉬지 않아요, 오닐 씨. 그러니 매일 드세요. 돌로레스 씨가 알려 주실 수 있겠네요.

레빈 박사: 이제 중요한 얘기 하나 할게요. 10월이니까 메디케어 가입 기간이에요. 내년 플랜 보셨어요?
