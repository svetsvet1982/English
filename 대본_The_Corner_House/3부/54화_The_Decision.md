# THE CORNER HOUSE — 3부 54화

## The Decision (결정)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 3부 18화(통산 54화, 3부 마지막 화) · 시기: 2026년 11월 23일(월) · 난이도: B1~B2 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 개점 전 더 훅 → 오후의 바 → 밤의 바(문 닫은 뒤)
> - 등장인물: TOMMY, GINA, KOWALSKI, MARCUS, EDDIE, TASHA, MARISOL, MRS. ESPOSITO
> - 사건: 코왈스키가 하버라인의 공식 제안서를 가져온다. 선택지는 둘이다. 내년 6월부터 10년 계약에 월 1만 4천 달러, 또는 3월 31일까지 가게를 비우면 3만 달러. 답은 12월 15일까지다. 같은 날 마커스는 좋은 소식을 받는다. 시의 배달 노동자 충전소 프로그램에 선정됐고, 하버라인은 2년 월 3,300달러로 역제안을 받아들인다. 코왈스키는 "하버라인은 빚이 많다. 숫자가 안 맞으면 판다"고 비공식적으로 흘린다. 토미는 오후 내내 '만약'을 굴리다가 밤에 이웃들 앞에서 결정을 밝힌다. "난 아무 데도 안 가."
> - 학습 포인트: 가정문 종합(If I take… I'll / If I took… I'd / If I had… I would have), 결정·의지 표현(I'm not going anywhere / I've decided / I'm going to fight this / Count me in), 짧은 문장의 힘
> - 미국 현안: 상가 임대료 폭등과 자영업 생존, '조기 퇴거 보상금'(surrender payment), 고금리 속 부동산 투자사의 부채 부담
> - 연결: 토미 계약 만료 2027/5/31, 현 월세 6,500. 39화 은행 거절과 브루클린 네이버후드 펀드. 40화 마커스 역제안(3년·3,200). 47·49화 지나와의 약속. 52화 지나 퇴사(마지막 날 11/25, 남은 휴가 사용 중). 53화 '다리가 일하게 해'. 다음 화부터 4부. 56화 마리솔의 협동조합 제안 복선.

## 대본

[Scene: Monday, 10:00 a.m. The Hook, before opening. Chairs are on the tables. Kowalski stands at the bar with an envelope. Gina sits next to Tommy.]

KOWALSKI: Morning, Tommy. Gina. Sorry to come so early. I wanted to give this to you in person.
TOMMY: In person. That's never good. Good news comes by text.
KOWALSKI: It's the formal offer. For the commercial lease. From Harborline.
GINA: Okay. Two options. Option one: a new ten-year lease. Starting June first. Fourteen thousand dollars a month.
TOMMY: Fourteen thousand. Every month. For ten years.
GINA: With a three percent increase every year. So in year ten, it's about eighteen thousand.
TOMMY: Eighteen thousand. For a bar that makes forty-five thousand a year. Profit.
GINA: Option two: a "surrender payment." If The Hook leaves by March thirty-first, Harborline pays thirty thousand dollars.
TOMMY: Thirty thousand. To leave two months early.
GINA: And we have to answer by December fifteenth. In writing.
TOMMY: And if I say no to both?
KOWALSKI: Then your lease ends May thirty-first. And they find a new tenant. A coffee chain is interested. And a wine bar.
TOMMY: A wine bar. In Red Hook. On this corner.
KOWALSKI: I'm just the messenger, Tommy. I'm sorry.
GINA: Brian, can I ask you something? Off the record?
KOWALSKI: You can ask. I might not answer.
GINA: Why does Harborline want the money so fast? March? That's very fast.
KOWALSKI: Off the record? Interest rates. Harborline bought this building with a big loan. A very big loan. And the construction is over budget.
TOMMY: Over budget. Prime Build. The garbage guys.
KOWALSKI: The roof alone cost twice the estimate. And the DHCR complaint on 3B made the lawyers nervous.
GINA: So Harborline is in trouble?
KOWALSKI: I didn't say that. I said they're "stretched." Companies like Harborline don't keep buildings when the numbers stop working.
TOMMY: What do they do?
KOWALSKI: They sell. They always sell. Okay. I said too much. I have to go.
MARCUS: Wait! Brian! Before you go. Did you get my email? About my lease?
KOWALSKI: Marcus. Yes. Good news, actually. Your counteroffer was accepted. Mostly.
MARCUS: Mostly?
KOWALSKI: Two years, not three. Three thousand three hundred a month. And the charger installation is approved. In writing.
MARCUS: Two years at thirty-three hundred. Until twenty twenty-eight. I can live with that.
KOWALSKI: The city program helped. You were selected, right? Last week?
MARCUS: Yeah! I got the letter on Friday. They're installing the charging cabinet in January. Behind my shop.
TOMMY: Congratulations, Marcus. Really. Come here.
MARCUS: Tommy, I'm sorry. I'm celebrating, and you just got that letter.
TOMMY: Don't be sorry. One of us should get good news. It's you. Good.
KOWALSKI: Okay. I'm really going now. Tommy, take your time. December fifteenth.
TOMMY: I heard you, Brian. December fifteenth. Ride safe. Fix your brakes.

[Scene: 3:00 p.m. The bar is open but quiet. Eddie sits on his stool. Tommy stares at the letter. Tasha cuts lemons.]

EDDIE: You've read that letter nine times, Tommy. It won't change.
TOMMY: I know. I'm doing math. Bad math. "If" math.
EDDIE: "If" math. Okay. Do it out loud. I'm a good listener.
TOMMY: If I take the thirty thousand, I'll pay my debts. The credit card. The floor. I'll have maybe ten thousand left.
EDDIE: And then?
TOMMY: Then I'm fifty-eight. With no bar. And ten thousand dollars. I'll move in with Gina. I'll pick up Luca. I'll get fat.
TASHA: You're already a little fat, Tommy.
TOMMY: If I took the ten-year lease, I'd lose money every month. In two years, I'd be bankrupt. Worse than now.
EDDIE: So option two is impossible. And option one is... what?
TOMMY: Option one is safe. Sad and safe. Like a hospital bed.
EDDIE: And the third option?
TOMMY: There's no third option. Says right here. Two options.
EDDIE: There's always a third option. "No." You say no. And you fight.
TOMMY: Fight with what? I have no money. No lease. The bank said no.
EDDIE: You have a building full of people who said no. I said no to forty thousand. Dolores said no. Marisol said no.
TASHA: I said no to a raise at the wine bar. Did I tell you that?
TOMMY: What wine bar?
TASHA: In Cobble Hill. They offered me two dollars more an hour. Last month. I said no.
TOMMY: Why? Two dollars is a lot, Tasha.
TASHA: Because nobody there knows my name. Here, everybody knows my name. And my mother's name. And my abuela's empanadas.
EDDIE: You see? She stayed. For this.
TOMMY: Tasha, if I had known, I would have given you the raise.
TASHA: You couldn't have. You don't have two dollars. I've seen the books.
EDDIE: Tommy. Let me ask you one question. Only one.
TOMMY: Okay.
EDDIE: If Angela were sitting here. On this stool. What would she say?
TOMMY: That's not fair, Eddie.
EDDIE: I know. I'm old. I don't have to be fair. What would she say?
TOMMY: She'd say... "Tommy, you have a big mouth for a small man. Use it."
TASHA: Ha! That sounds like her!
TOMMY: She'd say, "If they want my bar, they'll have to carry us out. Both of us."
EDDIE: Like the dock rats. We don't leave. We get carried out.
TOMMY: Okay. I need to talk to everybody. Tonight. After closing.
TASHA: Everybody? The whole building?
TOMMY: Everybody who said no. Tell them eleven o'clock. The bar. I'll make coffee.
TASHA: Then nobody will come.
TOMMY: Fine. I'll buy coffee. From the Swedish place. Eight dollars a cup.
EDDIE: Now that's a man who has decided something.

[Scene: 11:15 p.m. The Hook, closed. The lights over the bar are on. Gina, Marisol, Mrs. Esposito, and Eddie sit at one table.]

MRS. ESPOSITO: Tommy, it's eleven fifteen. Some of us have a cat to feed.
TOMMY: Biscuit is fat, Dolores. She can wait. Okay. Thank you for coming. I'll be quick.
GINA: Quick-ish?
TOMMY: Quick-ish. Today Harborline gave me two options. Pay fourteen thousand a month. Or take thirty thousand and leave by March.
MARISOL: That's not an offer. That's a threat with a ribbon on it.
TOMMY: So I sat here all afternoon. Doing "if" math. If I take the money. If I took the lease. If I had done things differently.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And?
TOMMY: And I've decided. I'm not taking either one. I'm not going anywhere.
EDDIE: Say it again. Slower. For the people in the back.
TOMMY: I'm. Not. Going. Anywhere.
MARISOL: Tommy! Yes!
GINA: Dad. Are you sure? This is a big decision. If it goes wrong, you get nothing. Not even the thirty thousand.
TOMMY: I know. I'm sure. If I take their money, I'll regret it for the rest of my life. That's a big regret. You told me, small regrets only.
GINA: I did say that. Okay. Then I'm with you. Count me in.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Count me in too. I'm seventy-six. I've never been in a real fight. Except with the sanitation department.
EDDIE: Count me in. I have Jimmy's hook now. I'm dangerous.
TOMMY: But I need help. I don't know how to fight a company. I know how to pour beer. And make bad coffee.
MARISOL: Okay, so here's the thing. You don't fight them alone. We fight together. Rule number four.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Never sign alone. It's on everybody's fridge.
TOMMY: So what do we do? Tomorrow? Specifically?
GINA: Step one: I write a letter. We reject both options. Politely. In writing. Before December fifteenth.
MARISOL: Step two: I call Danielle Brooks. Commercial tenants have fewer rights. But maybe there's something.
GINA: Step three: I call the Brooklyn Neighborhood Fund. Linda Chen's contact. Friday. My first free Friday.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Step four: I make a list. Every problem with this building since August. With dates. I already have forty-one.
EDDIE: And step five? There's always a step five.
TOMMY: I don't know step five. Brian said something strange this morning. Off the record.
MARISOL: What did he say?
TOMMY: He said Harborline has a big loan. The construction is over budget. He said, "When the numbers stop working, they sell. They always sell."
MARISOL: They sell. Wait. Say that again.
TOMMY: "They always sell." Why? What's that face?
MARISOL: Tommy. Teresa's letter. Sal's letter. Do you still have a copy?
TOMMY: It's in the drawer. Under the register. Why?
MARISOL: What did Sal write? Exactly?
GINA: "Offer it first to the people who live in it. They made it a home. I only made it a building."
MARISOL: The people who live in it. Us.
TOMMY: Marisol, what are you thinking?
MARISOL: I'm thinking... what if Harborline sells? What if the next buyer isn't another company?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Who else would buy a building? Who has that kind of money?
MARISOL: Nobody has that kind of money. Alone. But together? With help? I don't know yet. Give me a week.
EDDIE: A week. I'm eighty-three, Marisol. Make it fast.
MARISOL: A week, Eddie. I promise. Can we meet again? After Thanksgiving?
TOMMY: Thanksgiving. Here. Everybody. I'll cook. Not the coffee. Just the turkey.
GINA: Dad, you're really doing this.
TOMMY: I'm really doing this. Your mother named this place The Hook. Because people get hooked on this corner.
GINA: I know. I remember.
TOMMY: Well, I'm hooked. And hooks don't let go. Go home, everybody. We start tomorrow.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **가정문 종합**
  - 1형(현실적인 미래): *If I take the thirty thousand, I'll pay my debts.* — 실제로 고를 수 있는 선택.
  - 2형(현재·미래의 가정): *If I took the ten-year lease, I'd lose money every month.* / *If Angela were sitting here, what would she say?*
  - 3형(과거에 대한 가정): *If I had known, I would have given you the raise.* / *You couldn't have.*
  - 혼합: *If it goes wrong, you get nothing.* — 일반적 사실처럼 단정한다.
- **결정·의지 표현**
  - *I've decided.* / *I'm not going anywhere.* — "난 아무 데도 안 가." 3부의 핵심 문장.
  - *I'm not taking either one.* — "둘 다 안 받아."
  - *Count me in.* — "나도 끼워 줘." 동참 선언.
  - *I can live with that.* — "그 정도면 받아들일 수 있어."
  - *We start tomorrow.* — "내일 시작한다."
- **surrender payment** — 임차인이 계약을 일찍 끝내고 나가는 대가로 받는 돈.
- **stretched** — 재정적으로 빠듯한, 무리한. *over budget* 예산 초과.
- **a threat with a ribbon on it** — "리본 단 협박." 좋은 척 포장한 압박.
- **I'm just the messenger.** — "난 전달만 하는 사람이야."
- **For the people in the back.** — "뒤에 있는 사람들도 들리게." 다시 크게 말해 달라는 농담.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- **I'm. Not. Going. Anywhere.** — 단어마다 끊어 읽는다. 강세는 *not*과 *any-*. 마지막은 확실히 내린다.
- 짧은 문장의 힘: *I've decided.* / *Count me in.* / *We start tomorrow.* — 짧을수록 천천히, 단단하게.
- 토미의 '만약' 계산은 혼잣말처럼 낮은 소리로. *If I take… / If I took…*의 시제 차이를 들리게 한다.
- 코왈스키의 *Off the record?*는 목소리를 낮추고 주변을 살피듯.
- 마리솔의 *They sell. Wait. Say that again.* — 생각이 번쩍 떠오르는 순간. 속도를 늦추고 *Wait*에서 멈춘다.
- 마지막 토미의 *And hooks don't let go.* — 3부를 닫는 문장. 조용하지만 단호하게.
