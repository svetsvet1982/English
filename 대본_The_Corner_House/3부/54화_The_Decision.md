# THE CORNER HOUSE — 3부 54화

## The Decision (결정)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 3부 18화(통산 54화, 3부 마지막 화) · 시기: 2026년 11월 23일(월) · 난이도: B1~B2 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 개점 전 더 훅 → 오후의 바 → 밤의 바(문 닫은 뒤)
> - 등장인물: TOMMY, GINA, KOWALSKI, MARCUS, EDDIE, TASHA, MARISOL, MRS. ESPOSITO
> - 사건: 코왈스키가 하버라인의 공식 제안서를 가져온다. 선택지는 둘이다. 내년 6월부터 10년 계약에 월 1만 4천 달러, 또는 3월 31일까지 가게를 비우면 3만 달러. 답은 12월 15일까지다. 같은 날 마커스는 좋은 소식을 받는다. 시의 배달 노동자 충전소 프로그램에 선정됐고, 하버라인은 2년 월 3,300달러로 역제안을 받아들인다. 코왈스키는 "하버라인은 빚이 많다. 숫자가 안 맞으면 판다"고 비공식적으로 흘린다. 토미는 오후 내내 '만약'을 굴리다가 밤에 이웃들 앞에서 결정을 밝힌다. "난 아무 데도 안 가."
> - 학습 포인트: 가정문 종합(If I take… I'll / If I took… I'd / If I had… I would have), 결정·의지 표현(I'm not going anywhere / I've decided / I'm going to fight this / Count me in), 짧은 문장의 힘
> - 미국 현안: 상가 임대료 폭등과 자영업 생존, '조기 퇴거 보상금'(surrender payment), 고금리 속 부동산 투자사의 부채 부담
> - 연결: 토미 계약 만료 2027/5/31, 현 월세 6,500. 39화 은행 거절과 브루클린 네이버후드 펀드. 40화 마커스 역제안(3년·3,200). 47·49화 지나와의 약속. 52화 지나 퇴사(마지막 날 11/25, 남은 휴가 사용 중). 53화 '다리가 일하게 해'. 다음 화부터 4부. 56화 마리솔의 협동조합 제안 복선.

## 대본

[Scene: Monday, 10:00 a.m. The Hook, before opening. Chairs are on the tables. Kowalski stands at the bar with an envelope. Gina sits next to Tommy.]

KOWALSKI: Morning, Tommy. Morning, Gina. Sorry to come by so early, but I wanted to give this to you in person.
TOMMY: In person. That's never good, Brian. Good news always comes by text.
KOWALSKI: It's the formal offer for the commercial lease. It comes straight from Harborline.
GINA: Okay, let me read it. There are two options. Option one is a new ten-year lease, starting June first, at fourteen thousand dollars a month.
TOMMY: Fourteen thousand. Every month. For ten years.
GINA: And there's a three percent increase every year, so by year ten, it's about eighteen thousand.
TOMMY: Eighteen thousand a month, for a bar that makes forty-five thousand a year in profit, if we're lucky.
GINA: Option two is something they call a "surrender payment." If The Hook leaves by March thirty-first, Harborline pays us thirty thousand dollars.
TOMMY: Thirty thousand dollars, just to leave two months early.
GINA: And we have to answer by December fifteenth. It has to be in writing.
TOMMY: And what happens if I say no to both?
KOWALSKI: Then your lease ends on May thirty-first, and they find a new tenant. A coffee chain is already interested, and so is a wine bar.
TOMMY: A wine bar. In Red Hook. On this corner, after twenty years.
KOWALSKI: Look, I'm just the messenger, Tommy. I'm sorry, I really am.
GINA: Brian, can I ask you something? Off the record?
KOWALSKI: You can ask. But I might not answer.
GINA: Why does Harborline want this done so fast? By March? That's very fast for a company like that.
KOWALSKI: Off the record? It's the interest rates. Harborline bought this building with a big loan, a very big loan, and the construction is over budget.
TOMMY: Over budget. That's Prime Build, right? The garbage guys.
KOWALSKI: The roof alone cost twice the estimate. And the DHCR complaint on 3B made the lawyers very nervous.
GINA: So Harborline is in trouble? Is that what you're saying?
KOWALSKI: I didn't say that. I said they're "stretched." Companies like Harborline don't keep buildings when the numbers stop working.
TOMMY: So what do they do then?
KOWALSKI: They sell. They always sell. Okay, I've already said too much, so I have to go.
MARCUS: Wait! Brian! Before you go, did you get my email about my lease?
KOWALSKI: Marcus. Yes, I did, and it's good news, actually. Your counteroffer was accepted. Mostly.
MARCUS: Mostly? What does "mostly" mean?
KOWALSKI: It's two years, not three, at three thousand three hundred a month. And the charger installation is approved, in writing.
MARCUS: Two years at thirty-three hundred, until twenty twenty-eight. Okay. I can live with that.
KOWALSKI: The city program helped you a lot. You were selected last week, right?
MARCUS: Yeah! I got the letter on Friday. They're installing the charging cabinet behind my shop in January.
TOMMY: Congratulations, Marcus. Really, I mean it. Come here, give me a hug.
MARCUS: Tommy, I'm sorry, man. I'm standing here celebrating, and you just got that letter.
TOMMY: Don't be sorry. One of us should get good news today, and it's you. That's good.
KOWALSKI: Okay, now I'm really going. Tommy, take your time with it, but remember, December fifteenth.
TOMMY: I heard you, Brian. December fifteenth. Ride safe out there, and fix your brakes.

[Scene: 3:00 p.m. The bar is open but quiet. Eddie sits on his stool. Tommy stares at the letter. Tasha cuts lemons.]

EDDIE: You've read that letter nine times now, Tommy. I counted, and it still says the same thing.
TOMMY: I know, Eddie. I'm doing math. Bad math. "If" math.
EDDIE: "If" math. Okay, then do it out loud. I'm a good listener, and I've got nowhere to be.
TOMMY: If I take the thirty thousand, I'll pay my debts, like the credit card and the new floor. Then I'll have maybe ten thousand left.
EDDIE: And then what?
TOMMY: Then I'm fifty-eight, with no bar and ten thousand dollars. I'll move in with Gina, I'll pick up Luca every day, and I'll get fat.
TASHA: You're already a little fat, Tommy. Just saying.
TOMMY: If I took the ten-year lease, I'd lose money every month. In two years, I'd be bankrupt, and that's worse than now.
EDDIE: So the lease is impossible. And the money is... what?
TOMMY: The money is safe. Sad and safe, like a hospital bed.
EDDIE: And what about the third option?
TOMMY: There's no third option, Eddie. It says so right here. Two options.
EDDIE: There's always a third option, and it's called "no." You say no, and then you fight.
TOMMY: Fight with what? I have no money and no lease, and the bank already said no.
EDDIE: You have a building full of people who said no. I said no to forty thousand dollars. Dolores said no, and Marisol said no.
TASHA: I said no to a raise at a wine bar, actually. Did I ever tell you that?
TOMMY: What wine bar? When was this?
TASHA: It's in Cobble Hill. Last month they offered me two dollars more an hour, and I said no.
TOMMY: Why would you say no? Two dollars an hour is a lot of money, Tasha.
TASHA: Because nobody there knows my name. Here, everybody knows my name, and my mother's name, and my abuela's empanadas.
EDDIE: You see, Tommy? She stayed, and she stayed for this.
TOMMY: Tasha, if I had known, I would have given you the raise myself.
TASHA: You couldn't have, Tommy. You don't have two dollars, because I've seen the books.
EDDIE: Tommy, let me ask you one question. Just one, and then I'll be quiet.
TOMMY: Okay. Go ahead.
EDDIE: If Angela were sitting here, on this stool, what would she say?
TOMMY: Come on, that's not fair, Eddie.
EDDIE: I know it's not. But I'm old, so I don't have to be fair. What would she say?
TOMMY: She'd say... "Tommy, you have a big mouth for a small man. Use it."
TASHA: Ha! That sounds exactly like her!
TOMMY: And she'd say, "If they want my bar, they'll have to carry us out. Both of us."
EDDIE: Like the dock rats, Tommy. We don't leave. We get carried out.
TOMMY: Okay. I need to talk to everybody tonight, after closing.
TASHA: Everybody? You mean the whole building?
TOMMY: Everybody who said no. Tell them eleven o'clock, here at the bar, and I'll make coffee.
TASHA: Well, if you make the coffee, then nobody will come.
TOMMY: Fine. I'll buy the coffee from the Swedish place, even though it's eight dollars a cup.
EDDIE: Now that's a man who has decided something.

[Scene: 11:15 p.m. The Hook, closed. The lights over the bar are on. Gina, Marisol, Mrs. Esposito, and Eddie sit at one table.]

MRS. ESPOSITO: Tommy, it's eleven fifteen already. Some of us have a cat at home who needs to be fed.
TOMMY: Biscuit is fat, Dolores, so she can wait. Okay, everybody, thank you for coming. I'll be quick.
GINA: Quick-ish?
TOMMY: Quick-ish. Today Harborline gave me two options. I can pay fourteen thousand a month, or I can take thirty thousand and leave by March.
MARISOL: That's not an offer, Tommy. That's a threat with a ribbon on it.
TOMMY: So I sat here all afternoon, doing "if" math. If I take the money. If I took the lease. If I had done things differently.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And? What did you decide?
TOMMY: And I've decided. I'm not taking either one. I'm not going anywhere.
EDDIE: Say it again, Tommy. Slower this time, for the people in the back.
TOMMY: I'm. Not. Going. Anywhere.
MARISOL: Tommy! Yes!
GINA: Dad, are you sure? This is a big decision, and if it goes wrong, you get nothing. Not even the thirty thousand.
TOMMY: I know that, and I'm sure. If I take their money, I'll regret it for the rest of my life. That's a big regret, and you told me, small regrets only.
GINA: I did say that, didn't I? Okay. Then I'm with you, all the way. Count me in.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Count me in too. I'm seventy-six, and I've never been in a real fight, except with the sanitation department.
EDDIE: Count me in. I have Jimmy's hook now, so I'm dangerous.
TOMMY: But I need help, because I don't know how to fight a company. I know how to pour beer and make bad coffee, and that's about it.
MARISOL: Okay, so here's the thing. You don't fight them alone, because we fight together. That's rule number four.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Never sign alone. It's on everybody's fridge, Tommy.
TOMMY: So what do we actually do tomorrow? Specifically, I mean.
GINA: Step one: I write a letter that rejects both options. We do it politely and in writing, and we send it before December fifteenth.
MARISOL: Step two: I call Danielle Brooks. Commercial tenants have fewer rights than residential ones, but maybe there's something she can find.
GINA: Step three: I call the Brooklyn Neighborhood Fund, using Linda Chen's contact. I'll do it on Friday, which is my first free Friday.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Step four: I make a list of every problem with this building since August, with dates. I already have forty-one.
EDDIE: And what's step five? There's always a step five.
TOMMY: I don't know step five yet. But Brian said something strange this morning, off the record.
MARISOL: What did he say? Tell me exactly.
TOMMY: He said Harborline has a big loan, and the construction is over budget. He said, "When the numbers stop working, they sell. They always sell."
MARISOL: They sell. Wait. Say that again.
TOMMY: "They always sell." Why? What's that face you're making?
MARISOL: Tommy, think about Teresa's letter. Sal's letter. Do you still have a copy of it?
TOMMY: Sure, it's in the drawer, under the register. Why do you need it?
MARISOL: What did Sal write? I need the exact words.
GINA: "Offer it first to the people who live in it. They made it a home. I only made it a building."
MARISOL: The people who live in it. That means us.
TOMMY: Marisol, what are you thinking? I can see the wheels turning.
MARISOL: I'm thinking... what if Harborline sells? And what if the next buyer isn't another company?
MRS. ESPOSITO: But who else would buy a building? Who has that kind of money around here?
MARISOL: Nobody has that kind of money alone. But together, with some help? I don't know yet, so give me a week.
EDDIE: A week. I'm eighty-three years old, Marisol, so please make it fast.
MARISOL: One week, Eddie, I promise. Can we all meet again after Thanksgiving?
TOMMY: Thanksgiving is here, with everybody, and I'll cook. Not the coffee, though. Just the turkey.
GINA: Dad, you're really doing this, aren't you?
TOMMY: I'm really doing this. Your mother named this place The Hook, because people get hooked on this corner.
GINA: I know, Dad. I remember.
TOMMY: Well, I'm hooked. And hooks don't let go. Go home, everybody. We start tomorrow.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **가정문 종합**
  - 1형(현실적인 미래): *If I take the thirty thousand, I'll pay my debts…* — 실제로 고를 수 있는 선택.
  - 2형(현재·미래의 가정): *If I took the ten-year lease, I'd lose money every month.* / *If Angela were sitting here, on this stool, what would she say?*
  - 3형(과거에 대한 가정): *If I had known, I would have given you the raise myself.* / *You couldn't have, Tommy.*
  - 혼합: *If it goes wrong, you get nothing.* — 일반적 사실처럼 단정한다.
- **결정·의지 표현**
  - *I've decided.* / *I'm not going anywhere.* — "난 아무 데도 안 가." 3부의 핵심 문장.
  - *I'm not taking either one.* — "둘 다 안 받아."
  - *Count me in.* — "나도 끼워 줘." 동참 선언.
  - *I can live with that.* — "그 정도면 받아들일 수 있어."
  - *We start tomorrow.* — "내일 시작한다."
- **surrender payment** — 임차인이 계약을 일찍 끝내고 나가는 대가로 받는 돈.
- **stretched** — 재정적으로 빠듯한, 무리한. *over budget* 예산 초과.
- **a threat with a ribbon on it** — "리본 단 협박." 좋은 척 포장한 압박.
- **I'm just the messenger.** — "난 전달만 하는 사람이야."
- **For the people in the back.** — "뒤에 있는 사람들도 들리게." 다시 크게 말해 달라는 농담.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- **I'm. Not. Going. Anywhere.** — 단어마다 끊어 읽는다. 강세는 *not*과 *any-*. 마지막은 확실히 내린다.
- 짧은 문장의 힘: *I've decided.* / *Count me in.* / *We start tomorrow.* — 짧을수록 천천히, 단단하게.
- 토미의 '만약' 계산은 혼잣말처럼 낮은 소리로. *If I take… / If I took…*의 시제 차이를 들리게 한다.
- 코왈스키의 *Off the record?*는 목소리를 낮추고 주변을 살피듯.
- 마리솔의 *They sell. Wait. Say that again.* — 생각이 번쩍 떠오르는 순간. 속도를 늦추고 *Wait*에서 멈춘다.
- 마지막 토미의 *And hooks don't let go.* — 3부를 닫는 문장. 조용하지만 단호하게.
