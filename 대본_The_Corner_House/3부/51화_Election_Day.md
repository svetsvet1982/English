# THE CORNER HOUSE — 3부 51화

## Election Day (선거일)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 3부 15화(통산 51화) · 시기: 2026년 11월 3일(화) 아침·밤 · 난이도: B1~B2 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 동네 초등학교 체육관 투표소 → 더 훅
> - 등장인물: MRS. ESPOSITO, EDDIE, JESSE, MARISOL, DIEGO, TOMMY, FRANKIE, MARCUS
> - 사건: 중간선거일. 에스포지토 부인은 1994년부터 투표소 선거 사무원이다. 에디는 1964년 이후 한 번도 투표를 빠뜨리지 않았다. 실직한 제시는 처음으로 중간선거에 투표한다. 밤에는 더 훅에서 개표 방송을 보며 가상의 주 하원 후보 두 사람(애나 루이스, 폴 도널리)을 두고 마리솔과 사촌 프랭키가 '정중하게' 맞선다. 토미의 바 규칙: "사람 말고 의견을 놓고 싸워라."
> - 학습 포인트: 선거 어휘(ballot, polling site, poll worker, turnout, candidate, early voting, mail-in ballot, too close to call), 정중한 반대 화법(I see your point, but… / Fair enough. / Let's agree to disagree. / With respect, …)
> - 미국 현안: 2026년 중간선거, 생활비·임대료·소상공인 부담·홍수 대비·치안 등 동네의 쟁점, 투표하지 못하는 영주권자, 개표가 늦어지는 박빙 선거 (실제 정당명·정치인 언급 없음)
> - 연결: 아르투로(30년 영주권자)가 드디어 시민권 신청서를 작성했다(61화 복선). 프랭키는 42화에서 베팅 앱을 지웠다. 제시는 46화에서 실직했다.

## 대본

[Scene: Tuesday, 7:10 a.m. The gym of the elementary school on Sullivan Street. Folding tables, flags, and a long line. Mrs. Esposito sits behind a table with a badge.]

MRS. ESPOSITO: Good morning! Next, please. Name and address?
EDDIE: Edward O'Neill. Van Brunt Street. You know my address, Dolores. You live under me.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Rules are rules, Eddie. I'm a poll worker today. Not your neighbor.
EDDIE: Since when?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Since six a.m. Until nine p.m. Since nineteen ninety-four, actually.
EDDIE: Thirty-two years. They should give you a medal.
MRS. ESPOSITO: They give me two hundred and fifty dollars and a cold sandwich. Sign here, please.
EDDIE: There. My signature looks worse every year.
MRS. ESPOSITO: It looks like a heartbeat. That's good enough. Here's your ballot. And a privacy sleeve.
DIEGO: Mrs. Esposito! You work here?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Diego! No school today?
DIEGO: Schools are closed on Election Day. Because the gyms are for voting.
MARISOL: And because the teachers need a break. Good morning, Dolores.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Good morning, Marisol. Go to the table on the left. Your election district is over there.
DIEGO: Mom, can I fill in the bubbles for you?
MARISOL: No, mijo. Only I can touch my ballot. That's the law.
DIEGO: Then can I watch?
MARISOL: You can stand next to me. But the vote is secret.
JESSE: Hey, everybody. Is this the right line? I've never voted here before.
EDDIE: Jesse! You're up early. For an unemployed man.
JESSE: I'm unemployed, not dead. And I wanted to vote before my brain wakes up and gets nervous.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Name, please?
JESSE: Jesse Moore. I just moved to Gowanus. Am I still at this site?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Let me check. You updated your address in August. Your new site is on Third Avenue.
JESSE: Wait. Seriously? I'm at the wrong place?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Don't panic. You can vote there. It's a fifteen-minute walk. Or you can fill out an affidavit ballot here.
JESSE: What's an affidavit ballot?
MRS. ESPOSITO: A paper ballot in an envelope. They check it later. But your real site is safer. It counts tonight.
JESSE: Then I'll walk. Thanks, Mrs. E. You just saved my vote.
EDDIE: Wait, kid. Is this your first time?
JESSE: My first midterm. I voted for president twice. But the middle ones? I always skipped.
EDDIE: The small ones are the main show. The small ones decide your rent. Your schools. Your streets.
EDDIE: My first vote was nineteen sixty-four. I was twenty-one. My father walked me to the booth.
EDDIE: I haven't missed an election since. Not one. Not even the one with the hernia.
JESSE: Okay. No pressure. Sixty-two years of perfect attendance.
MARISOL: Jesse, don't forget. Turn the ballot over. There are proposals on the back.
JESSE: There's a back? Who designs these things?
MARISOL: Read them before you go in. They're about the city. Not just the candidates.
DIEGO: Mom, why isn't Abuelo here? He always says the news is important.
MARISOL: Abuelo can't vote, mijo. He's a permanent resident. Not a citizen.
DIEGO: But he lived here forever! Forty-two years!
MARISOL: I know. But guess what? Last week he finally filled out the citizenship form.
DIEGO: Really? Abuelo is going to be American?
MARISOL: He already is, in his heart. Now he wants the paper too. He said, "I want to vote before I die."
MRS. ESPOSITO: Tell him I'll be here. I'll be ninety, but I'll be here.
EDDIE: Okay. I'm going in. Wish me luck.
DIEGO: Good luck! Pick the good one!
EDDIE: I always pick the good one. The problem is, the other people pick the other good one.

[Scene: 9:40 p.m. The Hook. The TV shows results. The bar is full and loud.]

TOMMY: Okay, everybody! Quiet! The local results are coming!
FRANKIE: Turn it up, Tommy! I want to hear Donnelly win!
MARISOL: And I want to hear Ruiz win. So turn it up for both of us.
TOMMY: Before anybody says anything, the rule. You know the rule.
MARCUS: Argue about ideas. Not about people.
TOMMY: Thank you, Marcus. And nobody throws anything. Except money. At me.
FRANKIE: Look at the numbers. State Assembly. Donnelly, fifty-one percent. Ruiz, forty-nine.
MARISOL: With thirty percent counted. It's early, Frankie.
FRANKIE: Early, but I like it.
JESSE: I voted! On Third Avenue. I got the sticker. Look.
MARCUS: Nice. Turnout is high this year, they said. Higher than the last midterm.
TOMMY: Good. Everybody's angry. Angry people vote.
FRANKIE: Can I say why I like Donnelly? Politely?
TOMMY: Politely. One minute.
FRANKIE: I run a small business. Painting. Three guys and a van. Every year, more fees. More permits. More taxes.
MARISOL: That's fair.
FRANKIE: And the van. Every time I go into Manhattan, I pay the toll. That's a hundred dollars a week. Donnelly says he'll fight for small guys like me.
MARISOL: I see your point. I really do. Small businesses are struggling. Look at Tommy.
TOMMY: Leave me out of this. I'm the referee.
FRANKIE: And safety. My cousin's store got broken into twice. Donnelly wants more cops on the street.
MARCUS: With respect, Frankie, my bike was stolen too. Twice. But more cops didn't bring them back.
FRANKIE: Okay. Fair enough. But it doesn't hurt.
MARCUS: Maybe. Maybe not. I'm still thinking about it.
TOMMY: Marisol. Your turn. One minute.
MARISOL: Thank you. I like Ruiz because of rent. Half this building is fighting a landlord right now. Half this neighborhood is scared.
FRANKIE: I get that.
MARISOL: Ruiz supports a law. It gives tenants the first chance to buy their building. Before a big company buys it.
FRANKIE: Tenants buying buildings? With what money?
MARISOL: With help. With loans. With a fund. It works in other cities.
FRANKIE: Come on, Marisol. That sounds like a dream.
MARISOL: With respect, Frankie, a lot of things sounded like dreams. Before they happened.
FRANKIE: Okay. I hear you. But who pays for the fund? Guys like me. With more taxes.
MARISOL: Maybe. Or maybe the big companies pay. The ones that buy buildings and empty them.
JESSE: Can I add one thing? Ruiz also wants the flood walls finished. Faster.
FRANKIE: Okay, see, I agree with that. My basement flooded last week. Again.
MARISOL: You agree with something! Write it down, Tommy!
TOMMY: Noted. Frankie and Marisol agree on water. History tonight.
MARCUS: Look. New numbers. Sixty percent counted. Ruiz, fifty percent. Donnelly, fifty percent.
FRANKIE: Fifty-fifty? You're kidding me.
TOMMY: Now it's a real game. Like the Knicks in the fourth quarter.
FRANKIE: Don't talk to me about the fourth quarter. I deleted the app, remember?
TOMMY: I remember. I'm proud of you.
JESSE: So who are you voting for, Marcus? Or did you already vote?
MARCUS: I voted early. On Saturday. No line. Five minutes.
FRANKIE: And? Who did you pick?
MARCUS: That's between me and the ballot, my friend.
FRANKIE: Come on! Just tell me!
MARCUS: I'll tell you this. I picked one person from each side. On different races.
MARISOL: A split ticket! Marcus, you're a mystery.
MARCUS: I'm not a mystery. I'm a small business owner and a tenant. I'm both of you.
TOMMY: That's the smartest thing anybody said tonight.
FRANKIE: Okay. Seventy-five percent counted. Donnelly is up. By three hundred votes.
MARISOL: Three hundred votes. That's nothing. That's two buildings.
MARCUS: And there are mail-in ballots. They count those later. It could take days.
TOMMY: The TV says it. "Too close to call."
FRANKIE: Too close to call. So we go home and we don't know.
MARISOL: That's democracy, Frankie. Sometimes you wait.
FRANKIE: I hate waiting.
MARISOL: Me too. That's one more thing we agree on.

[Scene: 11:30 p.m. The bar is almost empty. Frankie puts on his jacket.]

FRANKIE: Marisol. Before I go. No hard feelings, right?
MARISOL: No hard feelings. Let's agree to disagree. On most things.
FRANKIE: On most things. But not on the water. And not on Tommy's coffee.
TOMMY: Hey! The coffee is not on the ballot.
MARISOL: It should be. Proposal number three. "Fix the coffee."
FRANKIE: I'd vote yes. And I don't vote yes on anything.
MARCUS: See? Red Hook is united. Against one cup of coffee.
TOMMY: Get out of my bar. All of you. Thank you for voting. Go to bed.
FRANKIE: Night, cousin. Hey, if Ruiz wins, you'll tell me about this tenant fund thing? For real?
MARISOL: For real. I'll tell you everything. Bring questions. Bring your angry face.
TOMMY: Thanks, everybody. This was a good night.
JESSE: Nobody won. But it was a good night.
TOMMY: That's a neighborhood, Jesse. Nobody wins. Everybody shows up.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **선거 어휘**
  - *ballot* 투표용지 / *polling site* 투표소 / *poll worker* 선거 사무원 / *election district* 선거구
  - *privacy sleeve* 투표용지 가림 폴더 / *affidavit ballot* 선서 투표용지(명부에 문제가 있을 때 쓰는 임시 투표, 나중에 확인)
  - *early voting* 사전 투표 / *mail-in ballot* 우편 투표 / *turnout* 투표율 / *candidate* 후보 / *proposal* (투표용지 뒷면의) 주민 발의안
  - *split ticket* 선거마다 다른 쪽 후보를 고르는 교차 투표 / *too close to call* 박빙이라 승자를 예측할 수 없음
- **정중한 반대 화법**
  - *I see your point. I really do.* — 상대 말을 먼저 인정한다.
  - *With respect, …* — "외람되지만…" 반대 의견 앞에 붙인다.
  - *Fair enough.* — "그건 일리 있네."
  - *I hear you. But…* — "무슨 말인지 알아. 그런데…"
  - *Let's agree to disagree.* — "서로 생각이 다르다는 걸 인정하자."
  - *No hard feelings, right?* — "기분 나쁜 거 없지?"
- **That's between me and the ballot.** — "그건 나랑 투표용지만 아는 거야." 지지 후보를 밝히지 않을 때.
- **I'm unemployed, not dead.** — "실직했지, 죽은 건 아니야."
- **perfect attendance** — 개근.
- **Turn it up.** — 소리 좀 키워.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- 후보 이름 강세: **RU**-iz [루이스, 첫음절], **DON**-nel-ly [다널리, 첫음절].
- 숫자는 또렷하게: *fifty-one percent* [핍티원 퍼센트], *three hundred votes*.
- **With respect, | Frankie, | …** — 쉼표마다 쉬며 부드럽게. 반대하는 말일수록 목소리를 낮추고 천천히 읽는다.
- *I see your point.*는 *point*를 올리고, 뒤의 *I really do.*로 진심을 실어 내려 준다.
- 부인은 선거 사무원답게 사무적인 톤: *Name and address? / Sign here, please.*
- 마지막 토미의 *Nobody wins. | Everybody shows up.* — 두 문장을 대칭으로, 천천히.
