# THE CORNER HOUSE — 3부 40화

## Marcus's Shop (마커스의 가게)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 3부 4화(통산 40화) · 시기: 2026년 9월 말, 토요일 → 화요일 · 난이도: B1~B2 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: Bell Bikes → 같은 가게(협상) → 바 The Hook
> - 등장인물: DIEGO, MARCUS, LUIS, KOWALSKI(관리 회사 담당자), MARISOL, TOMMY
> - 사건: 12월 31일에 끝나는 마커스의 상가 계약. 관리 회사 담당자 코왈스키가 두 가지 선택지를 내민다. 5천 달러 받고 12월에 나가거나, 1년만 월 4,200달러(60% 인상)에 갱신하거나. 마커스는 소방 점검 통과 기록과 시의 '안전 충전소' 신청을 근거로 3년·월 3,200달러의 역제안을 한다.
> - 학습 포인트: 협상과 조건(as long as / provided that / in writing / a counteroffer / on the table), 미래의 불확실성
> - 미국 현안: 상가 임대료 보호 부재, 사모펀드 리모델링 속 소상공인 퇴출, 배달 노동자용 안전 충전소, 주거 건물의 리튬 배터리 보험 문제
> - 연결: 결정 기한 10월 31일, 답은 2~3주 뒤. 11월 시청 현장 방문 → 80화 확장. 루이스가 배달 기사 지지 서명을 모으기로. 코왈스키는 파크 슬로프에서 자전거 통근(브레이크가 물렁함).

## 대본

[Scene: Saturday, 10:00 a.m. Bell Bikes. Diego puts a new chain on the blue bike. Luis sits on a stool.]

DIEGO: Marcus, the chain is on! Look! It turns!
MARCUS: Nice, mechanic. Now check the tension. Not too tight, not too loose.
DIEGO: Like Goldilocks.
MARCUS: Exactly like Goldilocks. Who taught you that?
DIEGO: Eddie. He says everything in life is Goldilocks.
LUIS: Marcus, mail for you. The guy left it on the counter.
MARCUS: Thanks, Luis. How's the new battery?
LUIS: Perfect. Certified. No balloon. I sleep better now.
MARCUS: Good. That's what I like to hear.
DIEGO: What's the letter? It has the Harborline logo.
MARCUS: Let me see... "Bridgewater Property Management. Re: Your commercial tenancy."
LUIS: That doesn't sound good.
MARCUS: "Your lease expires December thirty-first. The owner is reviewing the use of your space. We'd like to schedule a meeting to discuss options."
DIEGO: What does "the use of your space" mean?
MARCUS: It means they're thinking about what's inside these walls. Maybe a bike shop. Maybe not.
LUIS: If you close, where do the delivery guys go? You're the only shop that fixes e-bikes for a fair price.
MARCUS: I know, Luis. I know.
DIEGO: Your lease ends in December? Not May like Tommy's?
MARCUS: Mine's shorter. I signed for three years in twenty twenty-three. Sal didn't want a long lease for a bike shop.
LUIS: When's the meeting?
MARCUS: They want Tuesday. Ten a.m. Here.
DIEGO: Can I come?
MARCUS: It's a school day, little man. But I'll tell you everything. Promise.
LUIS: Bring somebody. Don't meet them alone.
MARCUS: Rule four. I know. I'll ask Marisol.

[Scene: Tuesday, 10:00 a.m. Bell Bikes. A man in a fleece vest with a tablet.]

KOWALSKI: Mr. Bell? Brian Kowalski. Bridgewater. Thanks for meeting.
MARCUS: Morning. This is Marisol Reyes. She's helping me today.
KOWALSKI: Ms. Reyes. The association president, right? I've heard about you.
MARISOL: Only good things, I hope.
KOWALSKI: Mostly thorough things. Okay. Shall we sit?
MARCUS: Sit on the bench. Watch out for the grease.
KOWALSKI: Thanks. So, I'll get right to it. The owner is planning a major renovation. Including the ground floor.
MARCUS: Including my shop.
KOWALSKI: Including your shop. There are two options. I'll explain both. Then you can think about it.
MARCUS: I'm listening.
KOWALSKI: Option one. You leave at the end of December. The owner pays you five thousand dollars for moving costs. And you get your full security deposit back.
MARCUS: And option two?
KOWALSKI: Option two. You renew for one year. At the new rent. Forty-two hundred a month.
MARCUS: Forty-two hundred? I pay twenty-six hundred now. That's sixty percent more.
KOWALSKI: The market rate for this corner is higher than that. The owner is giving you a discount. Because you're an existing tenant.
MARISOL: A one-year lease? Why only one year?
KOWALSKI: Because of the renovation schedule. The owner wants flexibility.
MARCUS: Flexibility for him. Not for me. I can't plan a business one year at a time.
KOWALSKI: I understand. I'm just presenting the options.
MARISOL: Can I ask what the owner wants to do with the space? If Marcus leaves?
KOWALSKI: Honestly? They're talking about a coffee shop. Or a lobby. For the renovated apartments.
MARCUS: A lobby. They want to turn my shop into a lobby.
KOWALSKI: Possibly. Nothing is decided.
MARCUS: Mr. Kowalski, can I tell you what this shop does? Before you decide?
KOWALSKI: Please.
MARCUS: I fix about thirty bikes a week. Most are e-bikes. For delivery workers. Their bikes are their jobs.
KOWALSKI: Okay.
MARCUS: Without a fair repair shop, they ride with bad brakes. Bad batteries. They get hurt. Or they start fires.
KOWALSKI: Actually, the fires are part of the concern. The insurance company asked about lithium batteries. In a residential building.
MARCUS: I know. That's why I'm careful. I have a fireproof storage box. Certified chargers only. I don't charge anything overnight.
MARISOL: And the fire department inspected the shop in June. Marcus passed. I have a copy.
KOWALSKI: You do? That's... helpful. I didn't know that.
MARCUS: There's something else. I applied to a city program. For a safe charging station. For delivery workers.
KOWALSKI: A charging station? Here?
MARCUS: In the back of the shop. With fireproof cabinets. The city pays for most of the equipment. If I get it.
KOWALSKI: When will you know?
MARCUS: In November. They visit next month.
MARISOL: If Marcus gets it, this corner has a city-funded safety project. Inside your building. That's good for your insurance, actually.
KOWALSKI: Hm. That's an interesting point. I'll mention it to the owner.
MARCUS: So here's my counteroffer.
KOWALSKI: Go ahead.
MARCUS: I'll renew. But for three years. Not one.
KOWALSKI: Three years.
MARCUS: At thirty-two hundred. Not forty-two.
KOWALSKI: That's a big difference. A thousand a month.
MARCUS: As long as I'm here, the building has no empty storefront. No construction in this space. And a safety program the city likes.
KOWALSKI: The owner may not agree.
MARCUS: Then tell the owner a lobby doesn't pay rent. I do.
MARISOL: And one more condition. Provided that Marcus gets the city program, the lease includes permission to install the charging station.
KOWALSKI: Permission for the installation. In writing.
MARISOL: In writing. Everything in writing.
KOWALSKI: Your association really likes writing.
MARISOL: Writing is how tenants survive.
KOWALSKI: Okay. I can't promise anything. I'll take the counteroffer to Mr. Whitlock.
MARCUS: When will I hear back?
KOWALSKI: Two weeks. Maybe three.
MARCUS: And if he says no?
KOWALSKI: Then the two original options stay on the table. Until October thirty-first.
MARCUS: So I have until October thirty-first to decide my future.
KOWALSKI: I'm sorry. I know it's stressful. For what it's worth, I like bike shops.
MARCUS: Do you ride?
KOWALSKI: Every day. From Park Slope. My brakes are a little soft lately.
MARCUS: A little soft. Bring it in. I'll fix it. At the current price.
KOWALSKI: Ha. Okay. Maybe I will.
MARISOL: Thank you for your time, Mr. Kowalski.
KOWALSKI: Thank you, both. I'll be in touch.

[Scene: That evening. The Hook. Marcus and Marisol at the bar.]

TOMMY: So? How did it go with the property guy?
MARCUS: He offered me two bad options. I offered him one good one.
MARISOL: Marcus was great. Calm. Clear. He made a real counteroffer.
TOMMY: What did they offer?

TOMMY: Far from us.

TOMMY: Diego would fix his own bike. You taught him.
MARCUS: He's good. He's really good. Better than I was at ten.

TOMMY: I'll help. I'll paint the wall. For free.

TOMMY: I paint better than I make coffee. That's not hard.

MARCUS: Luis would. He'd get twenty guys to sign.
MARISOL: Then let's do it. Letters from the people who use the shop. The city loves that.
TOMMY: The city loves letters. Dolores loves letters. Everybody loves letters.
MARCUS: Except Richie.
TOMMY: Except Richie. Richie hates letters.
MARCUS: Okay. One more battle. "We'll figure it out."
TOMMY: That's your line. I'll steal it for today. "We'll figure it out."

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **조건 표현** — *as long as …*(~하는 한) / *provided that …*(~라는 조건으로, 격식). *As long as I'm here, the building has no empty storefront. / Provided that Marcus gets the city program, the lease includes permission…*
- **협상 어휘** — *options, a counteroffer*(역제안), *renew*(갱신하다), *on the table*(협상 테이블 위에 있는, 유효한), *I'll take the counteroffer to Mr. Whitlock.*(전달하겠다), *hear back*(답을 듣다), *be in touch*(연락하다).
- **I'll get right to it.** — "바로 본론으로 들어가죠."
- **Re:** — (공문) "~에 관하여."
- **the use of your space** — 공간의 용도.
- **an existing tenant** — 기존 세입자.
- **flexibility for him, not for me** — 같은 단어로 입장 차이를 드러내는 대조.
- **For what it's worth, …** — "도움이 될지 모르겠지만, …" 위로나 사적인 의견 앞에.
- **thorough** — 철저한, 꼼꼼한. *Mostly thorough things.*(좋은 얘기라기보다 '꼼꼼하다'는 얘기)
- **five grand** — 5천 달러(구어). grand = 1,000달러.
- **Goldilocks** — 동화 「골디락스와 곰 세 마리」. "너무 뜨겁지도 차갑지도 않은 딱 알맞은 상태"의 비유.
- **a lobby doesn't pay rent. I do.** — 짧고 강한 협상 논리.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- **as long as** [애즐롱애즈], **provided that** [프러**바**이디드 댓] — 조건절 뒤에서 쉬고 주절을 분명하게.
- **forty-two hundred / twenty-six hundred / thirty-two hundred** — 앞 숫자에 강세. *forty-TWO* vs *thirty-TWO*로 마커스가 대비시킨다.
- **counteroffer** [**카**운터r오퍼r], **renovation** [레너**베**이션], **lithium** [리씨엄].
- 마커스는 화내지 않는다. 낮고 차분하게, 숫자와 사실로 말한다. *Then tell the owner a lobby doesn't pay rent. I do.* — 마지막 *I do*에 무게.
- 코왈스키는 친절하지만 거리를 둔 사무적 톤. 자전거 얘기에서만 사람 냄새가 난다.
