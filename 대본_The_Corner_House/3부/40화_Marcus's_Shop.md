# THE CORNER HOUSE — 3부 40화

## Marcus's Shop (마커스의 가게)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 3부 4화(통산 40화) · 시기: 2026년 9월 말, 토요일 → 화요일 · 난이도: B1~B2 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: Bell Bikes → 같은 가게(협상) → 바 The Hook
> - 등장인물: DIEGO, MARCUS, LUIS, KOWALSKI(관리 회사 담당자), MARISOL, TOMMY
> - 사건: 12월 31일에 끝나는 마커스의 상가 계약. 관리 회사 담당자 코왈스키가 두 가지 선택지를 내민다. 5천 달러 받고 12월에 나가거나, 1년만 월 4,200달러(60% 인상)에 갱신하거나. 마커스는 소방 점검 통과 기록과 시의 '안전 충전소' 신청을 근거로 3년·월 3,200달러의 역제안을 한다.
> - 학습 포인트: 협상과 조건(as long as / provided that / in writing / a counteroffer / on the table), 미래의 불확실성
> - 미국 현안: 상가 임대료 보호 부재, 사모펀드 리모델링 속 소상공인 퇴출, 배달 노동자용 안전 충전소, 주거 건물의 리튬 배터리 보험 문제
> - 연결: 결정 기한 10월 31일, 답은 2~3주 뒤. 11월 시청 현장 방문 → 80화 확장. 루이스가 배달 기사 지지 서명을 모으기로. 코왈스키는 파크 슬로프에서 자전거 통근(브레이크가 물렁함).

## 대본

[Scene: Saturday, 10:00 a.m. Bell Bikes. Diego puts a new chain on the blue bike. Luis sits on a stool.]

DIEGO: Marcus, the chain is on! Look, I turned the pedal and it actually turns!
MARCUS: Nice work, mechanic. Now check the tension, because it can't be too tight or too loose.
DIEGO: Like Goldilocks.
MARCUS: Exactly like Goldilocks. Who taught you that?
DIEGO: Eddie did. He says everything in life is Goldilocks, even coffee and haircuts.
LUIS: Marcus, there's mail for you. The guy left it on the counter while you were in the back.
MARCUS: Thanks, Luis. How's the new battery working out for you?
LUIS: Perfect. It's certified, and there's no balloon this time. I sleep better now, because I'm not worried about a fire at home.
MARCUS: Good. That's exactly what I like to hear from my customers.
DIEGO: What's the letter? It has the Harborline logo up in the corner.
MARCUS: Let me see... "Bridgewater Property Management. Re: Your commercial tenancy."
LUIS: That doesn't sound good. Nobody writes "Re" when they have good news for you.
MARCUS: "Your lease expires December thirty-first. The owner is reviewing the use of your space. We'd like to schedule a meeting to discuss options."
DIEGO: What does "the use of your space" mean? It's a bike shop, so it's used for bikes.
MARCUS: It means they're thinking about what should be inside these walls. Maybe it's a bike shop, and maybe it's not.
LUIS: If you close, where do the delivery guys go? You're the only shop around here that fixes e-bikes for a fair price.
MARCUS: I know, Luis. Believe me, I know.
DIEGO: Your lease ends in December? It's not in May like Tommy's?
MARCUS: Mine's shorter. I signed for three years in twenty twenty-three, because Sal didn't want a long lease for a bike shop.
LUIS: So when's the meeting?
MARCUS: They want Tuesday at ten a.m., right here in the shop.
DIEGO: Can I come? I can sit in the corner and take notes for you.
MARCUS: It's a school day, little man. But I'll tell you everything when you get home, I promise.
LUIS: Bring somebody with you, and don't meet them alone. They always send people who are good at talking.
MARCUS: That's rule four, and I know it by heart. I'll ask Marisol to come.

[Scene: Tuesday, 10:00 a.m. Bell Bikes. A man in a fleece vest with a tablet.]

KOWALSKI: Mr. Bell? I'm Brian Kowalski from Bridgewater. Thanks for meeting with me this morning.
MARCUS: Morning. This is Marisol Reyes, and she's helping me today.
KOWALSKI: Ms. Reyes, the association president, right? I've heard a lot about you at the office.
MARISOL: Only good things, I hope.
KOWALSKI: Mostly thorough things. Okay, shall we sit down?
MARCUS: Sit on the bench, but watch out for the grease. It doesn't come out of fleece.
KOWALSKI: Thanks for the warning. So, I'll get right to it. The owner is planning a major renovation of the building, including the ground floor.
MARCUS: Including my shop.
KOWALSKI: Including your shop. There are two options, and I'll explain both of them. Then you can take some time and think about it.
MARCUS: Okay. I'm listening.
KOWALSKI: Option one: you leave at the end of December. The owner pays you five thousand dollars for moving costs, and you get your full security deposit back.
MARCUS: And option two?
KOWALSKI: Option two: you renew for one year at the new rent, which is forty-two hundred a month.
MARCUS: Forty-two hundred? I pay twenty-six hundred now. That's sixty percent more than I'm paying today.
KOWALSKI: The market rate for this corner is actually higher than that. The owner is giving you a discount because you're an existing tenant.
MARISOL: A one-year lease? Why only one year, if he really wants to keep a good tenant?
KOWALSKI: It's because of the renovation schedule. The owner wants flexibility while the work is going on.
MARCUS: Flexibility for him, not for me. I can't plan a business one year at a time, because nobody buys new equipment for twelve months.
KOWALSKI: I understand, believe me. I'm just presenting the options that I was given.
MARISOL: Can I ask what the owner wants to do with the space if Marcus leaves?
KOWALSKI: Honestly? They're talking about a coffee shop, or maybe a lobby for the renovated apartments.
MARCUS: A lobby. They want to turn my shop into a lobby with a plant and a couch.
KOWALSKI: Possibly. But nothing is decided yet.
MARCUS: Mr. Kowalski, before anybody decides anything, can I tell you what this shop actually does?
KOWALSKI: Please.
MARCUS: I fix about thirty bikes a week, and most of them are e-bikes for delivery workers. For those guys, their bikes are their jobs.
KOWALSKI: Okay.
MARCUS: Without a fair repair shop, they ride with bad brakes and bad batteries. Then they get hurt, or they start fires where they live.
KOWALSKI: Actually, the fires are part of the concern. The insurance company asked about lithium batteries in a residential building.
MARCUS: I know, and that's why I'm careful. I have a fireproof storage box, I use certified chargers only, and I never charge anything overnight.
MARISOL: And the fire department inspected the shop in June. Marcus passed, and I have a copy of the report right here.
KOWALSKI: You do? That's... helpful, actually. I didn't know that.
MARCUS: There's something else. I applied to a city program for a safe charging station for delivery workers.
KOWALSKI: A charging station? Here, in this building?
MARCUS: In the back of the shop, with fireproof cabinets. If I get it, the city pays for most of the equipment.
KOWALSKI: And when will you know whether you got it?
MARCUS: In November. They're coming to visit the shop next month, before they decide.
MARISOL: If Marcus gets it, this corner has a city-funded safety project inside your building. That's actually good for your insurance, not bad.
KOWALSKI: Hm. That's an interesting point, and I'll mention it to the owner.
MARCUS: So here's my counteroffer.
KOWALSKI: Go ahead.
MARCUS: I'll renew, but for three years, not one.
KOWALSKI: Three years.
MARCUS: At thirty-two hundred, not forty-two.
KOWALSKI: That's a big difference. It's a thousand dollars a month.
MARCUS: As long as I'm here, the building has no empty storefront and no construction in this space. And it gets a safety program that the city likes.
KOWALSKI: I have to be honest with you. The owner may not agree to that.
MARCUS: Then tell the owner a lobby doesn't pay rent. I do.
MARISOL: And there's one more condition. Provided that Marcus gets the city program, the lease includes permission to install the charging station.
KOWALSKI: Permission for the installation. In writing, I assume.
MARISOL: In writing. Everything in writing.
KOWALSKI: Your association really likes writing.
MARISOL: Writing is how tenants survive. People forget their promises, but paper stays in the drawer.
KOWALSKI: Okay. I can't promise anything, but I'll take the counteroffer to Mr. Whitlock this week.
MARCUS: When will I hear back from him?
KOWALSKI: Two weeks, maybe three. These decisions usually take a while, so please be patient.
MARCUS: And if he says no?
KOWALSKI: Then the two original options stay on the table until October thirty-first.
MARCUS: So I have until October thirty-first to decide my whole future.
KOWALSKI: I'm sorry, and I know it's stressful. For what it's worth, I like bike shops, and I don't enjoy this part of my job.
MARCUS: Do you ride?
KOWALSKI: Every day, from Park Slope. My brakes have been a little soft lately, though.
MARCUS: A little soft. Bring it in, and I'll fix it at the current price.
KOWALSKI: Ha. Okay, maybe I will.
MARISOL: Thank you for your time, Mr. Kowalski.
KOWALSKI: Thank you, both of you. I'll be in touch as soon as I hear something.

[Scene: That evening. The Hook. Marcus and Marisol at the bar.]

TOMMY: So? How did it go with the property guy?
MARCUS: He offered me two bad options, so I offered him one good one.
MARISOL: Marcus was great. He was calm and clear, and he made a real counteroffer with real numbers.
TOMMY: What did they offer you?
MARCUS: Leave in December with five grand, or stay one year at forty-two hundred.
TOMMY: Forty-two! For your little shop? That's crazy, because people pay less than that for a whole apartment.
MARCUS: I asked for three years at thirty-two, plus permission for the charging station.
TOMMY: Three years at thirty-two. Could you really pay that every month?
MARCUS: It's tight, very tight. But if I get the city program, I'll have more customers, charging fees, and more repairs, so the numbers can work.
MARISOL: And three years is long enough to plan. Honestly, that's the most important part of the deal.
TOMMY: And if they say no?
MARCUS: Then I don't know. Maybe I move to a smaller space in Sunset Park, far from my customers.
TOMMY: Far from us.
MARCUS: Far from you, and far from Diego. Who would fix his bike if I left?
TOMMY: Diego would fix his own bike, because you taught him how.
MARCUS: He's good. He's really good, and he's better than I was at ten.
MARISOL: Let's not think about moving yet. Let's think about November and the city visit.
MARCUS: The city visit. I need the shop to look perfect when they walk in.
TOMMY: I'll help. I'll paint the back wall for you, and I'll do it for free.
MARCUS: Do you paint like you make coffee?
TOMMY: I paint better than I make coffee. That's not hard, believe me.
MARISOL: And what about the delivery guys? Could they write support letters for the program?
MARCUS: Luis would, for sure. He'd get twenty guys to sign it in one afternoon.
MARISOL: Then let's do it. Letters from the people who actually use the shop are exactly what the city loves to see.
TOMMY: The city loves letters. Dolores loves letters. Everybody loves letters.
MARCUS: Except Richie.
TOMMY: Except Richie. Richie hates letters, because they usually mean somebody's angry with him.
MARCUS: Okay, so it's one more battle. "We'll figure it out."
TOMMY: That's your line, but I'll steal it for today. "We'll figure it out."

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **조건 표현** — *as long as …*(~하는 한) / *provided that …*(~라는 조건으로, 격식). *As long as I'm here, the building has no empty storefront and no construction in this space. / Provided that Marcus gets the city program, the lease includes permission to install the charging station.*
- **협상 어휘** — *options, a counteroffer*(역제안), *renew*(갱신하다), *on the table*(협상 테이블 위에 있는, 유효한), *I'll take the counteroffer to Mr. Whitlock this week.*(전달하겠다), *hear back*(답을 듣다), *be in touch*(연락하다).
- **I'll get right to it.** — "바로 본론으로 들어가죠."
- **Re:** — (공문) "~에 관하여."
- **the use of your space** — 공간의 용도.
- **an existing tenant** — 기존 세입자.
- **flexibility for him, not for me** — 같은 단어로 입장 차이를 드러내는 대조.
- **For what it's worth, …** — "도움이 될지 모르겠지만, …" 위로나 사적인 의견 앞에.
- **thorough** — 철저한, 꼼꼼한. *Mostly thorough things.*(좋은 얘기라기보다 '꼼꼼하다'는 얘기)
- **five grand** — 5천 달러(구어). grand = 1,000달러.
- **Goldilocks** — 동화 「골디락스와 곰 세 마리」. "너무 뜨겁지도 차갑지도 않은 딱 알맞은 상태"의 비유.
- **a lobby doesn't pay rent. I do.** — 짧고 강한 협상 논리.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- **as long as** [애즐롱애즈], **provided that** [프러**바**이디드 댓] — 조건절 뒤에서 쉬고 주절을 분명하게.
- **forty-two hundred / twenty-six hundred / thirty-two hundred** — 앞 숫자에 강세. *forty-TWO* vs *thirty-TWO*로 마커스가 대비시킨다.
- **counteroffer** [**카**운터r오퍼r], **renovation** [레너**베**이션], **lithium** [리씨엄].
- 마커스는 화내지 않는다. 낮고 차분하게, 숫자와 사실로 말한다. *Then tell the owner a lobby doesn't pay rent. I do.* — 마지막 *I do*에 무게.
- 코왈스키는 친절하지만 거리를 둔 사무적 톤. 자전거 얘기에서만 사람 냄새가 난다.
