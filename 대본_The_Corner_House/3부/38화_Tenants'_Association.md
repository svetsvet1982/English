# THE CORNER HOUSE — 3부 38화

## Tenants' Association (세입자 협회)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 3부 2화(통산 38화) · 시기: 2026년 9월 중순 · 난이도: B1~B2 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 4B 프리야와 샘의 집 → 2B 에스포지토 부인의 집(협회 세 번째 회의)
> - 등장인물: SAM, PRIYA, MRS. ESPOSITO, EDDIE, MARISOL
> - 사건: 관리 회사가 같은 날 모든 세대에 '자발적 이주 패키지'를 보낸다. 에디 4만, 부인 3만 5천, 마리솔 1만 2천, 결혼을 앞둔 프리야·샘 1만 달러. 결혼 비용이 걱정인 샘과 "이웃을 팔지 말라"는 부인이 부딪치고, 마리솔이 '네 가지 규칙'으로 합의를 이끈다.
> - 학습 포인트: 토론 표현(I hear you, but… / That's not fair. / Let me propose something.), 합의 화법, 표결, 말 끊기와 사과
> - 미국 현안: 바이아웃의 계산법(일시금 vs 평생 오를 월세), 정당 사유 퇴거 보호법(10세대 넘게 가진 집주인에게 적용, 인상률 상한), 무료 세입자 법률 지원
> - 연결: 세입자 변호사 브룩스와 10월 면담 → 45화. 협회 공동 답신. 샘의 식기세척기 농담.

## 대본

[Scene: Tuesday evening. Apartment 4B. Sam holds a letter.]

SAM: Priya, come look at this. It just came from Bridgewater.
PRIYA: Another letter? What do they want now? Is it the inspection schedule?
SAM: No, it's... an offer. They're calling it a "voluntary relocation package."
PRIYA: For us? But we're market rate, and our lease ends in December anyway.
SAM: Read it. "We are pleased to offer you ten thousand dollars if you move out by October thirty-first."
PRIYA: Ten thousand dollars? Just to leave two months early?
SAM: Ten thousand. That's half of the wedding, actually more than half.
PRIYA: Sam, we said we wanted to stay here. We told everyone on the roof that night.
SAM: I know, I know. But think about it. In December, they can raise our rent a lot, or maybe they won't even renew us.
PRIYA: Marisol said that Good Cause Eviction might protect us.
SAM: Might. And "might" doesn't pay for a wedding.
PRIYA: And where would we even go? In October, with a whole wedding to plan?
SAM: My cousin says Jersey City has apartments that are bigger than this one, and they come with a dishwasher.
PRIYA: A dishwasher. You want to leave all our friends for a dishwasher.
SAM: That's not fair. I want to leave a fight that we might lose, and take a sure thing instead.
PRIYA: There's a meeting tonight at Dolores's place. Let's go and just listen first.
SAM: And then what?
PRIYA: And then we decide together. Not tonight, and not alone.
SAM: Okay, together. But I'm bringing the letter with me.
PRIYA: Bring the letter. I bet everybody will have one.

[Scene: 8:00 p.m. Apartment 2B. Chairs in a circle. Everyone holds the same envelope.]

MRS. ESPOSITO: Order, please. This is the third meeting of the Van Brunt Corner Tenants' Association. Now, who has a letter?
EDDIE: I have a letter. They're offering me forty thousand dollars if I leave by January.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Thirty-five thousand for me. Apparently they think I'm cheaper than Eddie.
PRIYA: We got ten thousand, but only if we're out by October thirty-first.
MARISOL: Twelve thousand for me and Diego, and our deadline is November.
SAM: So everybody got one, and on the same day.
MARISOL: That's how it works. Everyone gets an offer at the same time, everyone gets a deadline, and everyone feels the pressure.
EDDIE: Forty thousand. I never had forty thousand dollars in my life, not all at one time.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Eddie! You're not actually thinking about it, are you?
EDDIE: I'm not thinking about it. I'm just saying the number out loud, because it's a big number.
MARISOL: It is a big number, but let's do the math. Eddie, your rent is about nine eighty now, after the new lease, right?
EDDIE: It's nine eighty-two, since July.
MARISOL: A one-bedroom in Red Hook at market rate costs about thirty-two hundred. That's twenty-two hundred more every month.
SAM: So forty thousand dollars would last...
MARISOL: About eighteen months. After that, Eddie pays market rent for the rest of his life, or he leaves the city.
EDDIE: Eighteen months, and then what? Florida, with Michael? No thanks, there are too many old people down there.
MRS. ESPOSITO: See? It's not really money. It's a trap with money inside.
SAM: Okay, but for us, it's different, because we're market rate and our lease ends anyway.
MARISOL: I hear you, Sam. It is different for you. But not completely.
SAM: How is it not completely different?
MARISOL: Because Good Cause Eviction may apply. Harborline owns more than ten apartments in the city, so the small-landlord exemption doesn't apply to them.
PRIYA: What does that mean for us? Can you say it in simple words?
MARISOL: It means that at renewal, they can't raise your rent by a crazy amount without a good reason. There's a limit, and they need a reason not to renew you.
SAM: So what's the limit?
MARISOL: It's tied to inflation, so it's inflation plus five percent, with a cap of ten percent. I'll check the exact number for this year.
PRIYA: So ten percent is the maximum. Not thirty, and not fifty.
MARISOL: Right. And if they try to raise it more than that, you can fight it in housing court.
SAM: Fight it in court, while we're planning a wedding.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Weddings are easier than court, believe me. I've done both.
SAM: Then why are they offering us ten thousand, if they can't just kick us out?
MARISOL: Because a lawyer and a court case cost them time and money too. Ten thousand is cheaper, and you leave quietly.
EDDIE: They want quiet. That's what they're really buying here, quiet.
SAM: Look, I'm not saying we should take it. I'm saying that ten thousand dollars is real, and the rest is just "maybe."
MRS. ESPOSITO: Sam, if you leave, they renovate your apartment and rent it for five thousand. Then the next family leaves, and then the next one.
SAM: That's not my responsibility, Dolores.
MRS. ESPOSITO: It is if you live in a building!
PRIYA: Dolores, please. He's just being careful, that's all.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Careful is good. But selling your neighbors is not careful.
SAM: Whoa. Selling my neighbors? That's not—
MARISOL: Okay. Stop. Everyone, stop. We're not going to fight each other, because that's exactly what they want.
EDDIE: She's right. On the docks, the bosses always tried this trick. They paid one guy more, and then the union broke.
SAM: I'm not breaking anything. I'm just trying to protect my family, my future family.
MARISOL: I know, Sam, and that's fair. Nobody here is going to judge you for it. Right, Dolores?
MRS. ESPOSITO: ...Right. I'm sorry, Sam. I'm scared. When I'm scared, I get loud.
SAM: I'm scared too. When I'm scared, I count money.
PRIYA: We're all scared, and that's exactly why we're here tonight.
MARISOL: Then let me propose something. It's not a decision for each person, but a rule for all of us.
EDDIE: Like a union rule.
MARISOL: Like a union rule. Rule one: nobody signs anything for thirty days, not alone and not under pressure.
PRIYA: Thirty days works for us, because our deadline is October thirty-first. That's more than thirty days away.
MARISOL: Good. Rule two: before anyone signs, they talk to a tenant lawyer, not Harborline's lawyer.
SAM: But where do we find a tenant lawyer? We can't afford to pay a lawyer right now.
MARISOL: There's free legal help in this city, and my office knows a great tenant lawyer named Ms. Brooks. I already called her.
MRS. ESPOSITO: You called a lawyer? Already?
MARISOL: I called yesterday. She can meet all of us in October, for free.
EDDIE: A lawyer for free. Now I've heard everything.
MARISOL: Rule three: we negotiate together. If anyone wants to take an offer, we ask for more together, as an association.
SAM: More? You really think they'd pay more?
MARISOL: Ten thousand is the first offer, and first offers are always low. Ms. Brooks says buyouts in this neighborhood go much higher.
SAM: How much higher are we talking?
MARISOL: For a stabilized apartment, sometimes six figures. For market rate, it's less, but it's still more than ten.
SAM: Huh. So even if we leave, we leave smart, and we do it together.
MARISOL: Exactly. Whatever you decide, you don't decide alone. And you don't decide cheap.
PRIYA: I like that. What do you think, Sam?
SAM: I... yeah. I like that too, and I think it's fair.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And rule four: nobody lets the inspectors in alone. Somebody from the association is always there with them.
EDDIE: With a notebook.
MRS. ESPOSITO: With a notebook, and with a phone for taking photos.
MARISOL: Good. So we have four rules: thirty days, a lawyer first, negotiate together, and nobody alone with inspectors. Let's vote.
MRS. ESPOSITO: All in favor?
EDDIE: In favor.
PRIYA: In favor.
MARISOL: In favor. Sam?
SAM: ...In favor. But I want it written down that we can still leave if we want to.
MARISOL: Of course. Write it down, Dolores: "Every member keeps the right to make their own choice."
MRS. ESPOSITO: Written. And I'm adding, "With the association's help."
SAM: Okay. Then yes, it's unanimous.
EDDIE: And what about Tommy? He's not here, because the bar is busy tonight.
MARISOL: Tommy texted me. He said, "Count me in, whatever you decide. And tell Eddie not to take the forty."
EDDIE: Ha! Tell Tommy I'm not taking the forty. I'm taking the stairs, every day, until I die.
PRIYA: Eddie!
EDDIE: What? It's true. I've done fifty-two years of stairs in this building, and that's still my plan.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Okay, next item. How do we answer all these letters?
MARISOL: We send one letter from the association, for everybody. "We received your offers. Our members are reviewing them with legal counsel. Please direct all communication to the association."
SAM: Legal counsel. That sounds pretty serious.
MARISOL: That's the point. We want them to know that we're serious.
EDDIE: So will they stop sending offers after that?
MARISOL: No, they'll send more, and maybe bigger ones. There may be pressure too, like phone calls and visits.
MRS. ESPOSITO: If they call me, I'll just tell them to call the association.
PRIYA: And what if they knock on our door?
MARISOL: Be polite and take the paper. Say "I'll review it with my lawyer," and then close the door.
SAM: "I'll review it with my lawyer." I like how that sounds, actually.
EDDIE: I'm going to say it to everybody, to the mailman and even to the cat.
MRS. ESPOSITO: The cat already has a lawyer, and it's me.
PRIYA: Sam, are you okay?
SAM: I'm okay. I think we're doing the right thing, even if I'm still thinking about the dishwasher.
PRIYA: Our next apartment will have a dishwasher, and it'll be this one, after we win.
SAM: After we win. Okay, I can live with that.
MARISOL: Meeting adjourned. Thank you, everyone. We did good tonight, and I mean that.
EDDIE: We did good. And nobody threw a plate, so that's a win.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **토론 표현** — *I hear you, Sam. It is different for you. But not completely.*(상대 말을 인정하고 반론) / *That's not fair.* / *That's not my responsibility.* / *Let me propose something.* / *That's exactly what they want.*
- **말 끊기와 사과** — *Whoa. Selling my neighbors? That's not—* / *Okay. Stop. Everyone, stop.* / *I'm sorry, Sam. I'm scared. When I'm scared, I get loud.*
- **합의 화법** — *It's not a decision for each person, but a rule for all of us. / Whatever you decide, you don't decide alone. / Every member keeps the right to make their own choice.*
- **표결·회의 표현** — *Order, please. / Next item. / All in favor? / Unanimous. / Meeting adjourned.*(회의를 마칩니다)
- **a voluntary relocation package / a buyout / a deadline / six figures** — 자발적 이주 패키지 / 바이아웃 / 마감 / 여섯 자리 숫자(10만 달러 이상).
- **a sure thing** — 확실한 것.
- **It's a trap with money inside.** — "안에 돈이 든 덫이야."
- **legal counsel / housing court** — 법률 대리인 / 주택 법원.
- **Please direct all communication to…** — "모든 연락은 ~로 해 주십시오." 공문 표현.
- **I'll review it with my lawyer.** — "변호사와 검토하겠습니다." 압박을 받을 때 쓰는 안전한 대답.
- **Now I've heard everything.** — "별일 다 보겠네."
- **Count me in.** — "나도 끼워 줘, 찬성이야."

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- **I hear you, | but…** — hear you에서 고개를 끄덕이듯 내리고 쉰 다음 but을 분명히.
- 샘과 부인의 충돌은 점점 빨라지다가 마리솔의 *Okay. Stop.*에서 정지. 그 뒤로는 모두 목소리가 낮아진다.
- **voluntary** [**발**런테리], **relocation** [릴로우**케**이션], **exemption** [이그**젬**션], **adjourned** [어**저**r언드].
- 숫자: *forty thousand, thirty-five thousand, nine eighty-two, thirty-two hundred, twenty-two hundred*. 비교되는 숫자는 앞 숫자에 강세.
- 에디의 *I'm taking the stairs, every day, until I die.*는 웃으며 단호하게.

프리야: 우리 다 무서워요. 그래서 오늘 밤 여기 모인 거잖아요.

마리솔: 이 도시엔 무료 법률 지원이 있고, 우리 사무실이 브룩스 변호사님이라는 훌륭한 세입자 변호사를 알아. 벌써 전화해 뒀어.

에디: 공짜 변호사. 별일 다 보겠네.
마리솔: 규칙 3. 함께 협상한다. 누가 제안을 받아들이고 싶으면, 협회로서 함께 더 달라고 한다.
샘: 더요? 정말 더 줄 거라고 생각해요?
마리솔: 1만 달러는 첫 제안이고, 첫 제안은 늘 낮아. 브룩스 변호사님 말로는 이 동네 바이아웃은 훨씬 높대.
샘: 얼마나 높은 얘기예요?
마리솔: 안정화 아파트는 가끔 여섯 자리 숫자까지. 시세는 그보단 적지만, 그래도 1만보단 많아.
샘: 흠. 그럼 나가더라도 똑똑하게 나가는 거네요. 그것도 같이.
마리솔: 바로 그거야. 뭘 결정하든 혼자 결정하지 않는 거야. 그리고 싸게 결정하지 않는 거고.
프리야: 그거 좋다. 샘, 어떻게 생각해?
샘: 나도… 응. 좋아. 공평한 것 같아.

에디: 수첩 들고.
에스포지토 부인: 수첩 들고요. 그리고 사진 찍을 휴대폰도요.
마리솔: 좋아요. 그럼 규칙은 네 개예요. 30일, 변호사 먼저, 함께 협상, 점검 때 혼자 두지 않기. 투표해요.
에스포지토 부인: 찬성하시는 분?
에디: 찬성.
프리야: 찬성.
마리솔: 찬성. 샘은?
샘: …찬성. 근데 우리가 원하면 나갈 수도 있다는 걸 적어 두면 좋겠어요.

에스포지토 부인: 적었어요. 그리고 "협회의 도움을 받아서"라고 덧붙일게요.
샘: 좋아요. 그럼 찬성, 만장일치네요.

마리솔: 토미가 문자 보냈어요. "나도 끼워 줘. 뭘 정하든. 그리고 에디한테 4만 받지 말라고 해."

에디: 왜? 사실인데. 이 건물에서 52년 동안 계단을 탔고, 그게 아직도 내 계획이야.
