# THE CORNER HOUSE — 2부 32화

## Overcharged (과다 청구)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 2부 14화(통산 32화) · 시기: 2026년 8월 1일(토) → 그 주 저녁 · 난이도: B1~B2 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 고와너스의 새 방(제시의 이삿날) → 바 The Hook
> - 등장인물: MEGAN, JESSE, KYLE, TOMMY, MARISOL, MRS. ESPOSITO
> - 사건: 제시가 마침내 '문 달린 방'으로 이사한다. 그 주에 도착한 3B의 임대료 이력에 따르면 카일의 집은 2018년까지 임대료 안정화 아파트였고, 그 뒤 등록만 끊겼다. 카일은 3년 넘게 매달 1,100달러를 더 낸 셈이다. 주 정부에 과다 청구 이의를 제기하기로 한다.
> - 학습 포인트: 법적 표현 입문(file a complaint, evidence, the legal rent, willful, treble damages), 주장과 근거 세우기
> - 미국 현안: 2019년 주법 이후 안정화 해제 금지, 임대료 이력 무료 신청, 과다 청구 6년 소급, 고의일 경우 3배 배상
> - 연결: 리치가 2019년부터 서류를 맡음 → 등록 중단 의혹(매각가를 높이려는 것일 가능성). 에스포지토 부인의 1995년부터의 수첩이 증거가 될 수 있음. 4B·4C 이력도 신청 예정. 제시도 고와너스 방 이력을 신청.

## 대본

[Scene: Saturday, August 1. Gowanus. Jesse carries a box up the stairs. Megan opens the door.]

MEGAN: Jesse! You made it! Welcome home!
JESSE: Home. I really like that word, because I haven't been able to say it in four months.
MEGAN: Do you need any help with the boxes? I'm stronger than I look.
JESSE: There are only three boxes, plus a bag and a plant. Tasha gave me the plant as a going-away present.
MEGAN: That's it? That's all your stuff?
JESSE: When you live on somebody's couch, you learn to live small.
MEGAN: Fair. Okay, here's your room, and here's the window. The real one, which opens to the outside.
JESSE: Wow. Light. Actual sunlight. And a door, which means I can close a door.
MEGAN: You can close it and lock it, too. Here's your key.
JESSE: My own key, and my name on a lease. I finally feel like an adult.
MEGAN: Leo is at work, but he says hi. And Gerald says... nothing, because Gerald never says anything.
JESSE: Where is Gerald? I want to meet him properly.
MEGAN: He's in his tank in the living room. He's sleeping on his favorite rock.
JESSE: Hi, Gerald. I'm the new guy. Please don't escape while I'm sleeping.
MEGAN: One more thing. The landlord sent a welcome letter, and the lease rider came with it.
JESSE: What's a rider? Is it like a horse thing?
MEGAN: It's extra pages with more rules. And there's a page about rent stabilization. It says the apartment is "not subject to rent stabilization."
JESSE: Okay. Is that normal? I've never seen one before.
MEGAN: I think so? Everybody I know gets that page, and nobody reads it.
JESSE: Marisol says everybody should check their rent history, even if the landlord says the apartment isn't stabilized.
MEGAN: Who's Marisol?
JESSE: She's my old neighbor and a paralegal, and she knows everything about leases.
MEGAN: Then ask her, because I've never checked, and we've been here three years.
JESSE: I'll ask her tonight, since I'm working at the bar anyway.
MEGAN: Wait, you have a job at a bar and a marketing job?
JESSE: That's how you pay twelve hundred dollars a month in New York.

[Scene: Wednesday, 7:00 p.m. The Hook. Kyle comes in. Marisol is waiting with a thick envelope.]

KYLE: Jesse! How's the new place? Is it better than my couch?
JESSE: Kyle! It has a door, and a lizard, and I love it. How's Queens?
KYLE: My mom does my laundry, which is great. But she also asks me about my love life every single day. It's... a lot.
TOMMY: What brings you back to Red Hook, Kyle? Did you miss my coffee?
KYLE: No offense, Tommy, but no. Marisol called me, because my rent history finally came from the state.
MARISOL: It came yesterday, to your old address, so I picked it up for you. Kyle, sit down. You'll want to sit down for this.
KYLE: That sounds bad. People only say that when it's bad.
MARISOL: It's not bad for you. In fact, it might be very good. Look at this, because this is the history for Three B.
JESSE: My old couch! I mean, Kyle's apartment.
MARISOL: Look here, at twenty eighteen. The apartment is registered as rent-stabilized, and the legal rent is sixteen twelve.
KYLE: Sixteen hundred and twelve dollars? In twenty eighteen? But I was paying twenty-eight hundred!
MARISOL: Now look at the next years. Twenty nineteen, twenty twenty, twenty twenty-one... there's nothing. No registration at all.
TOMMY: What does that mean? Did they just forget to send it in?
MARISOL: It means the owner stopped registering it and started calling it "market rate." But I don't think he was allowed to do that.
KYLE: Why not? Isn't it his building?
MARISOL: In twenty nineteen, the state changed the law. Before that, landlords could take apartments out of stabilization when the rent got high enough, or after big renovations.
JESSE: And after twenty nineteen? What changed?
MARISOL: After twenty nineteen, mostly no, because that loophole ended. So if your apartment was stabilized in twenty eighteen, it's probably still stabilized today.
KYLE: So my rent... should have been sixteen hundred, plus a few small increases every year?
MARISOL: Something like that, so maybe seventeen hundred now. Not twenty-eight hundred, and definitely not thirty-three hundred.
KYLE: That's... eleven hundred dollars a month that I paid for no reason.
TOMMY: How long were you there, Kyle?
KYLE: Three years. Almost four, actually.
JESSE: Eleven hundred times... forty months? That's forty-four thousand dollars!
KYLE: Forty-four thousand. I need to sit down again, and I'm already sitting.
MARISOL: Don't spend it yet. First, we need to file a complaint with the state housing agency, and it's called a rent overcharge complaint.
KYLE: How do I do that? Do I need a lawyer, or can I do it myself?
MARISOL: You can file it yourself, because it's just a form. But a lawyer helps, especially if the owner fights.
TOMMY: Richie will fight. Richie fights about everything, even the price of ice.
KYLE: Okay. So what do I need for the complaint?
MARISOL: You need evidence. That means your leases, all of them, and proof that you paid, like bank statements. And this rent history, of course.
KYLE: I have my leases in a box at my mom's, in my old bedroom closet.
MARISOL: Good. And what about your payments? Did you pay by check, or did you pay online?
KYLE: Online, through the landlord's app. I think I can download the whole history from there.
MARISOL: Perfect, because that's strong evidence. It shows every month, with dates and amounts.
JESSE: So what's the argument? Like, what do you actually say in the complaint?
MARISOL: You say: The apartment was stabilized. It was never legally deregulated. I was charged more than the legal rent. I want the difference back.
TOMMY: Can he get all of it back? All forty-four thousand?
MARISOL: The law lets you go back six years, so yes, all of his time there. And if the overcharge was willful...
KYLE: Willful? What does that mean?
MARISOL: It means on purpose, if the owner knew what he was doing. Then the penalty can be three times the overcharge, and that's called treble damages.
KYLE: Three times? That's a hundred and thirty-two thousand?
MARISOL: That's the maximum, and it depends on the evidence. The agency decides, and it can take a long time, maybe a year or more.
KYLE: A year. Okay. For that kind of money, I can wait a year.
TOMMY: Wait. If Three B is stabilized... what about the other apartments? What about Priya and Sam, or the empty one?
MARISOL: That's exactly what I was thinking. We should request the rent history for every apartment, including Four B and Four C.
JESSE: And what about my new place in Gowanus? The rider says it's "not subject to rent stabilization."
MARISOL: Request it anyway, because it's free. A rider is the landlord's opinion. The history is the record.
JESSE: The landlord's opinion. I like that a lot. I'll request it tonight after my shift.
KYLE: Marisol, why would Richie do this? Do you think he knew?
MARISOL: I don't know yet. Maybe Sal's office made a mistake years ago, or maybe somebody did it on purpose.
TOMMY: Sal wasn't like that, because Sal was old school. But Sal stopped doing the paperwork years ago, and Richie took over.
MARISOL: Then the real question is who stopped the registrations, and why they did it.
JESSE: Is it because a stabilized apartment is worth less to a buyer?
MARISOL: Exactly. A building full of market-rate apartments sells for more money. A lot more.
TOMMY: So Richie makes the building look more expensive on paper, and then he sells it to Harborline.
MARISOL: That's a theory. Not a fact. Not yet. But I have to admit that it's a strong theory.
KYLE: So my complaint... could actually help the whole building, not just me?
MARISOL: It could. If the state agrees that Three B is stabilized, it becomes a public record, and Harborline will see it. It changes the math.
TOMMY: It changes the math. I like that. For once, the math is on our side instead of theirs.
KYLE: Then I'm filing, definitely. I'm doing it for me, and for the building.
JESSE: Even though you moved to Queens?
KYLE: Even though. I lived here four years, and Mrs. Esposito yelled at me for four years. That makes it my building.
TOMMY: That's the most Brooklyn thing anyone has ever said in this bar.
MARISOL: Okay, let's do this properly. Kyle, bring your leases on Saturday, and I'll help you fill out the form.
KYLE: Saturday. I'll be here, I promise.
JESSE: Can I help too? I'm really good with spreadsheets, because I use them all day at my marketing job.
MARISOL: Yes. Make a table that shows every month, what he paid, what the legal rent was, and the difference.
JESSE: A spreadsheet of injustice. That's my specialty.
TOMMY: Gina will be jealous when she hears about this, because she loves spreadsheets more than people.

[The door opens. Mrs. Esposito with her clipboard.]

MRS. ESPOSITO: What's going on here? Why is everybody so serious on a Wednesday night?
TOMMY: Dolores, sit down. It's about Kyle's apartment. It was stabilized the whole time.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I knew it! I knew it! I told Sal in twenty nineteen, "Why is the young man paying so much?"
MARISOL: You told Sal? What did he say to you?
MRS. ESPOSITO: He said, "Ask Richie. Richie does the papers now."
KYLE: "Ask Richie." Just like the note under the door.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Like the note. Everybody says "ask Richie," but nobody ever gets an answer from Richie.
MARISOL: Dolores, do you remember the date when you told Sal?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Of course, because I write everything down. Let me check my notebooks, since I have notebooks from nineteen ninety-five.
TOMMY: Of course you do.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And all of you laughed at my notebooks for years.
JESSE: Nobody's laughing now, Mrs. Esposito.
MARISOL: If you have a note from twenty nineteen, that's evidence. It shows the owner was told about the problem, so it helps with "willful."
MRS. ESPOSITO: Then I'll find it tonight, even if I have to stay up until midnight.
KYLE: Mrs. Esposito, I'm sorry about the party. Actually, I'm sorry about all the parties.
MRS. ESPOSITO: You were loud, but you were also overcharged. Those are two different crimes, and only one of them is yours.
KYLE: Ha. Fair enough.
TOMMY: Okay, everybody, a round on me. This one's for Kyle, our first fighter.
KYLE: Can I just have water? I have to drive back to Queens, and my mom's waiting for me.
JESSE: His mom is waiting for him. At thirty years old.
KYLE: Hey. My mom makes dinner. And I might get forty-four thousand dollars back, or maybe a hundred thirty-two.
MARISOL: Don't count it yet, because the agency is slow. Very slow.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Slow is fine. I'm a patient woman, and I have notebooks.
TOMMY: And now we have evidence. For the first time, we have something real on paper, not just a note under the door.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **법률 기본 어휘** — *the legal rent*(법정 월세), *registered*(등록된), *deregulated*(규제가 해제된), *a rent overcharge complaint*(과다 청구 이의 제기), *file a complaint*(이의를 제기하다), *evidence*(증거), *a public record*(공적 기록), *willful*(고의적인), *treble damages*(3배 배상), *the agency*(주택 담당 기관).
- **주장 세우기(마리솔의 문장 구조)** — 사실 → 법 → 결론 → 요구. *The apartment was stabilized. It was never legally deregulated. I was charged more than the legal rent. I want the difference back.*
- **That's a theory. Not a fact. Not yet.** — 추측과 사실을 구분하는 신중한 말하기.
- **a lease rider** — 임대 계약서에 붙은 추가 조항 문서.
- **not subject to (rent stabilization)** — (임대료 안정화) 적용 대상이 아님.
- **A rider is the landlord's opinion. The history is the record.** — "부속 조항은 집주인 의견이고, 이력은 기록이다." 대조 구문.
- **No offense, but…** — "기분 나쁘게 듣지 마, 근데…"
- **What brings you back?** — "무슨 일로 다시 왔어?"
- **It changes the math.** — "계산이 달라진다(판이 바뀐다)."
- **old school** — 구식의, 옛날 방식대로 정직한.
- **take over** — (일을) 넘겨받다.
- **Don't count it yet.** — "아직 세지 마(김칫국 마시지 마)." (Don't count your chickens before they hatch.)

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- **연도와 금액을 구분해서 읽기**: *twenty eighteen*(연도) / *sixteen twelve*(1,612달러) / *twenty-eight hundred*(2,800달러). 같은 숫자 리듬이라 맥락으로 구분된다.
- **forty-four thousand** [포r리 포r 싸우즌], **a hundred and thirty-two thousand**.
- **deregulated** [디**레**귤레이티드], **registration** [레지스**트레**이션], **treble** [트레블], **willful** [윌펄].
- 마리솔의 설명은 단계마다 쉬고, 중요한 단어(*stabilized, never, more, back*)에 강세.
- 부인의 *I knew it! I knew it!*은 크고 빠르게. 오랜 의심이 풀리는 순간.
- 마지막 토미의 대사는 조용하지만 단단하게. 2부의 전환점이다.
