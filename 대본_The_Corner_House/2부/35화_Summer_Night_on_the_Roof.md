# THE CORNER HOUSE — 2부 35화

## Summer Night on the Roof (옥상의 여름밤)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 2부 17화(통산 35화) · 시기: 2026년 8월 말, 토요일 밤 · 난이도: B1~B2 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 건물 옥상 (긴 식탁, 촛불, 맨해튼 야경)
> - 등장인물: MRS. ESPOSITO, DIEGO, MARISOL, TOMMY, EDDIE, PRIYA, JESSE, SAM
> - 사건: 에스포지토 부인이 13화부터 숨겨 온 계획을 실행한다. 어머니의 접시 열두 개와 한 번도 쓰지 않은 식탁보로 건물 사람들을 옥상 만찬에 초대한다. 토미와 30년 앙금을 풀고, 젊은 이웃들은 "Have you ever…?" 게임을 하다 집을 잃을까 두려운 마음을 꺼낸다.
> - 학습 포인트: 회상, 화해 표현(No hard feelings / I'm apologizing for the tone), 현재완료 경험(Have you ever…? / I've never… / I've lived here for…)
> - 미국 현안: 불안정한 주거 속 '오래 머물 곳'에 대한 갈망, 이웃 공동체
> - 연결: 디에고의 소원 "이사 가지 않게 해 주세요" → 36화 매각 확정. 에디는 1976년 200주년 불꽃놀이를 이 옥상에서 봤다. 에스포지토 부인은 이 건물에 31년째.

## 대본

[Scene: Saturday, 7:30 p.m. The roof. A long table with a white tablecloth. Mrs. Esposito sets out blue plates.]

MRS. ESPOSITO: Careful with those plates, Diego, because they're from Italy. They're from nineteen fifty-five, which makes them older than your grandmother.
DIEGO: I'm being so careful. Look, I'm walking like a robot so that I don't trip.
MARISOL: Dolores, this is beautiful. The lights, the flowers, and the tablecloth all look like something from a movie.
MRS. ESPOSITO: The tablecloth was my mother's too. I've never used it, not once, until tonight.
MARISOL: Never? Why not? It's too pretty to keep in a drawer.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I was always waiting for a special occasion. And then I waited thirty years, and the special occasion never came.
DIEGO: So is tonight a special occasion?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Tonight, I decided it is. That's how special occasions work. You decide.
MARISOL: How many people are coming tonight?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Twelve. I have twelve plates, so exactly twelve people are coming. I counted three times this afternoon.
DIEGO: Who's coming? Do I know all of them?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Everybody in the building is coming. Eddie, Tommy, Gina and Luca, Priya and Sam, you two, and Jesse. And Tasha and Marcus, because they're almost the building.
MARISOL: That's... eleven, if I counted right. Plus you, it's twelve.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Plus me, it's twelve. I've done the math, because I'm the secretary and that's my job.
DIEGO: Does Tommy know about the dinner?
MRS. ESPOSITO: No. Tommy thinks he's coming up here to fix my air conditioner, on the roof.
MARISOL: Ha! He's going to be so surprised when he walks through that door.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Good. Tommy surprised me with eight-dollar beer, so now I surprise him.
DIEGO: What are we eating? Please say there's dessert.
MRS. ESPOSITO: We're having my lasagna, two whole trays. Tasha is bringing empanadas, and Priya is bringing samosas.
DIEGO: Lasagna and empanadas and samosas! That's the best dinner ever!
MARISOL: That's the most New York dinner I've ever heard of, honestly.
MRS. ESPOSITO: That's the point. Okay, let's put the candles on the table. Diego, the matches are in my bag.
DIEGO: Can I light them myself? I'm ten now, so I'm old enough.
MARISOL: You can do it with me, and you have to be careful.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Here they come. I can hear Eddie on the stairs, because he always sings when he climbs.
MARISOL: Really? What does he sing?
MRS. ESPOSITO: It's the same song every time, "Danny Boy." He sings it very slowly, one step and one word.
DIEGO: Shh! They're coming! Everybody, hide!
MARISOL: We can't hide, Diego. We're on a roof, and there's nothing up here but a table.

[The roof door opens. Tommy carries a toolbox. Eddie is behind him.]

TOMMY: Dolores? Where's the air conditioner? You said it was broken and that it was an emergency... Whoa.
EDDIE: Surprise, Tommy! You should see your face right now.
TOMMY: What is this? A table? Candles? Lasagna? Am I dreaming?
MRS. ESPOSITO: It's a dinner for the building. These are my twelve plates, and they're finally being used.
TOMMY: Dolores Esposito made a dinner party for us. I've known you for twenty years, and I've never seen anything like this.
MRS. ESPOSITO: You've never been invited before, that's why. Sit down. You're next to me.
TOMMY: Next to you? Should I be worried about that?
MRS. ESPOSITO: A little, yes. Now sit.
PRIYA: Dolores, this is incredible, and thank you so much for inviting us. I brought samosas, and they're my mom's recipe.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Thank you, dear. Put them next to the empanadas, because I think they'll be good friends.
EDDIE: I haven't been on this roof since the Bicentennial, back in nineteen seventy-six. Peggy and I watched the fireworks from right here.
PRIYA: From here? Not from the pier? I thought everybody went to the water.
EDDIE: The pier was for the ships, and the roof was for the fireworks. You could see everything from up here: Manhattan, the Statue, and the whole harbor.
TOMMY: Look at it now, though. Look at Manhattan, all lit up like a birthday cake.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Okay. Before we eat, I want to say something. Tommy, don't make a face.
TOMMY: I didn't make a face. I was just sitting here.
MRS. ESPOSITO: You were going to. Listen. I've lived in this building for thirty-one years, and I've complained a lot.
EDDIE: A lot.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Eddie, thank you. I've complained about the noise, the sign, the trash, and the beer prices.
TOMMY: And the sign. Two feet is two feet, as you always say.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Two feet is two feet, and that's still true. But this summer, I learned something important.
PRIYA: What did you learn, Dolores?
MRS. ESPOSITO: When the note came, nobody complained. Everybody helped: Marisol, Kyle, Jesse, Tommy, and even the cat.
EDDIE: Especially the cat. That cat deserves a medal.
MRS. ESPOSITO: So tonight, I want to say thank you. And I'm sorry. For all the complaining.
TOMMY: Dolores... You don't have to apologize to anybody here.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I'm not apologizing for the rules. The rules were right. I'm apologizing for the tone.
TOMMY: Ha. Okay, then I'm sorry too, for the eight-dollar beer and for the sign.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Does that mean you're going to move the sign?
TOMMY: No, I'm not going to move it. But I'm sorry about it.
MRS. ESPOSITO: That's very Brooklyn of you. No hard feelings.
TOMMY: No hard feelings. Truly.
EDDIE: Now kiss and make up, you two.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Eddie! Absolutely not, not in a million years.
PRIYA: Can I add something? Sam and I have only been here for two years. But this summer, it finally felt like home, and that's because of all of you.
TOMMY: Two years is enough, so you're family now. That means you suffer with us.
PRIYA: We'll suffer with you, and we'll do it happily.
EDDIE: To the building. Twelve plates, one building.
TOMMY: To the building, and to Dolores, who is the best secretary on Van Brunt Street.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I'm the only secretary on Van Brunt Street, so that's easy.
PRIYA: To Dolores!
EDDIE: Now, the lasagna, before it gets cold. My stomach has been waiting since the stairs, and it's an old stomach.
TOMMY: Dolores, this lasagna... This is incredible. Who taught you how to make it?
MRS. ESPOSITO: My mother taught me, with these plates, in nineteen sixty-two. That was the year my brother broke one of them.
EDDIE: The famous chip! Everybody in Red Hook has heard about that chip.

[Scene: 10:00 p.m. The older neighbors have gone down. The young ones sit at the end of the table.]

JESSE: Okay, who wants to play a game? Tess taught me this one, and it's called "Have you ever."
SAM: How does it work? Are there points?
JESSE: There are no points. Someone asks, "Have you ever...?" And then everyone answers, honestly.
DIEGO: Can I play too? Please?
MARISOL: You can play, but only the kid version.
JESSE: Okay, the kid version. I'll start. Have you ever been to the top of the Empire State Building?
DIEGO: Yes! I went in third grade with my class, and it was so windy that my hat almost flew away.
PRIYA: Never. I've lived in New York for twelve years, but I've never been up there.
SAM: Me neither. Locals never go, because it's only for tourists.
MARISOL: I went once. In two thousand nine. It was on a first date, and the guy was afraid of heights.
JESSE: On a first date? Wait, was it Diego's dad?
MARISOL: No, thank God, it wasn't. That guy cried in the elevator all the way down.
DIEGO: Ha! He cried! A grown-up cried in an elevator!
SAM: Okay, it's my turn now. Have you ever eaten a whole pizza by yourself?
JESSE: Yes, I have. It was last year, during a very bad week that I don't want to talk about.
DIEGO: Not yet. But I really want to try someday, maybe on my birthday.
MARISOL: Not in this house, you won't.
PRIYA: Here's a harder one. Have you ever lived somewhere for more than ten years?
MARISOL: Yes, in this building. I've been here ten years, ever since Diego was born.
SAM: Never. I moved every two years, first for college and then for jobs. This is the longest I've stayed anywhere as an adult.
JESSE: Never, not since I left home. I went to college, and then I lived in four apartments in five years. And on a couch.
DIEGO: My whole life! I've lived here my whole life, so I win!
PRIYA: That's the dream, right? You find one place, and you stay there for a long time.
SAM: That's why we want to stay here after the wedding. If they let us, I mean.
MARISOL: If they let us. That's the question for all of us now, isn't it?
JESSE: Have you ever... been scared of losing your home?
MARISOL: Jesse, that's not the kid version.
DIEGO: I'm scared, a little. When Mom reads letters at night, she makes a face, and she doesn't know I'm watching.
MARISOL: Oh, baby. I didn't know you saw that.
DIEGO: I see everything, just like Tommy does. I'm a bartender.
PRIYA: Ha! He's a bartender now.
MARISOL: Listen, Diego. Whatever happens, we're together. And the building is together now, too. Just look at this table.
DIEGO: Twelve plates.
MARISOL: Twelve plates, one building. Remember that.
SAM: Have you ever seen a shooting star? Look! Up there! Did you see it?
DIEGO: I saw it! I saw it! It went right over Manhattan!
JESSE: Make a wish, everybody, quick, before it's too late.
PRIYA: Done.
SAM: Done.
MARISOL: Done. Diego, what about you?
DIEGO: I can't say it out loud, or it won't come true.
JESSE: Eddie says that on the roof, you can say it. Roof rules.
DIEGO: Okay. I wish we never have to move.
MARISOL: Me too, baby. Me too.
JESSE: Then that's the wish, for all twelve plates.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **현재완료로 경험 말하기** — *Have you ever been to…? / I've never been up there. / I've never used it, not once, until tonight. / I've lived in this building for thirty-one years / This is the longest I've stayed anywhere as an adult.* 과거의 특정 시점 없이 '지금까지'의 경험을 말할 때.
- **과거형과의 차이** — 시점이 분명하면 과거형. *I went once. In two thousand nine.*
- **Me neither.** — "나도 (안 해 봤어)." 부정문에 동의할 때. 긍정이면 Me too.
- **화해 표현** — *I'm sorry. For all the complaining. / I'm not apologizing for the rules. / I'm apologizing for the tone. / No hard feelings.*(앙금 없어요) / *kiss and make up*(화해하다, 원래는 '키스하고 화해하다'라는 관용구).
- **a special occasion** — 특별한 날. *That's how special occasions work. You decide.*
- **don't make a face** — 인상 쓰지 마.
- **You suffer with us.** — "이제 우리랑 같이 고생하는 거야." 가족으로 받아들이는 농담.
- **Not in this house, you won't.** — "이 집에선 안 될걸." 부모의 단호한 말투.
- **a shooting star / make a wish** — 별똥별 / 소원을 빌다.
- **Roof rules.** — "옥상 규칙이야." 즉석에서 만든 규칙.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- **Have you ever…?** [해뷰 에버r] — have you가 [해뷰]로 붙는다. 끝을 올린다.
- **I've never** [아이브 네버r] — 've를 분명히. *I never…*로 들리지 않도록.
- 부인의 연설은 한 문장씩 끊어서, 진심을 담아 천천히. *I'm apologizing for the tone.*에서 미소.
- **No hard feelings. / No hard feelings. Truly.** — 두 사람이 같은 말을 주고받는다. 두 번째는 더 따뜻하게.
- 게임 장면은 가볍게 시작해 *Have you ever... been scared of losing your home?*에서 분위기가 가라앉는다. 그 앞에서 한 박자 쉰다.
- 디에고의 소원은 작고 또렷하게. 마지막 대사는 조용히.

디에고: 엄청 조심하고 있어요. 보세요, 안 넘어지려고 로봇처럼 걷고 있어요.
마리솔: 돌로레스, 정말 아름다워요. 조명이랑 꽃이랑 식탁보까지 전부 영화에 나오는 것 같아요.
에스포지토 부인: 식탁보도 우리 엄마 거예요. 한 번도 안 썼어요. 단 한 번도. 오늘 밤 전까지는.
마리솔: 한 번도요? 왜요? 서랍에 넣어 두기엔 너무 예쁜데요.
에스포지토 부인: 늘 특별한 날을 기다렸거든요. 그러다 30년을 기다렸는데, 특별한 날은 끝내 안 오더라고요.
디에고: 그럼 오늘이 특별한 날이에요?
에스포지토 부인: 오늘 밤은 내가 그렇다고 정했어. 특별한 날은 원래 그렇게 생기는 거야. 정하는 거야.
마리솔: 오늘 밤 몇 명이나 와요?
에스포지토 부인: 열두 명. 접시가 열두 개니까 딱 열두 명이 와요. 오늘 오후에 세 번 셌어요.
디에고: 누가 와요? 제가 다 아는 사람이에요?
에스포지토 부인: 건물 사람 전부 와. 에디, 토미, 지나랑 루카, 프리야랑 샘, 너희 둘, 그리고 제시. 그리고 타샤랑 마커스. 그 둘도 거의 건물 사람이니까.
마리솔: 제가 맞게 셌으면… 열한 명이네요. 부인까지 열두 명.
에스포지토 부인: 나까지 열두 명. 계산 다 했어요. 내가 서기고, 그게 내 일이니까요.
디에고: 토미 아저씨도 저녁 얘기 알아요?
에스포지토 부인: 아니. 토미는 옥상에 내 에어컨 고치러 올라오는 줄 알아.
마리솔: 하하! 저 문으로 들어오면 엄청 놀라겠다.
에스포지토 부인: 잘됐지. 토미는 8달러 맥주로 날 놀라게 했으니까, 이번엔 내가 놀라게 하는 거예요.
디에고: 뭐 먹어요? 디저트 있다고 해 주세요.
에스포지토 부인: 내 라자냐, 두 판 통째로. 타샤가 엠파나다 가져오고, 프리야가 사모사 가져와.
디에고: 라자냐에 엠파나다에 사모사! 역대 최고의 저녁이에요!
마리솔: 솔직히 제가 들어 본 저녁 중에 제일 뉴욕다운 저녁이네요.
에스포지토 부인: 그게 핵심이에요. 좋아요, 촛불을 식탁에 올리자. 디에고, 성냥은 내 가방에 있어.
디에고: 제가 직접 켜도 돼요? 저 이제 열 살이라 그 정도는 할 수 있어요.
마리솔: 엄마랑 같이 하면 돼. 그리고 조심해야 해.
에스포지토 부인: 온다. 에디가 계단 오르는 소리가 들려. 계단 오를 때 늘 노래를 부르거든.
마리솔: 정말요? 무슨 노래요?
에스포지토 부인: 매번 같은 노래, "대니 보이." 아주 천천히 불러. 한 계단에 한 단어씩.
디에고: 쉿! 온다! 다들 숨어요!
마리솔: 못 숨어, 디에고. 여기 옥상이고, 식탁 말곤 아무것도 없어.
토미: 돌로레스? 에어컨은 어디 있어요? 고장 나서 급하다면서요… 우와.
에디: 깜짝 놀랐지, 토미! 지금 네 얼굴 좀 봐야 하는데.
토미: 이게 뭐예요? 식탁? 촛불? 라자냐? 내가 꿈꾸는 건가?
에스포지토 부인: 건물 사람들을 위한 저녁이에요. 내 접시 열두 개인데, 드디어 쓰네요.
토미: 돌로레스 에스포지토가 우리를 위해 저녁 파티를 열었다. 부인을 안 지 20년인데, 이런 건 처음 봐요.
에스포지토 부인: 초대받은 게 처음이니까 그렇죠. 앉아요. 내 옆자리예요.
토미: 부인 옆자리요? 그거 걱정해야 하나요?
에스포지토 부인: 네, 조금은요. 이제 앉아요.
프리야: 돌로레스, 정말 대단해요. 초대해 주셔서 너무 감사해요. 사모사 가져왔는데, 우리 엄마 레시피예요.
에스포지토 부인: 고마워요. 엠파나다 옆에 둬요. 둘이 좋은 친구가 될 것 같으니까.
에디: 1976년 200주년 이후로 이 옥상에 처음 올라와 봐. 페기랑 바로 여기서 불꽃놀이를 봤지.
프리야: 여기서요? 부두가 아니라요? 다들 물가로 가는 줄 알았어요.

토미: 근데 지금 저것 좀 봐요. 맨해튼이 생일 케이크처럼 불이 다 켜졌어요.

토미: 인상 안 썼는데요. 그냥 앉아 있었어요.

에스포지토 부인: 에디, 고마워요. 소음, 간판, 쓰레기, 맥주값에 대해서 불평했죠.
토미: 그리고 간판. 늘 말씀하시듯이, 2피트는 2피트.
