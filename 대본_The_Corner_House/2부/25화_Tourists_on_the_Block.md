# THE CORNER HOUSE — 2부 25화

## Tourists on the Block (블록 위의 관광객들)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 2부 7화(통산 25화) · 시기: 2026년 6월 말 · 난이도: B1~B2 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 밤 11시 반 건물 앞 계단 → 다음 날 바 The Hook(블록 협회 긴급회의)
> - 등장인물: MRS. ESPOSITO, EDDIE, TOMMY, AHMED, MARISOL, ZOE, LUKAS
> - 사건: 빨래방 위층 빈집이 불법 단기 숙소가 되어 매일 밤 여행 가방 소리가 난다. 블록 협회가 긴급회의를 연다. 매상이 오른 토미·아메드와 잠을 못 자는 에스포지토 부인, '새 이웃' 조이의 시선이 엇갈린다. 결국 '먼저 대화, 그다음 신고'로 합의.
> - 학습 포인트: 찬반과 양가감정(On the one hand… on the other hand…), 일반화(Most tourists are… / Tourists usually…), 표결 표현
> - 미국 현안: 뉴욕시 단기 임대 등록제(2023년 Local Law 18 — 30일 미만 임대는 등록 필수, 호스트 거주, 손님 2명까지), 과잉 관광, 빈집이 숙소가 되며 줄어드는 주택
> - 연결: 빨래방 위층은 플로리다로 간 델가도 부인의 집, 아들이 운영하는 것으로 추정. 루카스는 그린포인트의 합법 호스텔로. 조이가 '적당히 친절한' 편지를 쓰기로.

## 대본

[Scene: 11:30 p.m. The front stoop. Mrs. Esposito sits in her robe. Eddie opens his window above.]

MRS. ESPOSITO: Eddie. Eddie, look down at the street. It's happening again, and it's another group with suitcases.
EDDIE: Dolores, it's eleven thirty at night. What are you doing out on the stoop in your robe?
MRS. ESPOSITO: I'm counting suitcases, that's what. This is the fourth group this week, and they're all going into the apartment above the laundromat.
EDDIE: I heard the wheels rolling on the sidewalk from my bed. It sounded like a train going right under my window.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Every night there are new people. They type a code on the door, they go inside, and they're loud until two in the morning.
EDDIE: The German boy seems nice, though. His name is Lukas, and he watched the game with us in the bar.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Lukas is nice, I'll give him that. But last night there were eight people in one apartment. Eight! And they were all singing.
EDDIE: Singing what?
MRS. ESPOSITO: I have no idea, because it was in Portuguese. And they sang it very, very badly.
EDDIE: Ha. That sounds like The Hook on a Saturday night after the third round.
MRS. ESPOSITO: It's not funny, Eddie. They left their trash on the sidewalk, pizza boxes and bottles, right next to my bins.
EDDIE: Well, that's not nice. Somebody should tell them where the trash goes on this block.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And who even owns that apartment? Nobody lives there anymore, nobody at all. It used to be Mrs. Delgado's place.
EDDIE: Mrs. Delgado moved in with her daughter in Florida last year. I still miss seeing her on the block.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And now it's a hotel, a secret hotel, right here on our block. Can you believe it?
EDDIE: Is that even legal? I thought you needed some kind of permission to do something like that.
MRS. ESPOSITO: No, it's not legal! Marisol says there's been a law against it since twenty twenty-three.
EDDIE: Then why do people keep doing it, if it's against the law?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Money, of course. With the World Cup in town, they can charge three hundred dollars a night.
EDDIE: Three hundred a night? Mrs. Delgado paid eight hundred a month for that same apartment.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Exactly. And that's why there are no apartments left for young people like Jesse, who are looking everywhere.
EDDIE: Here comes another group now. I count five suitcases, and one of them is the size of a refrigerator.
MRS. ESPOSITO: That's it. Tomorrow morning, I'm calling an emergency block meeting, and everybody is coming.
EDDIE: At The Hook, I suppose?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Where else? Tommy has plenty of chairs, and he has coffee too. Bad coffee, but it's coffee.

[Scene: The next day, 10:00 a.m. The Hook, before opening. Chairs in a circle.]

MRS. ESPOSITO: Okay, everyone. Order, please. This is an emergency meeting of the block association, so phones away.
TOMMY: An emergency? Is there a fire I should know about? Because this is my bar.
MRS. ESPOSITO: The emergency is above the laundromat. Somebody is running an illegal hotel up there.
AHMED: Oh, I know the one. They come into my store at midnight and buy chips, water, and beer.
MARISOL: Okay, so here's the thing. Let me explain the law first, and then we can discuss it. Okay?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Go ahead. You're the expert here.
MARISOL: Since twenty twenty-three, short-term rentals have to be registered with the city. That means any stay under thirty days.
ZOE: Registered? You mean like a license, the way a restaurant needs one?
MARISOL: Yes, like a license. And the host has to live in the apartment during the stay, and there can only be two guests.
TOMMY: So renting out a whole empty apartment to strangers for a weekend is what, exactly?
MARISOL: It's illegal, totally illegal. The only exception is when the stay is for thirty days or more.
AHMED: But the websites still show them, don't they? I looked last night, and there were hundreds.
MARISOL: Some of them are hidden. The listing says "thirty-day minimum" on the site, and then the host tells you, "Just stay three days."
MRS. ESPOSITO: That's exactly what's happening here. So we report it to the city, and we do it today.
TOMMY: Wait, wait. Let's not move too fast, Dolores. We should think this through first.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Too fast? Tommy, they sing in Portuguese at two a.m., and I have to get up at six!
TOMMY: I know, I know. But listen. On the one hand, they're noisy. On the other hand, they spend money. Here, on this block.
AHMED: That's true, and I have the numbers to prove it. My sales are up thirty percent this month. Thirty!
TOMMY: Mine too. Between the World Cup and the tourists, it's the best June I've had in twenty years.
MRS. ESPOSITO: So you're saying money is more important than sleep? More important than my sleep?
TOMMY: That's not what I said. I said it's complicated, and it really is.
ZOE: Can I say something? I'm kind of new here, so maybe I see it a little differently than you do.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Go ahead, dear. You're the one from the glass building, right?
ZOE: I am. And honestly, some people look at me like I'm a tourist too, just because I'm new.
MARISOL: That's... fair. Some people do, and I'm sorry about that.
ZOE: But I live here. I pay rent here, and I'm on this block every single day. Tourists are here for three days, and then they're gone. That's the difference.
TOMMY: So what do you think we should do about it?
ZOE: I think tourists are fine. Most tourists are nice. The problem is the apartment, because it should be a home for someone who stays.
MARISOL: That's exactly the point. Every illegal hotel is one less apartment for a family, or for somebody like Jesse.
AHMED: But it's just one apartment. If we're honest, does it really change anything?
MARISOL: It's one apartment on this block, but there are thousands in the city. Before the law, there were tens of thousands of listings.
AHMED: Hm. Tens of thousands. That's a lot of homes that nobody actually lives in.
TOMMY: Okay, I get it. But what about the people who come for the World Cup? Where are they supposed to sleep?
MARISOL: In hotels, or in legal rentals where the host actually lives in the apartment.
TOMMY: Hotels in Manhattan cost five hundred a night right now, and that's for a tiny room.
ZOE: Tourists usually have more money than the people who live here. I think they can handle it.
TOMMY: Not the young ones, though. Lukas is a student, and he's counting every dollar.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Lukas is not the problem. The host is the problem, because someone is making money from an empty home.
AHMED: So who is the host? Does anybody actually know who's behind it?
MRS. ESPOSITO: I saw him once, picking up the keys from the lockbox. He was a young man with a very nice car.
MARISOL: The building belongs to the Delgado family, so maybe the son is renting it out while his mother is in Florida.
AHMED: The Delgado son? He comes into my store all the time, and he tips well.
TOMMY: See? Everybody who tips well is suddenly a good person in this neighborhood.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Okay, enough talking. Let's vote. Who wants to report it to the city? Raise your hand.
MARISOL: Wait. Before we vote, what about talking to the Delgado son first? Neighbor to neighbor, before we call anybody.
ZOE: I like that idea. Reporting can be the second step, and talking can be the first.
TOMMY: That's very Brooklyn. You talk first, and then, if that doesn't work, you call the city.
MRS. ESPOSITO: And if he doesn't listen to us? What do we do then?
MARISOL: Then we report it, with photos, dates, everything. We'll make it very easy for the city.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I already have photos. I have thirty-two photos of suitcases on my phone.
AHMED: Thirty-two photos of suitcases? Dolores, with all respect, you need a hobby.
MRS. ESPOSITO: This is my hobby.
TOMMY: Okay. I can live with that plan. We talk first, and we save the report for later.
AHMED: Me too, talk first. But slowly, please, until the World Cup is over.
MARISOL: Ahmed!
AHMED: I'm joking. Half joking. Okay, maybe a third joking.
ZOE: I'll write him a short letter. Friendly but clear. I'm good at that, because I write for a nonprofit.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Friendly but clear. Okay, that sounds good. But not too friendly, or he won't take us seriously.
ZOE: Medium friendly.
MARISOL: Good. So the plan is a letter to the Delgado son, then a meeting with him, and then, if needed, a report.
TOMMY: All in favor? Raise your hands nice and high.
MRS. ESPOSITO: In favor.
AHMED: In favor.
ZOE: In favor.
TOMMY: In favor. That makes it unanimous. Write it down, Madam Secretary.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Already done.
MARISOL: One more thing. This is about the apartment, not about the tourists. So let's be nice to the tourists, okay?
MRS. ESPOSITO: I'm always nice.
TOMMY: Dolores...
MRS. ESPOSITO: I'm mostly nice.

[The door opens. Lukas, with a big suitcase.]

LUKAS: Hello, everyone. Sorry to interrupt your meeting. Tommy, do you have a minute?
TOMMY: Lukas! Sure, come in. What's wrong? You look really stressed.
LUKAS: My host canceled this morning. He said I have to leave by noon, because there was "a problem with the neighbors."
MRS. ESPOSITO: Oh.
ZOE: Oh no.
LUKAS: I don't understand. I was always quiet, and I always took my trash inside, like he told me.
MRS. ESPOSITO: It wasn't you, Lukas. It was the others, the singers. You were the quiet one.
LUKAS: And he refunded only half of my money. I still have two more games next week, in New Jersey.
MARISOL: I'm so sorry, Lukas. That host was renting illegally, and you didn't know that. It's not your fault.
LUKAS: Illegal? But he had five-star reviews! Everybody said he was a great host.
TOMMY: Five stars and zero license. So where are you going to stay now?
LUKAS: I don't know yet. The hotels are five hundred a night, and I'm only a student.
ZOE: There's a hostel in Greenpoint that's totally legal. It's sixty a night, and I have a friend who works there.
LUKAS: Sixty! That's possible, even for me. Thank you so much!
MARISOL: And this time, keep every receipt. Always ask for a receipt, so you can prove what you paid.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Lukas, I'm sorry. Maybe I helped start all this, because I took a lot of pictures.
LUKAS: Pictures? Of me?
MRS. ESPOSITO: Of your suitcase. It's a very nice suitcase, by the way. Very good wheels.
LUKAS: Thank you. I think.
MRS. ESPOSITO: On the one hand, you're a tourist. On the other hand, you're a very nice boy, and you take your trash inside.
LUKAS: You know, in Germany we have the same problem. In Berlin, people are very angry about apartments that are only for tourists.
MARISOL: It's the same problem everywhere, in every city that people love to visit.
TOMMY: Leave your suitcase here, behind the bar. Go see the hostel, and come back later to pick it up.
LUKAS: Really? Thank you, Tommy. You're the best host in New York.
TOMMY: I'm a bar, not a hotel. But for you, the suitcase can stay, with a license from Dolores.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **On the one hand, … On the other hand, …** — "한편으로는 ~, 다른 한편으로는 ~." 장단점을 나란히 놓을 때. (On the one hand, they're noisy. On the other hand, they spend money.)
- **It's complicated.** — "복잡해." 한쪽 편을 들기 어려울 때.
- **일반화 표현** — *Most tourists are nice. / Tourists usually have more money than the people who live here. / Some people look at me like I'm a tourist too / Everybody who tips well is suddenly a good person* most, usually, some으로 일반화의 강도를 조절한다.
- **That's exactly the point.** — "바로 그게 핵심이야."
- **one less apartment** — 집이 한 채 줄어드는 것.
- **short-term rental / register / a license / a lockbox** — 단기 임대 / 등록하다 / 허가 / 열쇠 보관함.
- **rent (something) out** — 세를 놓다.
- **neighbor to neighbor** — 이웃 대 이웃으로, 공식 절차 전에 직접.
- **I can live with that plan.** — "그 계획이면 받아들일 수 있어."
- **Order, please. / All in favor? / In favor. / Unanimous.** — 회의 진행 표현. "정숙해 주세요." "찬성하는 분?" "찬성." "만장일치."
- **Half joking.** — "반은 농담이야."
- **medium friendly** — 조이가 만든 말. "적당히 친절하게."

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- **On the one hand, | they're noisy. | On the other hand, | they spend money.** — 두 덩어리를 같은 높이로 시작해 대칭을 만든다. hand 뒤에서 쉰다.
- **registered** [레지스터r드], **unanimous** [유**내**너머스] — 둘째 음절 강세.
- **thirty percent / thirty days / thirty-two photos** — 플랩 T [써r리].
- 부인의 *Exactly.* *That's it.*은 짧고 단호하게, 아메드의 농담은 가볍게.
- 표결 장면 *In favor. / In favor. / In favor.*는 리듬이 핵심. 같은 말을 각자 다른 목소리로.
- **Oh. / Oh no.** — 루카스의 말에 부인과 조이가 동시에 놀라며 미안해하는 짧은 반응.
