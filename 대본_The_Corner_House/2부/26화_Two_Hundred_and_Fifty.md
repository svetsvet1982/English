# THE CORNER HOUSE — 2부 26화

## Two Hundred and Fifty (250주년)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 2부 8화(통산 26화) · 시기: 2026년 7월 4일(토), 미국 건국 250주년 독립기념일 · 난이도: B1~B2 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 레드훅 부두(오전, 범선 행렬) → 건물 뒷마당 바비큐(오후) → 부두(밤, 불꽃놀이)
> - 등장인물: DIEGO, EDDIE, MARISOL, TOMMY, PRIYA, SAM, ARTURO
> - 사건: 뉴욕항에 세계 각국의 대형 범선이 모인다. 에디는 50년 전 1976년 건국 200주년 때 같은 부두에서 범선을 봤다. 오후 바비큐에서 각자의 이민 내력을 나누고, 프리야와 샘이 결혼 소식을 전한다.
> - 학습 포인트: 행사·역사 어휘, 큰 숫자와 연도 읽기, 감탄, fewer vs less
> - 미국 현안: 건국 250주년(2026), 이민자의 나라라는 정체성(정치적 논쟁 없이 각자의 이야기로)
> - 연결: 프리야·샘 내년 봄 결혼, 부두 결혼식 아이디어 → 85화. 양복 남자가 화요일에 또 왔음. 디에고의 약속: 2076년에 이 부두로.

## 대본

[Scene: Saturday, July 4, 10:00 a.m. The pier in Red Hook. Crowds by the railing. Tall ships on the harbor.]

DIEGO: Eddie! Look! There's another one! With three masts!
EDDIE: That's a tall ship, kid. A real sailing ship. Like the old days.
MARISOL: How many are there? I counted twenty already.
EDDIE: The radio said more than forty. From all over the world. For the two hundred and fiftieth birthday.
DIEGO: America is two hundred and fifty years old?
EDDIE: Today. July fourth, twenty twenty-six. Seventeen seventy-six plus two hundred fifty.
DIEGO: That's so old! Older than you!
EDDIE: A little older than me. Not much.
MARISOL: Diego, what happened in seventeen seventy-six? You learned it in school.
DIEGO: The Declaration of Independence! They signed a paper. In Philadelphia.
EDDIE: Good. And what did the paper say?
DIEGO: That America was free from England. And... "all men are created equal."
MARISOL: Very good. And women too, we hope.
EDDIE: It took a while for that part. A long while.
DIEGO: Look at that one! The sails are white and red!
EDDIE: I think that one's from Spain. Look at the flag in the back.
MARISOL: It's so beautiful. All the sails on the water. With the Statue of Liberty right there.
EDDIE: You know, I stood on this pier fifty years ago. On the same day.
DIEGO: Fifty years ago? In nineteen... seventy-six?
EDDIE: Nineteen seventy-six. The Bicentennial. Two hundred years. They had tall ships then too. Operation Sail.
MARISOL: You saw this before? The same thing?
EDDIE: Almost the same. I was thirty-three. Peggy was next to me. Michael was on my shoulders. He was four.
DIEGO: Your son was four? Now he's old!
EDDIE: Now he's fifty-four. With a daughter in college.
MARISOL: What was it like? In seventy-six?
EDDIE: Crowded. Hot. Like today. But the pier was different. It was a working pier. Ships, cargo, longshoremen.
DIEGO: You worked here?
EDDIE: Right there. Where the IKEA is now. That was all docks. I unloaded coffee from Brazil. Bananas from Ecuador.
MARISOL: And now people buy furniture there.
EDDIE: Now people buy furniture and eat meatballs. Times change.
DIEGO: Were there fireworks? In seventy-six?
EDDIE: Huge fireworks. Over the harbor. The biggest I ever saw. Until tonight, maybe.
MARISOL: Are you coming tonight? To see them from here?
EDDIE: I wouldn't miss it. Fifty years. Same pier. Different knees.
DIEGO: Eddie, how many people live in America?
EDDIE: Ask your mother. She's smarter than me.
MARISOL: About three hundred and forty million. In seventeen seventy-six, about two and a half million.
DIEGO: Two and a half million? That's less than Brooklyn!
MARISOL: Exactly. Brooklyn has about two and a half million people. The whole country was the size of Brooklyn.
EDDIE: And they all spoke with Brooklyn accents, probably.
DIEGO: Really?
EDDIE: No. I'm kidding.
MARISOL: Okay, let's go. Tommy is starting the barbecue at two.
DIEGO: Bye, ships! See you in two hundred fifty years!
EDDIE: Make it fifty, kid. Then you can tell your grandkids.

[Scene: 3:00 p.m. The backyard behind the building. Tommy at an old grill. Flags on the fence.]

TOMMY: Burgers are almost ready! Hot dogs in five minutes! Who wants cheese?
PRIYA: Me! Cheese on everything. It's a holiday.
SAM: Tommy, these smell amazing. What's in them?
TOMMY: Secret. Okay, salt, pepper, and a little love. And a grill from nineteen ninety-eight.
MARISOL: Priya, Sam, have you met my dad? Papi, this is Priya and Sam. From Four B.
ARTURO: Nice to meet you. Mucho gusto.
PRIYA: Nice to meet you too! Happy Fourth!
ARTURO: Happy Fourth. My forty-second Fourth of July in America.
SAM: Forty-second! You've seen more than me. I'm only thirty-four.
ARTURO: And where are your families from? If I can ask.
PRIYA: My parents came from India. From Gujarat. In nineteen ninety. They opened a pharmacy in Edison, New Jersey.
ARTURO: A pharmacy! Hard work.
PRIYA: Very hard. Seven days a week. My dad still works there. He's sixty-eight.
SAM: My family's been here longer. My great-great-grandparents came from Ireland and Germany. In the eighteen eighties. To Ohio.
TOMMY: And mine came from Naples. In nineteen ten. To Columbia Street. Right over there.
MARISOL: So everyone at this table came from somewhere.
TOMMY: Everyone in this city came from somewhere. Except the people who were here first.
ARTURO: That's true. Very true.
PRIYA: My dad says the Fourth of July is his favorite holiday. More than Diwali. Don't tell my mom.
SAM: Why?
PRIYA: He says on Diwali, he celebrates where he came from. On the Fourth, he celebrates where he chose to be.
ARTURO: Your father is a wise man. That's exactly how I feel.
MARISOL: Papi, really?
ARTURO: Really. I chose this place. Even when it's hard. Even when the news is scary. I chose it.
TOMMY: That's a good toast. Raise your drinks. To choosing.
PRIYA: To choosing!
SAM: To choosing. And to Tommy's burgers.
TOMMY: Thank you. Okay, Sam, take this plate. Careful, it's hot.
SAM: Thanks. Hey, Tommy, any news about the building? Priya told me about the note.
TOMMY: No news. Richie doesn't call back. The guy in the suit came again on Tuesday.
PRIYA: Our lease ends in December. We were thinking about... well, about staying longer. Maybe for a long time.
MARISOL: For a long time? Why? What's happening?
PRIYA: We're getting married. Next spring!
MARISOL: Oh my God! Congratulations!
TOMMY: Married! On the Fourth of July we find out! Mazel tov! Congratulations!
SAM: Thanks. We wanted to tell everybody today. A good day for news.
ARTURO: ¡Felicidades! Where is the wedding?
PRIYA: We don't know yet. Somewhere with water. Maybe even here. The pier.
TOMMY: On the pier! With the Statue of Liberty behind you!
MARISOL: That would be perfect.

[Scene: 9:28 p.m. The pier again. Darkness. Everyone looks at the sky.]

DIEGO: Is it starting? Is it starting?
EDDIE: Patience. Nine thirty. Two more minutes.
TOMMY: The whole neighborhood is here. Look at this crowd.
MARISOL: Everybody from the block. Ahmed. Keisha and Malik. Even the glass building.
EDDIE: Fifty years ago, there were more people. But less phones.
DIEGO: Fewer phones, Eddie.
EDDIE: Fewer phones. Thank you, professor.
PRIYA: Look! Over there! The first one!
DIEGO: Whoa! It's huge! It's red!
TOMMY: Here they come. Oh, wow. Look at that.
MARISOL: Gold ones! Like rain! Gold rain!
DIEGO: That one was a heart! A heart in the sky!
EDDIE: In seventy-six, there were no hearts. Just big circles. Boom, boom.
PRIYA: Listen to the crowd. Everybody's saying "oooh."
TOMMY: "Oooh" is the same in every language.
DIEGO: Red, white, and blue! All together!
EDDIE: That's the one. That's the big one.
MARISOL: Eddie, are you okay? You're quiet.
EDDIE: I'm okay. I'm just... I'm thinking about Peggy. She stood right here.
MARISOL: She would love this.
EDDIE: She would. She'd say, "Edward, it's too loud." And then she'd hold my hand.
DIEGO: I'll hold your hand, Eddie. If you want.
EDDIE: Thank you, kid. I'd like that.
TOMMY: Look. The ships are lit up too. All the masts. With little lights.
PRIYA: It's like a painting. A moving painting.
DIEGO: Eddie, in fifty years, will there be fireworks again? For three hundred?
EDDIE: In twenty seventy-six. Three hundred years. Yes. Bigger, probably.
DIEGO: I'll be sixty! I'll be old!
EDDIE: Sixty is not old. Sixty is the middle.
MARISOL: Will you come here? To this pier?
DIEGO: Yes. And I'll bring my kids. And I'll tell them about Eddie.
EDDIE: Tell them I said the coffee from Brazil came right here. On this pier.
DIEGO: And that you saw the tall ships twice!
EDDIE: Twice. Not many people can say that.
TOMMY: Happy two hundred fiftieth, everybody. Happy birthday, America.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **역사 어휘** — *the Declaration of Independence*(독립선언서), *sign*(서명하다), *free from England*, *all men are created equal*(모든 사람은 평등하게 태어났다), *the Bicentennial*(200주년), *the two hundred and fiftieth (birthday)*(250주년).
- **연도·큰 숫자 읽기** — *seventeen seventy-six, nineteen seventy-six, twenty twenty-six, twenty seventy-six* / *three hundred and forty million, two and a half million*.
- **서수 + 행사** — *my forty-second Fourth of July, the two hundred and fiftieth birthday*.
- **fewer vs less** — 셀 수 있는 명사는 fewer(fewer phones), 셀 수 없는 명사는 less(less noise). 구어에서는 less를 섞어 쓰는 사람도 많아서 디에고가 '교수님'처럼 고쳐 준다.
- **It took a while.** — "시간이 좀 걸렸지."
- **I wouldn't miss it.** — "절대 놓치지 않을 거야."
- **Make it fifty.** — "50으로 하자(50년 뒤로 정정)."
- **Mucho gusto. / ¡Felicidades! / Mazel tov!** — 스페인어 "반가워요", "축하해요", 이디시어 "축하해"(뉴욕 사람들이 출신과 상관없이 쓴다).
- **Raise your drinks. To ~!** — 건배 표현.
- **Patience.** — "참아, 기다려."
- **lit up** — 불이 밝혀진.
- **감탄** — *Whoa! / Look at that. / Oh, wow. / That's the big one.*

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- **연도는 두 자리씩**: *seventeen | seventy-six*, *twenty | twenty-six*. 200주년 *Bicentennial* [바이센**테**니얼].
- **two hundred and fiftieth** [투 헌드러드 앤 **피**프티어θ] — 마지막 th를 혀끝으로.
- **Declaration of Independence** [데클러**레**이션 어브 인디**펜**던스] — 두 군데 강세.
- 불꽃놀이 장면은 짧은 감탄을 높게, 에디의 회상은 낮고 느리게. 대비가 장면을 만든다.
- **"Oooh"**는 다 같이 길게 내린다.
- 마지막 대사 *Happy birthday, America.*는 생일 축하처럼 따뜻하게.
