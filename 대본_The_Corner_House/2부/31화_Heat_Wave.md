# THE CORNER HOUSE — 2부 31화

## Heat Wave (폭염)

> **표기 전용 정보 (낭독하지 않음)**
> - 부/화: 2부 13화(통산 31화) · 시기: 2026년 7월 말, 수요일 · 난이도: B1~B2 · 턴 수: 120
> - 장소: 바 The Hook(오전) → 3A 에디의 집(오후 2시) → 레드훅 도서관 무더위 쉼터
> - 등장인물: TASHA, TOMMY, DIEGO, MRS. ESPOSITO, EDDIE, MARISOL
> - 사건: 체감 42도의 폭염 경보. 에디의 창문형 에어컨이 밤새 고장 나 실내가 34도까지 오른다. 에스포지토 부인과 디에고가 고집 센 에디를 도서관 무더위 쉼터로 데려가고, 마리솔이 시의 무료 에어컨 지원을 신청해 준다. 동네 전력이 깜빡인다.

[Scene: Wednesday, 10:00 a.m. The Hook. The old air conditioner roars. Tasha fans herself with a menu.]

DIEGO: Oh. Oh, that's amazing. I'm never leaving, not even for lunch.

TOMMY: It's working. It's from twenty eleven, so it's tired, just like me.
TASHA: The city sent a heat advisory to my phone. It says to stay inside, drink water, and check on your neighbors.
TOMMY: Check on your neighbors. Eddie. Wait, did anybody check on Eddie today?
DIEGO: I'll check! It's almost two, and that's Biscuit's visiting hours, so Mrs. Esposito and I always go together.
TOMMY: Good. And take him a big bottle of cold water from the cooler. Actually, take two bottles.
TASHA: You know what my Con Ed bill was last month? Three hundred and ten dollars, and that's for a one-bedroom.
TOMMY: Three ten? Mine was twenty-four hundred for the bar, because of the coolers, the AC, and the ice machine.
TASHA: Twenty-four hundred! For one month!
TOMMY: In July, electricity is my biggest cost after rent, and it goes up every year.
DIEGO: Why does it go up every year? The sun is the same.
TOMMY: Because everybody uses more AC, and summers keep getting hotter. And Con Ed says the power lines are old, so they need to fix them.
TASHA: Everything in this city is old. The trains, the power lines, the boilers in every building.
TOMMY: And me.
TASHA: And you. And Eddie, although he'd never admit it.
DIEGO: Is the heat really dangerous? Or do grown-ups just say that?
TOMMY: It's dangerous for old people, yes, and for little kids. And for people who work outside all day, like the delivery guys.
TASHA: Marcus says three guys almost fainted yesterday while they were riding their bikes.
TOMMY: Tell Marcus to send them here. They can have free water, all day, as much as they want.
DIEGO: I'm going to Eddie's now. Can I take the water with me?
TOMMY: Take it. And call me if anything's wrong over there. I mean anything, okay?

EDDIE: That thermometer is older than you are. It always says more than it really is.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Eddie. Look at me. You're red, and you're sweating. When did you last drink some water?

MRS. ESPOSITO: Coffee is not water! Diego, give him the bottle right now.

EDDIE: Thank you, kid. Okay... Ah. That's good. That's really cold.

MRS. ESPOSITO: No law? In this heat?
MARISOL: Not yet. There's a debate, and some people want a "maximum temperature" law. It would be like the heat law, but for summer.
EDDIE: That makes sense, because summers are getting worse. When I was a kid, ninety degrees was rare, and people talked about it for weeks.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Look up there. The lights are flickering.
MARISOL: Uh-oh. There are too many air conditioners running in the neighborhood, so the power is struggling.
DIEGO: Is it a blackout? Like the one in nineteen seventy-seven?
EDDIE: No, no. In nineteen seventy-seven, it went completely black, and it stayed black all night. This is just a flicker.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Con Ed asked everybody to use less power this afternoon. I got a text about it at lunch.
MARISOL: But everybody's using more, because it's ninety-eight degrees outside.
EDDIE: That's the problem with heat. Everybody needs the same thing at the same time, and there isn't enough.
MARISOL: Okay, the lights are back, good. Eddie, let's start the application. Where do you keep your Social Security letter?
EDDIE: It's in my apartment, in a kitchen drawer with Peggy's old recipes.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I'll get it later, when it's cooler. Don't you dare go back there now.
EDDIE: Yes, ma'am. Bossy.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Organized.
DIEGO: Eddie, while we wait for the website, can you tell me the blackout story from nineteen seventy-seven?
EDDIE: Oh, that's a good one. It was July, and it was hot like today. And at nine thirty at night, the whole city went dark.
DIEGO: The whole city? Even Manhattan?
EDDIE: Every light and every train stopped at once. Peggy and I sat on the stoop with a candle, and all the neighbors came out to sit with us.
MARISOL: That sounds like today, actually. Everyone is together in one place, just trying to stay cool.
EDDIE: That's New York. When it's too hot to be alone, we sit together, and somebody always brings the water.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **더위 어휘** — *ninety-eight degrees(약 37°C), it'll feel like a hundred and eight(체감 약 42°C), the humidity(습도), a heat advisory(폭염 주의보), a cooling center(무더위 쉼터), dizzy(어지러운), sweating(땀을 흘리는)*.
- **과장 표현** — *I'm literally melting. / I'm a puddle. / I'm never leaving, not even for lunch. / It sounds like a helicopter that's about to crash.* literally는 원래 '글자 그대로'지만 구어에서 강조어로 과장에 자주 쓴다.
- **불평 표현** — *Everything in this city is old. / It goes up every year. / No law? In this heat?*
- **Check on your neighbors.** — "이웃이 괜찮은지 살펴보세요." check on + 사람.
- **Don't mind the mess.** — "지저분해도 신경 쓰지 마."
- **on paper** — 서류상으로는.
- **That's a good point.** — "좋은 지적이네(반박할 수 없네)."
- **Luck won't help you. Cotton will.** — "운은 널 못 도와. 면 옷은 도와주지." 대조로 설득하는 문장.
- **I'll admit it.** — "인정할게."
- **Don't you dare (go back there).** — "감히 (거기 돌아가기만) 해 봐." 강한 금지.
- **bossy / organized** — 이래라저래라 하는 / 체계적인. 같은 행동을 보는 두 관점.
- **flicker / a blackout** — 깜빡이다 / 대정전.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- **literally** [**리**러럴리] — 플랩 T. 과장할 때는 첫 음절을 길게 끈다.
- **humidity** [휴**미**더리], **advisory** [어드**바**이저리], **thermometer** [써r**마**미러r].
- 온도는 숫자만으로: *ninety-one, ninety-three, ninety-eight, a hundred and eight*. 다 섭씨가 아니라 화씨.
- 에디의 고집(*I'm fine. / That's my age.*)은 느긋하게, 부인의 반박(*That's not your age. That's the heat.*)은 빠르고 단호하게.
- **Bossy. / Organized.** — 각각 한 단어로 툭. 두 번째 반복에서는 둘 다 웃음을 참으며.
- 1977년 정전 이야기는 이야기꾼처럼 천천히, *the whole city went dark*에서 목소리를 낮춘다.
