[Scene: Wednesday, 10:00 a.m. The Hook. The old air conditioner roars. Tasha fans herself with a menu.]

TASHA: Tommy, it's ten a.m. and it's already ninety-one degrees. Ninety-one!
TOMMY: The radio says ninety-eight this afternoon. It'll feel like a hundred and eight. With the humidity.
TASHA: A hundred and eight. I'm literally melting. Look at me. I'm a puddle.
TOMMY: You're not a puddle. You're a little shiny.
DIEGO: Tommy, can I stay here today? It's so hot upstairs. Our fan just blows hot air.
TOMMY: Sure, kid. The bar has air conditioning. Sit there. Under the vent.
DIEGO: Oh. Oh, that's amazing. I'm never leaving.
TASHA: Is that AC even working? It sounds like a helicopter.
TOMMY: It's working. It's from twenty eleven. It's tired. Like me.
TASHA: The city sent a heat advisory. Stay inside. Drink water. Check on your neighbors.
TOMMY: Check on your neighbors. Eddie. Did anybody check on Eddie today?
DIEGO: I'll check! It's almost two. Biscuit's visiting hours. Mrs. Esposito and I go together.
TOMMY: Good. And take him a big bottle of water. Two bottles.
TASHA: You know what my Con Ed bill was last month? Three hundred and ten dollars. For a one-bedroom.
TOMMY: Three ten? Mine was twenty-four hundred. For the bar. The coolers, the AC, the ice machine.
TASHA: Twenty-four hundred! For one month!
TOMMY: In July, electricity is my biggest cost after rent. It goes up every year.
DIEGO: Why does it go up?
TOMMY: Everybody uses more AC. Summers get hotter. And Con Ed says the power lines are old.
TASHA: Everything in this city is old. The trains, the power lines, the boilers.
TOMMY: And me.
TASHA: And you. And Eddie.
DIEGO: Is it dangerous? The heat?
TOMMY: For old people, yes. And little kids. And people who work outside. Like the delivery guys.
TASHA: Marcus says three guys almost fainted yesterday. On their bikes.
TOMMY: Tell Marcus to send them here. Free water. All day.
DIEGO: I'm going to Eddie's. Can I take the water now?
TOMMY: Take it. And call me if anything's wrong. Anything.

MRS. ESPOSITO: That's not your age. That's the heat. That's how it starts.

MRS. ESPOSITO: Con Ed asked everybody to use less power this afternoon. I got a text.
MARISOL: But everybody's using more. Because it's ninety-eight degrees.
EDDIE: That's the problem with heat. Everybody needs the same thing at the same time.
MARISOL: Okay, the lights are back. Good. Eddie, let's start the application. Where's your Social Security letter?
EDDIE: It's in my apartment. In a drawer. With Peggy's recipes.
MRS. ESPOSITO: I'll get it later. When it's cooler. Don't you dare go back there now.
EDDIE: Yes, ma'am. Bossy.
MRS. ESPOSITO: Organized.
DIEGO: Eddie, while we wait, can you tell me the blackout story? Nineteen seventy-seven?
EDDIE: Oh, that's a good one. It was July. Hot like today. And at nine thirty at night, the whole city went dark.
DIEGO: The whole city?
EDDIE: Every light. Every train. Peggy and I sat on the stoop with a candle. All the neighbors came out.
MARISOL: Sounds like today. Everyone together. Trying to stay cool.
EDDIE: That's New York. When it's too hot to be alone, we sit together.

## 표현 정리 (낭독하지 않음)

- **더위 어휘** — *ninety-eight degrees(약 37°C), it'll feel like a hundred and eight(체감 약 42°C), the humidity(습도), a heat advisory(폭염 주의보), a cooling center(무더위 쉼터), dizzy(어지러운), sweating(땀을 흘리는)*.
- **과장 표현** — *I'm literally melting. / I'm a puddle. / I'm never leaving. / It sounds like a helicopter.* literally는 원래 '글자 그대로'지만 구어에서 강조어로 과장에 자주 쓴다.
- **불평 표현** — *Everything in this city is old. / It goes up every year. / No law? In this heat?*
- **Check on your neighbors.** — "이웃이 괜찮은지 살펴보세요." check on + 사람.
- **Don't mind the mess.** — "지저분해도 신경 쓰지 마."
- **on paper** — 서류상으로는.
- **That's a good point.** — "좋은 지적이네(반박할 수 없네)."
- **Luck won't help you. Cotton will.** — "운은 널 못 도와. 면 옷은 도와주지." 대조로 설득하는 문장.
- **I'll admit it.** — "인정할게."
- **Don't you dare (go back there).** — "감히 (거기 돌아가기만) 해 봐." 강한 금지.
- **bossy / organized** — 이래라저래라 하는 / 체계적인. 같은 행동을 보는 두 관점.
- **flicker / a blackout** — 깜빡이다 / 대정전.

## 낭독 포인트 (낭독하지 않음)

- **literally** [**리**러럴리] — 플랩 T. 과장할 때는 첫 음절을 길게 끈다.
- **humidity** [휴**미**더리], **advisory** [어드**바**이저리], **thermometer** [써r**마**미러r].
- 온도는 숫자만으로: *ninety-one, ninety-three, ninety-eight, a hundred and eight*. 다 섭씨가 아니라 화씨.
- 에디의 고집(*I'm fine. / That's my age.*)은 느긋하게, 부인의 반박(*That's not your age. That's the heat.*)은 빠르고 단호하게.
- **Bossy. / Organized.** — 각각 한 단어로 툭. 두 번째 반복에서는 둘 다 웃음을 참으며.
- 1977년 정전 이야기는 이야기꾼처럼 천천히, *the whole city went dark*에서 목소리를 낮춘다.
